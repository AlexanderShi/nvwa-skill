### S063 · Generation of disjointly constrained bilinear programming test problems (Computational Optimization and Applications 1992, DOI 10.1007/bf00249639)
**Read**: abstract · **Coauthors**: P. H. Calamai, J. J. Júdice · homepage: COAP 1 (1992) 299–306
- **D1**: Bilinear programs with disjoint constraints need test problems "with known solutions and properties" (abstract).
- **D2**: Disjoint low-dimensional bilinear programs are combined into a separable problem, and a simple random change of variables then hides the separability (abstract).
- **D8**: A test-problem construction technique; the abstract makes no algorithmic or theoretical claim (abstract).
- **Method links**: new-pattern candidate: controlled-difficulty test-problem generators ✅ (abstract). Method 5 ⚠ variant (abstract): here the testbed is *generated* with known answers. It is not an assembled collection with profiles.
- Transferable technique: to get large test instances with a known optimum, assemble them from tiny subproblems you can solve by hand. Then apply a random transformation of variables so that the structure is not visible to the solver (abstract).
- Quote (abstract): "The proposed construction technique applies a simple random transformation of variables to a separable bilinear programming problem that is constructed by combining disjoint low-dimensional bilinear programs."

### S100 · Parametric linear programming techniques for the indefinite quadratic programming problem (IMA Journal of Management Mathematics 1992, DOI 10.1093/imaman/4.4.343)
**Read**: abstract · **Coauthors**: J. J. Júdice, P. M. Pardalos · homepage: IMA J. Math. Appl. Business & Industry 4 (1993) 343–349, so the year is 1993, not 1992
- **D1**: Finding a global minimum of an indefinite QP (abstract).
- **D2**: A multiparametric LP technique built on the spectral decomposition of the quadratic form's matrix. When the matrix has rank 1, the problem reduces to a single-parameter LP, solved by an extended linear parametric procedure (abstract).
- **D8**: The extended procedure is efficient in general. The paper also gives an example on which it takes an exponential number of steps (abstract).
- **Method links**: new-pattern candidate: state where you lose ✅ (abstract; a worst-case example against the paper's own method). Method 2 ⚠ variant (abstract): the worst case here is a combinatorial step count, not an evaluation-complexity bound.
- Transferable technique: next to a method that works well in practice, publish the instance family on which it is exponential (abstract).
- Quote (abstract): "However, an example is presented showing that this technique may take an exponential number of steps."

### S033 · Generating linear and linear-quadratic bilevel programming problems (SIAM Journal on Scientific Computing 1993, DOI 10.1137/0914049)
**Read**: abstract · **Coauthors**: P. H. Calamai · homepage: SISC 14 (1993) 770–782
- **D1**: Test problems are needed for linear and linear-quadratic bilevel programs (abstract).
- **D2**: Single-parameter, two-variable problems are combined into a separable multivariable problem, and the separability is then disguised by a simple transformation (abstract).
- **D8**: The generator costs little computation and gives the user control over the number and type of minima and over data density (abstract).
- **Method links**: new-pattern candidate: controlled-difficulty test-problem generators ✅ (abstract). This is the second generator in this batch, alongside S063; S026, S053 and Algorithm 728 are already in the corpus. Method 5 ⚠ variant (abstract): a generated testbed, with no profiles mentioned.
- Transferable technique: expose difficulty knobs (number and type of local minima, data density) as generator parameters. Experiments can then vary one property at a time (abstract).
- Quotes (abstract): "The separability is then disguised using a simple transformation." · "The proposed technique, which requires very little computational effort, allows the user control over, among other things, the number and type of minima and the data density."

### S004 · Descent approaches for quadratic bilevel programming (Journal of Optimization Theory and Applications 1994, DOI 10.1007/bf02191670)
**Read**: abstract · **Role**: core · **Coauthors**: G. Savard (GERAD), J. J. Júdice · homepage: JOTA 81 (1994) 379–399. It first appeared as GERAD Cahier G-92-36 (Sep 1992), whose abstract matches.
- **D1**: Bilevel programs whose inner problem is strictly convex quadratic (abstract).
- **D2**: A pivot-step descent method, which may miss local optimality, is paired with a modified steepest-descent method that fixes this. The paper adds new exact step-size rules and a hybrid of the two methods (abstract).
- **D8**: Two algorithms and a hybrid, plus a proof that checking local optimality in bilevel programming is NP-hard (abstract).
- **Method links**: Method 1 ⚠ variant, an early precursor (abstract): a fast step generator without a guarantee is combined with a method that restores the guarantee. But the guarantee is local optimality, and there is no poll or sufficient-decrease skeleton. New-pattern candidate: prove the problem hard beside the algorithm (abstract; also S069).
- Transferable technique: if your fast method lacks a guarantee, present (i) the fast method, (ii) a guaranteed method and (iii) a hybrid that switches between them, and compare all three (abstract).
- Quotes (abstract): "The first algorithm is based on pivot steps and may not guarantee local optimality." · "It is proved that checking local optimality in bilevel programming is a NP-hard problem."

### S021 · A comparison of block pivoting and interior-point algorithms for linear least squares problems with nonnegative variables (Mathematics of Computation 1994, DOI 10.1090/s0025-5718-1994-1250776-4)
**Read**: abstract · **Role**: core · **Coauthors**: L. F. Portugal, J. J. Júdice · homepage: Math. Comp. 63 (1994) 625–643
- **D1**: Large-scale linear least squares with nonnegative variables (NVLSQ) (abstract).
- **D2**: Block principal pivoting is compared with a predictor-corrector interior-point method. Each is implemented with normal equations and with corrected seminormal equations (CSNE) (abstract).
- **D8**: Implementation advice: use normal equations for predictor-corrector. For block pivoting also use them, but possibly switch to CSNE in the last iterations. Computational experience shows both methods are efficient at large scale (abstract).
- **Method links**: Method 5 ⚠ variant (abstract): a computational comparison with implementation-level recommendations. The abstract mentions no released solver and no performance profiles.
- Transferable technique: compare algorithms × linear-algebra back-ends as a grid, and make the recommendation phase-dependent (for example, switch the linear solver near convergence) rather than one global verdict (abstract).
- Quote (abstract): "This type of approach should also be used in the implementation of the block principal pivoting method, but a switch to the CSNE method may be useful in the last iterations of the algorithm."

### S001 · Introduction to Derivative-Free Optimization (Society for Industrial and Applied Mathematics 2009, DOI 10.1137/1.9780898718768)
**Read**: abstract (publisher blurb with contents) · **Role**: core · **Coauthors**: A. R. Conn, K. Scheinberg · MPS-SIAM Book Series on Optimization, about xii + 277 pp. (book homepage mat.uc.pt/~lnv/idfo/)
- **D1**: Missing derivatives, often together with noise or nonsmoothness, as the core difficulty (abstract).
- **D2**: The book is organized tools-first. Part I covers sampling and modeling (linear, interpolating, regression and underdetermined models, and ensuring well-poisedness). Part II covers frameworks (directional and simplicial direct search, simplex-derivative line search, and derivative-free trust-region and interpolation methods). Part III reviews surrogate management and constrained extensions (abstract, contents).
- **D8**: Presented as the first contemporary comprehensive treatment. It includes convergence analysis for modified Nelder–Mead, implicit filtering, wedge methods and minimum-Frobenius-norm models, plus a software appendix (abstract).
- **Method links**:
  - new-pattern candidate: component-taxonomy field map ✅ (abstract; the shared sampling and modeling tools are said to reveal the algorithms' "differences and similarities").
  - Method 1 ⚠ variant (abstract): popular heuristics (Nelder–Mead, implicit filtering) receive convergence theory in *modified* form. The abstract does not say what the modification is.
  - Method 5 ✅ at metadata level: the book homepage posts errata for the first and second printings and a note deriving quadratic-regression error bounds. It also carries a software appendix (abstract).
- Transferable technique: when writing a survey or monograph, put the shared analytical tools (here, sample-set geometry) before the algorithms. Each algorithm chapter can then say which tool it uses and how it differs (abstract, contents).
- Quotes (abstract): "It contains a comprehensive description of the sampling and modeling tools needed for derivative-free optimization; these tools allow the reader to better understand the convergent properties of the algorithms and identify their differences and similarities." · "Introduction to Derivative-Free Optimization is the first contemporary comprehensive treatment of optimization without derivatives."

### S108 · Optimization by space transformation and decomposition (— 2019, no DOI/arXiv)
**Read**: abstract, taken from the open preprint PDF (PolyU IRA, marked as the preprint version, dated 30 Dec 2019, PDF pp. 1–2; now saved as NEW-PDF) · **Coauthors**: S. Gratton (ENSEEIHT/INPT), Z. Zhang (PolyU)
- **D1**: Smooth, not necessarily convex, unconstrained problems that cannot be solved, or even stored, in the full space and so need divide-and-conquer. A second use is trust-region methods with inaccurate gradients (abstract, pp. 1–2).
- **D2**: A forward transformation maps the gradient to an auxiliary, possibly lifted, space, where a model is built and minimized. A backward transformation maps the step back. A trust region globalizes the scheme, and the analysis checks forward/backward consistency. The scheme specializes to parallel space decomposition, extending Schwarz domain decomposition with RAS, WRAS and ASH overlap handling plus a coarse-space correction (abstract, p. 1).
- **D8**: Iteration bounds of O(ε⁻²) for nonconvex, O(ε⁻¹) for convex and O(log ε⁻¹) for strongly convex problems. Sharp bounds on gradient inaccuracy keep the complexity order and change only the constants. Beyond those bounds, behaviour degrades sharply in the experiment. The paper proposes TR parameters adapted to the gradient accuracy level (abstract, pp. 1–2).
- **Method links**:
  - Method 2 ✅ (abstract, p. 1): iteration complexity under nonconvex, convex and strongly convex classes, with inexactness shown to affect only constants.
  - Method 1 ⚠ variant (abstract, p. 1): step generators from domain decomposition, which the abstract says achieved remarkable success in domain decomposition, are placed inside a trust-region skeleton rather than a poll and sufficient-decrease skeleton.
  - new-pattern candidate: deterministic inexactness tied to the step ✅ (abstract, p. 2).
  - new-pattern candidate: import a neighbouring field's certified tool through a specialist coauthor ⚠ (abstract, p. 1; that Gratton is the domain-decomposition specialist is inferred from coauthorship).
- Transferable technique: derive the largest relative gradient error the method tolerates as a function of its own algorithmic parameters. Then show numerically that crossing it breaks the method, and turn the bound into a parameter recommendation for a given accuracy level (abstract, p. 2).

### S112 · A package for optimization based on space decomposition (— 2022, no DOI/arXiv)
**Read**: abstract, taken from the PolyU IRA record (hdl 10397/92528, dated 2022, "Other"). The archive is a 6 kB zip of MATLAB code, whose file list and the option header of `sdec.m` were inspected. **Coauthors**: S. Gratton, Z. Zhang
- **D1**: Software companion to S108, for unconstrained optimization by space decomposition (repository abstract).
- **D2**: A MATLAB implementation using (restricted) additive Schwarz synchronization and coarse-space correction, globalized trust-region style (repository abstract). Files: `sdec.m`, `set_decomp.m` (header "Gratton Zhang 2017") and `set_decomp2.m` (zip listing).
- **D8**: The contribution is software only. The option header of `sdec.m` (lines 13–28) records the authors' own ablation verdicts on coarse-space choices, such as 'pfs' (good), 'dfs' (not good) and 'grad' (OK, not very good). It also notes that the last-successful-step switch made no significant difference (lines 26–28).
