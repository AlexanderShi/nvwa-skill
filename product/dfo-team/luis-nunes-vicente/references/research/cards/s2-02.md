# Paper cards · batch s2-02 (supplement)

Batch intent: s2-02 collects eight supplement items from Vicente's Coimbra transition years (2001–2005), when his work moved from derivative-based local convergence theory towards surrogate and derivative-free optimization. The items are: a pattern-search note for user-provided points (S106, 2001); two degenerate-NLP papers with S. J. Wright (S054, local theory of a stabilized primal–dual method; S076, numerics of Wright's stabilized SQP); a structured-class paper and a general-class sequel on a new multipliers method (S095, S098); two space-mapping papers (S062 single-author, S044 with Hintermüller); and the guest editorial of the 2004 Optimization and Engineering special issue on surrogate optimization (H001). Three questions: (a) do Method 1 (heuristic or surrogate inside a convergent skeleton) and Method 4 (change one object, keep the skeleton and proof) already shape this pre-core work? (b) How far do these papers meet Method 5 (code, collection, profiles), given that only two of the eight (S076, S044) report numerical data? (c) How does the optimal-control application class (Taste #6) set the entry point?
Focus dimensions: D1, D2, D3, D4 (brief), D5 (one line), D8 — the supplement short card. D6 and D7 appear only in the digest, one line each.
Material roles: all supplement — S106 (LNCS 2001, 4 pp.) · S054 (COAP 2002) · S095 (EJOR 2002) · S062 (Optim. Eng. 2003; the file is the 24 May 2002 preprint) · S076 (COCOS 2002 workshop, LNCS 2003) · S098 (JOTA 2003) · H001 (guest editorial, Optim. Eng. 2004) · S044 (SIAM J. Optim. 2005). ### S106 · Pattern search methods for user-provided points (Lecture Notes in Computer Science 2001, pp. 95–98, DOI 10.1007/3-540-45718-6_11)
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: P. Alberto, F. Nogueira (Coimbra physics), H. Rocha (Viseu) · **Position in trajectory**: first derivative-free publication in this corpus; a four-page announcement of the method whose theory and molecular-geometry numerics are in S025 (ref. [1], p. 4). **Read**: full (4 pages).

- **D1 Problem & entry**: Unconstrained min f(x), x ∈ Rⁿ, with expensive evaluations and parallel processors available (p. 1). The entry is an application fact: in molecular geometry optimization the user can propose new points by physically relevant geometric transformations of the atom cluster, and these may lower the total energy (p. 1). The question is how pattern search can take such points without losing its convergence properties (p. 1).
- **D2 Core idea & algorithm design**: Use patterns that fill the space around x_k, and project each user point onto the mesh. f is evaluated only at the projected mesh point, never at the user point, which regularizes the algorithm and keeps the convergence properties of [1] (p. 1). Algorithm 1 (pp. 2–3): in the search step, each of N_p processors obtains a user point u^p and evaluates f at x_k + Δ_k V z^p, where z^p solves the integer program min_{z∈W} ‖u^p − (x_k + Δ_k V z)‖ (1). In the poll step, the method picks the positive basis that contains the vector v_k maximizing ⟨u_{k+1} − x_k, v⟩/‖u_{k+1} − x_k‖ (2), orders its points by increasing angle to u_{k+1} − x_k, and evaluates them in groups of N_p until the first simple decrease (p. 3). The mesh expansion and reduction exponents may also come from user information (p. 3). Unchanged: simple decrease, the integer lattice and positive bases (p. 2).
- **D3 Assumptions & model**: No assumptions are stated in this note; convergence is referred to [1] (p. 1). Design choices: V is a finite set of positive bases, and W = {n e_i : n ∈ N}, so that problems (1) and (2) cost on the order of |V|n floating-point operations (pp. 2–3).
- **D4 Analysis (brief)**: None; the note has no theorem, and convergence is deferred to [1] (p. 1).
- **D5 Experiments**: none. The paper instead describes the software: serial and MPI-parallel Fortran 95 codes, downloadable from a web site, with two user routines (func, trial_point) and integer/real `userpar` vectors that tell the user how much effort to put into a trial point (p. 4).
- **D8 Contribution & limits**: Algorithm and software announcement (pp. 1, 4). The paper does not discuss limits: it has no theorem, no numerics and no future-work paragraph (pp. 1–4).

**Method links**:
- Method 1 ✅ evidence (pp. 1–3): the user's physics-based proposal sits in the search step, and convergence comes from the mesh and the poll. This is the earliest instance in the corpus (2001), before SID-PSM and PSwarm.
- Heuristic 7 ⚠ variant (p. 3): the poll is ordered, but by an external hint direction rather than by a simplex gradient built from past evaluations.
- Method 5 ⚠ variant (p. 4): code released (Fortran 95/MPI, web site), but this note has no collection and no profiles.
- Taste #6 ✅ evidence (p. 1): the design is forced by molecular-geometry practice.
- new-pattern candidate: project the domain/heuristic proposal onto the convergence structure (pp. 1–3). This is the same method as S025, so it is not an independent third paper for the pattern.
- new-pattern candidate: use guidance information to order the poll (p. 3).
- new-pattern candidate: minimal application oracle / matrix-free interface (p. 4): two user routines plus an effort parameter.

**Transferable techniques**:
- To let an external generator propose points without breaking a lattice-based convergence proof, evaluate only at the mesh point nearest to the proposal, found by a small integer program. Choose the lattice generator set W so that this projection costs O(|V|n) (pp. 1–3).
- Use the same proposal twice. In the poll, choose the positive basis with the best cosine to the proposal direction and poll in increasing-angle order, so that a good hint saves evaluations (p. 3).
- Put an explicit effort parameter in the user-routine interface, so that the proposal can be greedy in the search step and conservative in the poll (p. 4).

**Quotes**:
- "it is the user and the optimization algorithm together, and not the optimization algorithm alone, that lead the" (p. 1)

### S054 · Local convergence of a primal-dual method for degenerate nonlinear programming (Computational Optimization and Applications 2002, DOI 10.1023/a:1019798502851)
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: S. J. Wright (Argonne) · **Position in trajectory**: derivative-based local theory from the Coimbra years. It extends Wright's stabilized-SQP idea to primal–dual interior points and is motivated by the optimal-control class of Vicente's thesis work (ref. [11], p. 2). **Read**: partial. Read: pp. 1–9 and 12–15, i.e. all statements, parts (A), (B) and (E) of the proof of Lemma 4.1, and the proofs of Lemma 4.2 and Theorem 4.3. Skimmed: parts (C)–(D) of the proof of Lemma 4.1 (pp. 10–11).

- **D1 Problem & entry**: min f(x) s.t. g(x) = 0, x ≥ 0 (1.1) (p. 1). The local theory of interior-point methods (El-Bakry et al.; Martinez–Parada–Tapia; Yamashita–Yabe; affine-scaling papers including Vicente [18]) assumes LICQ (p. 2). Entry: in optimal control and design with state bounds, LICQ requires |N_y| + |N_u| ≤ n_u, which is unlikely when n_y ≫ n_u (p. 2). So LICQ is replaced by MFCQ, following Wright's stabilized SQP [19, 20] (p. 2). Ralph–Wright and Tseng obtain related results but impose centrality and monotonicity (pp. 2–3).
- **D2 Core idea & algorithm design**: The paper modifies the classical primal–dual method in two respects (p. 3):
  1. In the Newton system (3.7), X and Z are replaced by X̃ and Z̃, with x̃_i = max(floor, x_i) and z̃_i = max(floor, z_i). The floor (a parameter with subscript min in (3.4)) is of the order of the distance to the primal–dual solution set S ((3.4), (3.10), pp. 4–5). It replaces the centrality condition and achieves the same effect (pp. 4–5).
  2. A unit step is accepted even when some x or z components become nonpositive, provided the negative parts and the new residual are bounded by the current residual raised to a power in (1, 2) (tests (3.11a–b), p. 6). Otherwise the method takes a fraction-to-the-boundary step with 1 − α = O(residual) (Algorithm 3.1, p. 6).

  Unchanged: Newton on the perturbed KKT system (3.1), with centering parameter c = O(residual²) (pp. 4, 6). There is no globalization. The authors expect that safeguards can be added, and they include (3.11b) only because it should decrease a merit function later (pp. 6–7).
- **D3 Assumptions & model**: A.1 f and g C² near x*; A.2 KKT; A.3 strict complementarity for some multiplier; A.4 second-order sufficiency for all multipliers in S_D; A.5 MFCQ (pp. 3–4). Under A.1–A.5 the residual ‖(∇ℓ, g, min(x, z))‖ (3.8) is Θ(dist to S) (3.9), citing Facchinei–Fischer–Kanzow, Hager–Gowda and Wright (p. 5). By Gauvin's result, S_D is bounded iff MFCQ holds (Lemma 2.1, p. 4). The analysis also assumes that the iterates enter a strictly complementary neighbourhood N_c(ε) (4.1) (p. 7), an assumption the Discussion calls significant (p. 14).
- **D4 Analysis (brief)**:
  - Lemma 4.1 (p. 7): if c ≤ C·residual² (4.3), the step is O(residual) in N_c(ε). The proof has five labelled parts. (A) SVD of the active-constraint Jacobian and an orthogonal change of variables (pp. 7–8). (B) The block M22 = V2ᵀ X̃_N Z̃_N⁻¹ V2 is positive definite with eigenvalues Θ(residual); the full column rank of V2 is shown by contradiction with the full column rank of ∇g (pp. 9–10). (C–D) Block elimination and right-hand-side sizes (pp. 10–11, skimmed). (E) Component bounds (p. 12).
  - Lemma 4.2 (p. 12): a step with |1 − α| ≤ residual gives negative parts and a new residual of order residual², by Taylor expansion (pp. 12–13).
  - Theorem 4.3 (p. 13): from a start in a smaller neighbourhood with small residual, the iterates stay in N_c(ε) and converge q-quadratically to a point of S. A Cauchy-sequence argument gives a single limit (p. 14).
- **D5 Experiments**: none.
- **D8 Contribution & limits**: Local theory only (p. 7). The limits are stated concretely. The neighbourhood-entry assumption is significant and needs algorithmic enhancements, the key requirement being to avoid x_i → 0 and z_i → 0 simultaneously (p. 14). Failure of strict complementarity is not covered (pp. 3, 14). Future directions: explicit index-set guesses (Potra–Sheng) or Wright's LP-based identification [21], which would allow a start from any point close to S (pp. 14–15).

**Method links**:
- Method 4 ⚠ variant, local and derivative-based (pp. 3, 5–6): the primal–dual Newton skeleton is kept, and only the coefficient matrix and the step-acceptance rule change. Here the acceptance test is widened to admit infeasible unit steps that make excellent progress.
- Taste #3 ✅ evidence (p. 3): exactly two modifications of the classical method.
- Taste #6 ✅ evidence (p. 2): state-constrained optimal control is the example that breaks LICQ.
- Method 2: no link (a local q-quadratic rate, no counts).
- new-pattern candidate: weakest condition the proof touches (pp. 4–5). Centrality is replaced by floors that give only what the proof uses: small components bounded below by a multiple of the residual.
- new-pattern candidate: name where the proof technology breaks (p. 14).
- new-pattern candidate: forward-compatible acceptance test (pp. 6–7). A condition the current proof does not need is kept because the planned globalization will need it.

**Transferable techniques**:
- Use an error-bound residual ‖(∇ℓ, g, min(x, z))‖ that is Θ(distance to the solution set) under MFCQ and second-order sufficiency, both as the progress measure and to set every algorithmic parameter: floor Θ(residual), centering O(residual²), 1 − α = O(residual) (pp. 5–6).
- Replace a path-following centrality condition by componentwise floors in the Newton matrix. The floors leave non-vanishing components untouched and keep the matrix well defined even after an infeasible unit step (pp. 4–6).
- Local quadratic-convergence template: (i) the step is O(residual); (ii) a unit step gives residual O(residual²); (iii) induction keeps the iterates in the neighbourhood; (iv) summable steps give a Cauchy sequence and a single limit even when the multiplier set is not a singleton (pp. 7, 12–14).
- For degenerate Jacobians, rotate into SVD range/null-space coordinates and prove the key block nonsingular by contradiction with the rank assumption (pp. 7–10).

**Quotes**:
- "a step length of 1 may be taken even when it leads to new iterates that have nonpositive" (p. 3)
- "The key requirement is that the iterates should avoid the situation in" (p. 14)

### S095 · Local analysis of a new multipliers method (European Journal of Operational Research 2002, DOI 10.1016/s0377-2217(02)00328-4)
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: none (single author) · **Position in trajectory**: augmented-Lagrangian work for the optimal-control class; the general-NLP sequel is S098 (Avelino & Vicente). **Read**: partial. Read: §1, §2, §4 (theorem statements, proofs of Theorems 4.1–4.2), §5 (algorithm, assumptions, statement of Theorem 5.1, the first page and the end of its proof, Corollary 5.1), §6, §7 and the appendix. Skimmed: the §3 derivative formulas (pp. 3–5) and the body of the proof of Theorem 5.1 (pp. 10–14).

- **D1 Problem & entry**: min f(y, u) s.t. c(y, u) = 0, (y, u) ≥ 0, with c_y nonsingular. This is the all-at-once form of optimal-control and design problems with state and control bounds (pp. 1–2); in the black-box form (2), state bounds become nonlinear inequality constraints on u (p. 2). Classical multipliers methods handle inequality multipliers through slacks, square slacks or nondifferentiable penalties and must impose their nonnegativity explicitly. Conn–Gould–Toint (LANCELOT) minimize the augmented Lagrangian within simple bounds (p. 2). Entry: make the bound multipliers z_y the ones that are updated explicitly (p. 2).
- **D2 Core idea & algorithm design**: The state-equation multipliers are eliminated through the first-order conditions, λ(y, u, z_y) = −c_y⁻ᵀ(∇_y f − z_y) (3). They are then substituted into a Fletcher-type augmented Lagrangian P(y, u; z_y, μ) = f + cᵀλ(y, u, z_y) + (1/2μ)cᵀc (p. 3; μ names the penalty parameter, whose glyph is lost in the file). Algorithm 5.1 (p. 8):
  1. Minimize P within the bounds (y, u) ≥ 0 for fixed z_y^k and μ_k.
  2. Update z_y by (15), which comes from the first-order conditions (6) of the penalized problem and keeps z_y nonnegative with no max(0, ·) operator (pp. 2, 8).
  3. Update μ.

  When the cross term involving ∇²_yy ℓ c_y⁻¹c is negligible, (15) becomes the classical update premultiplied by c_yᵀ (p. 8). λ is updated implicitly through P (p. 2).
- **D3 Assumptions & model**: Assumptions 2.1: f and c three times continuously differentiable, c_y nonsingular (p. 3). Assumptions 5.1: nondegeneracy (LICQ), second-order sufficiency and strict complementarity at (x*, z*) (p. 8). P is not exact in the traditional sense because it depends on z_y, but Theorems 4.1–4.4 establish partial exactness (pp. 2, 5–7):
  - limit points of penalized KKT points as μ_k → 0 are KKT points of (1) (Theorem 4.1, p. 5);
  - first-order conditions are equivalent at feasible points (Theorem 4.2, p. 6);
  - second-order conditions transfer in both directions for small enough penalty parameter (Theorems 4.3–4.4, pp. 6–7).
- **D4 Analysis (brief)**:
  - Theorem 5.1 (pp. 8–9): for (z_y, μ) in D = {‖z_y − z_y*‖ < min{δ, δ/μ}, 0 < μ ≤ μ̄}, the localized penalized problem has a unique C¹ solution. Its distance to (x*, z*) is bounded by the penalty parameter times ‖z_y − z_y*‖ (the paper's own summary, p. 8).
  - The proof follows Bertsekas' Prop. 2.4: substitute one equation into another, change variables to (r, s), check that the Jacobian of the resulting system at r = s = 0 is nonsingular on the whole parameter interval (the y–u cross derivative vanishes there), then apply the implicit function theorem (pp. 9–10; the rest skimmed). The bound constraints and the cross term shrink the convergence region from δ/μ to min{δ, δ/μ} (p. 8).
  - Corollary 5.1 (p. 14): z_y^k converges linearly when liminf μ_k > 0 and superlinearly when μ_k → 0. The linear-rate constant depends on the condition number of c_y* (p. 14).
  - §6 (pp. 15–16): for the dual function d(z_y), ∇d = c_y⁻¹c (52), and the Hessian is given by (53). The update is a scaled ascent step on d, and Newton's method on d gives a q-quadratic variant.
- **D5 Experiments**: none; the numerics are announced for a forthcoming paper (p. 16).
- **D8 Contribution & limits**: New algorithm plus local theory for a structured class (p. 16). Limit stated: in general NLP, least-squares multipliers and an orthogonal null-space basis raise efficiency questions, and the method is perhaps better suited to the structured class, where the null-space basis and the adjoint multipliers appear naturally (p. 16). Future work: general NLP with globalization and numerics; inner/outer iteration counts; active-set identification; degeneracy and failure of strict complementarity (p. 16).

**Method links**:
- Method 4 ✅ evidence (pp. 2, 8): the multipliers-method skeleton and Bertsekas' proof are kept. Only the choice of explicitly updated multipliers changes, and the paper states the extra difficulty this creates for the proof.
- Taste #3 ✅ evidence (p. 8): with the cross term neglected, the update is the classical one premultiplied by c_yᵀ.
- Taste #6 ✅ evidence (pp. 1–2, 16): optimal control and design with state bounds; implementable with state and adjoint solves or sensitivities (p. 16).
- Method 5: no link (no numerics; announced for later, p. 16).
- new-pattern candidate: state where you lose (p. 8): a smaller convergence region than Bertsekas'.
- new-pattern candidate: structured class first, general class in a follow-up (p. 16; carried out in S098).

**Transferable techniques**:
- Eliminate one family of multipliers through a first-order formula (the adjoint formula λ = −c_y⁻ᵀ(∇_y f − z_y)) and embed it in the augmented Lagrangian. The update for the remaining multipliers then comes from the subproblem's own first-order conditions and is nonnegative for free (pp. 2–3, 8).
- Before the convergence theorem, prove a two-way exactness bridge (KKT and second-order conditions transfer between the penalized and the original problem for a small enough penalty parameter). Then reuse the positive definiteness it gives inside the implicit-function proof (pp. 5–7, 9).
- Give the dual reading of a new update: compute ∇d and ∇²d of the dual function, identify the update as a scaled ascent step, and name the Newton-on-the-dual variant with a quadratic rate (pp. 15–16).

**Quotes**:
- "The update formula maintains these multipliers zy nonnegative without any" (p. 2)
- "Our new multipliers method is perhaps better tailored to problems of the form (1) and (2) where the null" (p. 16)

### S062 · Space mapping: Models, sensitivities, and trust-regions methods (Optimization and Engineering 2003, DOI 10.1023/a:1023968629245)
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: none (single author) · **Position in trajectory**: Vicente's first surrogate (space-mapping) paper; the file is the 24 May 2002 preprint (p. 1). S044 builds on it and repairs its scalar-model definition. **Read**: full (15 pages).

- **D1 Problem & entry**: Space mapping (Bandler et al. 1994; Broyden-based aggressive space mapping; trust-region globalization by Bakr et al.) uses a cheap, less accurate coarse model and a map from fine to coarse parameters, so that most evaluations happen on the coarse model (p. 1). Entry, as the abstract states it: to organize the mathematical and algorithmic aspects of the technique (p. 1). This means three things: (i) the sensitivities of the map when the models are vector-valued and the least-squares residual is nonzero; (ii) provably convergent trust-region methods for space-mapping surrogates; (iii) the shape of the surrogate when the models are scalar (pp. 1–2).
- **D2 Core idea & algorithm design**:
  - Part 1 (vector-valued models, m > n, after Bakr et al. [4]): P(x) = argmin ½‖c(x̂) − f(x)‖² (1) (p. 3). Differentiating its optimality condition gives the sensitivities G(x)J_P(x) = J_c(P(x))ᵀJ_f(x), with G = Σ(c_i − f_i)∇²c_i + J_cᵀJ_c. The surrogate gradient needs only one solve with G, by an adjoint-type computation (pp. 3–4).
  - Trust-region model (pp. 4–5): composing linear models of c and P with the quadratic merit function H gives a quadratic model. A fraction of Cauchy decrease (9) and Powell's bound then give (11), which the paper calls key to global convergence (p. 5).
  - Algorithms: Algorithm 2.1 (p. 6) minimizes H∘c∘P. Algorithm 2.2 (p. 9) minimizes Bakr et al.'s blended surrogate H{w c∘P + (1 − w)f} with inexact J_f and J_P, controlled by a Carter-type relative-gradient-error condition (17) (p. 8).
  - Part 2 (scalar models): P(x) = argmin ½‖x̂ − x‖² s.t. ĝ(x̂) = g(x) (21), or the least-squares solution (22) when that constraint is infeasible (p. 10). The definition is chosen so that P can reasonably be assumed point-to-point (p. 2).
- **D3 Assumptions & model**: H is quadratic (e.g. the squared ℓ2 norm), although in engineering H may be based on the ℓ1 norm (pp. 2–3). The argmin is unique and G(x) is invertible. Conditions 2.1 translate the trust-region theorem's assumptions on the surrogate into conditions on f, c and P (pp. 6–7). The author flags the strongest of them, that P is well defined and smooth, as hard to verify and problem dependent, and more plausible when m ≫ n (p. 7). Part 2 assumes g and ĝ C¹ and P point-to-point; the author calls the latter certainly strong, problem dependent and guaranteed only under special convexity assumptions (p. 14).
- **D4 Analysis (brief)**:
  - Part 1 imports the trust-region theory. Theorems 2.1 and 2.2 are cited from Conn–Gould–Toint §6.4 and from Carter / Conn–Gould–Toint §8.4 (pp. 6, 9). Corollary 2.1: driving the blend weight w_k → 0 gives lim ‖∇H_f(x_k)‖ = 0, i.e. stationarity for the fine model (p. 9). Using fine-model values in the ratio instead would need Conn–Gould–Toint §10.6 conditions (14), which may be expensive, and nothing guarantees them (p. 7).
  - Part 2, Theorem 3.1 (p. 11): the surrogate g_P is regular (it has directional derivatives). The proof has three parts (pp. 11–12). Where S(x) ≠ ∅ and ∇ĝ(P(x)) ≠ 0, g_P = g. Where S(x) = ∅, P(x) is a stationary point of ĝ and g_P is locally constant (flat). At the kinks between the two, the one-sided derivatives are computed case by case.
  - Diagnostic: a multiplier λ(y_k) → ∞ while ‖P(y_k) − y_k‖ does not tend to 0 signals the approach to a kink (p. 13).
- **D5 Experiments**: none. There are three 1-D illustrations: g = x² with ĝ = (x̂ − 1)² + 1 or (x̂ − 2)² + 1 (Fig. 1, p. 11), and an unbounded fine model with a bounded surrogate (Fig. 2, p. 14).
- **D8 Contribution & limits**: Framework and theory. Limits stated: the well-definedness assumptions are strong (pp. 7, 14); when m ≤ n the map becomes point-to-set and the surrogates nonsmooth (p. 10); the surrogate can be flat near coarse minimizers, so space mapping based only on minimizing g_P should be used with caution and abandoned when flatness appears (p. 14). Extensions: second-order results (called less realistic), line searches and quasi-Newton updates (p. 10); Banach or Hilbert spaces and ellipsoidal norms (p. 13); the nonsmooth trust-region theory of Dennis–Li–Tapia, applicable because g_P is regular (p. 14).

**Method links**:
- Method 1 ⚠ variant (pp. 5–6, 9): the cheap coarse model lives inside a trust-region skeleton with Cauchy decrease and a ratio test. But the guarantee is stationarity of the surrogate, and stationarity of the fine model needs w_k → 0 (Corollary 2.1, p. 9).
- Method 4 ✅ evidence (pp. 6–9): the same trust-region skeleton serves two surrogates; only the model, the ratio's numerator and a gradient-accuracy condition change.
- Taste #6 ✅ evidence (pp. 1–2): space mapping comes from electromagnetic and engineering design.
- Methods 2, 3 and 5: no link.
- new-pattern candidate: bridge inequality to a classical proof (p. 5): (11) is Powell's bound, after which Conn–Gould–Toint's theorem applies.
- new-pattern candidate: abstract assumptions, then verify per instance (pp. 6–7): Conditions 2.1.
- new-pattern candidate: reusable 1-D pathology example (p. 11; reused in S044, Fig. 3.1, pp. 5–6).

**Transferable techniques**:
- For a surrogate defined by an argmin, differentiate its optimality condition to get the sensitivities. Then compute the surrogate gradient adjoint-style, with one linear solve instead of n (pp. 3–4).
- Build the trust-region model by composing linear models with the exact outer quadratic. The model is then quadratic, and Powell's Cauchy bound applies unchanged (pp. 4–5).
- When a surrogate blends a mapped coarse model with a fine-model linearization through a weight w, prove convergence for fixed w, then drive w_k → 0 to transfer stationarity to the fine model (p. 9).
- Explain a surrogate's shape with a 1-D fine/coarse pair that can be solved by hand and shows every regime: surrogate equal to the fine model, flat, and kinked (pp. 11–13).

**Quotes**:
- "the situation is highly problem dependent" (p. 7)
- "there is no guarantee that the surrogate" (p. 7)

### S076 · Numerical behavior of a stabilized SQP method for degenerate NLP problems (International Workshop on Global Optimization and Constraint Satisfaction, COCOS 2002, LNCS 2003, DOI 10.1007/978-3-540-39901-8_10)
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: E.-S. M. E. Mostafa (Coimbra, on leave from Alexandria), S. J. Wright (Wisconsin); Vicente is the corresponding author (p. 1) · **Position in trajectory**: numerical companion to the degenerate-NLP line (S054 is ref. [9]). **Read**: full (19 pages; the convergence plots only through their captions and the text).

- **D1 Problem & entry**: NLP with inequality and equality constraints where LICQ and/or strict complementarity may fail at the solution (pp. 1–2). Entry: Wright's sSQPa (stabilized SQP with constraint identification) has a superlinear local theory (Theorem 1, p. 5). The authors collect degenerate problems to document its numerical behaviour, with an eye to NLP subproblems in global optimization, where degeneracy and infeasibility matter (p. 1).
- **D2 Core idea & algorithm design**: The paper restates sSQPa for mixed constraints (pp. 3–5):
  - Solve the stabilized QP (3), which has extra multiplier variables and a stabilization parameter equal to a power in (0, 1) of the KKT residual η (p. 4).
  - Accept the step if the residual drops superlinearly. Otherwise estimate the active set by (4), split it into strong and weak constraints with procedure ID0 (repeated LPs (5), p. 6), and reset the multipliers by the interior-multiplier LP (6) (p. 7).

  The authors' own adaptations:
  - A second LP to enlarge the multipliers gave little gain, because (6) already gives near-largest multipliers (pp. 7–8).
  - The initial multipliers come from a nonnegative least-squares problem (7) instead of the LP, which worked better (p. 8).
  - After trials, the decrease-test parameter was changed to 0.05 (pp. 8–9).
- **D3 Assumptions & model**: Assumption 1: C² with Lipschitz second derivatives, MFCQ, KKT and a form of second-order sufficiency; neither LICQ nor strict complementarity is assumed (p. 2). All theory is cited from Wright [12]: Theorem 1, superlinear convergence with q-order one plus an exponent whose glyph is lost in the file (p. 5); Lemmas 1–3 on active-set estimation, correctness of ID0 and the LP (6) (pp. 5–7).
- **D4 Analysis (brief)**: No new analysis; the theory is restated from [12] (pp. 5–7). The experiments are set up to confirm two theoretical claims: the superlinear rate, and that near S only the initial ID0 call is needed (p. 5).
- **D5 Experiments**:
  - Setup: Matlab with Matlab's LP and QP solvers, no globalization, stopping at residual ≤ 10⁻⁸ (p. 8). Twelve degenerate problems (Hock–Schittkowski variants and problems from Facchinei–Fischer–Kanzow, Anitescu, de Miguel–Murray and Wright), each from three starting points at increasing distance, with plots of log‖z − z*‖ and of the log residual against iterations (pp. 8–14).
  - Comparisons: iteration counts against NPSOL, SNOPT, NITRO and LOQO on two problems (Tables 1 and 3, pp. 9, 12), and a table restated from Anitescu (Table 4, p. 13).
  - Failures reported: on HS13 (rank deficient; it satisfies neither the KKT nor the Mangasarian–Fromovitz constraint qualification) sSQPa approaches a point other than z* from all three starts, and NPSOL, SNOPT and NITRO also fail to converge to the solution (p. 10); convergence on problem 10 is only linear and weak (p. 13); ID0 misidentifies the active set on problems 2 and 7 (Table 5, p. 15).
  - Feasibility mode (objective set to zero): four residual measures compared over 57 trials (Table 6, p. 16), and two infeasible problems with a fifth measure (pp. 16–18).
- **D8 Contribution & limits**: Empirical study. The abstract claims that sSQPa is efficient and robust without globalization (p. 1). It converges from remote points on most problems and finds least-infeasible points with nearby rank deficiency (p. 18). The limits are shown problem by problem (above). There is no conclusion section, and the global-optimization use is stated as a goal and work in progress (p. 1).

**Method links**:

### S098 · Updating the multipliers associated with inequality constraints in an augmented Lagrangian multiplier method (Journal of Optimization Theory and Applications 2003, DOI 10.1023/b:jota.0000005444.50285.4d)
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: C. P. Avelino (Ph.D. student, Coimbra; p. 1) · **Position in trajectory**: general-NLP sequel of S095 (ref. [6], p. 15). **Read**: partial. Read: §1–§4. Skimmed: the appendix proof (pp. 8–15), of which the step headings A–F, the end of step F and the closing remark were read.

- **D1 Problem & entry**: min f(x) s.t. h(x) = 0, x ≥ 0 (1.1) (p. 1). In the classical multipliers method the equality multipliers λ are updated explicitly and the bound multipliers w are approximated implicitly (p. 2). The question is whether the implicit and explicit roles can be interchanged (p. 2), generalizing S095's structured class (c_y invertible) to general NLP (p. 3).
- **D2 Core idea & algorithm design**: λ(x, w) is the least-squares multiplier −(∇hᵀ∇h)⁻¹∇hᵀ(∇f − w). The penalty function is P(x, w, μ) = f + hᵀλ(x, w) + (1/2μ)hᵀh (p. 2). Each outer iteration minimizes P within x ≥ 0 (1.4) and then sets w_{k+1} = ∇P(x_k, w_k, μ_k) (1.5), which is nonnegative by the first-order conditions of (1.4) (p. 2). The gradient (2.2) is split with the orthogonal projector ZZᵀ onto N(∇hᵀ) (pp. 3–4). Algorithm 2.1 has no globalization (p. 4).
- **D3 Assumptions & model**: A.1: f and h C³, ∇h of full rank (p. 3); the authors note that an implementation might need only first or second derivatives (p. 3). A.2: nondegenerate point, second-order sufficiency, strict complementarity (p. 6).
- **D4 Analysis (brief)**:
  - Theorem 3.1 (pp. 5–6): the second-order conditions of the original problem transfer to the penalized problem for every penalty parameter up to an explicit threshold μ̄. The threshold is the reciprocal of the largest eigenvalue of (∇hᵀ∇h)⁻¹∇hᵀ∇²ℓ∇h(∇hᵀ∇h)⁻¹, or any positive value if that matrix is negative semidefinite (p. 6).
  - Theorem 3.2 (pp. 6–7) has the same form as S095's Theorem 5.1, with region min{δ, δ/μ}. Corollary 3.3 (p. 7): a linear rate when liminf μ_k > 0, superlinear when μ_k → 0.
  - The appendix proof has six steps (p. 9): (A) preparing the system (p. 9); (B–C) nonsingularity at zero and at positive penalty parameter (p. 10); (D) the implicit function theorem (p. 11); (E) the bounds (p. 11); (F) optimality of x̃ (p. 14). It follows the structure of Bertsekas' Prop. 2.4 (p. 8). The constants grow with the condition number of ∇hᵀ∇h (p. 15).
- **D5 Experiments**: No data shown; the conclusions state that numerical results on small problems are competitive with LANCELOT (p. 8).
- **D8 Contribution & limits**: Local theory for general NLP. The limits are stated concretely:
  - each inner iteration is relatively costly (linear systems with ∇hᵀ∇h, and second derivatives in the gradient's cross term) (p. 7);
  - globalization in the style of Conn–Gould–Toint remains open, because ∇P ≠ ∇ℓ except at feasible points ((4.1), p. 8);
  - the convergence neighbourhood is smaller than in the original method (p. 3).

**Method links**:
- Method 4 ✅ evidence (pp. 2, 8): the multipliers-method skeleton and Bertsekas' proof structure are kept; only which multipliers are explicit changes.
- Method 5 ✗ contradiction (p. 8): a competitiveness claim against LANCELOT with no table, collection or profiles, which is warning sign #5 of the skill. This is an early-career data point.
- new-pattern candidate: name where the proof technology breaks (p. 8, eq. (4.1)).

### S044 · Space mapping for optimal control of partial differential equations (SIAM Journal on Optimization 2005, DOI 10.1137/s105262340342907x)
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: M. Hintermüller (Rice and Graz; PDE-constrained optimization; p. 1) · **Position in trajectory**: the second space-mapping paper. It fixes the kinks of S062's scalar definition ([27], pp. 3–5) and applies space mapping to PDE optimal control. **Read**: full (24 pages; the figures only through their captions).

- **D1 Problem & entry**: min J(y, u) subject to a PDE state equation, reduced to min J_red(u) ((1.1)–(1.2), p. 2); the discretized problems are huge and possibly nonlinear (p. 1). The existing multilevel alternative, MG/OPT (Lewis–Nash), combines fine-grid iterations with coarse-grid solves but needs convexification to enforce descent in the nonconvex case (pp. 2–3). Entry: space mapping allows a coarse model that is not just a coarser grid but a simpler (e.g. linearized) surrogate, even on the same grid (p. 3). But the earlier definition of S062 produces kinks and flat regions (pp. 3–5).
- **D2 Core idea & algorithm design**: Four elements.
  1. A Tikhonov-regularized space mapping, P(x) = argmin {α/2‖r̂(x̂) − r(x)‖²_M + ½[ĝ(x̂) − g(x)]²} (2.1) (p. 3). It yields a C¹ surrogate when G(x) is uniformly nonsingular (Theorem 2.1, p. 4). Comparing optimality conditions gives the sizing rule αλ(x) ≈ ĝ(x̂) − g(x), where λ is the multiplier of the nonsmooth definition; the smaller α, the closer the two surrogates (p. 5; Fig. 3.1, p. 6).
  2. Aggressive space mapping (Bandler et al.) in a trust-region framework, Algorithm 4.1 (pp. 6–7).
  3. A new Broyden update (pp. 8–9). The secant equation BΔx = ΔP is supplemented by ∇ĝᵀBΔx = Δĝ, which reflects the use of DP in ∇g_P. Since both conditions cannot hold at once, the update minimizes ‖BΔx − ΔP‖ subject to the new condition. The solution (Proposition 5.1, p. 8) perturbs ΔP along ∇ĝ. The perturbation is damped by σ_k ∈ (0, 1], and σ_k = 0 recovers classical Broyden (p. 9).
  4. The PDE setting. P(u) matches restricted fine states, restricted controls and reduced-objective values with weights α1, α2, α3 ((6.1), p. 10); sensitivities come from differentiating the optimality conditions ((6.8), pp. 11–12). Algorithm 6.1 (pp. 12–13) has two remedies for the underdetermined trust-region subproblem when H > h: regularize towards a reference control u_d, or restrict the step to the coarse space (pp. 13–14). Section 7 gives adjoint gradients and cheaper hybrid approximations of the fine gradient (pp. 14–16).
- **D3 Assumptions & model**:
  - Theorem 2.1 (p. 4): g, ĝ, r and r̂ C¹, and P point-to-point. If the argmin is not single-valued, one picks the least-norm local minimizer or the one closest to a reference value. For C¹, ĝ and r̂ are twice differentiable and G is uniformly nonsingular.
  - Theorem 4.1 (p. 7): g_P C¹ with uniformly continuous gradient and bounded below; a fraction of Cauchy decrease; bounded model Hessians; B_k satisfying Carter's relative-error condition (4.4). Exact sensitivities satisfy (4.4) trivially (p. 7). Whether the Broyden matrices satisfy it is not discussed.
- **D4 Analysis (brief)**: The theory is imported. Theorem 2.1(1) comes from marginal-function theory (Rockafellar [25]); Theorem 2.1(2) from the derivation before the theorem (p. 4). Theorem 4.1 comes from Conn–Gould–Toint §8.4 with Carter's condition, and the authors say that investigating it further is not their goal (p. 7). The paper's own proof is that of Proposition 5.1: vectorize B, introduce one Lagrange multiplier, and solve in closed form (pp. 8–9).
- **D5 Experiments**:
  - Setup (pp. 16–18): two 2-D semilinear elliptic control problems on (0, 1)², with a five-point stencil, nine-point prolongation, Newton-CG PDE solves, a limited-memory Broyden that stores vectors only, and semismooth Newton for the bound-constrained trust-region subproblem.
  - Example 1 (simplified Ginzburg–Landau, 255 × 255 fine grid): 4 iterations at every coarsening level. The CPU-ratio against a FAS nonlinear multigrid solver (NMG_OC) run to the same residual is 0.089–1.040, and the res-ratio against the prolongated coarse solution is 0.011–0.044 (Table 8.1, p. 19). New vs classical Broyden (σ_k = 0.1 vs 0) gives CPU-ratio 0.072 vs 0.089 (Table 8.2, p. 19), and the sensitivity to σ_k is reported (p. 20).
  - Example 2 adds an oscillatory term; space mapping recovers more fine-scale structure than the prolongated coarse solution (pp. 21–22). A nested-iteration variant is slightly better (p. 22).
- **D8 Contribution & limits**: Algorithmic framework, a new update and an application (p. 22). Limits stated:
  - space mapping is not designed as a fast fine-grid solver (pp. 3, 19);
  - at some coarse level the CPU advantage vanishes (CPU-ratio ≈ 1, p. 19);
  - bounds on the control are not treated (p. 22);
  - the fine-gradient approximations of §7.2 are presented but not tested (p. 15).

  Future work: several coarse models, and different optimizers per level in a multigrid spirit (pp. 22–23); space mapping as the core of a fast fine solver, in a different framework (p. 3).

**Method links**:
