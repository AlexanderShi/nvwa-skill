### S108 · Optimization by space transformation and decomposition (preprint, Dec 30 2019; no DOI/arXiv in the batch list)
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: S. Gratton (ENSEEIHT/INPT Toulouse), Z. Zhang (Hong Kong PolyU). Author order Gratton, Vicente, Zhang (p. 1). Vicente is at Lehigh and CMUC, Coimbra (p. 1). · **Position in trajectory**: This is the same trio as probabilistic descent (GRVZ 2015, ref. [66]) and probabilistic trust-region rates (ref. [67]), both cited on p. 102. It also cites Bandeira–Scheinberg–Vicente 2014 (ref. [3], p. 99) and Vicente's 2003 space-mapping paper (ref. [144], p. 106). It is a derivative-based trust-region paper; no derivative-free component appears in the parts read. **Read**: partial. See the page lists above.

- **D1 Problem & entry**:
  - **Three trends** (p. 3): divide-and-conquer for problems that cannot be solved, or even stored, on one machine; the use of inaccurate information; and worst-case complexity. The framework is offered as a unified view of the first two, with a theory that meets the third (pp. 3–4).
  - **Entry 1, decomposition**: domain decomposition's key techniques are "little explored" in optimization, unless the problem discretizes an infinite-dimensional one. These are overlap handling (Restricted Additive Schwarz, ASH) and coarse-grid correction (p. 5). Plain Additive Schwarz "does not converge in general as an iterative solver": on a decoupled problem it doubles the update on the overlap (p. 5). The stated question is how to bring these techniques into nonlinear optimization so that global convergence is guaranteed and the standard complexity bounds are kept (p. 5).
  - **Entry 2, inaccurate gradients**: the sources are finite precision, adjoint and lower-level solves, deliberate inexactness for speed, and quantized or perturbed gradients (pp. 6–7). The existing trust-region results for inexact gradients are "asymptotic" only (p. 70).
  - **Entry move**: stop treating the inaccuracy as noise and treat it as a linear transformation of the true gradient, R_k = ĝ_k∇f(x_k)ᵀ/‖∇f(x_k)‖² (p. 7).
  - Transferable: pose an inexact oracle as a *map* applied to the exact one, so that an existing structural theory applies unchanged (p. 7, p. 70).
- **D2 Core idea & algorithm**:
  - **Algorithm 2.1** (p. 11) has four phases:
    - Forward transformation: a model h_k on ℝ^{N_k} with ∇h_k(0) = R_k∇f(x_k).
    - Approximate minimization of h_k.
    - Backward transformation: s_k = T_k d_k.
    - Update.
  - **Why T_k ≠ R_kᵀ**: setting T_k = R_kᵀ is "tempting". It is violated on purpose in RAS/ASH for efficiency, and it fails intrinsically for inaccurate gradients. That discrepancy is what makes the framework nontrivial (pp. 11–12).
  - **Algorithm 3.1** (p. 13) globalizes with an unchanged classical trust region: the ratio ρ_k = [f(x_k) − f(x_k+s_k)]/[h_k(0) − h_k(d_k)], acceptance when ρ_k > η₀, and radius update thresholds at η₁. With R_k = T_k = I it reduces to classical trust region (p. 12). The *only* change from classical trust region is the pair (R_k, T_k). The natural bounds that keep R_k and T_k from distorting length and angle, (3.4)–(3.5), are shown to be insufficient (p. 13; Example 6.1, p. 78).
  - **Space decomposition** (Algorithm 5.1, p. 50):
    - Decomposition: ∇h^i_k(0) = R^i∇f(x_k).
    - Parallel subspace minimization.
    - Synchronization: s_k = Σ T^i d^i_k.
    - It is cast as an instance of Algorithm 2.1 through the lifted space ℝ^N, N = ΣN_i (p. 5; §5.4). It is claimed as "the first space decomposition framework" that covers (W)RAS and (W)ASH for problem (1.1) (p. 49).
  - **Coarse space** (Algorithms 4.1–4.2, pp. 38–39): s_k = ω_k T_k d_k + (1−ω_k)C_kᵀδ_k, with the predicted reduction weighted the same way (4.3). The coarse step needs no sufficient decrease (p. 40).
  - **Inaccurate gradients** (Algorithm 6.1, p. 71): R_k as above, T_k = I (p. 70). Armijo gradient descent with inaccurate gradients is rewritten as "a disguised trust-region method" (p. 79; Algorithm 6.3, p. 80).
  - Transferable: when importing a technique from another field, identify the *one* object that differs from the classical method (here the pair R_k, T_k) and keep the rest of the algorithm verbatim (pp. 12–13).
- **D3 Assumptions**:
  - Standard assumptions (pp. 14–15):
    - 3.1: f bounded below, ∇f L_f-Lipschitz on ℝⁿ. The global Lipschitz condition is taken "For simplicity"; a level-set neighbourhood would do (p. 14).
    - 3.2: the model gradients share a Lipschitz constant L_h.
    - 3.3: a Cauchy-type fraction α of decrease.
  - **New assumptions** (pp. 18–19):
    - 3.4, angle condition: sin ϕ_k ≤ β(Δ_k/‖∇h_k(0)‖)^p. It holds automatically with β = √(2L_h), p = 1/2 if the step reduces h_k at least as much as the truncated steepest-descent step (Prop. 3.1, p. 19). It is waivable when T_k = R_kᵀ (p. 18).
    - 3.5: ‖T_k‖ ≤ τ.
    - 3.6: ‖R_k g_k‖ ≥ κ‖g_k‖.
    - 3.7, consistency: g_kᵀT_kR_kg_k/‖R_kg_k‖² ≥ λ > η₁. This is called "generally indispensable" (p. 20). It reduces to the usual η₁ < 1 when T_k = R_kᵀ, and to Carter's ζ < 1 − η₁ for inexact gradients (p. 20).
  - **Scope**: deterministic, smooth, unconstrained, first-order only (p. 12). R_k and T_k are linear; nonlinear maps and Banach spaces are deferred (p. 12, p. 86).
- **D4 Proof skeleton (brief)**:
  - **Key lemma** (Lemma 3.1, p. 17): ρ_k ≥ (g_kᵀT_kR_kg_k/‖R_kg_k‖²)(1 − tan ϕ_k tan ψ_k − L_hΔ_k/(α‖R_kg_k‖)) − L_f‖T_k‖²Δ_k/(α‖R_kg_k‖). The angles are ϕ_k between −d_k and R_kg_k, and ψ_k between T_kᵀg_k and R_kg_k.
  - **How it is proved**: first a heuristic first-order estimate (3.14)–(3.20). It rests on the geometric fact that the angle between −d_k and T_kᵀg_k is at most ϕ_k + ψ_k, so cos φ_k ≥ cos ϕ_k cos ψ_k − sin ϕ_k sin ψ_k (p. 16, Fig. 3). Then the Taylor remainders are bounded (pp. 17–18). The bound is tight for n = 2 (p. 18).
  - **The chain**:
    - Prop. 3.2: ρ_k > η₁ once Δ_k/‖∇h_k(0)‖ is below an explicit threshold (p. 20).
    - Prop. 3.3: Δ_k ≥ μ‖g̃_k‖, where g̃_k is the smallest gradient so far. Proof by contradiction at the first violating index (pp. 21–22).
    - Lemma 3.2: predicted reduction ≥ ½ακμ‖g̃_k‖² (p. 22).
    - Lemma 3.3: counting. From Δ_{K′} ≤ γ₁^{K′−S}γ₂^{S}Δ₀ follows K ≤ [log(γ₂⁻¹γ₁)/log γ₁]|S_K| + log(γ₁Δ₀⁻¹μ‖g̃_{K−1}‖)/log γ₁. The argument is credited to adaptive-regularization analyses (pp. 22–23).
    - Result: O(ε⁻²) nonconvex (Thm 3.2, p. 25), O(ε⁻¹) convex and level-bounded (Thm 3.3, p. 26), O(log ε⁻¹) strongly convex (Thm 3.4, p. 27).
  - **Lemma-dependency map**: the paper states that the two lemmas depend on the other assumptions only through Prop. 3.3. This "map of logic reliance" is then used to re-derive the theory under other radius rules and settings (Thms 3.5, 3.6, 3.8, 4.1, G.1) (p. 23).
  - **Lim-type convergence**: ‖g_k‖ → 0 holds without sufficient decrease (η₀ = 0) if the radius is never expanded while Δ_k > μ̄‖∇h_k(0)‖ (Thm 3.7, p. 33). This answers Yuan's counterexample (p. 25, p. 33).
  - **Decomposition**: the conditions are η₁ < 1 for AS, ASH, WASH and RASH, and η₁ < θ⁻¹ for (W)RAS, where θ is the multiplicity of the overlap (Thm 5.1 with Prop. 5.3, pp. 57–58).
  - **Inaccurate gradients**:
    - Thm 6.1: ζ < min{η₁⁻¹ − 1, 1} for ‖ĝ−g‖ ≤ ζ‖g‖ (p. 72).
    - Thm 6.2: ζ < 1 − η₁ for ‖ĝ−g‖ ≤ ζ‖ĝ‖ (p. 74).
    - Thm 6.3: the largest admissible region, the ball ‖ĝ − g/(2η₁)‖ < ‖g‖/(2η₁) (pp. 76–77).
  - **Sharpness** (Example 6.1, pp. 77–78): f = ‖x‖²/2 with ĝ outside that ball. Then ρ_k ≤ η₁ forever, so Δ_k ≤ γ₁^kΔ₀, and the iterates stall within Δ₀/(1−γ₁) of x₀.
  - **The price of inexactness**: the complexity order is unchanged, and the constants grow by a problem-independent factor. The factor is < 52 for one correct significant digit (ζ = 1/2, η₁ = 1/4, p = 1/2; p. 74) and < 82 for ζ = 55% of type II (p. 75).
  - Transferable: prove the key ratio bound heuristically with one picture first, then rigorously (pp. 16–18). Afterwards, write down which lemmas every theorem actually uses, so that each later variant only has to re-prove those (p. 23).
- **D5 Experiments (one line)**:
  - Setup:
    - Decomposition: 15 CUTEst/Powell problems at n = 10³ (m = 10) and n = 10⁴ (m = 10²), overlap n_o ∈ {0, 2, 4, 6}, AS vs RAS vs no decomposition. Iterations are counted to ‖g_k‖ ≤ 10⁻⁶‖g₀‖ with a 10⁴ cap, and failures are marked "–" (Table 2, pp. 63–65).
    - Coarse spaces SS/FS/DF/CG on chrosen with n = 6400, m = 2…256, plus coupled variants that add ½xᵀVVᵀx with random V (Table 3, pp. 65–67).
    - A 128² Plateau problem: generic vs structure-aware partition, with HEM coarse space (Table 4, pp. 67–69).
    - Inexact gradients: ĝ = ∇f + ζ‖∇f‖e, 150 perturbed problems, η₁ and ζ swept on a 1/50 grid, and a new "ε-effectiveness" score (pp. 80–84).
    - The GNU Octave fminunc audit (pp. 97–98).
  - Results: RAS beats AS whenever the partition overlaps (p. 65). HEM gives more than 100× fewer iterations at 64–256 subspaces (p. 69). Effectiveness collapses exactly past the bound η₁ < (1+ζ)⁻¹, most sharply at ζ = 9/10 (Fig. 7, p. 83).
  - Cost is counted in iterations; there are no performance profiles, and no code release is mentioned.
  - Transferable: sweep the algorithm parameter across the theoretical threshold and draw the threshold as a vertical line on the effectiveness plot (Figs. 7–8, pp. 83–84).
- **D8 Contribution & limits**:
  - **Contributions**:
    - Theory: one trust-region framework with global convergence and three complexity classes (pp. 24–29).
    - Algorithm: space decomposition with RAS/ASH and coarse space (§5).
    - Practice: a rule for choosing η₁ given the gradient accuracy, η₁ < (1+ζ)⁻¹ or η₁ < 1 − ζ (pp. 84–85), and a patch for Octave's fminunc/fsolve radius rule (p. 98).
  - **Limits stated concretely**:
    - The implementation is "unsophisticated" (p. 62). The framework is "not proposed as a specific algorithm" and must be tailored to each problem (p. 61).
    - Coarse spaces are "highly problem-dependent" (p. 61). It is "unrealistic" to expect RAS to work equally well on all problems (p. 65).
    - The generic partition failed at m = 128 on Plateau, where roughly half of the coupling was lost (p. 69).
    - The η₁ = 1/2 behaviour is "not covered by our theory" (p. 83).
    - Only academic test problems were tried (p. 87).
  - **Future directions** (pp. 85–87):
    - Levenberg–Marquardt and cubic-regularization globalizations; the latter needs higher-order transformations.
    - Nonlinear R_k, T_k and Banach spaces.
    - Separate subspace radii; coarse models from space mapping or surrogates.
    - Use as preconditioners (ASPIN); asynchronous variants.
    - Constraints and nonsmoothness for group lasso and deep networks.
    - Relative errors that are bounded only in probability; random transformations with probabilistic requirements; SGD.

**Method links**:
- Method 2 ✅ evidence. The bounds are O(ε⁻²), O(ε⁻¹) and O(log ε⁻¹) for the nonconvex, convex and strongly convex classes (Thms 3.2–3.4, pp. 25–27). The stated goal is that "standard worst-case complexities of nonlinear optimization methods are preserved" (p. 5). The price of an inexact gradient is written as a problem-independent constant factor (pp. 73–75). This is the *name the price* step of Method 2. Unlike the DFO papers, the bounds count *iterations*, and no n-dependence is discussed in the parts read.
- Method 1 ⚠ variant. The PDE community's efficient but non-convergent technique is kept, not redesigned: Additive Schwarz, RAS and coarse correction (p. 5). A trust-region ratio test (p. 13; Thm 5.1, p. 58) supplies the convergence, rather than a poll step with sufficient decrease. The coarse step, like a search step, needs no decrease of its own (p. 40).
- Method 3 ⚠ variant. An accuracy requirement is relaxed to the largest region the step-size logic tolerates, and the threshold is shown not to be crossable (Example 6.1 and Fig. 7, pp. 77–83). This matches Method 3 step 5. The relaxation is deterministic, though: a bounded relative error. The probabilistic version is listed as future work, citing the trio's own probabilistic-model papers (p. 87).
- Method 4 ⚠ variant. The trust-region skeleton and the ratio test stay the same. What changes is the step map, not the notion of success. The price is a compatibility condition on the threshold η₁ (Assumption 3.7; η₁ < θ⁻¹ for RAS, p. 58).
- Method 5 ⚠ variant. There is a named collection (CUTEst/Powell, p. 63) with failures reported, but no profiles and no released code. There is, however, a fix to existing software (Octave fminunc, pp. 97–98).
- Taste #4 (*theory that explains the numerics*) ✅ strong. Octave's drop at ζ ≈ 0.39 is predicted from the framework (p. 98).
- Existing candidates matched:
  - *nest the known theories as special cases*: classical TR at R = T = I (p. 9); inexact gradients (p. 70); Armijo line search (pp. 79–80).
  - *abstract assumptions, then verify per instance*: Prop. 5.3 (p. 57); Thms 6.1–6.3 (pp. 72–77).
  - *read the proof's parameter coupling as a finding*: η₁ decides the tolerable error (pp. 37–38, 84).
  - *negative example for the rejected alternative*: the length-and-angle conditions (6.25) fail (p. 78).
  - *assumption-boundary test suite*: the η₁ × ζ sweep (pp. 80–84).
  - *audit a fashionable tool and publish the negative result*, variant: audit a widely used solver and patch it (pp. 97–98).
  - *state where you lose*: pp. 61, 65, 69, 83.
- New-pattern candidate: **lemma-dependency map for reuse across variants** (p. 23).

**Transferable techniques**:
- Encode an inexact oracle as a rank-one linear map of the exact one, R_k = ĝ_kg_kᵀ/‖g_k‖². Any theory for transformed gradients then covers it, and the choice of R_k is irrelevant because only R_kg_k enters the analysis (p. 7, p. 72).
- Bound a trust-region ratio through two angles: step vs model gradient, and forward vs backward image of the gradient. Use φ ≤ ϕ + ψ to get the factor (1 − tan ϕ tan ψ). Then add an angle assumption on the step that is automatically met by Cauchy-decrease steps (pp. 16–19).
- Obtain complexity without an α-forcing function. Lower-bound Δ_k by μ‖g̃_k‖ (proof by contradiction at the first violating index). Then count successful iterations from Δ_K ≤ γ₁^{K−S}γ₂^{S}Δ₀ (pp. 21–25).
- To show a tolerance bound is sharp, pair an exact analytic failure (f = ‖x‖²/2, exact step, ρ_k ≤ η₁ forever) with a numerical sweep that places the theoretical threshold on the plot (pp. 77–84).
- Score partial success with ε-effectiveness, min{1, log(‖∇f(x_out)‖/‖∇f(x₀)‖)/log ε}, averaged over the problem set. With an inexact gradient, stop at ε̂ = ε(1−ζ)/(1+ζ) so that the true-gradient criterion is certified (pp. 81–82).
- To show when a component (here the coarse space) matters, inject controlled coupling ½xᵀVVᵀx into a benchmark, with V drawn dense-random or sparse-random, and report averages over 10 draws (pp. 66–67).
- Turn the theorem into a parameter rule (η₁ < (1+ζ)⁻¹), then test a widely used solver against it and publish the patch (pp. 84–85, 97–98).

### S113 · A Review of Multi-Objective Optimization: Theory and Algorithms (talk slides, Lehigh ISE, Apr 21 2020; no DOI/arXiv)
**Role**: supplement (talk slides, treated as a stated teaching view) · **Coauthors**: Suyun Liu (Vicente's PhD student). Both authors are listed at the ISE Department, Lehigh University (p. 1). Every content slide carries the footer "Suyun Liu" (pp. 3–46; the outline slides on pp. 2, 15, 25 and 40 have no footer), so the slides were presumably presented by Liu. The teaching view is therefore joint, and it is attributed to Vicente only as coauthor. · **Position in trajectory**: The talk falls between Fliege–Vaz–Vicente 2019 (MO steepest-descent complexity, cited on p. 33 and listed on p. 46) and the Liu–Vicente stochastic multi-gradient and fairness work that SKILL.md cites (2021–2022). **Read**: full (all 46 file pages; the figures are only captions).

- **D1 Problem & entry**: The talk defines the problem min F(x) = (f₁,…,f_m) over Ω, with decision space, objective space and image F(Ω) (p. 3). It builds Pareto dominance from a six-point discrete table: no point minimizes both objectives, point 3 is dominated by 2, and P = {1, 2, 4, 5} (p. 4). It then gives the weak and strict Pareto sets and the inclusion P_s ⊆ P (pp. 6–8), with two LP examples where P_s ⊊ P and P_s = P (pp. 9–10). Existence holds for compact Ω and continuous F. In the convex case, local Pareto implies global Pareto (p. 11). Pareto points map to the boundary of F(Ω) (p. 13). Front shapes can be convex, concave, mixed or disconnected (SP1, FF1, JOS2, ZDT3; p. 14).
  - Transferable: open a multi-objective talk with a six-point table in which every concept is visible: dominated, weak and strict Pareto (pp. 4–7).
- **D2 Core idea & structure**:
  - **The outline's two routes** (p. 2): scalarization "(entire Pareto front)" versus gradient-based methods "(single Pareto point)".
  - **Weighted sum**:
    - Nonnegative weights give P and positive weights give P_s (p. 16). Nonnegative weights are necessary and sufficient in the convex case (p. 17).
    - Positive weights miss strict Pareto points. With f_i = −x_i on the unit disc, (1, 0) ∈ P_s is reached only with μ₂ = 0 (pp. 18–19).
    - P_s = P when F is strictly convex (p. 20). One slide is titled "Non-convexity in weighted-sum method"; it is a figure only (p. 21).
  - **ε-constraint**: needs no convexity. Each solution is in P, and a feasible x is in P_s iff, for some vector ε*, it solves the ε-constrained problem for every choice of objective l (pp. 22–24).
  - **Gradient-based methods**:
    - Pareto stationarity: no d with J_F(x)d < 0; equivalently 0 lies in the convex hull of the gradients (pp. 26–27).
    - A componentwise sufficient-decrease (Armijo) lemma: F(x+td) < F(x) + σtJ_F(x)d (p. 28).
    - Fliege–Svaiter steepest descent through the dual min-norm problem over the simplex, d(x) = −Σλ_i∇f_i(x), which recovers −∇f₁(x) when m = 1 (pp. 29–30). θ(x) = 0 iff x is stationary (p. 31). Algorithm 1 uses backtracking (p. 32).
    - Newton's method for R^m-strongly convex F through a QCQP subproblem (pp. 35–38).
  - **Slide slips**: p. 29 writes the steepest-descent subproblem as argmax_d min_i −∇f_i(x)ᵀd + ½‖d‖². Read literally, that is unbounded above. The next build, p. 30, replaces it with argmin_d max_i ∇f_i(x)ᵀd + ½‖d‖². In Algorithm 2, step 6 updates the iterate with α_k d_k although the direction is s_k (p. 38).
  - **Class map** (p. 41):
    - *A priori* methods: weighted sum ("non-convexity is an issue"), ε-constraint ("infeasibility is an issue"), reference points and goal programming.
    - *A posteriori* methods that iteratively update lists of nondominated points: evolutionary methods with "no theoretical convergence guarantee", and mathematical-programming methods with convergence "for one point on the Pareto front".
    - A four-panel figure shows list updating: add perturbed points, apply descent steps, remove dominated points, and the front moves (p. 42).
    - Stochastic multi-objective optimization splits into SAA-type multi-objective methods and weighting-type stochastic methods, which are the slide's own two labels (p. 43).
  - **Metrics**: purity, and the maximum-hole Γ and spread Δ, with formulas (pp. 44–45).
  - Transferable: present each multi-objective method as a single-objective method plus one changed object: a min-norm convex combination for the direction, a componentwise Armijo test for acceptance. Show the m = 1 collapse explicitly (pp. 28–32).
- **D3 Assumptions**:
  - Steepest descent: F is C¹ (p. 26), with Lipschitz gradients for the convergence theorem (p. 33). The nonconvex rate needs at least one f_i bounded below (p. 33).
  - Newton: R^m-strongly convex and C² F (p. 35). Local quadratic convergence requires uniformly positive definite, Lipschitz Hessians and a start near x* (p. 39).
  - No noise or constraints appear in the gradient part; the stochastic slide is a classification only (p. 43).
- **D4 Results presented (brief)**: No proofs are given. The talk states results from the literature:
  - Accumulation points are stationary (Fliege–Svaiter 2000).
  - min_{i<k}‖d_i‖ and |θ(x_k)| are O(1/√k) for nonconvex F (Fliege et al. 2019) (p. 33).
  - Strongly convex F: a linear rate in iterates and in the λ*-weighted gap. Convex F: an O(1/k) rate in a weaker gap weighted by averaged λ (p. 34).
  - Newton converges q-quadratically, and ‖s(x_k)‖ r-superlinearly (p. 39).
  - The rates are laid out class by class (nonconvex / convex / strongly convex), mirroring single-objective gradient descent (pp. 33–34).
- **D5 Experiments**: none (review talk).
- **D8 Contribution & limits**:
  - The contribution is pedagogical: a map from problem features to method classes, with each class's failure mode named (p. 41), and the metrics to compare fronts (pp. 44–45).
