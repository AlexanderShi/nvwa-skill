### S037 · Trust-region interior-point algorithms for minimization problems with simple bounds (Applied Mathematics and Parallel Computing, Festschrift for Klaus Ritter 1996, DOI 10.1007/978-3-642-99789-1_7)
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: J. E. Dennis · **Position in trajectory**: Rice PhD period, derivative-based trust-region interior-point work that predates the DFO line; the file is TR94-42 (Nov. 1994, revised Nov. 1995; p. 1), not the Festschrift print. **Read**: full (10 pages).

- **D1 Problem & entry**: Box-constrained minimization, a ≤ x ≤ b, f continuously differentiable (problem (1), p. 2). Entry point: Coleman and Li's diagonal affine scaling (which the authors call elegant, p. 2) scales both the model and the trust region; once the trust radius may exceed one, the ellipsoid no longer enforces the bounds (pp. 4–5), and when the solution lies strictly inside the box, a trust region shaped by the distance to a non-active bound may need many iterations to move off that bound (p. 5). The question: keep the scaling in the model but drop it from the trust region?
- **D2 Core idea & algorithm design**: Two TRIP algorithms (Algorithm 3.1, p. 6) that differ only in the trust-region matrix S_k: S_k = D_k^p (scaled, ellipsoidal) vs S_k = I (unscaled) (pp. 4–6). The model is scaled with Coleman–Li's D(x), built from the gradient sign and the distances to the bounds (pp. 3–4); strict feasibility is kept by the box σ_k(a − x_k) ≤ s ≤ σ_k(b − x_k) (p. 4). Steihaug–Toint conjugate gradients and Powell's dogleg are reused, changed only in the step-length rule that keeps the point strictly feasible (Algorithm 5.1, pp. 7–8). Unchanged: fraction of Cauchy decrease, ratio test, a simple radius update that is stated to suffice for the proofs (pp. 5–6).
- **D3 Assumptions & model**: f C¹ and bounded below on a compact level set; the Hessian approximations H_k and S_k⁻¹D_k^p only uniformly bounded (Theorem 4.1, p. 7). The result holds for any scaling power p ≥ 1/2 (p. 3, p. 5). The Cauchy step is defined along D_k^{2p}g_k with the box constraints included (6), unlike Coleman–Li's definition (p. 5).
- **D4 Analysis (brief)**: A single new lemma (Lemma 4.1, pp. 6–7) lower-bounds the model decrease of any step satisfying the Cauchy condition by ½‖ĝ‖ min(‖ĝ‖/‖Ĥ‖, ‖ĝ‖Δ/‖S⁻¹D^pĝ‖, σ‖ĝ‖/‖h‖∞). The proof minimizes the 1-D quadratic along the scaled gradient on [0, T_k], where T_k is the first of the trust-region and bound limits, and treats the interior and boundary minimizers separately (pp. 6–7). With the three denominators bounded, this is Powell's form, and the global result follows the same steps as Coleman–Li's Theorem 3.5 (p. 7). Result: lim ‖D_k^p g_k‖ = 0 (Theorem 4.1, p. 7). No rate.
- **D5 Experiments**: MATLAB 4.2a; 23 problems from Conn–Gould–Toint, each in an interior-solution (U) and a constrained (C) version, n = 8–30; gradient and function evaluation counts per problem (Table 1, p. 9) and totals 866/998 for S_k = D_k vs 855/942 for S_k = I (Table 2, p. 10). Coleman–Li's method is described but not run; its stopping rule differs (p. 8, p. 10).
- **D8 Contribution & limits**: Three novelty points: an improved interior-point algorithm, a Cauchy-decrease analysis in which the Cauchy direction's scaling differs from the trust-region norm, and dogleg/CG extensions to the bounded subproblem (pp. 2–3). Limits (visible, mostly unstated): first-order only, whereas Coleman–Li report second-order KKT convergence and a q-quadratic rate (p. 8); the advantage of S_k = I in the totals is small (Table 2, p. 10), and the claim for larger problems is stated as a belief (p. 10). No future-work section.

**Method links**:
- Method 4 ✅ evidence, early and derivative-based (pp. 5–7): the trust-region skeleton and ratio test are kept; only the trust-region shape and the Cauchy-step definition change, and one lemma carries the old proof over.
- Taste #3 (minimal modification) ✅ evidence (pp. 7–8): CG and dogleg are reused and changed only where strict feasibility forces it.
- Method 5 ⚠ variant (pp. 8–10): per-problem evaluation counts on a standard collection, but no profiles, no external baseline run and no code release.
- Method 2: no link (asymptotic result only, p. 7).
- new-pattern candidate: bridge inequality to a classical proof — prove one lemma that puts the new decrease into Powell's Cauchy form, then cite the existing global proof (pp. 6–7). The same move appears in S031 (p. 10) and S039 (pp. 6–7).

**Transferable techniques**:
- Decouple the scaling of the model from the scaling of the trust region, and test both choices (S_k = D_k vs S_k = I) under one convergence theorem (pp. 4–6).
- After changing the Cauchy step, minimize the 1-D model along the (scaled) Cauchy direction on [0, T], where T is the first blocking constraint, and bound the interior-minimizer and boundary cases separately; the result is Powell's bound with modified denominators (pp. 6–7).
- Extend Steihaug–Toint CG to a box-constrained subproblem by changing only the maximal-step computation (step 2.2) so that iterates stay strictly feasible (pp. 7–8).

**Quotes**:
- "In both algorithms, the trial step computation is very simple and convenient with respect to staying strictly inside B." (p. 2)
- "From this table we observe that the second algorithm (Sk = In) performed better. We believe that this will be more clearly the case in larger problems." (p. 10)

### S031 · On the convergence theory of trust-region-based algorithms for equality-constrained optimization (SIAM Journal on Optimization 1997, DOI 10.1137/s1052623494276026)
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: J. E. Dennis · **Position in trajectory**: Rice PhD period; the file is TR94-36 (Oct. 1994, revised Apr. 1996; p. 1), written while Vicente was a graduate student at Rice (p. 2, footnote); a detailed treatment is in his thesis [35] (p. 4). **Read**: partial — §1–§3 (all lemma statements; proofs of Lemmas 3.1, 3.2, 3.5, 3.9 and Theorems 3.1–3.2 read; proofs of Lemmas 3.3, 3.6–3.8 skimmed), §4, statements of the §5–§6 theorems, §7 (Theorem 7.1 statement, Theorem 7.2 with proof), §8. Proofs of Theorems 5.1, 5.2 and 7.1 not read.

- **D1 Problem & entry**: min f(x) s.t. C(x) = 0 (1.1) (p. 3). Dennis, El-Alem and Maciel (DEM) proved global convergence to a stationary point for a general trust-region SQP class whose step components only need a fraction of Cauchy decrease (pp. 2–3). Gap: no second-order result for this class; the existing second-order results (Byrd–Schnabel–Shultz, Coleman–Yuan, El-Alem) need the exact normal component, least-squares multipliers and an orthogonal null-space basis (p. 4). Entry: generalize Moré–Sorensen's unconstrained second-order theory to the DEM class (p. 2, p. 3).
- **D2 Core idea & algorithm design**: Keep DEM's algorithm (quasi-normal + tangential step, augmented Lagrangian merit function, El-Alem's penalty update; Algorithm 2.1, p. 8). Add: a fraction of optimal decrease for the tangential component on subproblem (2.5) (conditions (2.6), p. 7), the exact Hessian (A.5, p. 9) and two conditions, ∇ℓᵀs^n ≤ κ‖C‖Δ (2.3) and ‖Δλ‖ ≤ κΔ (2.8) (p. 6, p. 8). The introduction explains why: the optimal-decrease condition only yields an O(Δ²) model decrease, so any increase along the quasi-normal step must also be O(Δ²) (p. 4).
- **D3 Assumptions & model**: DEM's A.1–A.4 (smoothness, full column rank of ∇C, boundedness of W_k, H_k, λ_k) plus A.5 (exact Hessian, Lipschitz second derivatives) (p. 9). The multiplier condition (2.8) is not needed for first-order convergence (p. 8); it is added only for the second-order result. The added conditions are verified for (i) the exact normal component with least-squares multipliers (Lemma 4.2, p. 18) and (ii) discretized optimal control with adjoint multipliers and the quasi-normal step (4.4) (Lemma 4.1, p. 17).
- **D4 Analysis (brief)**: Contradiction proof with the structure of DEM's (p. 10). Assume ‖Wᵀ∇ℓ‖ + ‖C‖ + γ_k > tol, where γ_k is the multiplier of the tangential trust-region subproblem, i.e. the negative-curvature measure (2.10) (p. 9). The KKT identity of that subproblem gives a model decrease ≥ ½τ(1 − r)²γ_kΔ² (2.11) (p. 10). A case split on ‖C_k‖ ≤ αΔ_k, with α given explicitly in (3.12), gives pred ≥ κΔ² (Lemmas 3.5–3.6, pp. 11–13); then the penalty parameter is bounded and the radius bounded below (Lemmas 3.7–3.8, pp. 13–14), so the merit function decreases without bound (Theorem 3.1, p. 15). Theorem 3.2 (pp. 15–16): a limit point with C = 0, Wᵀ∇f = 0 and a positive semidefinite reduced Hessian. Under a positive definite reduced Hessian the radius stays bounded away from zero and all iterations are eventually successful (Theorem 5.2, p. 20), which gives q-quadratic rates for two concrete choices (Theorems 6.1–6.2, pp. 20–21). Side result: the linearized-constraint subproblem tends to fall into the hard case (Theorem 7.2, p. 23).
- **D5 Experiments**: none.
- **D8 Contribution & limits**: Pure theory, positioned as a complement to DEM (p. 23). Limits are implicit rather than stated: exact second derivatives are required (A.5, p. 9); no numerics; the cost of computing a fraction-of-optimal-decrease step is handled by pointing to GQTPAR, Rendl–Wolkowicz and Sorensen (p. 7). §8 lists no future work (p. 23).

**Method links**:
- Method 4 ✅ evidence, pre-DFO (p. 4, p. 10): the algorithm and proof structure are kept; only conditions on the step components and multipliers are strengthened, upgrading the conclusion from first to second order.
- Method 2 ⚠ variant, era effect (pp. 15, 20–21): the finished result is a limit statement, a radius bounded away from zero and a local q-quadratic rate — not an evaluation count.
- Taste #6 ✅ weak evidence (p. 5, pp. 16–17): discretized optimal control is introduced as the justification for the general form of the algorithms and theory (p. 5).
- new-pattern candidate: minimal-condition upgrade — find the weakest extra conditions that upgrade a known theorem, explain in the introduction why each is needed, then verify them on concrete structured classes (p. 4, pp. 17–18).
- new-pattern candidate: bridge inequality to a classical proof (p. 10), as in S037 and S039.

**Transferable techniques**:
- For second-order convergence of a trust-region method, use the subproblem's KKT multiplier γ_k as the curvature measure: H + γWᵀW positive semidefinite gives q(0) − q(s*) ≥ ½γΔ², which plays the role of the Cauchy bound (pp. 9–10).
- When a step has two components, split on ‖C_k‖ ≤ αΔ_k with α written out explicitly (3.12), so that the constraint terms are dominated by the tangential decrease (pp. 11–12).
- Check each added hypothesis against the natural choices of an application class (adjoint multipliers, s^n = (−C_y⁻¹C, 0)) and record it as a one-line lemma (Lemma 4.1, p. 17).

**Quotes**:
- "The proof of (3.2) although simpler has the same structure as the proof given in [7]." (p. 10)
- "We also prove that if the algorithm is well behaved (for instance if the trust radius is uniformly bounded away from zero), then this subproblem has a natural tendency to fall into the so{called hard case." (p. 4)

### S039 · Two-step algorithms for nonlinear optimization with structured applications (SIAM Journal on Optimization 1999, DOI 10.1137/s1052623498334396)
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: A. R. Conn, C. Visweswariah (both IBM T. J. Watson) · **Position in trajectory**: the work started during Vicente's visit to IBM Watson (p. 1, footnote); K. Scheinberg is thanked for help with the numerics and the §6.3 explanation (p. 24). A derivative-based precursor of Method 1 (a free extra step inside a convergent skeleton). **Read**: full; the Tables 4–5 rows (pp. 20–21) and the Hessian derivation in §6.3 (pp. 19–21) were skimmed.

- **D1 Problem & entry**: With expensive function and gradient evaluations, each iteration should extract as much improvement as possible (p. 1). When a subset of variables (slacks, a minimax variable) enters the objective in a known functional form, a second, cheap update of those variables can follow the classical step (p. 1). The alternative, eliminating those variables (Golub–Pereyra), is acknowledged (p. 2).
- **D2 Core idea & algorithm design**: After the trust-region step s_k, take any ŝ_k with f(y + s + ŝ) < f(y + s) (greedy Algorithm 2.2, p. 4; conservative Algorithm 2.3, p. 5). Only ared/pred change: ared = f(y) − f(y + s + ŝ), and pred = model decrease of s + actual decrease of ŝ ((3)–(4), p. 4). Using the model decrease at y + s + ŝ instead is rejected because ŝ is not computed from the model (p. 4). Remark 3.1: the new ratio equals (t + ρ)/(t + 1), increasing in t when ρ < 1, so every step the classical test accepts is still accepted and some rejected steps become acceptable (p. 7). Applications: an explicit slack update (27) needing no extra function or gradient evaluation (p. 13), and a minimax slack-plus-z update from a scalar equation solved in O(m) operations (p. 15).
- **D3 Assumptions & model**: f twice continuously differentiable (p. 2); ‖H_k‖ bounded (5) and the first step satisfies a fraction of Cauchy decrease (6) (p. 5). The lim-inf result needs nothing of ŝ (Theorem 3.1, p. 6); the lim result needs ‖ŝ‖ ≤ κΔ (13) or a decrease ≥ κ‖ŝ‖ (12), plus a uniformly continuous gradient (Theorem 3.2, p. 7). The property the applications actually give (decrease ≥ κ‖ŝ‖², (21)) is too weak; the authors think the theorem with (21) alone is unlikely to hold (p. 11) and add a scaling safeguard instead (Algorithm 3.1, p. 10).
- **D4 Analysis (brief)**: Bridge: since the second step's actual decrease is nonnegative, pred still satisfies Powell's Cauchy bound (8) (p. 6); ared − pred involves only the first step (11), so |ared − pred| = o(Δ) (p. 7); the rest is Moré's argument (Theorems 4.10 and 4.14 of [20]; pp. 6–8). Second-order convergence and the radius behaviour follow Moré–Sorensen and Moré (Theorems 3.3–3.5, pp. 9–10). The two-step Newton method keeps the q-quadratic rate via the perturbation lemma (Corollary 4.1, pp. 11–12). Proposition 5.1 (pp. 13–14) proves the quadratic decrease of the slack update by a two-case convexity argument.
- **D5 Experiments**: Modified LANCELOT (SBMIN) in three versions on 56 CUTE problems (26 non-minimax, 30 minimax; p. 16), with INFORM, iterations, CPU and objective tables; 15% fewer iterations on average, with the outliers HS109, HAIFAM and POLAK6 named and excluded (p. 16). Circuit problems from IBM's JiffyTune (pp. 17–18, Table 6 p. 22) and static-timing circuits where the advantage grows with size (p. 18, Table 7 p. 23). A failure section on six minimax problems (§6.3, pp. 19–24; Table 8 p. 24).
- **D8 Contribution & limits**: Framework, convergence theory, LANCELOT implementation and industrial use in microprocessor circuit design (p. 23). Limits are stated concretely: on some minimax problems the second step drives iterates toward points where the augmented-Lagrangian Hessian is ill-conditioned (pp. 21–22); a larger initial penalty parameter (100) and a CG tolerance of 10⁻¹² improve most of these cases (pp. 22–24); the lim result under (21) alone is left open (p. 11). Future work: strategies for minimax problems (p. 23).

**Method links**:
- Method 1 ✅ evidence, derivative-based precursor (p. 4, p. 7): a free, structure-exploiting step is added to a provably convergent trust-region step, and it can only help acceptance (Remark 3.1). ⚠ Variant in the glue: acceptance runs through a redefined ratio, not a sufficient-decrease forcing function.
- Method 4 ✅ evidence (p. 4, pp. 6–7): the trust-region skeleton and radius update are untouched; only ared/pred change, and the classical proof carries over.
- Method 5 ✅ evidence (pp. 16–24): a standard collection (CUTE), the strongest in-house baseline (unmodified LANCELOT), per-problem tables and a real application; no profiles (1999).
- Taste #6 ✅ evidence (pp. 17–18, p. 23): circuit tuning at IBM, where one function evaluation can take more than ten minutes of CPU time (p. 18).
- new-pattern candidate: failure-analysis section — isolate the problems where the new method is worse, explain the mechanism, test a fix (§6.3, pp. 19–24).
- new-pattern candidate: bridge inequality to a classical proof (p. 6), as in S037 and S031.

**Transferable techniques**:
- When you add a non-model step to a trust-region method, set pred = (model decrease of the model step) + (actual decrease of the extra step). Then pred keeps the Cauchy bound, ared − pred involves only the model step, and the standard proof goes through (p. 4, pp. 6–7).
- Show that a modified acceptance ratio dominates the classical one by writing it as (t + ρ)/(t + 1) and noting monotonicity in t (Remark 3.1, p. 7).
- When the natural property of your extra step is too weak for the theorem, add a cheap scaling safeguard that enforces one of the sufficient conditions (Algorithm 3.1, p. 10).
- Report average gains with the outliers named and excluded explicitly (p. 16).

**Quotes**:
- "However, there are some minimax problems where the two-step algorithm performs poorly" (p. 16)
- "that two-step updating is unlikely to be helpful" (p. 17)

### S028 · Recovering risk-neutral probability density functions from options prices using cubic splines and ensuring nonnegativity (European Journal of Operational Research 2008, DOI 10.1016/j.ejor.2007.02.041)
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: A. M. Monteiro, R. H. Tütüncü · **Position in trajectory**: an optimization-modelling application (convex QP/SDP, finance) outside the DFO line; Vicente's affiliation is Coimbra, and his support note thanks IBM Watson and the IMA (p. 1). **Read**: full main text (pp. 1–19); the appendix on splines (p. 22 onward) and the figure pages (pp. 22–27) were skimmed.

- **D1 Problem & entry**: Estimating the risk-neutral density from option prices is underdetermined — far more states than observed prices — so structure must be imposed (p. 2). The paper catalogues parametric and nonparametric methods that can produce negative probabilities (pp. 6–7); the entry point is a nonparametric estimate with a nonnegativity guarantee (p. 7).
- **D2 Core idea & algorithm design**: Cubic-spline density with knots placed in a superset of the strikes (p. 3); variables are the spline coefficients y ∈ R^{4ns} (p. 7); call and put prices are linear in y (p. 9); the least-squares fit is a convex QP (7) (p. 9). Nonnegativity is handled in two ways: (a) only at the knots (8), which keeps a QP but is heuristic (pp. 9–10); (b) exactly, via the Bertsimas–Popescu SDP characterization of polynomial nonnegativity on an interval (Proposition 1, Corollary 1, pp. 10–11), giving problem (15). The quadratic objective is moved into a second-order cone constraint so that SDPT3 can solve it (p. 12).
- **D3 Assumptions & model**: One-period economy with European options; no arbitrage ⇔ existence of a risk-neutral measure (Definitions 1–2, Theorem 1, pp. 4–5). Market data are cleaned to satisfy Herzel's no-arbitrage conditions (Theorem 2, pp. 16–17).
- **D4 Analysis (brief)**: No convergence analysis. The device is the exact reformulation (Corollary 1 for cubic pieces, one 4×4 PSD matrix per interval, p. 11), plus the observation that the knot-only constraint is implied by the SDP one (x^s_00 ≥ 0, p. 11). Conditioning: the Hessian is ill-conditioned through x_s⁵ terms (p. 9), so the problems are scaled by the average knot (p. 13) and the Hessian is shifted by ξ = |λmin| (QP) or |λmin|·10³ (SDP) (p. 15).
- **D5 Experiments**: Black–Scholes data with a known lognormal answer (S = 50, r = 0.1, T − t = 0.5, σ = 0.2; 20 strikes, 25 knots; p. 14) and three S&P 500 CBOE data sets (p. 16); baseline: a mixture of two lognormals fitted with fmincon (LN2, pp. 13–14); residual tables (Table 1 p. 15; Table 2 p. 18); sensitivity to the number and spacing of knots (p. 15); robustness to uncleaned, arbitrage-containing prices (pp. 18–19).
- **D8 Contribution & limits**: A modelling contribution, claimed as the first spline method with a positivity guarantee (p. 3). Limits are stated concretely: the end knots need a margin of about 6% of the strike range (p. 15); knot placement matters more on market data (p. 18); the QP estimate takes small negative values (about 10⁻⁵) in the tails (p. 18); SDP is more sensitive to scaling than QP (p. 19). Future work: estimate volatility from the recovered density; robust QP/SDP under data uncertainty (p. 19).

**Method links**:
- Method 1 ⚠ variant (pp. 9–11, pp. 18–19): not a heuristic wrapped by a guarantee, but a cheap heuristic formulation (knot-only QP) set beside a guaranteed, costlier one (SDP), with the data showing when the guarantee matters.
- Method 5 ⚠ variant (pp. 13–19): synthetic data with a known answer, real market data and a parametric baseline; no problem collection, no profiles, no code release mentioned.
- Taste #6 ✅ evidence (p. 5, p. 16): a finance application on public market data.
- Methods 2–4: no link.
- new-pattern candidate: ground truth first — validate on data generated from a model whose answer is known before using real data (pp. 14–15).
- new-pattern candidate: clean the inputs with a characterization theorem (Herzel's necessary and sufficient no-arbitrage conditions) before fitting (pp. 16–17).

**Transferable techniques**:
- Enforce nonnegativity of a piecewise polynomial exactly with one small PSD matrix per piece (Bertsimas–Popescu), and keep the knot-only linear version as the cheap fallback (pp. 10–11).
- For an SDP solver that accepts only linear objectives, move a convex quadratic objective into an epigraph constraint written as a second-order cone (p. 12).
- Weight residuals by trading volume so that thinly traded, unreliable prices count less (p. 17).
- Rerun the method on the data you would have discarded (arbitrage-containing prices) and report whether the answer changes (pp. 18–19).

**Quotes**:
- "To our knowledge, this is the first spline function approach to risk-neutral density estimation with a positivity guarantee." (p. 3)
- "While the simpler QP approach is generally sufficient to recover an appropriate risk-neutral pdf both with simulated and market data, there are instances where the solution of the more difficult SDP model is necessary to obtain a nonnegative pdf estimate." (p. 19)

### S029 · Computation of sparse low degree interpolating polynomials and their application to derivative-free optimization (Mathematical Programming 2012, DOI 10.1007/s10107-012-0578-z; arXiv 1306.5729)
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: A. S. Bandeira, K. Scheinberg · **Position in trajectory**: the first paper in this batch where model accuracy holds only with high probability; the matching stochastic framework is deferred to future work (p. 6, p. 22) — the seed of the probabilistic-model line (Method 3). **Read**: partial — §1, §2, §3 skimmed, §4.1 statements, §4.2 basis construction (pp. 13–15), §4.3 with the proof of Theorem 4.5 (pp. 20–21), §5, §6. The proof of Lemma 4.1 (pp. 18–19) was not read. The file is arXiv v1, dated May 4, 2012 (p. 1).

- **D1 Problem & entry**: Determined quadratic interpolation needs (n+1)(n+2)/2 points; underdetermined minimum-Frobenius models are only provably fully linear (p. 2, pp. 7–8). Certifying a fully quadratic model needs a well-poised set of that size and possibly an O(n⁶) factorization (p. 5). Hessian sparsity could help, but earlier DFO work needs the sparsity structure in advance (p. 2, p. 8). Entry point: a reverse relationship — use compressed-sensing results to improve optimization (p. 2).
- **D2 Core idea & algorithm design**: Replace the minimum-Frobenius criterion (3) by min ‖α_Q‖₁ subject to the interpolation conditions (4), a linear program (p. 8). Theory uses random samples in an ℓ∞ ball and an orthonormal basis ψ (§4); the practical trust-region method uses deterministic, reused sample sets (Algorithm 5.1, pp. 23–24) with minimal geometry care: n+1 or more points nearby, far points dropped when Δ is small, and no radius reduction while fewer than n+1 points are available (pp. 22–23).
- **D3 Assumptions & model**: Lipschitz Hessian (Assumption 2.2, p. 4). Approximate Hessian sparsity: for each x0 and Δ an h-sparse fully quadratic model exists (Assumption 4.2, p. 17), weaker than a fixed sparsity pattern (Assumption 4.1, p. 16). The ℓ∞ ball is used because the basis construction does not extend to the ℓ2 ball (Remark 4.1, p. 16).
- **D4 Analysis (brief)**: Orthonormal basis ψ with K = 3 on the hypercube via Fubini (Definition 4.2, Theorem 4.4, pp. 13–15) → RIP for random samples (Theorem 4.1, p. 11) → noisy partially sparse recovery (Corollary 4.1, p. 16) → treat f minus the sparse fully quadratic model as noise with η = √p κ'ef Δ³ (proof of Theorem 4.5, p. 20) → convert coefficient error into value, gradient and Hessian errors with Lemma 4.1 (p. 18, pp. 20–21). Result: a fully quadratic model with probability > 1 − n^{−γ log p} from p = O(n (log n)⁴) points when h = O(n) (Theorem 4.5 and (21), pp. 20–21). The constants are impractical (c_total < 17190, p. 21).
- **D5 Experiments**: MATLAB; DFO-TR Frob vs DFO-TR l1 vs NEWUOA on 26 CUTEr problems, performance profiles in function evaluations at 10⁻⁴ and 10⁻⁶, with a rerun-with-tighter-tolerances fairness rule (pp. 25–27); 13 CUTEr problems with known Hessian sparsity, n = 20–22, where the ℓ1 version wins about 8 of 9 cases (pp. 26–29). Failures are shown: both versions fail on two problems (Table 1, p. 26) and hit the 5000-evaluation cap on GENHUMPS and WOODS, the ℓ1 version also on POWELLSG (Table 3, p. 29).
- **D8 Contribution & limits**: Theory plus a practical solver that beats NEWUOA on the test set (pp. 26–28). ### S034 · Smoothing and worst case complexity for direct-search methods in non-smooth optimization (IMA Journal of Numerical Analysis 2013, DOI 10.1093/imanum/drs027)
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: R. Garmanjani (doctoral scholarship, p. 1; his forthcoming PhD thesis is mentioned on p. 18) · **Position in trajectory**: extends Vicente's smooth direct-search complexity result ([34], Theorem 2.4, p. 5; reference list p. 21) to non-smooth f by smoothing; it is the source case of SKILL.md Research Heuristic 5. **Read**: full; §7 (pp. 12–13) and the proofs of §8 (pp. 14–16) skimmed.

- **D1 Problem & entry**: For smooth f, direct search with sufficient decrease has the same worst-case complexity as steepest descent (abstract, p. 1; Theorem 2.4, p. 5); no such bound exists for non-smooth f (p. 1). A direct extension fails: one needs the step size bounded below whenever some Clarke directional derivative is below −ε, but that direction can be far from all poll directions (p. 7); non-smooth global convergence exists only with dense directions and without rates (p. 7). Application motivation: h(F) with F a black box, e.g. ℓ1 data fitting (p. 2).
- **D2 Core idea & algorithm design**: Algorithm 4.1 (p. 7): run direct search (Algorithm 2.1, sufficient decrease ρ(α) = cα^p, p. 4) on f̃(·, µ_k) until α < r(µ_k), then set µ_{k+1} = σµ_k. The direct-search algorithm itself is unchanged; the only new objects are the smoothed objective and the inner stopping rule.
- **D3 Assumptions & model**: f locally Lipschitz with a known smoothing function (Definition 1.1, p. 2); f̃(·, µ) bounded below (Assumption 5.1, p. 8) with a gradient Lipschitz constant of order 1/µ (Corollary 6.1, p. 10); positive spanning sets with cosine measure bounded below (Assumption 2.1, p. 3). Stationarity is with respect to the smoothing function, 0 ∈ G_f̃(x*), which yields Clarke stationarity only for suitable smoothings (p. 2), e.g. the gradient-consistent one for ‖F‖₁ (Theorem 8.2, p. 15).
- **D4 Analysis (brief)**: Inner loop: successful iterations are bounded by sufficient decrease with α ≥ r(µ) = c2µ^q as the floor, unsuccessful ones by counting α-contractions; outer loops number log(ξ/µ0)/log σ, giving O(−log ξ · ξ^{−pq}) iterations (Theorem 6.1, pp. 9–10). At exit, the unsuccessful-iteration bound (3) gives ‖∇f̃‖ = O(ξ^{q−1} + ξ^{(p−1)q}) (Corollary 6.1, p. 10); balancing with q = 2, p = 3/2 gives O(n^{1/2}ξ) (Corollary 6.2, p. 10). Overall: O(n^{3/2}[−log ε + log n]ε⁻³) iterations and O(n^{5/2}[−log ε + log n]ε⁻³) evaluations ((8), p. 11), slightly better in the power of n than Nesterov's Gaussian-smoothing bound O(n(n+4)²ε⁻³) (p. 6, p. 11). Fixing µ ≤ ε from the start would cost O(n²ε⁻⁴) evaluations, which the authors use to argue for progressive reduction of µ (p. 11).
- **D5 Experiments**: 53 problems min ‖F(x)‖₁ from Moré–Wild (22 CUTEr vector functions, 2 ≤ n ≤ 12; p. 16); SID-PSM vs Ssid-psm (Algorithm 4.1 with SID-PSM inside, two outer iterations; p. 17); best-value differences (Fig. 1), a data profile at θ = 10⁻⁷ with budget 1500 (Fig. 2) and a performance profile at θ = 10⁻⁴ with f* taken over a wider solver set (Fig. 3) (pp. 17–18); ablations (search step off, cyclic poll ordering) are reported only in words (p. 18).
- **D8 Contribution & limits**: Global convergence and worst-case complexity with preliminary numerics (abstract, p. 1). Limits stated: the price is at least one order worse than smooth direct search, traced to the 1/µ growth of the gradient's Lipschitz constant (p. 19); even f + ‖Ax − b‖_p resisted a direct analysis (p. 19); a possible n-dependence of that Lipschitz constant is ignored (p. 11). Future work: bound and linear constraints (p. 19); ablation details deferred to the PhD thesis (p. 18).

**Method links**:
- Method 2 ✅ evidence (pp. 5–6, pp. 9–11): the bound is given in iterations and in evaluations with its n-dependence, set beside smooth direct search and Nesterov's derivative-free bound, and the price is named.
- Method 4 ✅ evidence (p. 7): the direct-search skeleton is kept; the objective becomes f̃(·, µ), and the only new rule is the inner stopping threshold r(µ). SKILL.md Research Heuristic 5 is this paper.
- Method 5 ✅ evidence (pp. 16–18): the group's own solver (SID-PSM) is reused inside the new method, with Moré–Wild data and performance profiles, and a reference value taken from a larger solver set (p. 18).
- new-pattern candidate: exponent balancing — choose the forcing-function power p and the stopping power q to equalize the two error terms, then report the optimized bound (p. 10); also in S032 (p. 15).
- new-pattern candidate: phrase the result as a Nesterov-style problem class (model / oracle / ε-solution) so that it reads as an upper complexity bound for the class (p. 11).

**Transferable techniques**:
- Turn a non-smooth problem into a sequence of smooth ones and stop each inner run on a step-size threshold r(µ) = cµ^q; convergence needs r(µ) → 0 and L_∇f̃(µ)·r(µ) → 0 (Theorem 5.3, pp. 8–9).
- For complexity with a varying parameter, bound each inner run using α ≥ r(µ) as the floor inside the forcing function, then multiply by the number of outer reductions (proof of Theorem 6.1, pp. 9–10).
- Compare against the naive fixed-parameter alternative inside the analysis to justify a continuation scheme (p. 11).
- In profiles on a small test set, take f* as the best value over a larger set of solvers than the two being compared (p. 18).

**Quotes**:
- "The idea is simple and consists of applying directly a direct-search method to the smoothing function with a fixed value of the smoothing parameter µ until a certain precision is achieved, after which the smoothing parameter is reduced and the process repeated." (p. 7)
- "One pays a price in smoothing non-smooth objective functions, in the sense that the bound in the worst case complexity is at least one order worse than the corresponding bound for smooth direct search." (p. 19)

### S032 · Trust-region methods without using derivatives: Worst case complexity and the non-smooth case (SIAM Journal on Optimization 2016, DOI 10.1137/151005683)
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: R. Garmanjani, D. Júdice · **Position in trajectory**: the trust-region counterpart of S034 and of Vicente's direct-search complexity line (pp. 2–3); builds on the general DFO trust-region setting of Conn, Scheinberg and Vicente [14] (p. 5; reference list p. 25). **Read**: partial — §1–§3 (with proofs of Theorems 3.1–3.2), §4 (Algorithm 4.1 and Corollary 4.1 read; Theorems 4.1–4.5 skimmed), §5 (Algorithm 5.1, bound (11), Lemmas 5.1 and 5.3 with proofs, Theorem 5.4, Corollary 5.1), §6, §7. The appendix proofs (pp. 23–27) were not read.

- **D1 Problem & entry**: The analysis of derivative-free trust-region methods has focused on global convergence, and little was known about their complexity (abstract, p. 1). Direct search already had O(ε⁻²), O(ε⁻¹) for convex f and a smoothing result (p. 2); the one composite trust-region attempt (Grapiglia–Yuan–Yuan) uses a restrictive setting and carries an extra |log ε| (p. 3).
- **D2 Core idea & algorithm design**: Two changes to the general derivative-free trust-region method of [14] (p. 5): (i) each inner iteration of the criticality step is counted as a regular iteration, which removes the incumbent models of [14]; (ii) the ratio uses f(x_k) − f(x_k + s_k) − c1Δ_k^p (Algorithm 3.1, p. 6), with p = 3/2 needed for smoothing. The smoothing trust-region method (Algorithm 4.1, p. 12) mirrors S034. For composite f = h(F): model F by a fully linear m and use the model criticality measure Ψ^m(x, Δ) both as the criticality test and as the predicted decrease (Algorithm 5.1, pp. 16–17).
- **D3 Assumptions & model**: F continuously differentiable with Lipschitz Jacobian on an enlarged level set (Assumption 2.1, p. 4); fully linear models with a model-improvement algorithm (Definition 2.1, pp. 4–5); f bounded below and model Hessians bounded (Assumptions 3.1–3.2, pp. 7–8); κ_ef, κ_eg = O(√n L) (Assumption 3.3, p. 10). Composite case: h convex, globally Lipschitz and bounded below (Assumption 5.1, p. 17).
- **D4 Analysis (brief)**: Bookkeeping device: six iteration types (C^r, C^nr, S, A, U, M; p. 7). |S| is bounded through the decrease on successful iterations and the radius floor Δ ≥ γC3ε^{1/min(p−1,1)} (Lemma 3.3, Theorem 3.1, p. 8); radius-reducing iterations by contraction counting; and |C^nr| ≤ |S| + |R|, |M| ≤ 2(|S| + |R|), because each of these is followed by an iteration of another type (proof of Theorem 3.2, pp. 9–10). Smooth case: O(L²nε⁻²) iterations and O(L²n²ε⁻²) evaluations (Theorem 3.3, Corollary 3.1, p. 11). Smoothing: O(n^{5/2}[|log ε| + log n]ε⁻³) evaluations after setting ξ = ε/(√n C) (Corollary 4.1, p. 15); the introduction states this bound without the √n from ξ, as O(n²|log ε|ε⁻³) (p. 3). Composite: |Ψ − Ψ^m| ≤ L_hκ_egΔ from full linearity alone ((11), p. 16), convexity gives Ψ^m(x, Δ) ≥ min{Δ, 1}Ψ^m (Lemma 5.1, p. 17), and the final count is O(ℓn²ε⁻²) evaluations, better by a |log ε| factor than [23] (Corollary 5.1, p. 20).
- **D5 Experiments**: A numerical illustration (§6 title) on the same 53 ℓ1 problems: the smoothing method Sdfo-tr vs the composite method Cdfo-tr (regression simplex gradients from 2n points, ℓ∞ trust region solved as an LP), then Sdfo-tr vs Ssid-psm from S034; data profiles (θ = 10⁻⁷, budget 1500) and performance profiles (θ = 10⁻⁴) (pp. 20–22).
- **D8 Contribution & limits**: Theory; the authors locate the novelty in the way the trust-region algorithms are analyzed, individually and together (p. 22). Limits stated: the bounds are the expected ones (p. 22); the smoothing method wins numerically although its bound is worse (p. 22). Future work: an ε⁻¹ bound for convex f, second-order points (already in Júdice's thesis), constraints (p. 22).

**Method links**:
- Method 2 ✅ evidence (p. 3, p. 11, p. 15, p. 20): worst-case bounds in iterations and evaluations with n- (and ℓ-) dependence, compared with direct search, gradient methods and the earlier composite bound.
- Method 4 ✅ evidence (pp. 5–6, pp. 16–17): the trust-region skeleton is kept; the acceptance ratio gets a −c1Δ^p term (a generalized sufficient-decrease test), and for composite f the measure Ψ replaces ‖g‖.
- Method 2 ⚠ variant (p. 22): the bound does not predict the ranking — the method with the worse bound performs much better in practice — and this is reported openly (Method 2, step 6; Taste #4).
- Method 5 ⚠ weak evidence (pp. 20–22): profiles on a small ℓ1 test set, presented as an illustration; no code release mentioned.
- new-pattern candidate: iteration taxonomy for complexity — classify iterations and bound each non-successful class by the successful and radius-reducing ones (p. 7, pp. 9–10).
- new-pattern candidate: exponent balancing (p = 3/2, q = 2; p. 15), as in S034.

**Transferable techniques**:
- To make a trust-region method's complexity depend on a chosen power, subtract c1Δ^p from the actual decrease in ρ_k; p = 2 recovers the classical bound and p = 3/2 matches the smoothing analysis (pp. 5–6).
- Count a multi-step inner procedure (the criticality step) one iteration at a time; here this removed a |log ε| factor (p. 3).
- Bound the bookkeeping iterations (model improvement, non-reducing criticality) by what type of iteration must follow them (proof of Theorem 3.2, p. 10).
- Get the error of a model criticality measure from full linearity alone: |Ψ − Ψ^m| ≤ L_hκ_egΔ through the Lipschitz constant of h (p. 16).

**Quotes**:
- "Despite the fact of exhibiting a worse WCC bound, the smoothing approach worked much better than the composite one, which does not come as a surprise given the absence of curvature exploration in the latter one." (p. 22)
- "The WCC bounds established in the various cases were the expected ones, matching existent bounds for derivative-free or derivative-based optimization." (p. 22)
