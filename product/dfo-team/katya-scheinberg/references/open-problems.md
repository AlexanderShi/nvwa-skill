# Open problems and candidate thesis directions (from Scheinberg's public 1997–2026 output)

> Research date: 2026-09-27 (full-text pass). The first version of this file used verified abstracts, talk abstracts and third-party preprints found by web search. The papers themselves have now been read (78 in full or in part; see `research/07-paper-cards.md`), so this version adds the open problems that the papers state in their own conclusions, limitation paragraphs and remarks (Part B), and updates Part A with what the full texts show. Citations like `[card S098, pp. 9, 23]` point to the paper card and the page of the full text.
>
> **Rules for using this file**
> - "Stated" means the paper or abstract names the gap, a belief, or a limitation. "Inferred" means the gap follows from what the verified papers do *not* cover (e.g., every title says "unconstrained"). Both are marked.
> - **Difficulty guesses are guesses** by the skill author, not Scheinberg's view. Ask your supervisor before scoping a thesis around any row. A problem may already be solved in work that this search did not find, or in the group's unpublished work.
> - Run the Taste quick-check in SKILL.md on any row before committing.

## How to pick (a suggested order, not Scheinberg's)

1. **First project (6–12 months):** row 8 or row 3 for one method (e.g. B11 or B12). Each has an existing template (T6/T9/T14), a clear deliverable (theorem plus equal-accuracy experiments) and a direct precedent in the group's papers.
2. **Core thesis chapter:** row 2 or row 4, or row 6's high-probability version. Each extends a 2025–2026 Scheinberg paper by one oracle assumption, which is the group's historical pattern (see 04-mentorship.md: each generation pushes one step; `research/08-deep-reading-synthesis.md` §8.2 for the turns).
3. **Stretch:** rows 1, 5 and 7, and B1, are high-risk. Pair them with a safer row.

**Before committing:** ask Scheinberg whether the row is already being done in the group, and whether the "stated" gap is still open. Unpublished work is not visible here, and several rows come from 2025–2026 preprints that may have moved on.
