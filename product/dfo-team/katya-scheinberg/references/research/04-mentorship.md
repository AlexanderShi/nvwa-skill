## 2. Patterns (inference from the table — secondary, my analysis)

- **Juniors first-author the stochastic-oracle papers.** In 2014–2026, the first author is nearly always a junior co-author; Scheinberg is typically last or second-to-last. This is consistent with a group model where a student/postdoc owns one oracle setting and one proof.
- **Continuity of lines across generations.** Menickelly (2018, 2019) → Paquette (2020) → Cao/Berahas (2022, 2024) → Jin/Xie (2024) → Chaudhry/Sun (2025–26): each generation inherits the probabilistic-oracle framework and pushes one step (expected → high-probability → corrupted/heavy-tailed; line search → trust region → Powell-style DFO). *Inference.*
- **Pairing a junior with an outside specialist.** Probability (Blanchet), complexity (Cartis), ML (Choromanski) specialists appear on papers with juniors. *Inference.*
- **Code lives with the junior co-author** (e.g., Cao's DFOTR repo). *Inference from one repo.*

### 7. Group practice visible in the record (inference unless marked)

1. **One student, one oracle setting, one proof line** (inference from the thesis titles plus papers): Chen = random models with noisy function values (STORM); Menickelly = random models and their rate analysis; Cao = model-based DFO under noise (estimators, noisy trust region); Xie = high-probability and sample complexity under unreliable data; Tang = ML structure (proximal quasi-Newton). Each thesis title names the setting.
2. **Author order is weak evidence of who led.** Many of these papers list authors alphabetically: Chen–Menickelly–Scheinberg; Blanchet–Cartis–Menickelly–Scheinberg; Berahas–Cao–Choromanski–Scheinberg; Jin–Scheinberg–Xie; Nguyen–Scheinberg–Tran; Chaudhry–Scheinberg–Sun. Scheinberg's late position follows from the surname. The non-alphabetical orders put the junior first: Tang–Scheinberg 2016; Cao–Berahas–Scheinberg 2024; Paquette–Scheinberg 2020 is alphabetical as well. **Correction to §2 above:** "juniors first-author" and "Scheinberg typically last" are mostly alphabetical artefacts. Do not infer contribution from order. Ask about the group's authorship convention.
3. **The collaboration continues after graduation** (observable): Xie co-authors arXiv:2511.19411 (2025) with Scheinberg after the 2024 PhD; Cao's 2024 journal paper appeared after the 2021 PhD.
4. **Postdocs bring an adjacent toolkit and first-author one line**: Paquette (line search), Berahas (noise and estimators; co-supervised with Curtis and Takáč), Chaudhry (Powell-style complexity). *Inference:* postdocs are used to open a new line (Powell-style complexity is Chaudhry's).
5. **External specialists on committees and papers**: S. M. Wild (Argonne) on Chen's committee. Menickelly later moved to Argonne and co-authored the Acta Numerica DFO survey with Wild. Blanchet (applied probability) and Cartis (complexity) appear on theory papers. *Inference:* students are connected to the DFO community outside the group early.
6. **Short conference version, then long journal version** (observable): NeurIPS 2021 → SIOPT 2024 for Jin/Xie; WSC 2023 / NeurIPS 2022 OPT workshop → arXiv:2308.13161 for Xie. *Inference:* students are encouraged to put out an early version at an ML or simulation venue.
7. **Teaching** (secondary, profile summary): at Lehigh Scheinberg taught linear and nonlinear optimization, optimization models and applications, and optimization methods for machine learning. The Curtis–Scheinberg 2017 TutORial and the 2025 Aisenstadt lectures are the public teaching materials.
