### S027 · Interior point trajectories in semidefinite programming (SIAM Journal on Optimization 1998, 8(4):871–886, DOI 10.1137/s105262349630009x)
**Read**: abstract · **Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: D. Goldfarb
- **D1 Problem & entry**: the entry is an explicit carry-over of Megiddo's work on LP trajectories to SDP. The object studied is the set of interior-point trajectories, including the central path (abstract).
- **D2 Key idea**: assume primal and dual strict feasibility. Show that both central paths exist and converge to the analytic centres of the optimal faces. Then generalise to a class of central-path-like trajectories through *any* given interior point, feasible or infeasible. Their convergence and the convergence of their derivatives are studied (abstract).
- **D8 Contribution**: theory only: existence, limits and derivatives of trajectories. The abstract states no algorithm, experiment or limitation (abstract).
**Method links**:
- Methods 1–6: no link. The setting is deterministic conic theory with no oracle, estimator or DFO model (abstract).
- new-pattern candidate: **LP-to-conic transplant**. A named LP result (here, Megiddo's trajectories) is re-proved for SDP (abstract). The same move appears in S040 (optimal partition, abstract) and, from secondary evidence, in S039 (Karmarkar's method).

**Transferable technique**: when you analyse a path-following method, do not stop at the central path. Define a family of central-path-like trajectories indexed by the starting point, feasible or infeasible, and prove convergence for the whole family. The result then covers warm and infeasible starts (abstract).

**Quotes**:
- "This work was inspired by the seminal work of Megiddo on linear programming trajectories" (abstract)
- "Under an assumption of primal and dual strict feasibility, we show that the primal and dual central paths exist and converge to the analytic centers of the optimal faces of, respectively, the primal and the dual problems." (abstract)

### S045 · A modified barrier-augmented Lagrangian method for constrained minimization (Computational Optimization and Applications 1999, DOI 10.1023/a:1008705028512)
**Read**: abstract · **Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: D. Goldfarb, R. Polyak, I. Yuzefovich
- **D1 Problem & entry**: constrained optimization with both inequality and equality constraints (abstract).
- **D2 Key idea**: the MBAL function treats inequalities with a modified-barrier term and equalities with an augmented-Lagrangian term. The method alternates primal minimisation of MBAL with multiplier updates. A dual problem is built on MBAL instead of the classical Lagrangian (abstract).
- **D8 Contribution**: Q-linear convergence for a large enough fixed barrier-penalty parameter under standard second-order conditions. Q-superlinear convergence follows if the parameter is increased after each multiplier update. A basic duality theorem is proved, and the MBAL dual keeps properties that the classical Lagrangian dual lacks (abstract).
**Method links**:
- Method 2 ⚠ variant (abstract): two classical methods (modified barrier, augmented Lagrangian) are kept and combined, each on the constraint type it suits. Nothing here concerns a weaker oracle, so this is only the keep-the-classical-method half of Method 2.

**Transferable technique**: state the rate in two tiers. First prove a linear rate for a fixed penalty parameter. Then show that increasing the parameter after each multiplier update upgrades the rate to superlinear. This separates what the method guarantees from what parameter scheduling buys (abstract).

**Quotes**:
- "For a large enough fixed barrier-penalty parameter the MBAL method is shown to converge Q-linearly under the standard second-order optimality conditions." (abstract)

### S077 · Numerically stable LDLT factorizations in interior point methods for convex quadratic programming (IMA Journal of Numerical Analysis 2008, 28(4):806–826, DOI 10.1093/imanum/drn058)
**Read**: abstract · **Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: D. Goldfarb
- **D1 Problem & entry**: the entry starts from Fletcher–Powell (1974), a stable LDLT update for a low-rank modification. The authors had made it product-form for LP IPMs (Goldfarb–Scheinberg 2004 = S050, card s2-01). Here they extend it to convex QP whose Hessian is, or is approximated by, a diagonal plus a low-rank matrix (abstract).
- **D2 Key idea**: stability follows from showing that the entries of the unit lower-triangular factor stay uniformly bounded as the duality gap goes to zero (abstract).
- **D8 Contribution**: a stability theory plus "Practicable versions" for structured QPs from SVMs and portfolio optimization (abstract).
**Method links**:
- Method 6 ✅ evidence (abstract): the diagonal-plus-low-rank Hessian structure of SVM and portfolio QPs is built into the IPM linear algebra.
- Method 2 ⚠ variant (abstract): a classical device (the Fletcher–Powell update) is kept and carried to a new problem class. This is deterministic numerical linear algebra, with no oracle.
- new-pattern candidate: **stability as uniform boundedness in the limit** (from s2-01, S050/S058). This abstract states the same proof target for the QP case: factor entries bounded as the gap goes to zero.
- new-pattern candidate: **extend own result to a harder class** (s2-01): LP (2004) to QP (here), stated in the abstract. The "list what breaks" part was not read.

**Transferable technique**: to certify numerical stability of a factorisation inside an IPM, make the proof target a uniform bound on the triangular factor's entries along the whole path as the duality gap goes to zero, not a per-iteration condition-number estimate (abstract).

**Quotes**:
- "Our new results are based on showing that the elements of the unit lower triangular matrix in the LDLT factorizations that arise in this context are uniformly bounded as the duality gap is driven to zero." (abstract)

### S001 · Introduction to derivative-free optimization (SIAM book 2009, MPS-SIAM Series on Optimization, DOI 10.1137/1.9780898718768)
**Read**: abstract (the SIAM book description with its table of contents). No full text. **Role**: core · **Coauthors**: A. R. Conn, L. N. Vicente
- **D1 Problem & entry**: optimization when derivatives are absent, often together with noise or lack of smoothness. The book claims to be the first contemporary comprehensive treatment (abstract).
- **D2 Key idea**: the sampling and modelling tools come first. Part I covers sample sets and linear models, interpolating, regression and underdetermined models, and well-poisedness. The algorithms come second. Part II covers directional and simplicial direct search, simplex-gradient line search, and derivative-free and interpolation-based trust-region methods. The tools are presented as what lets the reader understand the algorithms' convergence and compare them (abstract, contents list).
- **D8 Contribution**: a textbook and reference. It includes convergence analyses for modified Nelder–Mead, implicit filtering, wedge methods and minimum-Frobenius-norm model methods, plus a software appendix. The stated audience is engineers, scientists and graduate students (abstract).
**Method links**:
- Method 4 ✅ evidence (abstract): a whole chapter is given to geometry ("Chapter 6: Ensuring well poisedness and suitable derivative-free models"), and poisedness-based model tools form Part I. This matches SKILL.md, which already lists the book under Method 4 practice.
- Method 5 ⚠ partial (abstract): the tools are said to let readers "identify their differences and similarities". This is a comparative framing, but the description mentions no head-to-head experiments.
- new-pattern candidate (contrast): the *organize by the user's problem features* candidate (s2-03, S046) is not what this book does. The book is organised tools-first, then by method class (abstract, contents list).

**Transferable technique**: in a survey or thesis, put the shared analytical toolkit (sample-set geometry, model error bounds) in Part I. Present each algorithm family in Part II as a user of that toolkit. Convergence arguments can then be compared across families instead of repeated (abstract, contents list).

**Quotes**:
- "This book explains how sampling and model techniques are used in derivative-free methods and how these methods are designed to efficiently and rigorously solve optimization problems." (abstract)
- "It contains a comprehensive description of the sampling and modeling tools needed for derivative-free optimization; these tools allow the reader to better understand the convergent properties of the algorithms and identify their differences and similarities." (abstract)

### S042 · Learning sparse Gaussian Markov networks using a greedy coordinate ascent approach (ECML PKDD 2010, DOI 10.1007/978-3-642-15939-8_13)
**Read**: abstract · **Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: I. Rish
- **D1 Problem & entry**: sparse inverse covariance selection, which is equivalent to learning a sparse Gaussian Markov network (abstract).
- **D2 Key idea**: SINCO, a greedy coordinate-ascent method that keeps the network structure sparse as it goes and is easy to parallelise. Because it is greedy, its iterates at one small λ reproduce the regularisation-path behaviour (abstract).
- **D8 Contribution**: an algorithm plus an empirical study against glasso and COVSEL on structure-reconstruction error, especially false positives, and on time. The data are simulated networks plus biological and neuroimaging data (abstract). This is the conference version of the SINCO preprint S049 (card s2-02).
**Method links**:
- Method 5 ⚠ variant (abstract): a head-to-head comparison with the standard solvers, measured by recovery error and time, not at equal accuracy or on an evaluation count.
- Method 6 ⚠ weak (abstract): the coordinate choice is designed to preserve sparsity, but the abstract does not say how the structure is exploited.
- new-pattern candidate: **solver path as model-selection path** (s2-02, S049). The abstract states the claim directly (quote 1), so this is a second, independent statement of it.

**Transferable technique**: if your solver is greedy, check whether its iterate sequence at one fixed small regularisation value traces the same sparsity/accuracy trade-off as a full regularisation path. If it does, report stopping at a target number of links as a tuning-free alternative to a λ sweep (abstract).

**Quotes**:
- "Finally, we show that SINCO’s greedy nature allows reproduction of the regularization path behavior by applying the method to one (sufficiently small) instance of the regularization parameter λ only; thus, SINCO can obtain a desired number of network links directly, without having to tune the λ parameter." (abstract)

### S120 · Derivative Free Optimization of Complex Systems with the Use of Statistical Machine Learning Models (AFOSR final report 2015, DTIC ADA622645, DOI 10.21236/ada622645)
**Read**: abstract · **Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: none (sole PI)
- **D1 Problem & entry**: DFO methods that borrow techniques and models from statistical learning (abstract).
- **D2 Key idea**: build local models from *randomly* sampled points. These are fairly accurate with relatively few samples, but only with some high probability. When a model is accurate is not known; only the probability of accuracy is (abstract).
- **D8 Contribution**: the focus is new convergence theory for this setting. This is a grant report, and the specific results are not listed in the abstract (abstract).
**Method links**:

### S062 · On the construction of quadratic models for derivative-free trust-region algorithms (EURO Journal on Computational Optimization 2017, 5(4):501–527, DOI 10.1007/s13675-017-0081-7)
**Read**: abstract · **Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: A. Verdério, E. W. Karas, L. G. Pedroso
- **D1 Problem & entry**: sampling-based DFO trust-region methods for convex-constrained problems. The question is which conditions on the quadratic models ensure global convergence (abstract).
- **D2 Key idea**: split model quality into two conditions: (i) poisedness of the sample set, and (ii) a bound on the model-versus-objective error *at the sample points*. Condition (ii) is trivial for interpolation, and the paper proves it also holds for support-vector-regression models. Together they give a gradient error of order δ_k, where δ_k controls the sample-set diameter, separately from the trust-region radius (abstract).
- **D8 Contribution**: a global convergence proof for a two-radius trust-region method, plus preliminary experiments with and without noise (abstract). The Portuguese precursor is S121 (card s2-03).
**Method links**:
- Method 4 ✅ evidence (abstract): poisedness stays the certificate of model quality. The new element is a separate sampling radius δ_k, decoupled from the trust-region radius, which is Method 4 step 5's "sampling radius" as a design variable.
- Method 1 ✅ evidence, deterministic form (abstract): the gradient-error bound is O(δ_k), scaled to a radius the algorithm controls.
- new-pattern candidate: **reverse field import** (s01/S033; s2-03/S121). An ML regressor (SVR) is admitted as the DFO model builder once it meets condition (ii) (abstract).
- new-pattern candidate: **convergence by embedding in an existing framework** (s2-02). Conditions (i) and (ii) imply an O(δ_k) gradient error, which is the standard input to trust-region convergence (abstract).

**Transferable technique**: to admit a new model class into a model-based DFO method, split the quality requirement in two. Poisedness of the sample set is the part the algorithm controls. Bounded model error at the sample points is the part the model class must satisfy. Then prove only the second for the new class (abstract).

**Quotes**:
- "The first condition requires the poisedness of the sample sets, as usual in this context, while the other one is related to the error between the model and the objective function at the sample points." (abstract)
- "Although the second condition trivially holds if the model is constructed by polynomial interpolation, since in this case the model coincides with the objective function at the sample set, we show that it also holds for models constructed by support vector regression." (abstract)
