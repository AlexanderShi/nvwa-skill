### S111 · Collaborative Research: AF: Small: Adaptive Optimization of Stochastic and Noisy Function (NSF Award 2008434, 2020, no DOI)
**Read**: abstract (NSF abstract + NSF outcomes report, last modified 12/30/2024). **Coauthors**: none listed; the award is to Cornell University with Scheinberg as PI. It is funded by the CCF Algorithmic Foundations program, runs 10/01/2020–09/30/2024 and has $101,000 obligated (NSF record). The record lists 7 publications: Jin–Scheinberg (NeurIPS 2021), Scheinberg–Xie (WSC 2023), Berahas–Cao–Choromanski–Scheinberg (FoCM), Jin–Scheinberg–Xie (SIOPT 2024 and Math. Program. 2024), Cao–Berahas–Scheinberg (Math. Program.) and Berahas–Cao–Scheinberg (SIOPT 2021) (NSF record).
- **D1**: The entry point is the cost of tuning. SG and its variants are nonadaptive, so each task needs expensive data-dependent hyperparameter tuning. The goal is adaptive algorithms with theoretical guarantees (NSF abstract).
- **D2**: The adaptive methods are taken from deterministic optimization (line search, trust region). These lack optimal worst-case complexity, so the project focuses on adaptive cubic regularization for the stochastic setting. The analysis builds on a framework that views adaptive minimization as a renewal–reward stochastic process (NSF abstract).
- **D8**:
  - The outcomes report says the project relaxed noise assumptions to four cases: biased estimators; bounded irreducible noise; a positive probability of arbitrarily corrupted values; and biased estimators that are otherwise light-tailed.
  - It analysed step search, trust region and cubically regularized Newton. These methods converge at fast rates to a neighbourhood of stationary points, until the uncontrollable noise interferes, and bounds on that neighbourhood were derived (NSF outcomes report).
  - Training: an REU (undergraduate research) project with 3 undergraduates implemented standard and robust gradient estimators. The report says it found gaps between theory and practice that the PI plans to study (NSF outcomes report).

**Method links**:
- Method 3 ✅: the renewal–reward process framework is stated as the design basis in a document Scheinberg wrote as PI (NSF abstract).
- Method 2 ✅: adaptive stochastic methods built from deterministic line search, trust region and cubic regularization, motivated by avoiding tuning (NSF abstract).
- Method 1 ✅: the report lists the oracle failure types covered and the reachable neighbourhood, matching Method 1 step 3 (controllable vs irreducible error) (NSF outcomes report).

- **D8**:
  - High-probability complexity bounds for trust-region, cubic-regularization and step-search methods with noisy or unreliable inputs.
  - Adaptive stochastic ISTA/FISTA and a proximal trust-region method for convex composite problems.
  - Approximations of zero-one and ranking losses; accelerated methods for shuffled data.
  - Under review: nonsmooth stochastic trust region, a unified high-probability framework for unreliable inputs, adaptive regularization with cubics (ARC), sample complexity, and stochastic sequential quadratic programming (SQP) (NSF outcomes report).
