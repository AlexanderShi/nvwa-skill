### S158 · A penalty-interior point method combined with MADS for equality and inequality constrained optimization (arXiv preprint 2026, arXiv:2601.20811)
**Role**: core · **Coauthors**: A. Brilli (Sapienza Rome; first and corresponding author, p. 1), Y. Diouane, S. Le Digabel, E. J. Silva (NOVA Lisbon), C. Tribes · **Position in trajectory**: the next step in the constraint-handling line filter GPS (2004) → progressive barrier (2009) → linear equalities (2015) (p. 2). It imports the LOG-DS mixed penalty–log-barrier of the Rome/Lisbon line-search school into MADS (p. 3), and S172 names it as a future fusion target (S172 p. 20).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: MADS handles constraints with the extreme barrier (only feasible points are evaluated) and the progressive barrier, and PB was extended to *linear* equalities only, by a reformulation that eliminates variables (p. 2). Merit-function and linesearch DFO methods handle general constraints. LOG-DS treats inequalities by a log barrier and equalities by a penalty, but it assumes continuously differentiable functions and proves KKT results under smoothness (p. 3). The paper enters by rebuilding that smooth-setting method for nonsmooth blackboxes, with MADS as the inner solver (§1.2, p. 3). Problem (P0): min f s.t. g_ℓ ≤ 0, h_j = 0, with all functions valued in R ∪ {+∞} (p. 2).
- **D2 Core idea and algorithm design**:
  - Inequalities are split by their status at x0. ℓ goes to G_int if g_ℓ(x0) < 0, otherwise to G_ext, and G_ext also holds every equality (p. 3, p. 7).
  - The interior constraints are aggregated into one interior violation c_int(x) = −∏ min{1, −g_ℓ(x)} (and max g_ℓ outside Ω_int). The threshold at 1 makes log(−c_int) = Σ log min{1, −g_ℓ} ≥ 0, which removes the unbounded-below defect of pure log barriers (p. 4).
  - Exterior violation: c_ext = Σ max{0, g_ℓ}² + Σ h_j² (eq. 1, p. 4).
  - Merit: z(x; ρ) = f − ρ log(−c_int) + c_ext/ρ, with a single parameter ρ for both the barrier and the penalty (Def. 2.1, p. 5).
  - What is kept: Algorithm 1 is plain MADS on z(·; ρ_k) in Steps 0–2 (mesh as in OrthoMADS, optional Search, Poll in the frame) (p. 5–6).
  - What is new: Step 3, at unsuccessful iterations only. ρ ← θ_ρ ρ when Δ_{k+1} ≤ min{ρ_k^β, [φ^prox(x_k)]²} (eq. 2, p. 6–7). The stated rationale: high stationarity accuracy is pointless while ρ is large, and trial points must stay away from the barrier boundary (p. 7).
  - Practice (§4): a constraint moves from G_ext to G_int once a new incumbent satisfies it strictly (tolerance 10⁻¹⁴). This can happen only finitely often, and the analysis applies after the last switch (p. 18–19). Scaling constants: b_int = 1, and b_ext is a power of 10 matched to |f(x0)| (p. 19). Whenever the merit changes, z is recomputed on all cached points and the best one becomes the incumbent (p. 20).
- **D3 Assumptions and model**:
  - Global assumption: only bounded level sets of f (Assumption 1), which carries over to z because z ≥ f (p. 8).
  - Local assumptions: f Lipschitz near the end-path point, c_int and c_ext Lipschitz (inside the qualifications), and poll directions dense in the unit sphere (Def. 3.13, Thm 3.14, p. 14).
  - Two new constraint qualifications, each followed by a plain-language paragraph that relates it to MFCQ (p. 13, p. 16):
    - FCQ: at an infeasible point there is a direction that decreases c_ext, and also c_int if c_int = 0 (Def. 3.12, p. 13).
    - SCQ: Clarke derivatives of c_int are negative along hypertangent directions, and those of c_ext are negative just outside Ω_ext (Def. 3.16, p. 16).
  - Limits the authors state:
    - Stationarity is proved only for p = 0, because SCQ is incompatible with equalities: the hypertangent cone is empty when the feasible set has dimension below n (p. 16).
    - The threshold t is declared out of scope (p. 4).
    - The practical constants (β = 1 + 10⁻⁹, b_ρ = 10, b_c = 10¹⁰, θ_ρ = 10⁻², ρ0 = 10⁻¹) were set after preliminary tests and are said to be consistent with the analysis (p. 19–20).
- **D4 Analysis and proof devices**:
  - New vocabulary: *path-following subsequences* are the iterations where ρ decreases, all of them unsuccessful, and their limits are *end-path points*. The terms come from interior-point methods and replace MADS refining subsequences (Def. 3.1, p. 8). *Path-refining directions* add the condition c_int(x_k + d_k) ≤ c_int(x_k) (Def. 3.8, p. 11).
  - ρ_k → 0 (Thm 3.3, p. 9), by contradiction: with ρ fixed the method is plain MADS on z(·; ρ0), so liminf Δ_k = 0 (Prop. 3.2, cited rather than reproved). If the gate never fires, φ^prox → 0, so z → +∞, which contradicts the monotone decrease of z.
  - Δ_k → 0 on the path-following subsequence, so end-path points exist (Thm 3.4, Cor. 3.5, p. 9–10).
  - Key lemma (Prop. 3.6, p. 10–11): for large k the whole minimal frame lies strictly inside Ω_int. Lipschitz continuity of φ^prox and the gate Δ_{k+1} ≤ (φ^prox)² give φ^prox(x_k + d) ≤ φ^prox(x_k)(L_φ φ^prox(x_k)/θ_Δ + 1) < 0. Then log t ≤ t − 1 turns the failed-poll inequality into the barrier-free inequality (8).
  - Feasibility (Prop. 3.10, Thm 3.14, p. 12–15): multiply the failed-poll inequality by ρ_k and let ρ_k → 0. The objective term vanishes, leaving (c_ext)°(x̄; d) ≥ 0 in all (dense) directions, and FCQ turns this into c_ext(x̄) = 0. The case c_int(x̄) = 0 goes through path-refining directions (Remark 3.9, p. 12).
  - Stationarity (Prop. 3.18 via Clarke's mean-value theorem, Thm 3.19, p. 17–18): along hypertangent directions both violation terms are nonincreasing near x̄, so f°(x̄; d) ≥ 0 on the hypertangent cone.
  - All results are asymptotic. There are no rates or complexity bounds (§3).
- **D5 Numerical experiments**:
  - Implementation: a NOMAD 4 fork of release 4.5.2 with GMesh. The MADS-PIP class derives from the MADS-PB class, replacing the objective by the merit and setting infeasibility to 0 (p. 18). Poll: Ortho 2N. The Nelder–Mead search is disabled. Each method runs with and without the quadratic-model search (QMS), which builds per-output models and combines them into the merit (p. 21).
  - Protocol: data profiles in groups of n+1 evaluations; f* is the best feasible value found by any algorithm; an instance is a problem, an x0 and a seed (p. 20).
  - A second profile type counts the instances where a first feasible point is found (p. 20).
  - Equalities count as satisfied when |h_j| < 10⁻⁸. MADS-PB was modified to add |h_j|² to its infeasibility measure above the same threshold (p. 20–21).
  - Test sets:
    - (i) 14 inequality problems from the literature, n = 2–13, feasible and infeasible x0, 10 seeds, 780 runs per algorithm (p. 21, Table 1 p. 22).
    - (ii) solar6 (n = 5, 6 inequalities): the 30 repository starting points, 1,500 evaluations and about 14 h per run, with untuned CMA-ES added (p. 21–22).
    - (iii) 25 CUTEst problems with equalities, n = 4–41, up to 21 equalities, 5 seeds (Table 2, p. 23).
    - (iv) Aircraft Range MDO in IDF form, with 3 coupling equalities, 13 variables and 10 inequalities; 10 x0 × 10 seeds (p. 24–25).
  - Negative results are reported:
    - On (i) PIP is only slightly better, and the models matter more than PIP vs PB (p. 21).
    - On solar6 QMS degrades performance, and without models PIP beats PB only at small τ (p. 22).
    - On (iii)–(iv) PIP finds feasible points far more often: the PB variants fail on 70% of MDO instances, while PIP-QMS succeeds on 95% (p. 24–25).
  - Presentation slips:
    - The MDO paragraph speaks of performance profiles and names MADS-PB QMS as the dominant method right after saying MADS-PB fails on 100% of instances (p. 25). This reads as a naming slip; the curve itself is not recoverable.
    - Figure legends carry internal run labels (p. 22–25).
- **D6 Literature positioning**:
  - Constraint handling is framed as penalty / filter / barrier (p. 2), and two lineages are walked through:
    - direct search: Lewis–Torczon, the Audet–Dennis filter, MADS EB, PB, linear equalities (p. 2);
    - merit/linesearch DFO: Gratton–Vicente, Diouane, Lucidi–Sciandrone, Liuzzi–Lucidi, Fasano et al., sequential penalty, interior point, LOG-DS (p. 2–3).
  - Prior work is restated, not attacked. The only drawback stated for LOG-DS is its smoothness assumption (p. 3), yet LOG-DS is not run as a competitor: §5 uses only MADS-PB and CMA-ES.
  - The mesh-free sibling ADS is mentioned as an acceptance rule that needs neither a mesh nor sufficient decrease (p. 5).
  - References [4] and [5] are the same PB paper (p. 26).
- **D7 Writing and rhetoric**:
  - The contribution paragraph (§1.2, p. 3) has four sentences: the gap, the partition, the guarantees, and a hedged empirical claim (competitive with, and often outperforms).
  - Every new definition (FCQ, SCQ) is followed by an intuition paragraph (p. 13, p. 16).
  - The empirical verbs are hedged (seems to degrade, p. 22; suggest, p. 25) next to one strong priority claim (p. 25; see Quotes).
  - Editing slips:
    - Cross-references call propositions and definitions *Theorem* (e.g., Theorem 3.6 for Proposition 3.6, p. 12; Theorem 2.1 for Definition 2.1, p. 18).
    - The AMS codes 68Q25, 68R10 and 68U05 are computer-science classes (p. 1).
- **D8 Contributions and limits**:
  - Contribution levels: an algorithm (MADS-PIP); nonsmooth theory (feasibility even with equalities, Clarke stationarity for inequalities); software (a NOMAD 4 class); benchmark evidence on analytic sets and two realistic blackboxes (p. 25–26).
  - Limits are concrete and sit in the body: no stationarity with equalities (p. 16), threshold t out of scope (p. 4), constants set by preliminary tests (p. 19–20).
  - The conclusion (p. 25–26) has no future-work paragraph.

**Method links**:
- Method 2 ✅ evidence: equalities get their own mechanism (the exterior penalty), and inequalities are routed by their status at x0 (p. 3–4, p. 7). PB's failure on the equality-constrained sets (p. 24–25) is the empirical basis for the SKILL rule that equalities go to EQPB with Mads-PIP rather than Mads-PB (Method 2, step 3).
- Method 1 ⚠ variant: the Search stays free and heuristic (p. 5–7), but convergence is carried by the Poll *plus* a new outer-parameter gate at unsuccessful iterations (Step 3, p. 6–7), not by the Poll alone.
- Method 3 ⚠ variant: a two-rung ladder, feasibility under FCQ (equalities included) → Clarke stationarity under SCQ (inequalities only), with its boundary stated (p. 13–16). No counterexample is built; the pathological case is delegated to the PB paper's appendix (p. 16).
- Method 5 ✅ evidence (with a gap): data profiles in evaluation groups, feasibility profiles, analytic + CUTEst + two realistic blackboxes, and CMA-ES on solar6 (p. 20–25). The closest rival, LOG-DS, is not run.
- Method 4 ✅ evidence: the paper reuses solar6 with its 30 repository starting points, and aircraft_range in two formulations, MDF in set (i) and IDF in set (iv) (p. 22, p. 24).
- Method 6 ✅ evidence: MADS-PIP is a derived class of MADS-PB inside NOMAD 4 (p. 18).
- new-pattern candidate: *step-size-gated outer parameter* (p. 6–7, p. 9–11).
- new-pattern candidate: *cross-school import with a nonsmooth re-proof* (p. 3).

**Transferable techniques**:
- (D2) Bound a log barrier from below by thresholding each constraint at 1 before the log. Since log min{1, −g} ≥ 0, the merit cannot go to −∞ inside the feasible set (p. 4).
- (D2) Choose barrier or penalty per constraint by its sign at x0. Let a constraint migrate from penalty to barrier the first time an incumbent satisfies it strictly. Only finitely many migrations can occur, so the asymptotic analysis is untouched (p. 7, p. 18–19).
- (D2/D4) Gate the outer-parameter decrease on the inner step size: decrease ρ only when Δ_{k+1} ≤ min{ρ^β, dist²}, where dist is a proxy for the distance to the barrier boundary. This one inequality yields ρ → 0, Δ → 0, and a frame provably inside the barrier interior (p. 7, p. 9–11).
- (D4) Scale-then-limit: multiply a failed-poll inequality by the penalty parameter. As the parameter goes to 0 only the Clarke derivative of the violation survives, which proves that limit points are feasible (p. 12, p. 15).
- (D5) When comparing constraint-handling strategies, plot the portion of instances with a first feasible point beside the τ-solved data profiles, and use the same relaxed equality tolerance for every method (p. 20–21).
- (D5) Run the same engineering model in two formulations (MDF with inequalities only; IDF with added coupling equalities) to separate the effect of equalities from the physics (p. 22, p. 24).
- (Implementation) When the merit changes, re-score the whole evaluation cache under the new merit and continue from its best point (p. 20).

### S172 · Adaptive direct search algorithms with relaxable and quantifiable constraints (arXiv preprint 2026, arXiv:2607.05183)
**Role**: core · **Coauthors**: T. Denorme (student address at Polytechnique, p. 1), Y. Diouane, S. Le Digabel, C. Tribes · **Position in trajectory**: the mesh-free successor line. ADS with the extreme barrier only (G-2025-53 / arXiv 2507.23054, cited p. 2 and p. 21) → ADS-PB (this paper) → an announced fusion with MADS-PIP (S158) for equalities (p. 20). The analytic test set is S158's Table 1 (p. 16–17).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**: ADS enforces feasibility with the extreme barrier, which is called overly restrictive when violations can be quantified and reduced (p. 2). A second driver is the trend toward surrogate and quadratic-model DFO: a framework should admit model-based trial points without degrading them (p. 2–3). A toy problem makes the gap concrete (§2.3, p. 7–8): min x₁² + x₂² s.t. x₁ − 3x₂ ≤ 0 and x₂ − 3x₁ ≤ 0 from (5/3, 5/3). A model-based algorithm reaches x* = (0, 0) in fewer than ten evaluations, while MADS-PB's mesh projection keeps it from ever evaluating x* exactly (Fig. 2, p. 8). Constraints are assumed quantifiable and relaxable in the Le Digabel–Wild taxonomy, and failed evaluations are modelled as +∞ (p. 2).
- **D2 Core idea and algorithm design**:
  - ADS-PB combines the PB framework with ADS's mesh-free structure. The PB framework is Algorithm 1 (p. 7): feasible and infeasible incumbents; dominating, improving and unsuccessful iterations; the h_max rule (6) (p. 4–6).
  - Three changes relative to MADS-PB (p. 11):
    1. The Search is completely free in X; nothing is projected.
    2. Poll points closer than δ^k to any previously *successful* point are discarded without evaluation. The *punctured space* (Def. 1, p. 8) and its exclusion radius δ play the role of the mesh size (p. 8).
    3. A fourth iteration status, *reframing*: the Search finds a dominating point outside the punctured space, the Poll around it fails, and the incumbent still changes (p. 9–11).
  - Kept from MADS-PB: the two poll centers, polling along a normalized positive spanning set scaled by Δ^k (p. 9), and the h_max update (p. 10).
  - Parameters: (Δ, δ) increase after dominating iterations, stay put after improving ones, and decrease otherwise (eq. 9, p. 11). The set V_succ grows by at most one point per iteration (eq. 10, p. 11).
  - Implementation detail: poll centers are computed after the Search, because the Search can change the incumbents (p. 16).
- **D3 Assumptions and model**:
  - Global assumption: all evaluated points lie in a compact set (Assumption 1, p. 12).
  - Stationarity needs f Lipschitz near a feasible refined point, or h Lipschitz near an infeasible one (Thms 3.2–3.3, p. 14–15).
  - The increase/decrease rules are assumed to give δ/Δ → 0 whenever δ → 0 (p. 11).
  - No sufficient decrease is required (p. 1, p. 3). No density assumption on directions is made, so the conclusions hold only along refining directions in the hypertangent cone (Thm 3.2, p. 14).
- **D4 Analysis and proof devices**:
  - Thm 3.1 (p. 12–13) proves lim δ^k = lim Δ^k = 0, a full limit, by a packing argument. Points accepted while δ^k ≥ ε are pairwise at least ε apart but live in a compact set, which contradicts Bolzano–Weierstrass.
  - The authors stress that this is stronger than MADS-PB's liminf and comparable to sufficient-decrease results (p. 13).
  - Thm 3.2 (p. 14–15): a poll point skipped because it is too close to an old point is replaced by that cached point y^k = p + Δ(v + (δ/Δ)w) with ‖w‖ ≤ 1. Since δ/Δ → 0 the perturbed directions converge to v̂, and the Clarke–Jahn derivative (via [8, Prop. 3.9]) gives f°(x̂_F; v̂) ≥ 0.
  - Thm 3.3 (p. 15) proves the same for h at infeasible refined points. If such a point is feasible, it is a global minimizer of h.
  - Slip: the proof of Thm 3.2 writes p^k_F = x̂_F for all k ∈ L (p. 14) where convergence is meant.
- **D5 Numerical experiments**:
  - Implementation: a NOMAD development version, as a specialized variant of MADS-PB (p. 16).
  - Analytic set: the 14 problems of S158's Table 1, 780 instances (10 seeds). All methods share the quadratic-model, speculative and Nelder–Mead searches, and each is run with a 2n poll and an n+1 poll; MADS-PB n+1 is the NOMAD default (p. 16–17). Data profiles in groups of n+1 evaluations, τ ∈ {10⁻³, 10⁻⁵, 10⁻⁷} (Fig. 3, p. 18).
    - Result: ADS-PB beats MADS-PB for each poll type, and n+1 beats 2n, with the gap largest at τ = 10⁻⁷ (p. 17).
  - Blackboxes, against NSGA-II (pymoo), CMA-ES (cma) and LOGDS (the authors' MATLAB code) (p. 17–18):
    - STYRENE: n = 8, m = 11 including four non-relaxable constraints; 30 feasible repository x0 × 3 seeds.
    - SOLAR6: 30 LHS repository x0, 1 seed, about four CPU-days.
    - τ is loosened to {10⁻², 10⁻³, 10⁻⁴} for these costlier problems (p. 18).
  - Results (p. 18–19):
    - ADS-PB is best overall.
    - MADS-PB is competitive at moderate τ but falls behind at 10⁻⁴.
    - LOGDS is weak on STYRENE, which the authors explain by non-quantifiable constraints and hidden-constraint failures, and it converges prematurely on SOLAR6.
    - The evolutionary methods are far behind.
  - Two gaps in the write-up:
    - The gain is only hypothesised to come from quadratic-model points being evaluated without mesh projection (p. 19); no ablation isolates that mechanism.
    - The paper does not say how ADS-PB handled STYRENE's four non-relaxable constraints (p. 18).
- **D6 Literature positioning**:
  - The paper surveys constrained DFO (evolution strategies, the merit-function DS of Gratton–Vicente, LOG-DS, GA) and the model-based trend (subspace quadratic models, adaptive sampling, model-and-search) (p. 2), plus the 2025 direct-search survey (p. 3).
  - PB is presented as a framework separable from the mesh: it can be realized by merit-function methods too (p. 3–4). This is how the paper justifies moving PB to ADS.
  - Theorem strength is benchmarked explicitly against MADS-PB and against sufficient-decrease analyses [16, 26] (p. 13).
  - The critique of the mesh is made by the toy figure, not by attacking prior papers (p. 7–8).
- **D7 Writing and rhetoric**:
  - The contributions (§1.2, p. 3) come in three moves: a framework that removes two structural features (mesh, sufficient decrease), convergence under standard DFO assumptions, and a NOMAD implementation with experiments.
  - A three-panel figure partitions the (f, h)-plane into dominating, improving and unsuccessful regions (Fig. 1, p. 5–6).
  - The discussion is hedged (can achieve; likely due to, p. 20).
  - An explicit *Use of AI* statement: ChatGPT for figure layout and English only (p. 20).
  - Editing slips: a duplicated paragraph on V_succ (p. 11) and a subject–verb agreement error when announcing the three main differences (p. 11).
- **D8 Contributions and limits**:
  - Contribution levels: an algorithm (ADS-PB); theory (full-limit step-size convergence, Clarke stationarity of f at feasible and of h at infeasible refined points); software (a NOMAD variant); benchmarks (p. 20).
  - Future work (p. 20):
    - hybrids with sufficient decrease to obtain complexity guarantees;
    - a fusion of MADS-PIP and ADS-PB for relaxable equality and inequality constraints;
    - Search strategies designed for the absence of mesh projection, with their theoretical impact.
  - Experimental limitations (a single seed on SOLAR6, no ablation of the model effect) are not discussed.

**Method links**:
- Method 1 ⚠ variant: the Poll still carries the proof and the Search is free, but here the Search is also free of the mesh (p. 11). The Poll gains an exclusion filter, and the interplay between Search and Poll creates a new iteration status (reframing, p. 9–10). This matches the SKILL's note that the mesh restriction was acknowledged by moving to ADS.
- Method 2 ✅ evidence: relaxable and quantifiable constraints move from the EB to the PB (p. 2). A rival's poor results are explained by constraint semantics, namely non-quantifiable and hidden constraints on STYRENE (p. 18).
- Method 3 ✅ evidence (partial): the theorem's strength is positioned on the ladder explicitly (a full limit vs MADS-PB's liminf, p. 13). Stationarity is stated along refining directions only, since there is no density assumption (p. 14). No counterexamples are built.
- Method 5 ✅ evidence: a same-solver analytic comparison with identical search components (p. 16), then realistic blackboxes against three other families, one of them the rival's own code (p. 17–18). Tolerances are adapted to problem cost (p. 18).
- Method 4 ✅ evidence: STYRENE and SOLAR are run from the bbopt repository starting points (p. 18).
- Method 6 ✅ evidence: ADS-PB is a NOMAD variant that beats the current NOMAD default (MADS-PB n+1) (p. 17).
- new-pattern candidate: *toy pathology figure before the algorithm* (p. 7–8).
- new-pattern candidate: *same-solver controlled comparison with shared components* (p. 16, p. 20; also S158 p. 18).

**Transferable techniques**:
- (D1) Open with a 2-variable convex toy where the incumbent method's structural feature (here, mesh projection) visibly blocks an optimum that a simple model finds at once. Show the trajectory plot next to the convergence plot (p. 7–8).
- (D2) Replace a lattice with exclusion balls around accepted points. This keeps the property a mesh provides (only finitely many successes while the step stays bounded away from 0), without projecting trial points (p. 8, p. 12–13).
- (D4) Packing argument: accepted points pairwise ≥ ε apart inside a compact set ⇒ finitely many ⇒ the step size tends to 0 (a full limit, not a liminf) (p. 12–13).
- (D4) Absorb a skipped trial point: use the nearby cached point as a trial along the perturbed direction v + (δ/Δ)w, and design the updates so that δ/Δ → 0. The limiting direction, and hence the Clarke bound, is unchanged (p. 14–15).
- (D5) Compare the new method and the baseline inside the same solver with identical search components and two poll variants, and include the solver's current default as a baseline (p. 16–17).

**Quotes**:
- "By lifting these restrictions, the framework can directly exploit high-quality trial points generated by search heuristics, surrogate models, or hybrid strategies without altering them through mesh projection or rejecting them because of restrictive acceptance requirements." (p. 3)
- "The implementation within NOMAD enables a direct comparison with MADS-PB under identical algorithmic components." (p. 20)

### S173 · Multi-fidelity constraints in blackbox optimization (arXiv preprint 2026, arXiv:2601.06321)
**Role**: core · **Coauthors**: S. Alarie and M. Diago (Hydro-Québec), S. Le Digabel, X. Lebeuf (corresponding author, p. 1; funded by a Hydro-Québec doctoral excellence grant, p. 24) · **Position in trajectory**: successor to Inter-DS (COAP 2025) and to the interruptible and hierarchical extreme barriers (ORL 2022) (p. 4–5). Motivated by Hydro-Québec's PRIAD asset-management project (p. 3) and benchmarked on the group's solar suite (p. 17).

- **D1 Problem and entry point**:
  - The entry is an industrial cost wall. One high-fidelity PRIAD evaluation may take up to 145 days, and 2,000 evaluations in a week would need 45,000 CPUs (p. 3).
  - The second motivation is to fix the authors' own Inter-DS: many of its assumptions are lifted and its pathological cases avoided (p. 4).
  - The literature gap (p. 4):
    - multi-fidelity work is mostly unconstrained or penalized;
    - benchmarks use two fidelities;
    - low fidelities are used to approximate gradients or models.
  - The problem P(ϕ) has a discrete, increasing set of fidelities ϕ_1 < … < ϕ_L = 1, relaxable quantifiable constraints, and failures as +∞ (p. 1–2).
  - A classification diagram (Fig. 1, p. 3) sorts multi-fidelity problems into generic, with intermediary outputs (Def. 1, p. 2), and stochastic via SAA, and marks where IDS and DIDS apply.
- **D2 Core idea and algorithm design**:
  - Low fidelities are used *only to detect infeasibility, constraint by constraint*, and to interrupt the evaluation. They are never used to model the objective (p. 4, p. 6).
  - The *fidelity controller* is a wrapper around the blackbox, so any direct-search solver runs unchanged (p. 6).
  - Safeguard: a point deemed feasible below ϕ = 1 that would improve the incumbent is re-evaluated at the truth (Alg. 3.1, p. 7).
  - Each constraint is assigned a fidelity by subproblem Q. Q minimizes the estimated expected evaluation cost Σ λ_i ∏ p, subject to the assigned fidelity being *representative* (sign agreement with the truth at all higher fidelities) at every feasible sample point (Def. 2 p. 8; eqs. 2–6, p. 9).
  - IDS uses an offline sample (e.g., LHS), a cutting algorithm and exhaustive search; Q is solved in at most 1 s (Alg. 4.1–4.2, p. 12–14). An include_truth switch forces ϕ = 1 into the sequence (p. 14).
  - DIDS needs intermediary outputs. It rebuilds the sample around the incumbent as the smallest ball holding n+1 feasible cached points, and uses the closed-form assignment a*_j = i(j) (p. 16–17).
  - If no feasible sample point exists, every constraint goes to the truth, which removes Inter-DS's pathology by construction (p. 8, p. 23–24).
  - In NOMAD every constraint uses the extreme barrier, so that interrupted points are rejected (p. 7, p. 18).
- **D3 Assumptions and model**:
  - Costs λ are unknown but usually increase with ϕ (p. 2).
  - The satisfaction probabilities p are assumed independent across constraints (p. 9, p. 11).
  - Assumption 1, that the conditional truth evaluation has no cost impact, is declared false by the authors but needed for Thm 3 (p. 10). It becomes unnecessary for DIDS (p. 15).
  - Assumption 2a (an offline sample for IDS) is the alternative to Assumption 2b (intermediary outputs for DIDS) (p. 11, p. 15).
  - No convergence analysis of the solver on the wrapped problem is given. The wrapped P can return different values at the same point because it depends on f* and on a (p. 14, p. 16).
- **D4 Analysis and proof devices**: all proofs concern the assignment subproblem.
  - Lemma 1: representativity is monotone in the fidelity, by set inclusion (p. 10).
  - Thm 2: feasibility is conserved between the wrapper and the truth on H ∩ Ω (p. 10).
  - Thm 3: f_Q is the expected cost, as a product of non-interruption probabilities (p. 10–11).
  - Lemma 4 + Thm 5 use an exchange argument: moving every constraint assigned to a fidelity index outside ∪ i(j) down by one index strictly lowers the cost, which justifies the cuts (p. 13).
  - Thm 6 gives the DIDS optimum a*_j = i(j) by a similar exchange (p. 16).
- **D5 Numerical experiments**:
  - Problems: solar2/3/4 (n = 14–29 with integer and categorical variables, m = 12–16 with 4–6 multi-fidelity constraints; Table 1, p. 18).
    - solar7 is excluded, with the reason given: its optimal assignment was almost always the truth (p. 17–18).
    - The problems are run deterministically (seed 0) because SAA was too costly, and code was added to solar to expose intermediary outputs (p. 18).
  - Profiles: time-based data profiles counting only blackbox time, to emulate PRIAD (p. 18, p. 23).
  - Setup (p. 18–21):
    - NOMAD 4.4.0 defaults except the extreme barrier;
    - 11 fidelities {10⁻¹⁰, 0.1, …, 1};
    - one feasible x0 per problem;
    - 20 instances per problem, varied by NOMAD seed;
    - budgets of about 16 h.
  - Baselines: NOMAD on P(1), and Inter-DS. Both LHS-based methods use a 1,000-point LHS whose time is excluded from the profiles, justified as fairness (p. 18). Reader's note: this choice favours the two LHS-based methods (IDS, Inter-DS) over DIDS and base NOMAD.
  - Diagnostics: histograms of the last fidelity used (Figs 5, 7, 9) and tables of the evaluation-count factor vs the base case, the % of runs whose evaluation sequence diverges from the base case, and the % of evaluations before divergence (Tables 2–4, p. 20–23).
  - Results:
    - solar3: DIDS is best at τ = 0.01, and IDS without truth is worst because of feasibility errors (p. 19–20).
    - solar4: only DIDS helps, because the constraints' behaviour across fidelities depends on location (p. 20–21).
    - solar2: IDS is best with 6.86× more evaluations, and its feasibility mistakes led to better solutions. The authors hedge this as most likely chance and needing more instances (p. 22–23).
  - Cost: 6,333 hours of optimization time in total (8.65 months across several machines), which is why only NOMAD pairings were tested (p. 23).
- **D6 Literature positioning**:
  - Constraints are described with the KARQ taxonomy. A priori constraints are checked first at negligible cost and are not counted (p. 5).
  - The paper builds on the interruptible EB and hierarchical satisfiability of [5] and on the *preemption* principle from water-resources calibration [30] (p. 5).
  - It positions itself against multi-fidelity trust-region methods (March–Willcox; Ha–Mueller), Scout-Nd, and constrained multi-fidelity Bayesian optimization (p. 5–6), claiming a radically different angle (p. 4; see D7).
  - Self-critique of Inter-DS is explicit: it is harmful when the sample has no feasible point, and its sample-point selection is crucial (p. 4, p. 23–24).
- **D7 Writing and rhetoric**:
  - The contributions are three bullets that each invert a literature default: many discrete fidelities, direct search rather than models, and constraints handled directly (p. 4).
  - A classification figure comes before the method (p. 3).
  - Admissions are candid: an assumption declared false (p. 10); a surprising result called most likely chance (p. 23); a sentence that names a shortcoming of the study (p. 24).
  - Results are explained mechanistically by naming the constraint that triggered the interruptions: c2 (energy demand) needs high fidelity on solar3/4 (p. 19–20), and c6 (heliostats fitting in the field) is computed before any ray-tracing draw on solar2 (p. 22).
  - Typos: whetter, posses, has lead (p. 14, p. 19, p. 24).
- **D8 Contributions and limits**:
  - Contribution levels: algorithms (IDS, DIDS), light theory on the assignment subproblem, and an empirical study on the solar suite. The method is a wrapper and is not integrated into NOMAD (p. 6, p. 18).
  - Limits and future work are specific:
    - It is unclear whether feasibility mistakes help on solar2, and more instances are needed (p. 23–24).
    - Interrupting at low fidelity may bias the high-fidelity information used for assignment updates (p. 24).
    - The objective's multi-fidelity information is unused, to be integrated later (p. 4, p. 24).
    - IDS needs the constraints' fidelity behaviour to be roughly constant across the space; otherwise only DIDS helps (p. 24).
    - The motivating Hydro-Québec simulator is not yet available, so solar serves as a proxy (p. 3, p. 17).

**Method links**:
- Method 2 ⚠ variant: constraint semantics drive the design, through per-constraint assignment and KARQ (a priori constraints checked first, p. 5). But constraints the paper calls relaxable and quantifiable (p. 1) are run under the EB in NOMAD because interrupted outputs come from low fidelities (p. 7, p. 18). Algorithmic need overrides the relaxable → PB rule.
- Method 1 ⚠ variant: the idea lives in neither the Search nor the Poll but in a wrapper around the blackbox (p. 6), so the solver is untouched. The price is that no guarantee is claimed for the wrapped problem, which is not even a function of x (p. 14, p. 16).
- Method 4 ✅ evidence: the industrial problem (PRIAD) is proxied by the public solar benchmark, which is extended with intermediary-output code for this study (p. 17–18).
- Method 5 ⚠ variant: the budget currency is blackbox time rather than evaluation count (p. 18). There is no competitor from another family, only base NOMAD and the authors' own Inter-DS, justified by 6,333 hours of optimization time (p. 23).
- Method 3 ⚠ variant: the theory covers the auxiliary assignment problem, including an assumption declared false (p. 10). There is no convergence ladder for the optimizer.
- Method 6 ⚠ variant: NOMAD 4.4.0 is the host solver, but the contribution is a solver-agnostic wrapper; Inter-DS was also paired with PSO and Nelder–Mead in [3] (p. 18, p. 23).
- new-pattern candidate: *solver-agnostic evaluation wrapper* (p. 6).
- new-pattern candidate: *divergence-from-baseline diagnostics* (p. 20–23).
- new-pattern candidate: *self-successor paper that repairs one's own previous algorithm* (p. 4, p. 23–24).

**Transferable techniques**:
- (D1) State the cost wall with the partner's numbers on the first pages (days per evaluation, CPUs needed for a realistic budget) (p. 3).
- (D2) Put the idea in a wrapper around the blackbox, so it pairs with any solver, and prove properties of the wrapper rather than of the solver (p. 6–7).
- (D2) Add a fallback so that with no information the method behaves exactly like the baseline (no feasible sample ⇒ evaluate the truth). This removes a known pathology by construction (p. 8).
- (D2) Before accepting a cheap verdict that would change the incumbent, pay for one truth evaluation (p. 7).
- (D4) Prove cuts for a small combinatorial design subproblem by an exchange argument, then solve it by enumeration (p. 12–13).
- (D5) Explain wins and losses with divergence diagnostics: the evaluation-count factor vs the baseline, the % of runs whose trajectory diverges, and how early it diverges. Pair them with a histogram of where evaluations stopped (Tables 2–4, Figs 5/7/9).
- (D5) When evaluation cost dwarfs solver cost in the target application, build time-based profiles from blackbox time only, and state it (p. 18, p. 23).
