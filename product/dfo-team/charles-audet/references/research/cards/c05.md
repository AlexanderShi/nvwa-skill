# Batch c05 · paper cards (core)

**Batch intent**: See how Audet's group extended the MADS framework in 2024–25 along three axes, plus one theory paper. The axes are evaluation cost (a multi-fidelity interruption wrapper, S112), variable type (categorical variables in CatMADS, S121) and the mesh itself (ADS, which replaces the mesh with a "punctured space", S129). The theory paper is on the quality of poll directions (the cosine measure relative to a subspace, S120). The questions: does "free Search, rigid Poll" (Method 1) still hold once the mesh is dropped or the idea sits outside the solver? How do theory and experiments share the load in each paper?
**Focus dimensions**: D1–D8, all four papers being core. Emphasis on D2 (what is kept and what is changed relative to MADS), D4 (proof devices) and D5 (comparison protocol).
**Material roles**: S112 core · S120 core · S121 core · S129 core. Version caveats: S112's text file is arXiv v2 (5 Aug 2024), titled "Fidelity and interruption control for expensive constrained multi-fidelity blackbox optimization". The published title ("Inter-DS …") and the name "Inter-DS" do not occur in the file. S120 is arXiv v2 (24 Oct 2024), S121 arXiv v2 (23 Jun 2025) and S129 arXiv v1 (30 Jul 2025). No journal versions were seen.

### S112 · Inter-DS: a cost saving algorithm for expensive constrained multi-fidelity blackbox optimization (Computational Optimization and Applications 2025, DOI 10.1007/s10589-024-00645-w; arXiv:2312.13128)
**Role**: core · **Coauthors**: S. Alarie, M. Diago (Hydro-Québec), S. Le Digabel, X. Lebeuf (corresponding author; MSc work, ref. [43]) · **Read level**: full, pp. 1–24, including the reference list. The figures are data profiles whose curves are not in the text, so results are taken from the prose. · **Position in research trajectory**: the Hydro-Québec PRIAD line (p. 2). It continues the interruption and hierarchical-constraint work of ORL 2022 [18] (p. 4) and uses the SOLAR benchmark [44] as its test bed (p. 16).

- **D1 Problem & entry point**: The entry is an industrial time budget. Hydro-Québec's PRIAD asset-management blackboxes are expensive, and "the violation of some constraints can be predicted with low fidelity evaluations" (p. 2). Parallelism is ruled out with a number: it "would require thousands of processors" to finish within a month, and speedup degrades (Amdahl) (p. 2). The paper combines two older ideas: preemption, i.e. interrupting an evaluation that cannot beat the incumbent (Razavi et al. [9]), and raising fidelity towards convergence (Polak–Wetter [10, 11]) (p. 3). The literature gap is that multi-fidelity work is mostly unconstrained, uses two fidelities, and uses low fidelity to *guide sampling*, e.g. in BO. Here fidelity is used to *save cost* in constrained direct search, "the subject of very limited research" (p. 4). Problem (P): X = [ℓ, u] unrelaxable, c_j quantifiable, and f, c valued in ℝ ∪ {∞} so that barrier rejections and hidden-constraint failures can be encoded (p. 2). Fidelity φ lies in a finite subset of [0, 1], and outputs at a lower fidelity are *not* assumed to be deducible from a higher-fidelity run (p. 2).
  - Transferable: rule out the obvious fix (parallelism) with a back-of-envelope count before proposing the new mechanism (p. 2).
- **D2 Core idea & algorithm design**: The algorithm has three steps (p. 5; Alg. 3.2, p. 15).
  1. Latin-hypercube samples, centred on x⁰ with a sizing factor ρ (eqs. 1–2, p. 8), are evaluated at *every* fidelity. From them the paper estimates r̂_ij (fidelity i is "representative" for constraint j), p̂_ij (constraint j is violated at fidelity i) and t̂_i (mean time) (eqs. 3–5, p. 9).
  2. A biadjacency matrix B ∈ {0,1}^{L×m} assigns each constraint to the lowest fidelity trusted to predict its feasibility (p. 6). B minimises the expected evaluation time (eq. 9) subject to one fidelity per constraint (10) and a misidentification bound B_ij − ε ≤ r_ij (11) (pp. 9–10).
  3. Any solver runs with Algorithm 3.1 as its blackbox. Sub-evaluations proceed in increasing fidelity and stop as soon as an assigned constraint is violated. If no constraint is assigned to φ = 1 and the point would become the new best, one full-fidelity evaluation is forced first (pp. 7–8).

  What changes relative to classical practice is where the idea lives: "Multi-fidelity evaluations are used not to guide the exploration of the solution space, but rather to reduce overall computational time" (p. 6). The mechanism is a wrapper, so the solver is untouched (p. 14). Fidelities that no constraint uses are filtered out automatically (p. 6). Fig. 2 is a timeline showing one extra point fitting into the same time window (p. 7).
  - Transferable: an incumbent guard, i.e. trust cheap outputs for rejection, but re-evaluate at full fidelity only when a point is about to become the best (Alg. 3.1, pp. 7–8).
- **D3 Assumptions & model**: Assumptions 1–4 are needed "to derive the theoretical results" but "not required to apply the algorithm" (p. 10; p. 12).
  - A1: the extra full-fidelity checks are ignored by the model (p. 10).
  - A2: a constraint satisfied at its assigned fidelity stays satisfied at every higher fidelity. It is admitted to be "sometimes violated", in at most a proportion ε of cases (p. 10).
  - A3: p_ij is monotone decreasing in i. It "simplifies the proof of Lemma 2", and a more realistic assumption is deferred to Lebeuf's thesis [43] (p. 12).
  - A4: evaluation time increases with φ (p. 12).
  - Eq. (8) additionally assumes that violations of different constraints are independent (p. 10).

  The only convergence statement is "inherited" from the chosen solver (p. 14). No proof is given that the solver's guarantee survives outputs that misclassify feasibility with probability up to ε.
- **D4 Analysis & proof techniques**: The theory is about the *design subproblem* Q, not about the optimizer. Q is mixed-integer with a polynomial objective, so a general solver is avoided and Q is reduced to an exhaustive search (p. 11) in three steps.
  1. Eliminate y_i by setting it to the indicator that row i of B is non-empty, and replace (11) by the index i(j) = min{i : r_ij ≥ 1 − ε} (Q1, p. 11).
  2. Theorem 3: some optimal B uses only the fidelities I_F = ∪_j i(j) (pp. 11–13). Lemma 1 shows that moving every constraint on row i′ down to row i′ − 1 keeps B feasible (p. 12). Lemma 2 shows the move does not increase the cost: f(B) is split into four terms T_{<i′−1}, T_{i′−1}, T_{i′} and T_{>i′}, and each is compared using A3 and A4 (pp. 12–13). The move is repeated at most max_j(L − i(j)) times (p. 13).
  3. Drop the a-priori constraints (p. 14), then enumerate Ω_Q (p. 14).

  The size of Ω_Q (the enumeration cost) is not bounded anywhere in the text.
  - Transferable: shrink a combinatorial design problem to a small candidate set with a feasibility-preserving, cost-non-increasing exchange lemma, then enumerate (Lemmas 1–2, Thm 3, pp. 12–13).
- **D5 Numerical experiments**:
  - Test set: four SOLAR instances with fidelity-dependent constraints: solar2/3/4/7 with n = 14/20/29/7, m = 13/13/16/6, of which 5/5/7/2 are a-priori and 4/5/6/2 depend on fidelity (Table 1, p. 16).
  - Variants: NOMAD 4 in three versions, Inter PB, Inter EB, and a base case with NOMAD defaults at fidelity 1. "Comparisons with other blackbox solvers, and tuning the NOMAD parameters are beyond the scope" (p. 16).
  - Currency: blackbox time only, since solver time is negligible for PRIAD-like problems (p. 16).
  - Settings: n_H = 10⁴, ε = 0.05, and a 19-level fidelity set Φ, all "empirically determined" (p. 16).
  - Without x⁰: 20 runs per instance (p. 17). solar2 succeeds (Fig. 3, τ = 0.05 and 0.5). On solar3 and solar4, "every evaluated point is systematically estimated to be infeasible", so no curves are shown, while the base case finds many feasible points (p. 17).
  - With a feasible x⁰: ρ = 1/4, 1/10, 1/20 and 1/4 "based on preliminary results" (p. 17), and 20 NOMAD seeds (p. 18). The LH time is charged, so the curves start at 642.22 s, 45.64 s and 94.75 s (Figs. 4–6, pp. 18–19).
  - Results with x⁰: on solar2 the base case beats Inter EB at τ = 0.5 because of one bad run (p. 18). On solar3 and solar4, Inter EB is "significantly better" and Inter PB is comparable to the base case (p. 19). On solar7 the optimal B puts every constraint at φ = 1, so the method reduces to the base case, lagging only by the 181.07 s of sampling (p. 19).
  - Transferable: charge preprocessing cost on the time axis (curves start after the sampling time), and report the instance where the method correctly degenerates to the baseline (pp. 18–19).
- **D6 Literature positioning**: The paper continues the group's constraint line: PB [16], PEB [17], and the two-phase interruptible EB and hierarchical satisfiability [18] (pp. 3–4), with the taxonomy [12] (p. 3). It is positioned against BO/GP multi-fidelity methods (unconstrained, few fidelities) [19–25], GOSAC [27] and the model-adequacy framework used with MADS [30] (pp. 4–5). The critique of prior work concerns benchmarks: they are "analytical in nature", so realistic ones [40, 41] "should be favored" (p. 5). Novelty is hedged with "To the authors' knowledge" (p. 4). The claimed difference is that the method "selects relevant fidelity levels instead of assuming that a low-fidelity source is necessarily helpful" (p. 4).
- **D7 Writing & rhetoric**: The abstract carries the negative result: "When this condition is not met, the outcomes are contingent upon specific properties of the blackbox" (p. 1). A picture (the Fig. 2 timeline) comes before the model (p. 7). The discussion turns the results into a bullet list of "favorable conditions": scarce feasible points, accurate low-fidelity violation assessments, and homogeneous constraint behaviour inside the LH box (pp. 19–20). Proofs are short algebra with no worked example of Q. The title changed between arXiv and the journal (header of this card).
- **D8 Contribution & limitations**: The contribution is algorithmic (wrapper, B-matrix model, exhaustive-search reduction) plus empirical evidence on SOLAR. The limits are concrete. The method fails without x⁰ on solar3 and solar4 (p. 17). It depends on the homogeneity of constraints within the LH box (p. 20). The choice of EB or PB depends on the blackbox: EB when feasible points are scarce, PB otherwise (p. 20). B is computed once. Future work: a dynamic B with an updated model, ML classifiers [28, 29], trust-region tools, a "multi-fidelity barrier method", and application to PRIAD (p. 20). Funding came from an NSERC/Mitacs Alliance grant with Hydro-Québec (p. 20).

**Method links**:
- Method 2 ✅ evidence: every output has a type: unrelaxable bounds, quantifiable c_j, hidden failures set to ∞ (p. 2), and "a priori" constraints checked before the expensive process (p. 6). EB vs PB is tested as a design choice (p. 16). ⚠ Variant: here the barrier choice depends on the *wrapper* (interrupted points return non-true outputs, so "the EB might be more adapted", p. 16), not only on constraint semantics.
- Method 1 ⚠ variant: the new idea sits neither in the Search nor in the Poll but in the blackbox interface, and "The convergence guarantees of Algorithm 3.2 are inherited from those of the solver" (p. 14). That inheritance is asserted, not proved.
- Method 4 ✅ evidence: an industrial motivation (PRIAD, p. 2) is tested on the released SOLAR suite, "the only benchmark blackbox problem collection where constraints can depend on fidelity" (p. 16).
- Method 5 ⚠ variant: the currency is blackbox *time*, not the evaluation count, because the whole point is that evaluations have variable cost (p. 16). Preprocessing time is charged (pp. 18–19). ✗ No solver from another family (p. 16).
- Method 6 ✅ evidence: built on NOMAD 4, chosen because it "can easily be modified" (p. 16).
- Heuristic 9 ✅ evidence (exploit exposed structure, here fidelities).
- new-pattern candidate: *Solver-agnostic blackbox wrapper*: put the cost-saving mechanism at the blackbox interface so that any solver benefits (p. 14).

**Transferable techniques**:
- Wrap the blackbox, not the solver: the interruption logic lives in the evaluation function, so any solver (even a heuristic) can use it unchanged (p. 14).
- Replace a bi-objective design choice (time vs misclassification) with a threshold ε and minimise expected time alone (p. 9).
- Expected-cost model of sequential sub-evaluations: Σ_i t_i y_i Π_{k<i} P_k(B), where P_k(B) is the probability of no interruption at level k (eqs. 7–9, pp. 9–10).
- Exchange lemma + enumeration in place of a MINLP solver (Lemmas 1–2, Thm 3, pp. 12–13).
- Report failure instances and the degenerate-to-baseline instance explicitly (pp. 17, 19).

**Quotes**:
- "This is possible since the interruption mechanism is not implemented in the solver, but as a wrapper around the blackbox." (p. 14)
- "As a result, when using Algorithm 3.2 with those two instances, every evaluated point is systematically estimated to be infeasible." (p. 17)

### S120 · The cosine measure relative to a subspace (Computational Optimization and Applications 2025, DOI 10.1007/s10589-025-00701-z; arXiv:2401.09609)
**Role**: core · **Coauthors**: W. Hare, G. Jarry-Bolduc · **Read level**: full, pp. 1–22 (p. 23 is only a figure notice). · **Position in research trajectory**: a pure-theory paper from the Audet–Hare collaboration on the geometry that the Poll step relies on (positive spanning sets). It extends Hare & Jarry-Bolduc's 2020 cosine-measure algorithm [10] (p. 17) and targets subspace-decomposition methods, including Audet's own PSD-MADS [2] (p. 2).

- **D1 Problem & entry point**: The entry is the limitation of a standard tool. The cosine measure (introduced in [17], Kolda–Lewis–Torczon 2003) is defined only for finite sets and measures coverage of all of ℝⁿ. For a positive spanning set of a *proper* subspace it therefore "will always return cm (D) = 0", proved as Corollary 23 (p. 4; p. 11). The motivation is subspace-decomposition methods "recently gaining traction" [2, 5, 9, 16, 18, 27] (p. 2). Goals: define cm relative to a subspace, show its value for positive-spanning properties, give a deterministic algorithm, and (secondary) handle infinite sets (p. 2).
- **D2 Core idea & algorithm design**:
  - Definition 6: cm_L(D) = min over unit u ∈ L of sup over d ∈ D of uᵀd/‖d‖, with a cosine vector set (p. 4) and an active set (Def. 9, p. 6). It reduces to the classical cm when L = ℝⁿ and D is finite (p. 5).
  - Remark 7 and Example 8: the measure can be computed from projected vectors, but only if the zero vector is *kept* in the projected set (p. 5).
  - Algorithm 1 (p. 17) first normalises D, then decides whether pspan(D) = span(D) with an LP (Thm 14(vii)).
    - If pspan(D) = span(D): enumerate every basis B ⊆ D of span(D), compute γ_B = (1ᵀG(B)⁻¹1)^{−1/2}, u_B = γ_B(Bᵀ)^†1 and p̊_B = max u_Bᵀ D, and take cm = min_B p̊_B.
    - If not: solve the convex SOCP relaxation min over u ∈ span(D) with ‖u‖ ≤ 1 of max_d uᵀd (MATLAB coneprog, p. 18).
  - The algorithm modifies [10] to handle subspaces and sets that are not positive spanning (p. 17).
- **D3 Assumptions & model**: D is a non-empty set of non-zero vectors (p. 4). Main results assume D closed, which is harmless because closure does not change cm; a referee's example (a non-closed set with an empty active set) shows why the assumption is needed (p. 6; p. 21). Algorithm 1 needs D finite (p. 17). The error bounds assume ∇f Lipschitz (Thm 31(i)), or ∇²f Lipschitz plus a symmetric direction set (Thm 31(ii)), with finite radius Δ_D (p. 15).
- **D4 Analysis & proof techniques**:
  - Carathéodory extracts a finite positive spanning subset of an infinite set (Cor. 12, p. 7).
  - A half-space separation lemma (Rockafellar) gives the characterisation "pspan(D) = L iff every non-zero v ∈ L has some d with vᵀd > 0" (Lemma 17, Prop. 18, pp. 8–9).
  - The orthogonal split v = P_D v + v^⊥ gives Prop. 19 and Cor. 23 (pp. 9, 11).
  - Perturbation of an optimal cosine vector, u′ = u* + εv*, yields contradictions (Lemma 27, p. 13; Thm 38 with the norm facts of Lemma 37, pp. 19–20).
  - A reductionist descent to strictly smaller subsets proves "cm_D(D) = 0 iff D contains a finite proper subset V with pspan(V) = span(V)" (Thm 28, p. 14).
  - Error bound at a failed poll: combining the cosine inequality (9) with a first-order Taylor remainder gives ‖P_D∇f(x⁰)‖ ≤ ½ L_∇f cm_D(D)⁻¹ Δ_D (eq. 7, p. 15). For symmetric sets, multiplying the expansion along d by α_d² and subtracting the one along −α_d d cancels the Hessian term and gives an O(Δ_D²) bound (eqs. 8, 13, pp. 15–16).
  - Exactness of the convex relaxation when pspan ≠ span, by a normalisation argument (Lemma 33, p. 18).
  - Counterexamples mark each boundary: Example 8 (p. 5), the non-closed set (p. 6), the "floating ring" (positively independent, so no finite subset has the same positive span; p. 7), Example 15 (p. 8), {e₁, −e₁, e₂} for the converse of Prop. 19 (p. 9), and D₂ of Example 21, showing Cor. 23 cannot be an "iff" (p. 11). Example 21 is a running example revisited after each result (p. 10).
  - Transferable: the symmetric-direction cancellation trick that upgrades an O(Δ) gradient bound to O(Δ²) (Thm 31(ii), pp. 15–16).
- **D5 Numerical experiments**: none: "No datasets were generated or analysed during the current study" (p. 21). A MATLAB implementation of Algorithm 1 is "available upon request" (p. 21). The cost of enumerating all bases in step 2 is not discussed (not found in the text).
- **D6 Literature positioning**: The paper builds on Davis 1954 [7], Regis [21, 22], Hare & Jarry-Bolduc [10], Nævdal [20] and Hare et al. [11, 12]. Results are framed as extensions: "can be viewed as an extension of [7, Theorem 3.1]" (p. 11), and Theorem 38 as an extension of Prop. 17 in [10] (p. 20). Theorem 31(i) with L = ℝⁿ reproduces the textbook bound of Conn–Scheinberg–Vicente [6, Thm 2.8] (p. 15). Prior work is never criticised; the move is reformulation.
- **D7 Writing & rhetoric**: A definition → proposition → example rhythm. The paper is candid about incremental results: "the value of the following proposition is to show that it also holds for infinite sets" (p. 8). Hedging differs by location: the abstract says the definition "might be useful", while the introduction says the results "will be of high value" (pp. 1–2). The conclusion lists each result by number (p. 21), and a referee is credited for an example (p. 21).
- **D8 Contribution & limitations**: The contribution is theoretical: a definition, characterisations, two gradient error bounds and an exact algorithm. Limits: Algorithm 1 handles finite sets only, and an algorithm for infinite sets is "an obvious future research direction" (p. 17). The uses for subspace methods are suggested but untested: a stopping condition, "flags indicating when it is time switch to a different subspace" (p. 16), and Corollary 32 as a rule for what to try after a failed poll with a set that is not positive spanning (pp. 16–17).

**Method links**:
- Method 3 ✅ evidence: the characterisations come with counterexamples showing which hypothesis or which converse fails (pp. 5–11). ⚠ Variant: the ladder is over properties of *direction sets* (finite/infinite, closed, positive spanning of the span), not over smoothness of f.
- Method 1 ✅ evidence (the Poll carries the theory): the whole paper sharpens what a failed poll certifies (Thm 31, p. 15) and proposes the next poll when D is not positive spanning (Cor. 32, pp. 16–17).
- Method 3 limitation ⚠ partial counter-evidence: SKILL.md says quantitative guarantees are absent. Theorem 31 is a non-asymptotic error bound, ‖P_D∇f‖ = O(Δ_D) or O(Δ_D²) with explicit constants (p. 15). It is not a complexity rate.
- Method 6 ⚠ variant: the code is not shipped in NOMAD; the MATLAB code is "available upon request" (p. 21).
- new-pattern candidate: *Generalize-and-recover*: the new object reduces exactly to the classical one (cm_{ℝⁿ} = cm, p. 5), and the new theorem reproduces the textbook one (Thm 31(i) vs Thm 30, p. 15).

**Transferable techniques**:
- When a measure degenerates on the objects you care about (cm = 0 for any positive spanning set of a subspace), relativise it to the natural subspace, and check that the old measure is the special case L = ℝⁿ (pp. 4–5).
- Add one vector w = −Σ b_j to any set to make it positive spanning in its span, which gives the dichotomy "either the new step decreases f, or the projected gradient is small" (Prop. 16, Cor. 32, pp. 8, 16–17).
- Split an algorithm by an LP-decidable case: exact enumeration in one case, an exact convex relaxation in the other (Alg. 1, Lemma 33, pp. 17–18).
- After every "iff", give the smallest example that shows why a hypothesis cannot be dropped or why the converse fails (pp. 5–11).

**Quotes**:
- "The extended definition of the cosine measure might be useful for subspace decomposition methods." (p. 1)
- "The results herein will be of high value in understanding the subspace decomposition methods which are recently gaining traction in [2,5,9,16,18,27]." (p. 2)

### S121 · CatMADS: Mesh Adaptive Direct Search for constrained blackbox optimization with categorical variables (arXiv preprint 2025, arXiv:2506.06937)
**Role**: core · **Coauthors**: Y. Diouane, E. Hallé-Hannan (first and corresponding author, on PhD scholarships), S. Le Digabel, C. Tribes (p. 1) · **Read level**: full, pp. 1–29 (reference list skimmed). The data-profile figures survive only as axis labels, so results are taken from the prose. · **Position in research trajectory**: the successor to MV-MADS (Abramson–Audet–Chrissis–Walston 2009 [1]) and the 2001 mixed-variable GPS [5]. It follows Hallé-Hannan's framework papers on categorical and meta variables [8, 24] and ships with the Cat-Suite test collection [25] (p. 19).

- **D1 Problem & entry point**: Applications come first: hyperparameter tuning, SOLAR (turbine types), aircraft design (p. 2). The gap is that direct search is "underdeveloped" for categorical variables compared with BO and metaheuristics (p. 3). MV-MADS needs explicit user-defined neighbourhoods, which are "tricky or arbitrary to define" and "not user-friendly" (p. 3). It also treats integers as categorical and uses only the extreme barrier (p. 4). The gap is stated in one sentence: "the absence of a robust and rigorous optimization method for mixed-variables problems with constraints" (p. 3). There are five numbered objectives (p. 4). Meta variables are explicitly out of scope (p. 3).
- **D2 Core idea & algorithm design**: MADS's search/poll/mesh skeleton and the PB are kept; three things change.
  - Neighbourhoods come from a distance. The distance-induced neighbourhood N(x^cat; m) is the m+1 closest categorical components under d^cat (Def. 1, p. 7). Any user-defined neighbourhood of MV-MADS is induced by some distance, so this generalises it (p. 7). The categorical poll changes only x^cat and keeps x^qnt fixed (Def. 2, p. 7).
  - Integers move from the categorical side to the G-MADS granular mesh, with per-variable mesh and frame sizes (Defs. 3–5, pp. 7–8) and Householder directions (p. 9).
  - The PB replaces the EB, with feasible and infeasible polls and dominating/improving iterations (pp. 9–10).

  The ξ-extended poll is re-derived for the PB. Its trigger t_ξ accepts categorical neighbours whose relative deterioration is within ξ (Def. 7, eq. 9, p. 11). It then runs a finite sequence of quantitative polls around them, which need *not* use a positive spanning set (Def. 8, pp. 11–12).

  The instance (Alg. 2, p. 21):
  - d^cat is a weighted Euclidean distance on one-hot encodings. Its weights minimise the RMSE of an inverse-distance-weighting interpolation under 3-fold cross-validation on the DoE (p. 20).
  - The DoE uses 20% of the budget (p. 20).
  - The number of categorical neighbours is m = max(2, √|X^cat|); linear alternatives were not better in preliminary tests (p. 22).
  - Speculative and quadratic searches run on the quantitative variables only (p. 21).
  - In the poll, quantitative points are ordered by the quadratic models and evaluated before categorical points (p. 22).
  - Transferable: turn a hand-made discrete neighbourhood into "m nearest under a distance learned once from the DoE by cross-validated interpolation" (pp. 7, 20).
- **D3 Assumptions & model**: An initial point with finite f, and all iterates in a compact set (p. 12). The categorical set is finite (p. 3). The restriction f⋆ to the continuous variables is Lipschitz near the refined point, and refining directions are dense (Thms 2–3, p. 17). Theorem 4 needs lower semicontinuity at x⋆ and upper semicontinuity at y⋆ (p. 18). Neighbourhoods must converge, which the implementation ensures by fixing m and d^cat after some iteration (p. 15); in Alg. 2, d^cat is built once (p. 20).
- **D4 Analysis & proof techniques**:
  - Four mixed-variable local-minimum types in a hyphen/comma nomenclature: [cat−int−cont] (Def. 9, "not covered by CatMADS", p. 13), [cat, int, cont] (Def. 10, inspired by Torres et al. [46]), [cat−cont, int] (Def. 11) and [cat−cont, cat−int] (Def. 12) (pp. 12–14).
  - The refining subsequence adds the limits m(k) → m⋆ and N(x^cat_(k); m(k)) → N(x^cat_⋆; m⋆) (Def. 13, p. 15).
  - Theorem 1 extracts nested subsequences K₅ ⊆ … ⊆ K₁ using the finiteness of the categorical, integer and m sets, and inherits lim inf δ → 0 from G-MADS (p. 15).
  - Theorems 2–3 fix the categorical and integer parts along the refining subsequence and reduce to MADS/PB results on f⋆ or h⋆ (pp. 17–18).
  - Theorem 4 passes f(x_(k)) ≤ f(y_(k)) to the limit (p. 18).
  - A case split on f(x⋆) < f(y⋆) versus equality, where equality forces infinitely many extended polls, yields the stronger minima (p. 18).
  - The strength knob is explicit: ξ < 0 gives [cat, int, cont], ξ ∈ ℝ₊ gives [cat−cont, int], and ξ = ∞ gives [cat−cont, cat−int] (p. 20).
  - Proofs are short and mostly inherited ("partly given by construction", p. 15). No counterexamples to the hypotheses are given (none found).
- **D5 Numerical experiments**:
  - Test set: Cat-Suite, 32 problems, 16 unconstrained and 16 constrained (Tables 1–2, pp. 19–20). They are built from classic functions (Ackley, Rastrigin, HS…) by making continuous variables depend on categories or by defining the function case by case per category (p. 19).
  - Budget 250n (p. 22).
  - Competitors from other families: Pymoo (GA), Optuna (CMA-ES) and BoTorch (BO with a Gower kernel and Log constrained EI), all started from the same DoE (p. 22).
  - Fairness caveat stated: BoTorch is stopped by its own criterion because a full budget is too slow (over 24 h for Rastrigin with 1,000 evaluations) (p. 22).
  - 5 seeds give 160 instances. Data profiles at τ = 10⁻¹, 10⁻³ and 10⁻⁵, with f₀ defined separately for constrained problems (pp. 22–23).
  - ξ is tuned first by data profiles: ξ = 0.05 is best at small tolerances, and ξ = ∞ costs too much at 10⁻⁵ (p. 23; Fig. 6, p. 24).
  - Result: at τ = 10⁻⁵ on unconstrained problems, CatMADS solves about 70% vs 30% for Pymoo (p. 23; Fig. 7, p. 25).
  - All problems are analytical; no real blackbox is used despite the SOLAR and aircraft motivation (p. 2). No failure cases are reported (none found). Code and data are public (pp. 19, 24).
  - Transferable: tune your own knob by data profiles *before* the head-to-head, and publish that tuning figure (Fig. 6, pp. 23–24).
- **D6 Literature positioning**: Positioned against the BO kernel literature (one-hot relaxation, latent variables, matrix/hypersphere parametrisations) and metaheuristics (NSGA-II, PSO) (p. 5). The critique is direct: CatMADS's advantage is guarantees "which BO or metaheuristic methods lack" (p. 3). It improves on the group's own MV-MADS [1] and reuses G-MADS [13], the PB [7] and the 2001 extended poll [5] (p. 4).
- **D7 Writing & rhetoric**: Contributions are framed as five objectives (p. 4). Small visual toy examples (RG, RBG; Figs. 2–5, pp. 10–16) carry the definitions. A new nomenclature is coined (p. 13). The implementation is pre-emptively called "intentionally simple" (pp. 19, 24). Claims are strong: CatMADS "strongly dominates" (p. 23), and the abstract ends on "empirically efficient in addition to having strong theoretical foundations" (p. 1).
- **D8 Contribution & limitations**: The contribution has four layers: framework plus theory, an instance, a test suite (Cat-Suite), and a public prototype (p. 19). Limits: the method is local (p. 24). The DoE is "a considerable number of evaluations" (p. 20). The strongest minimum type is not covered (p. 13), and meta variables are excluded (p. 3). Future work: hybridisation with BO, exploration search steps, and application to hyperparameter optimisation of deep models (p. 24).

**Method links**:
- Method 1 ✅ strong evidence: searches (speculative, quadratic) bring the performance, polls carry the guarantees, and the optional extended poll is still "labeled as a poll, since it affects the convergence guarantees" (p. 6). Theorem 1 survives because each extended poll is a finite sequence (p. 15).
- Method 2 ✅ evidence: hidden constraints and unrelaxable violations are set to f = +∞, while relaxable/quantifiable constraints go to the PB (p. 2). Replacing MV-MADS's EB with the PB is objective 3 (p. 4).
- Method 3 ⚠ variant: the guarantee ladder runs along a *cost vs strength* axis (four minimum types selected by ξ; pp. 12–14, 20), not along smoothness. No counterexamples to the hypotheses are given.
- Method 4 ✅ evidence: a new public suite (Cat-Suite, 32 problems) and code release (pp. 19, 24). ⚠ Partial: the problems are analytical, not a real blackbox.
- Method 5 ✅ evidence: a per-evaluation budget, data profiles, 5 seeds, competitors from three other families, the same DoE for all, and the BoTorch fairness caveat stated (pp. 22–23).
- Method 6 ✅ evidence: the prototype is built on NOMAD's G-MADS, PB and searches (p. 19).
- Heuristic 10 ✅ evidence: a thesis-to-paper pipeline (Hallé-Hannan first and corresponding author, on PhD scholarships, p. 1).
- new-pattern candidate: *Generalize-and-recover*: distance-induced neighbourhoods contain every user-defined one (p. 7).
- new-pattern candidate: *Optimality strength as a user knob*: parameter ξ ↔ a named local-minimum type, then ξ is chosen empirically (pp. 20, 23).

**Transferable techniques**:
- A hyphen/comma naming scheme for partially-joint optimality notions ([cat−cont, int] etc.), with one picture per type (p. 13; Fig. 4, p. 14).
- Build mixed-variable test problems from classic continuous ones by making continuous variables depend on categories or by defining the function case by case per category (p. 19).
- Keep the categorical distance fixed after some iteration so that the neighbourhoods converge; that one line makes the refining-subsequence theory hold (p. 15).
- Fix the neighbour count at m ≥ 2 to avoid cycling between two categories (p. 22).

### S129 · Adaptive direct search algorithms for constrained optimization (arXiv preprint 2025, arXiv:2507.23054)
**Role**: core · **Coauthors**: T. Denorme, Y. Diouane, S. Le Digabel, C. Tribes · **Read level**: full, pp. 1–30. About 600 lines of the text file (p. 7, Fig. 3) are extracted mesh glyphs "⊕", skipped as figure content. · **Position in research trajectory**: the group's first mesh-free directional direct search. It reuses the "revealing poll" idea of the 2024 counterexample paper [15] (p. 7). It cites the 2004 tightness paper [5] for why MADS needs a rational τ (p. 11), and announces PB integration and NOMAD integration as next steps (p. 25).

- **D1 Problem & entry point**: The entry is two rival families, each with a weakness. MADS restricts trial points to a mesh but accepts any decrease. SDDS (sufficient-decrease direct search) places points anywhere but may reject genuine improvements (pp. 1–2). Two one-dimensional examples show one failure each (§2.3):
  - f₁ = γ(x+2)x⁵: SDDS with ρ(t) = γt² stalls at the saddle x = 0 because the decreases are too small, while MADS escapes (p. 5, Fig. 1).
  - f₂ = (x − 1/3)² with a quadratic-model search: SDDS reaches the minimiser at the fourth evaluation, while MADS wastes evaluations because the model's point is projected onto the mesh (pp. 5–6, Fig. 2).
  - Transferable: open with one minimal example per rival that isolates its specific failure, and later show that your method matches the winner on each (pp. 5–6, 20).
- **D2 Core idea & algorithm design**:
  - The punctured space R°ⁿ_k = {x : ‖x − y‖ ≥ δᵏ for all visited y ∈ Vᵏ} replaces the mesh (Def. 3.1, p. 7), and the acceptance rule stays a simple decrease.
  - The search has three outcomes (p. 9): *successful* (improving and inside the punctured space), *improving* (improving but too close to a visited point, so the poll is centred there and the point becomes the incumbent if the poll fails), and *unsuccessful*.
  - The poll set is P^k = {p^k + Δ^k v : v ∈ D^k} with normalised directions (p. 10). Poll points outside the punctured space are skipped without being evaluated (p. 10). The poll centre may differ from the incumbent (p. 9).
  - Updates: Δ changes by τ^{±1}, and δᵏ = min{Δᵏ, (Δᵏ)²/Δ⁰} (eqs. 2–3, p. 11). τ need not be rational, unlike MADS (p. 11).
  - Constraints are handled by the EB only (p. 3). ADS is presented as a family with "numerous distinct instances" (p. 6), with a non-opportunistic variant (p. 10).
  - Kept from MADS: the search/poll split, the frame parameter Δ and simple decrease. Changed: the mesh becomes an exclusion radius, and the search is no longer projected.
- **D3 Assumptions & model**: Assumption 1: the closure of the level set {x ∈ Ω : f(x) ≤ f(x⁰)} is compact (p. 12). f is Lipschitz near the refined point, refining directions lie in the hypertangent cone (Thm 4.4, p. 13), and a refining direction need only be *generated*, not evaluated (Def. 4.3, p. 13). The proof needs δᵏ/Δᵏ → 0 along the subsequence (p. 14).
- **D4 Analysis & proof techniques**:
  - Theorem 4.1 gives lim δᵏ = 0, a full limit rather than the lim inf of mesh methods. Infinitely many successes with δᵏ ≥ ε would give ε-separated points in a compact set, contradicting Bolzano–Weierstrass (p. 12). The authors place this "closer to that of sufficient decrease based methods" (p. 12).
  - Theorem 4.4 (Clarke–Jahn stationarity) introduces a *corrected poll point*. If the tentative point tᵏ was skipped, a visited yᵏ within δᵏ stands in for it, written yᵏ = pᵏ + Δᵏ(vᵏ + (δᵏ/Δᵏ)wᵏ) with ‖wᵏ‖ ≤ 1. The direction error therefore vanishes as δ/Δ → 0 (eq. 5, Fig. 6, p. 14), and MADS's Prop. 3.9 finishes the proof (p. 14).
  - Section 4.2 shows that OrthoMADS is an instance of ADS. With rational τ, the union of past meshes embeds in one finer mesh (Lemma 4.3, p. 15), so mesh points are at least µq^{−ẑ_k}δ̄ᵏ apart (Prop. 4.4, p. 16). The ADS instance shrinks δ by τ/q at failures and counts failures n_U^k (Table 1, p. 17). An induction then gives identical visited sets (Lemma 4.5, Thm 4.6, pp. 16–18). QRMADS follows the same way (p. 19).
  - Transferable: prove the flagship old algorithm is an instance of the new framework, via a parameter map and an induction on iterations (Table 1, Thm 4.6, pp. 17–18).
- **D5 Numerical experiments**:
  - Setup: ADS, MADS (OrthoMADS 2n) and SDDS are coded in one Python framework "to ensure fair and consistent comparison", with identical parameters except the acceptance mechanism. SDDS uses ρ(δ) = 10⁻²δ² (pp. 19–20).
  - Each method runs with and without a rudimentary quadratic search (COBYLA on the model when unconstrained, SciPy trust-constr when constrained, 5,000 inner evaluations, at most one search point). Performance was insensitive to the norm, so ℓ₂ is used (pp. 19–20).
  - Moré–Wild, 53 problems, smooth (ℓ₂) and nonsmooth (ℓ₁), 20 seeds, instance-based f*: comparable overall. ADS is faster in the smooth case and at τ = 10⁻² because skipped poll points save evaluations, while MADS's poll does better on nonsmooth problems at tight tolerances (p. 20; Fig. 7, p. 21).
  - CUTEst, 16 constrained problems with a feasible start, 20 seeds (Table 2, p. 22). The authors admit a bias toward MADS: three problems have trivial symmetric solutions aligned with the mesh (pp. 21–22). The quadratic search helps ADS and SDDS more than MADS, explained by projection near the feasible boundary (p. 22).
  - Mechanism statistics (Table 3, p. 23): total evaluations 532/625/560 for ADS/MADS/SDDS; 1 search point rejected by the punctured space; search efficiency 38%/23%/43% (SDDS highest); poll evaluations saved 27/0/0; infeasible evaluations 49%/53%/51%.
  - SOLAR10 (n = 5): 30 Latin-hypercube starting points, *one* seed, budget 40(n+1) = 240, 23 hours of computation. ADS is strong from the start (pp. 23–24; Fig. 9).
  - Simplified-Wing (n = 7, 3 constraints): 30 feasible Latin-hypercube starts published on GitHub, budget 150(n+1) = 1200. ADS "clearly outperforms" (p. 24; Fig. 10, p. 25).
  - The one-dimensional examples: ADS matches MADS on f₁ and SDDS on f₂ (p. 20).
  - Transferable: report mechanism counters (points rejected by the new rule, evaluations saved, infeasible evaluations) next to the profiles (Table 3, p. 23).
- **D6 Literature positioning**: The paper bridges the mesh family (GPS, MADS, OrthoMADS, QRMADS) and the sufficient-decrease family (Lucidi–Sciandrone forcing functions [40], GSS [36], CS-DFN [28], TREGO [26]) (pp. 2–4). It notes that sufficient decrease "allows worst-case complexity analyses" [18, 31, 39, 49] (p. 4). A similar set in GLODS [22] is credited, "used for another purpose" (p. 7). Neither rival is dismissed: each gets an example where it wins (pp. 5–6).
- **D7 Writing & rhetoric**: Examples come before the method (§2.3), followed by a flowchart (Fig. 4, p. 9) and pictures of the punctured space and the corrected poll point (Figs. 3, 5, 6). The framing is "a family" and "a new class". The abstract claims performance "compared to both"; the discussion scopes it to "the two real engineering blackbox problems" (p. 25). There are typos typical of a v1 ("Clark-Jahn" p. 13, "non-opportuinistic" p. 10).
- **D8 Contribution & limitations**: The contribution is algorithmic (a new DDS class), theoretical (lim δ = 0, Clarke–Jahn stationarity, OrthoMADS/QRMADS as instances) and empirical. Limits visible in the text: EB only, with PB left to future work (p. 25). SOLAR10 has one seed (p. 24). No complexity result is given for ADS, although p. 4 notes that sufficient decrease is what enables such analyses. Future work: the PB, adapting existing search strategies [11, 12], and integration into NOMAD (p. 25).

**Method links**:
- Method 1 ⚠ variant (important): the search is freed further. Its points are no longer projected onto a mesh, and an *improving* search point may re-centre the poll (p. 9). The poll still "ensures the theoretical convergence" (p. 4), and the proof is unchanged in spirit (p. 14). This is the group's answer to the Method 1 limitation "the mesh itself restricts trial points".
- Method 3 ✅ evidence: guarantees in the MADS format (refining subsequences, Clarke–Jahn, hypertangent cone; pp. 12–14). A hypothesis from the 2004 tightness paper (rational τ) is shown to be unnecessary for ADS (p. 11), and the design reuses the counterexample-driven "revealing poll" idea (p. 7).
- Method 5 ✅ evidence: one code base, seeds, data profiles in groups of n+1 evaluations, analytical plus two realistic blackboxes, an admitted test-set bias, mechanism statistics (pp. 19–24). ⚠ The competitors are all directional direct search; no solver from another family.
- Method 4 ✅ evidence: SOLAR10 and Simplified-Wing, with published starting points (pp. 23–24).
- Method 6 ✅ evidence: "ADS will be integrated into the NOMAD solver" (p. 25).
- new-pattern candidate: *Generalize-and-recover*: OrthoMADS and QRMADS are proved to be ADS instances (Thm 4.6, pp. 17–19).
- new-pattern candidate: *Rival-failure toy examples*: one tiny example per competing family (pp. 5–6).
- new-pattern candidate: *Reconcile two rival families with a third acceptance rule* (p. 2).

**Transferable techniques**:
- Replace a lattice with an exclusion-ball condition around visited points. It preserves the "iterates cannot accumulate while δ stays large" property that compactness proofs need, and it even strengthens lim inf to lim (Def. 3.1, Thm 4.1, pp. 7, 12).
- Corrected poll point: when a trial point is skipped because it is near a visited one, reuse the visited point as the poll evidence and bound the direction error by δ/Δ (eq. 5, p. 14).
- Coupled parameter rule δ = min{Δ, Δ²/Δ⁰}, which gives δ/Δ → 0 whenever δ → 0 (p. 11).
- Implement every compared method in one code base with identical parameters except the mechanism under study (p. 19).
- Admit test-set bias that favours a competitor (pp. 21–22).

**Quotes**:
- "This work introduces a new class of methods, Adaptive Direct Search (ADS), which uses a novel acceptance rule based on the so-called punctured space, avoiding both meshes and sufficient decrease conditions." (p. 1)
- "This projection introduces a displacement that may not decrease as fast as the accuracy of the model." (p. 22)
