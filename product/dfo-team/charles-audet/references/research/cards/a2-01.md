### S127 · Concavity cuts for the linear maxmin problem (Les Cahiers du GERAD G-94-52 1994, no DOI/arXiv)
**Read**: abstract. Source: GERAD page G-94-52 (October 1994, 20 pages; no open PDF). Full text not read. · **Coauthors**: B. Jaumard, G. Savard

- **D1 Problem & entry**: Cutting planes for the linear maxmin problem. The entry is to obtain valid concavity cuts for maxmin, which needs the problem in concave-programming form (abstract).
- **D2 Key idea**: The problem is reformulated as a concave program with linear constraints in two ways, a primal and a dual approach, and valid concavity cuts are computed from each (abstract).
- **D8 Contribution**: Algorithmic component: a method to compute valid cuts. The abstract names no experiments, and the rest was not read (abstract). The thesis version of the cuts for both symmetric reformulations is §2.4 of S070 (card b2-01, pp. 65–70).
- **Method links**: new-pattern candidate: reformulation as a lens (transfer tests, not just solutions) ✅ evidence: A concave-programming tool (concavity cuts) is carried over to maxmin through two reformulations, primal and dual. (abstract)
- **Quotes**: "We consider a primal and a dual approach." (abstract)

### S014 · Links between linear bilevel and mixed 0–1 programming problems (Journal of Optimization Theory and Applications 1997, 10.1023/a:1022645805569)
**Read**: abstract. Source: GERAD page G-95-20 (April 1995, revised December 1995, 36 pages). Full text not read. · **Coauthors**: P. Hansen, B. Jaumard, G. Savard

- **D1 Problem & entry**: Links between linear bilevel programming (BLP) and linear mixed 0-1 programming. The new reformulation is presented as one that needs no large finite constant, which implies that earlier ones did (abstract).
- **D2 Key idea**: A new MIP0-1 → BLP reformulation with no large constant. Proof that classical MIP branch-and-bound is embedded in a bilevel branch-and-bound: on any instance and its reformulation both produce the same sequence of subproblems (abstract).
- **D8 Contribution**: Theory: a reformulation plus an equivalence theorem between algorithms, with a by-product, a new BLP optimality test that uses nonstandard penalties (abstract). The thesis version is ch. 3 of S070 (card b2-01, Thm 3.6, pp. 96–97).
  - Transferable technique: To compare two algorithms that live in different problem classes, prove that through the reformulation they generate identical subproblem sequences. Then port the stronger class's tests (here, penalties) to the other class (abstract).
- **Method links**: new-pattern candidate: reformulation as a lens (transfer tests, not just solutions) ✅ evidence: The reformulation is used to compare algorithms and to port a test (penalties) from MIP to BLP, not only to move solutions. (abstract)
- **Quotes**: "A new reformulation of the linear mixed 0-1 programming problem into a bilevel linear programming one, which does not require the introduction of a large finite constant, is presented." (abstract) · "Moreover, we deduce a new optimality test for bilevel linear programming which makes use of nonstandard penalties." (abstract)

### S041 · A symmetrical linear maxmin approach to disjoint bilinear programming (Mathematical Programming 1999, 10.1007/s101070050072)
**Read**: abstract. Source: GERAD page G-96-06 (February 1996, revised May 1998, 23 pages), report version. Full text not read. · **Coauthors**: P. Hansen, B. Jaumard, G. Savard

- **D1 Problem & entry**: Disjoint bilinear programming (BILD), and the question of whether its optimal value is bounded (abstract).
- **D2 Key idea**: BILD is reformulated as two distinct linear maxmin problems. There is a simple bijection between their optimal solutions and those of BILD, and they have no more local optima than BILD. The necessary optimality conditions of both reformulations (complementarity and monotonicity) drive a finite, exact branch and bound (abstract).
- **D8 Contribution**: An algorithm and a complexity result: deciding boundedness is strongly NP-complete, and the algorithm can answer that question (abstract). The thesis version is ch. 2 of S070 (card b2-01).
  - Transferable technique: When you propose a reformulation, prove (i) a bijection between its optimal solutions and the original's and (ii) that it has no more local optima. "The reformulation adds no complexity" then becomes a theorem instead of a claim (abstract).
- **Method links**: new-pattern candidate: reformulation as a lens (transfer tests, not just solutions) ✅ evidence: Both symmetric reformulations are used at once, and their optimality conditions become the branching rules. (abstract)
- **Quotes**: "In that sense, the reformulations do not introduce additional complexity." (abstract) · "Determining if the optimal value of a bilinear programming problem is bounded is shown to be strongly NP-complete." (abstract)
