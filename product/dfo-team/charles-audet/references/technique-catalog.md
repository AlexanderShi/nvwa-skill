Each entry gives a one-line "how to use it" and the paper cards it rests on, as [card id pages]. Cards are in `research/cards/` and indexed in `research/07-paper-cards.md`; the aggregation is `research/08-deep-reading-synthesis.md` §7.

## 1. Proof devices

| # | Technique | How to use it | Cards |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | **Failed poll = one term of the generalized derivative** | Design the acceptance test so that a rejected trial point's inequality f(x_k + Δd) ≥ f(x_k) is one difference quotient in the limsup that defines the Clarke derivative; take the limsup along refining subsequences, and the main theorem reduces to density of the directions. | [S002 pp. 9–10; S001 p. 12; S007 pp. 16–17; S031 pp. 16–19; S061 p. 20; S129 pp. 13–14; S172 pp. 14–15; S158 pp. 16–18; S213 p. 89 *(programme)*] |
| P2 | **Integer lattice → liminf Δ = 0** | Write every iterate as x0 plus integer combinations of the directions scaled by the mesh size, with a rational τ, so bounded mesh indices force a finite lattice and a contradiction; for per-variable steps, write each as Δ0_j β_j^{r_j} with integer r_j. | [S002 p. 8; S011 p. 12; S031 p. 13; S001 p. 7; S061 pp. 9, 19] |
| P3 | **Exclusion balls + packing → lim δ = 0 without a mesh** | Require accepted points to stay δ-separated; compactness then bounds the number of successes at any fixed δ, so δ goes to zero as a full limit, not only a liminf. | [S129 pp. 7, 12; S172 pp. 12–13] |
| P4 | **Density of directions** | Derandomize with a low-discrepancy sequence and prove that each transformation (scale and round, Householder reflection) preserves density; tie the sequence index to the finest poll size; for random probes, count integers in intervals or bound volume ratios. | [S006 pp. 5–14; S001 pp. 14, 18; S090 pp. 14–15; S138 p. 8; S137 pp. 7–8, 16] |
| P5 | **Adversarial-but-legal counterexample by induction** | Choose the algorithm's free parameters (pattern, Search order, step parameters, tie handling) against it but within the rules; define f only where the iterates go, extended smoothly to keep level sets compact; prove closed-form iterates per periodic block by induction, with a table of all trial values. | [S025 TR pp. 4–12; S020 pp. 13–15; S007 pp. 19–21; S005 pp. 24–25] |
| P6 | **Scaled copies of one polynomial** | Build 1-D counterexamples from 2^ℓ p(x/2^ℓ) so that local minima accumulate geometrically at the point of interest; pair them with an algorithm instance whose Search jumps to the next minimizer and whose Poll always fails. | [S138 p. 4; S007 p. 19] |
| P7 | **One counterexample per hypothesis or converse** | After each theorem or equivalence, give the smallest example showing that a hypothesis is needed or that the converse fails; place it right before the assumption it motivates. | [S120 pp. 5–11; S151 pp. 6–7; S137 pp. 11–12, 17; S092 p. 11; S144 pp. 10–12; S070 pp. 43, 60, 84; B001 p. 19 *(textbook: the McKinnon counterexample gets its own section next to the Nelder–Mead convergence study; titles only; see T4)*] |
| P8 | **Eventually-static adaptation** | Let every adaptive rule (regrouping, constraint weights, barrier migrations, learned distances) change only finitely often, then apply the existing analysis after the last change; if it can adapt infinitely often, exhibit the 1-D example and prove the weaker property that remains. | [S074 pp. 12, 14; S144 pp. 10–12; S158 pp. 18–19; S121 p. 15] |
| P9 | **Instance membership by parameter map and induction** | To inherit a theorem, map the new algorithm's parameters onto the framework's and prove by induction on iterations that both take the same decisions; list the features that void the equivalence. | [S129 pp. 16–18; S006 p. 14; D001 p. 2; S123 p. 4; S027 pp. 14–16; S070 pp. 96–97] |
| P10 | **Mirror proof; scale-then-limit** | Re-run the objective's proof with (h, X) in place of (f, Ω) for the constraint-violation function; for penalty merits, multiply the failed-poll inequality by the penalty parameter so that only the violation's Clarke derivative survives in the limit. | [S007 p. 18; S172 p. 15; S158 pp. 12, 15] |
| P11 | **Symmetric-direction cancellation** | Combine Taylor expansions along d and −d (or −α d) to cancel the Hessian term, bound the evaluated side by the poll failure, and derive the contradiction or an O(Δ²) bound from the other side. | [S020 pp. 12–13; S120 pp. 15–16] |
| P12 | **Stochastic analysis toolkit** | For noisy variants: a potential ν(f − f_min) + (1 − ν)Δ² with a case split and a log-step submartingale dominated by a random walk; the strong law plus Borel–Cantelli for adaptive precision; a multi-timescale ODE argument for stochastic approximation. | [S053 pp. 12–19; S084 pp. 12–18; S150 pp. 14–17] |
| P13 | **Reformulation equivalence** | Prove that optimal (and local) solutions transfer between the original and the reformulated problem, e.g. hypertangent cones preserved by a linear surjection, or φ(y) ≥ Φ(χ(y)) with a continuity pull-back; then transport the existing theorem. | [S070 pp. 33–34; S064 pp. 8–10; S151 p. 6; S155 pp. 9–11; S062 pp. 5–6] |
| P14 | **Absorb skipped trial points** | When a poll point is skipped because it lies near a visited point, use the cached point instead and bound the direction error by δ/Δ, which goes to zero. | [S129 p. 14; S172 pp. 14–15] |
| P15 | **Route the proof through a named history property** | Prove "property ⇒ theorem" and "oracle ⇒ property" separately, so that different oracles (random, farthest-point, grid) plug into the same theorem. | [S137 pp. 3–8; S155 pp. 7–8] |
| P16 | **Exchange lemma plus enumeration** | For a small combinatorial design subproblem inside an algorithm, prove cuts by an exchange argument and then enumerate, instead of calling a MINLP solver. | [S112 pp. 12–13; S173 pp. 12–13] |
| P17 | **Relativize a degenerate measure** | When a classical measure degenerates on your objects (the cosine measure of a non-spanning set, profile units for bilevel methods), define it relative to the natural subspace or structure and check that the classical measure is the special case. | [S120 pp. 4–5; S156 p. 14] |

## 2. Algorithm-design moves

| # | Technique | How to use it | Cards |
|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | **Split an overloaded parameter** | When one parameter does two jobs (GPS's step size fixes both the lattice and the reach), split it into two parameters coupled only asymptotically; the old method is the case where they are equal. | [S001 p. 4; S061 pp. 11–12] |
| A2 | **Put the idea where the proof cannot see it** | Use the Search step, the evaluation order under opportunism, or a wrapper around the blackbox; recast a trick that would leave the mesh as a one-point Search. | [S001 p. 17; S016 pp. 6, 10; S072 p. 6; S114 p. 10; S143 pp. 7–10; S112 p. 14; S173 pp. 6–7; S039 pp. 6–7; S077 p. 7 *(2005: poll ordering by last success, surrogate value or simplex-gradient angle, none guaranteed to save time)*] |
| A3 | **Surrogate as ranker** | Order Poll and Search candidates by the surrogate and never prune the Poll; select and tune the model by (leave-one-out) order error or feasibility-flip counts, not by fit error. | [S016 pp. 5–6, 20; S046 pp. 12–13; S068 pp. 12–13; S167 p. 8; S023 pp. 13–14; S077 pp. 5, 7 *("surrogate" chosen so no approximation quality is implied)*] |
| A4 | **Two incumbents and a monotone barrier** | Keep a best feasible and a best undominated infeasible point, poll around both, and lower the violation threshold by dominance; a zero initial threshold with a feasible start recovers the extreme barrier. | [S007 pp. 6, 9–12; S005 pp. 6, 8–9; S063 pp. 11–12] |
| A5 | **Couple auxiliary parameters to the step size** | Tie any quantity that must vanish (estimate accuracy, penalty, smoothing, exclusion radius) to the step size, e.g. δ = min{Δ, Δ²/Δ0}, so the theory gets it for free. | [S053 p. 9; S158 pp. 6–7, 9–11; S129 p. 11; S140 pp. 6–7] |
| A6 | **Cheap-first gating, interruption and count flags** | Evaluate a priori and cheap constraints first; pass the current violation threshold into the simulator so a hopeless evaluation can abort; return a sentinel value and a flag saying whether the evaluation counts. | [S001 p. 4; S005 p. 9; S019 pp. 51, 64; S123 p. 3; S069 p. 10; S071 pp. 18–23; D006 p. 30; S064 p. 6; S077 p. 6 *(a cheaper turbulence model checks membership in X before the accurate simulation)*] |
| A7 | **Incumbent guard for cheap verdicts** | Trust low-fidelity or partial outputs to reject a point, but pay one full evaluation before a point becomes the incumbent; add a fallback that makes the method identical to the baseline when it has no information. | [S112 pp. 7–8; S173 pp. 7–8; S163 pp. 6–8] |
| A8 | **The cache as a data source** | Mine cached points for initial mesh sizes, variable sensitivities (bin the ranges), zero-cost constraint sensitivity, re-scoring after a merit change, and reuse as poll points. | [S019 pp. 98–99; S073 pp. 22–23; S125 p. 10; S158 p. 20; S172 pp. 14–15; S090 p. 12] |
| A9 | **Convergent runs as subroutines** | Build drivers (biobjective series, VNS, extended polls, decomposition) that call convergent MADS runs, so each call keeps its guarantee. | [S019 pp. 88, 90, 96–99; S027 pp. 11, 18–19; S013 p. 8; S021 p. 8] |
| A10 | **Parallelize without a new proof** | Let one worker run the certified poll (a single pollster point can be enough); force all other workers' trial points onto its mesh so they count as Search. | [S027 pp. 9, 14–17; S021 p. 11] |
| A11 | **Nested split** | Give DFO only the few variables that carry the pathology and solve the rest with a smooth or certified inner solver; when the inner problem is infeasible, return (minimal infeasibility, objective) to the progressive barrier rather than +∞. | [S151 pp. 3–8; S155 pp. 5–6; S186 pp. 4–5; S095 pp. 6–7] |
| A12 | **Cross-family transplant with retuning** | Re-prove an imported mechanism in your framework, retune every parameter that encoded the donor's evaluations per iteration, and publish the original-versus-retuned ablation. | [S063 pp. 13, 20–22; S053 pp. 2, 12–15; S158 pp. 1, 3; S116 p. 7] |
| A13 | **Optimality strength as a priced knob** | For categorical variables, let a user neighbour set define "local", trigger an extended poll only near nearly-as-good neighbours, and report the evaluation price of the stronger guarantee. | [S011 pp. 3, 8, 22; S028 pp. 13, 18; S031 pp. 4–6; S121 pp. 20, 23] |
| A14 | **Non-shrinking probes** | Add a revealing poll or covering step (random, or farthest from the history) to reach stronger guarantees (discontinuities, local minima), accepting almost-sure results. | [S090 p. 10; S138 pp. 6–9; S137 pp. 7–10] |
| A15 | **Hypotheses the user can enforce** | Replace a hypothesis that depends on how the run goes by an algorithmic choice that can be checked or enforced a priori, and say why. | [S031 p. 20; S137 pp. 8, 12] |
| A16 | **Constraint scaling** | Rescale constraints to a common range before aggregating them into h (e.g. by the largest violation seen so far) and re-anchor thresholds on the same stored point after rescaling. | [S144 pp. 9–10; D006 p. 15; S072 p. 8] |
| A17 | **Handover in the local method's own unit** | When chaining a global heuristic with MADS, share the cache, switch after as many non-improving evaluations as one failed poll, and initialize mesh sizes from the spread of the good points. | [S073 pp. 22–24; S166 pp. 7–8] |
| A18 | **Typed input for practitioner intuition** | When engineers beat the solver by hand, give their qualitative knowledge (monotonic trends) a typed input with an explicit "unknown" value, and switch to data-driven models once enough points exist. | [S143 pp. 7–10, 14] |
| A19 | **Free-step menu with verdicts** | Keep an explicit menu of candidate Search strategies and record for each when it pays and when it stops paying: empty when a local minimizer is enough, repeated last direction of limited value, random search useful only at the first iteration, surrogate-based search the best in the authors' experience. | [S077 pp. 5–6] |
| A20 | **Derivatives prune the Poll** | If some derivatives are available, skip the poll directions they show to be ascent directions; with a full gradient MADS becomes an approximate steepest descent with one evaluation per iteration, and partial derivatives cut poll evaluations without losing the convergence properties. | [S077 pp. 6–7] |
| A21 | **Bound or objective?** | When a client gives a bound, ask for its value; if the answer is "as small as possible", treat it as another objective and offer a trade-off (Pareto) curve instead of a weighted sum. One source; a pool candidate (`research/08-deep-reading-synthesis.md` §12.5). | [S077 pp. 11–12] |

## 3. Experiment protocols and benchmark construction

| # | Technique | How to use it | Cards |
|---|---|---|---|
| E1 | **Purpose-labelled problems** | Before the results, state the single point each test problem is meant to make; include the competitor's home ground and one hypothesis-violating stress run. | [S001 pp. 3, 19–25; S063 pp. 17–23; S034 p. 1] |
| E2 | **Same-solver controlled comparison** | Implement compared mechanisms in one code base that differs only in the studied mechanism, include the solver's current default, empty the Search to isolate the Poll, and keep a switch that disables all models. | [S001 pp. 4, 19; S029 pp. 14–15; S061 p. 14; S129 p. 19; S172 pp. 16–17, 20; S019 p. 83] |
| E3 | **Effort currency with explicit weights** | Charge every kind of effort in one currency (N = N_t + w·N_s; 1+τ per reformulated call; λ·N_UL + N_LL), charge preprocessing and phase-1 work on the same axis, and show the verdict at two or more weights. | [S128 p. 15; S155 pp. 19, 25, 27; S156 pp. 13–16; S112 pp. 18–19; S027 p. 22; S007 p. 25; D006 p. 22] |
| E4 | **Fair-baseline rules for profiles** | Use the same f0 and f* for all solvers, flag and drop instances no solver improves, plot h until the first feasible point and f afterwards, and check the f0 choice [S128 pp. 5–6, 10–11]. Performance profiles switch when the solver set changes; S128 shows this by removing the fastest solver [S128 pp. 9, 13–14], so re-plot without it to check a ranking. | [S128 pp. 5–6, 9–11, 13–14; S175 p. 11; S157 pp. 22–23] |
| E5 | **Concede the competitor's home ground** | Include the class where the rival is strong, adjust cross-machine times by CPU speed, and say where you lose. | [S015 pp. 9–15; S063 pp. 6, 17–23; S001 pp. 21–23; S070 pp. 81–82] |
| E6 | **Noise census, seeds and replications** | Before comparing on a stochastic blackbox, replicate one point many times (100 replications [S060 p. 8]) and judge differences against a ±2σ band [S060 pp. 11–12] (Micro-PRIAD uses ±3σ [D006 pp. 13–14]); run several seeds and report best, worst and spread. | [S060 pp. 8, 11–12; S016 p. 21; D006 pp. 13–14; S071 p. 25; S021 pp. 14–16; S027 p. 23; S006 p. 15; S150 pp. 18–19] |
| E7 | **Held-out tuning** | Tune parameters on one subset of problems (every k-th problem, one catchment) and report on the rest; fix first the parameters the objective could game. | [S057 pp. 17–18; S095 p. 9; S073 p. 6; S150 pp. 19–22] |
| E8 | **Price the proof** | When a heuristic seeds an exact or certified method, run the method cold and warm (the optimum supplied) and report evaluations to find versus evaluations to certify. | [S015 p. 9; S070 p. 168; S098 p. 4; S050 pp. 19–25; S011 p. 22] |
| E9 | **Audit inherited instances** | Re-derive or re-evaluate published instances and solutions with your own evaluator, and publish which ones collapse to easy cases or do not reproduce. | [S015 pp. 7–9; S070 pp. 125, 160; S010 p. 27; S028 pp. 14–15; S057 p. 14; S048 p. 20] |
| E10 | **Mechanism diagnostics beside profiles** | Add divergence-from-baseline tables, mechanism counters, cumulative outcome ratios, internal-quantity plots or direction-coverage plots next to the profiles. | [S173 pp. 19–23; S129 p. 23; S137 p. 15; S061 pp. 16–18; S006 p. 21] |
| E11 | **Report losses and reversals** | Report surrogate-to-truth reversals, the cases where the method loses (with the mechanism), and any test-set bias toward a competitor; give baselines the budget your method used. | [S074 pp. 20, 22; S107 pp. 17–19; S032 pp. 27–28; S092 pp. 22–25; S114 p. 11; S129 pp. 21–22; S073 p. 37] |
| E12 | **Audit the measuring instrument** | Re-solve inner problems with an independent referee before profiling; audit each indicator with a counterexample; test invariance claims with rescaled twin problems. | [S156 pp. 11–12, 17; S004 pp. 7–10, 19–21; S144 pp. 13–14; S099 p. 8] |
| E13 | **Time-based profiles when overhead matters** | Add wall-clock or blackbox-time profiles when a method's per-iteration cost or evaluation times differ, and say which time is on the axis. | [S068 pp. 14–15; S099 p. 23; S114 p. 12; D006 pp. 21–22; S173 pp. 18, 23] |
| E14 | **Parity check after a rewrite** | Restrict old and new versions to their common features, run several seeds on a smooth and a constrained set, and keep changed defaults reproducible behind a named switch. | [S021 p. 14; S202 p. 110] |
| E15 | **Benchmarking methodology early, one benchmarking section per problem class** | Put the comparison methodology (test sets, data collection, data analysis) before the main algorithm families, right after the naive ones, and add a benchmarking section wherever a new problem class is introduced (constrained, surrogate-based, biobjective). Textbook structure, co-authored with Hare; titles only. | [B001 pp. 9, 11, 15–21] |
| B1 | **Pathology list first** | Open a benchmark with the pathologies a real problem carries, and show each one present in at least one instance. | [S071 p. 2; S023 p. 7] |
| B2 | **Feasibility census and non-triviality certificate** | Publish Latin-hypercube feasibility rates against a solver's, output variability, and active constraints at the best-known point; add constraint models whose only job is to keep the optimum non-trivial. | [S175 p. 9; S071 pp. 11, 24–32; S157 pp. 7–8; S123 p. 8] |
| B3 | **Frozen, versioned, self-checking release** | Freeze the release, put the version in the instance name, and ship an in-house random generator plus a self-check so stochastic outputs match across platforms. | [S071 pp. 13–14, 20–21] |
| B4 | **Documented transformations and provenance** | Build a test set for a new variable class by named operators applied to known functions, record a provenance row per problem, and make added constraints feasible by construction. | [S130 pp. 5–7; S121 p. 19] |
| B5 | **Convert an existing realistic benchmark** | To test a new constraint class, convert the constraints of an existing realistic benchmark into that class and say so. | [S072 pp. 9–11] |
| B6 | **Scaled-down public twin** | When the partner's simulator cannot be released, publish a reduced public version that keeps its structure and pathologies. (S074 built a cheaper surrogate problem for testing outside the partner, with no public release stated [S074 pp. 3–4]; it is not a second instance.) | [D006 pp. 4, 6, 22] |
| B7 | **The I/O contract** | `bb.exe x.txt` prints the objective then c_j(x) ≤ 0; a non-zero exit status marks failure; bounds live in the optimizer's problem file. | [S019 pp. 39–41] |
| B8 | **Solver-selection guide** | Map solver requirements (variable types, constraints, noise) to benchmark instances so users can pick instances for their solver. | [S175 p. 7] |
| B9 | **Real test problems in the teaching material** | Ship real blackboxes with software alongside a textbook or course, with a section on their origin, and draw the exercises from them. | [B001 pp. 9, 12, 21] |

## 4. Writing moves

| # | Technique | How to use it | Cards |
|---|---|---|---|
| W1 | **Open with the pathologies and scope the tool negatively** | List the problem traits the method is built for and one sentence naming the classes where readers should use something else. | [S019 p. 11; S023 p. 7; S034 p. 1; S071 p. 3; S173 p. 3] |
| W2 | **State your own method's weakness as an inequality** | Open a methods paper with the precise weakness of your previous method, stated as the inequality it fails to deliver, then state the new target inequality. | [S001 pp. 1–2; S006 p. 2] |
| W3 | ***Why not keep the existing method?*** (template question) | Answer it with concrete user situations before the algorithm; S007 asks "what motivates us to undertake this research rather than to abandon the filter in favor of the barrier". | [S007 pp. 3–4] |
| W4 | **Toy pathology figure first** | Show a 1-D or 2-D toy where the incumbent method fails, with trajectories, before introducing the algorithm. | [S172 pp. 7–8; S129 pp. 5–6; S046 p. 13] |
| W5 | **Position by similarity and difference** | Use *as in X … unlike Y* clauses (template) in the abstract, and a literature table whose columns are the gaps the paper fills. | [S007 p. 1; S156 p. 10; S092 pp. 7, 10; S001 p. 1] |
| W6 | **Summarize the ladder** | Give a numbered hierarchy, or a case → theorem → assumptions table. | [S002 p. 12; S007 pp. 24–25; S090 p. 14; S077 pp. 9–10 *(in words, one rung per smoothness level, stated conditionally because a blackbox's smoothness cannot be measured)*] |
| W7 | **Confessions with a point** | Admit a weak result or a failed run and say what it shows; say which rung of the ladder is lost. | [S005 p. 24; S001 pp. 19, 25; S027 p. 17] |
| W8 | **Erratum style** | Restate the result, split it into checkable bullets, re-prove it in the final notation, and say in the first sentence whether the statement or only the proof is affected. | [D001 p. 1] |
| W9 | **Terminology hygiene** | Rename a concept to what the proof actually uses, and avoid clashes with engineering terms. | [S002 p. 3; S058 p. 8; S121 pp. 13–14; S077 pp. 2–3, 5 *("yes/no" and open constraints; surrogate rather than approximation)*] |
| W10 | **Status grid and open-problem ledger** | Map the problem family as a grid, mark cells trivial, solved (by whom, for which cases) or open, and list each open problem with what blocks it. | [S146 pp. 2–3; S040 pp. 2, 15–16; S093 p. 11; S108 pp. 18–19; S213 pp. 170–175 *(programme)*] |
| W11 | **One running example** | Thread one small realistic example through every definition and end with it fully instantiated. | [S058 pp. 5–6, 16–18; S006 pp. 4–7; S155 p. 2; B001 pp. 20–21 *(book scale: see T3)*] |
| W12 | **Explain an unusual hypothesis by analogy** | Relate it to Newton/SQP assumptions and prove its equivalence with a textbook constraint qualification in the smooth case. | [S001 pp. 8, 13; S007 p. 21] |
| W13 | **Simplifications with their bias** | State each modeling simplification together with the direction of the error it introduces. | [S071 p. 8; S015 p. 4] |
| W14 | **Release notes as a research map** | Cite the paper behind each software feature in the release notes. | [S019 pp. 103–107] |
| W15 | **Itemized forecast** | End an editorial or survey with an itemized list of expected developments. | [S034 p. 2; S077 pp. 10–12 *(the agenda drawn from issues users raise after a first success)*] |
| W16 | **Hedge by naming the missing experience** | When you hedge, say which experience is missing ("no actual experience without the mesh on real problems") instead of hedging in general. | [S077 p. 4] |
| W17 | **Credit the simple step, then add one sentence** | Credit a predecessor's simple observation explicitly as crucial, then say in one sentence what each successor adds (spanning sets → frames → dense directions). | [S077 p. 5] |
| W18 | **Named-change list for a revision** | In a new edition or a revised paper, list the changes as named bullets, each giving the chapter or section it touches and the reason for it. | [B001 pp. 9–10] |
| W19 | **Goal, enabling belief, audiences** | State the goal and the belief that makes it feasible as two sentences; name each audience with what it gets and what it is not given (the state of the art is left to papers). | [B001 pp. 13–14] |

## 5. Side-line devices (extremal geometry and exact optimization)

These come from the extremal-polygon and exact-optimization lines. They transfer to DFO only through certification tasks (certifying a sufficient condition inside a blackbox [S186 pp. 4–5], or certifying Pareto optimality [D003]); they are not part of the core methods.

| # | Technique | How to use it | Cards |
|---|---|---|---|
| X1 | **Numeric optimum first, certificate later** | Find the candidate numerically, then prove that no feasible point beats it by ε with a complete, rounding-safe solver (incumbent cutoff, ε-exclusion). | [S037 pp. 10–14; S117 p. 10; S142 p. 9; D003 pp. 6–7] |
| X2 | **Tolerance in the theorem** | Write the solver tolerance into the statement (value + ε) and check it exceeds the rounding of the model's constants. | [S142 p. 1; D003 p. 12] |
| X3 | **Optimality conditions as cuts** | When a relaxed case contains the incumbent case, add the first-order conditions or structural lemmas as constraints of the certifying model. | [S108 p. 14; S037 pp. 12–14; D003 pp. 13, 19–21] |
| X4 | **Minimax error equalization** | Choose a design parameter that trades two monotone error terms by equalizing them (exact-era cut and linearization points). | [S010 pp. 13–16; S070 pp. 147–152] |

## 6. Teaching devices (textbook front matter, co-authored with Hare)

From the open front matter of the 2nd edition [B001]: the prefaces are the joint voice of Audet and Hare, and chapter content is unread, so these rest on the prefaces and on titles. They are course-design moves, not research methods (`research/08-deep-reading-synthesis.md` §12.6).

| # | Technique | How to use it | Cards |
