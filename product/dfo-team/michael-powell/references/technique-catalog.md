# Technique catalog · M. J. D. Powell

> What this is: the transferable techniques found while reading Powell's papers in full, grouped into proof devices, algorithm-design moves, experiment protocols and writing moves. Each entry gives a one-line "how to use it" and the cards it rests on, as [card id pages]. Cards are in `research/cards/` and indexed in `research/07-paper-cards.md`; the aggregation is `research/08-deep-reading-synthesis.md` §7, whose numbering (P1–P24, A1–A25, E1–E13, W1–W14) is kept here so the two files can be cross-read.
>
> Date: 2026-09-27. Page numbers are those of the text versions read. For most papers after 1997 these are DAMTP report pages, not journal pages; each card's header names the version read. Evidence comes from 35 Scholar-list works read in full or in part, plus the Royal Society memoir (R007, secondary) and two interviews (X001, X002). Nothing here rests on an abstract-only or metadata-only card.
>
> Attribution: almost every DFO paper here is sole-authored. The co-authored texts are S001 (with Fletcher), S050 and S030 (with Yuan), S075 and S085 (with Faul), S074 (with Faul and Goodsell) and S078 (with Beatson and Tan). A technique listed here is a *device the papers use*, not a claim that Powell invented it; where a device is imported (symmetric Broyden, Moré–Sorensen, Goldfarb–Idnani, Broyden–Dennis–Moré) the card says from where. Techniques marked *(RBF)* or *(one project)* come from outside the DFO line or from a single project and are not used as SKILL.md evidence.
>
> Reading lesson (general): before reusing a number from any paper, check that its tables, captions and text agree, and that the version you read is the one you cite.

## 1. Proof devices

| # | Technique | How to use it | Cards |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | **Lagrange function as a determinant ratio** | To choose which interpolation point to replace, or where to put a geometry point, evaluate the candidate's Lagrange function: replacing a point multiplies \|det\| of the interpolation system by \|ℓ_t\| in any basis, and the update denominator is σ = det W⁺/det W, so maximize it. | [S014 p. 32; S068 p. 5; S025 p. 18; S093 pp. 9–13; S124 pp. 7–8; S018 pp. 21–22] |
| P2 | **Self-correcting update via an error matrix** | Define Ξ = W − H⁻¹ (or Bᵀ − H⁻¹) and show that one update zeroes the touched row and column and leaves the rest unchanged. *no error growth* and *errors erased once every point has moved* then follow without a floating-point analysis. | [S068 pp. 6–8; S025 pp. 16–17; S045 pp. 14, 26–28; S124 p. 10; S018 p. 13] |
| P3 | **Projection identity against rounding accumulation** | If an update multiplies the discrepancy J − H⁻¹ by a projection, rounding errors cannot accumulate; pair it with a device that keeps the steps spanning the space. | [S019 pp. 24–25] |
| P4 | **A representation whose shape forces an invariant** | If exact arithmetic implies rank r or a zero block, store the matrix as ZSZᵀ with exactly r columns and prove the zero block by cofactors, so the property holds in floating point by construction. | [S124 p. 14; S018 p. 13; S007 p. 17] |
| P5 | **Pythagorean identity for least-change projections** | For a Frobenius-norm projection onto an affine set that contains the true matrix, write ‖E_{k+1}‖² = ‖E_k‖² − ‖change‖², telescope to summable changes, and feed a Broyden–Dennis–Moré-type condition. Fast convergence follows without an accurate model. | [S072 pp. 4–5; S045 pp. 9–10; S047 pp. 18–19; S029 p. 10; S036 p. 5; S137 pp. 17–18; S108 p. 3] |
| P6 | **KKT (saddle) system of the least-change problem** | Derive the (m+n+1) system from the KKT conditions (drop the symmetry constraint: it comes for free), keep its inverse, read the Lagrange functions off its columns, and update it by a rank-two formula with denominator σ = αβ + τ². | [S045 pp. 7–17; S072 pp. 10–12; S018 p. 9; S007 pp. 14–17; S047 p. 19] |
| P7 | **Closed-form rounding magnitudes on a tunable example** | Build the smallest configuration with one geometric ratio (a cluster of radius η at distance ξ), compute every term's magnitude in closed form, and let the resulting power of ξ/η (here M⁴, M⁶, M⁸) set the practical threshold, such as when to shift the origin. | [S124 pp. 11–12, 15–16; S045 p. 19; S018 p. 29; S007 p. 32] |
| P8 | **Cheap analytic bound instead of an expensive selection quantity** | When a rule needs a quantity for every candidate (β_j for each point), prove an upper bound along the candidate family and select with the bound. | [S007 pp. 19–20; S036 p. 13; S025 pp. 7–9, 22] |
| P9 | **Counting and potential arguments for repair steps** | To show that repairs cannot go on for ever, use an index set that shrinks monotonically and resets on success (3n+3 iterations), a potential whose per-step increase is bounded and which a forced repair reduces, or a fixed-factor gain per restart against a bounded total. | [S093 pp. 13–19; S067 pp. 18–19; S014 pp. 45–46] |
| P10 | **Convergence under weak hypotheses** | Sandwich the radius to count successes (at least order ℓ/log ℓ), sum decreases over dyadic blocks into a divergent harmonic series, use an up-crossing argument between two levels, and work with the largest step fraction that stays in the level set. | [S129 pp. 7–11; S030 pp. 11–12] |
| P10b | **Upgrade lim inf to lim** | Assume ‖∇F‖ > 10ε infinitely often; pair each such index ℓ with the next index m where ‖∇F‖ < ε, and sum the decreases between them; if the total cannot stay finite while F is bounded below, the limit is zero. | [S093 pp. 25–27; S129 pp. 7–11] |
| P11 | **Safeguard inactivity** | Prove r_k → 1, so the trust region or relaxation becomes inactive, then cite the unsafeguarded method's rate theorem instead of re-proving it; for a penalty parameter use a stay-or-at-least-double rule, so a bound gives finitely many increases. | [S030 pp. 10, 15, 18; S050 pp. 3, 6–7] |
| P12 | **Normalization by invariance** | Use affine, orthogonal or origin-shift invariance to put the problem where the proof is easiest (∇²F(x*) = I, B diagonal, origin at x_k), and subtract whatever the approximation reproduces exactly (the Taylor quadratic). | [S139 p. 3; S165 p. 5; S014 pp. 12–13; S137 p. 5; S007 pp. 19–20; S068 p. 9; S121 pp. 8–9] |
| P13 | **Smallest-case reduction** | Shrink the setting until the algorithm's state collapses (n = 2 removes B_k; d = 1), prove or refute the claim there, and then say plainly whether it extends. | [S121 p. 3; S090 pp. 3, 14; R007 p. 9; S139 pp. 8–14] |
| P14 | **Failure proofs in exact arithmetic** | For convergence to a wrong point, find an exact reduction identity whose increments decay geometrically, so the total decrease is finite. Alternatives: build F from the iterates you want (a functional equation); rewrite a published counterexample as a recurrence with a closed form; when a counterexample is hard to find, parametrize the iterate sequence and solve the method's conditions as a feasibility problem. | [S115 pp. 37–39; S139 pp. 9–10; S029 pp. 5–6; S014 p. 14; S121 pp. 22–23] |
| P15 | **Equivalence theorem by induction** | To carry guarantees across a reformulation, show that the two step-defining QPs coincide under a linear map; exact line searches on equal potentials then give equal iterates. | [S115 pp. 28–31] |
| P16 | **Native-norm descent** *(RBF)* | Show that each stage is an exact line search in the error semi-norm; monotone decrease plus finite dimension gives convergence. | [S075 pp. 5–6; S085 pp. 8–12; S074 pp. 13–18] |
| P17 | **Operation-count bounds** *(one project)* | Normalize sequences of local operations with commutation and exchange rules to prove a lower bound; give matched upper and lower counts; choose free integers as powers of n that balance the dominant cost monomials. | [S148 pp. 8–24; S165 pp. 14–20] |
| P18 | **Robust root-finding** | Reparametrize a secular equation (1/‖d(λ)‖ rather than ‖d(λ)‖) until it is convex with a bounded derivative, bracket with Newton and false position, and use a failed Cholesky factorization as a Rayleigh-quotient lower bound. | [S165 pp. 6, 9–10; S186 p. 7] |
| P19 | **A theory bound becomes a runtime test** | Estimate the unknown constant of an error bound online as the largest ratio observed so far, switch the test on only after enough observations, and set its tolerance to the gain you forgo by not stepping. | [S068 p. 4; S025 pp. 10, 13; S093 p. 7] |
| P20 | **Induction carrying two invariants** | Prove finite termination by carrying a geometric invariant (conjugacy) and an operator invariant (eigenvectors of HG) together, and derive the update from the invariant the proof needs. | [S001 pp. 2–3] |
| P21 | **Range/null-space split** | Split the step into range-space and null-space parts and handle *range part large* and *range part small* with the two halves of a combined descent test; a trapezoid expansion then shows that the unit step is accepted. | [S050 pp. 4–5, 8–9; S030 pp. 7–8] |
| P22 | **Duality sign pattern** | Link a box-bounded dual to an ℓ₁ exact penalty through the sign pattern of the maximizer's components; intersect the feasible set with a small neighbourhood to apply a global-minimizer theorem at a strict local minimizer. | [S167 pp. 5–8, 12, 17] |
| P23 | **Approximation-theory devices** *(RBF)* | Use kernel moment identities, far-centre Vandermonde arguments and stencils from polynomial exactness; bound all higher-order terms before trusting an asymptotic design rule. | [S090 pp. 7–12; S089 pp. 5–6, 17–21] |
| P24 | **Limit sets, compactness and best approximation** | Study the limit set of the piecewise-linear iterate path (closed, connected, collinear) rather than the iterates; extract a uniform constant by compactness over n+1 directions; for a quadratic with exact line searches, rewrite the iteration as polynomial best approximation. | [S121 pp. 5–8; S139 pp. 7, 10–12] |

## 2. Algorithm-design moves

| # | Technique | How to use it | Cards |
|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | **Keep the framework, change one component** | Share one numbered flowchart with the predecessor, restrict or swap only the model space or the one component under study, and keep the incumbent as a special case, so every difference in the results is attributable to the change. | [S018 pp. 3, 5; S047 pp. 3, 11; S072 p. 9; S036 p. 7; S108 pp. 2, 4; S050 pp. 2–3; S030 pp. 2–5] |
| A2 | **Two radii** | Make ρ a never-increased lower bound on Δ; do not evaluate F for ‖d‖ < ½ρ; give up the current ρ through an explicit exit test, because successful models can make every step shorter than ½ρ. | [S014 pp. 26–28; S025 pp. 3, 11–14; S072 pp. 8–9; S018 pp. 4, 7, 27–29; S007 pp. 27–31] |
| A3 | **Accept any decrease** | Move to the trial point whenever F decreases, so x_k is always the best point found; do not add a sufficient-decrease rule that exists only for the proof. | [S047 p. 6; S072 p. 2; S129 p. 2; S093 pp. 4–5; S030 p. 21] |
| A4 | **Two causes of a failed model step** | Decide whether the step was too long (shrink Δ) or the sample set is degenerate (improve the geometry), and give each cause its own remedy and step type. | [S014 p. 25; S018 p. 6; S093 pp. 6–8] |
| A5 | **Geometry step and point selection** | Maximize \|ℓ_t\| in a ball for the geometry step; choose the point to drop by \|ℓ_t\| or \|σ_t\| times a power of its distance, or by the largest barycentric coordinate. | [S014 pp. 29, 32, 34; S025 p. 18; S072 p. 12; S018 pp. 23, 27–28; S007 pp. 12–13; S093 pp. 7–8] |
| A6 | **Initial points chosen for closed forms** | Place the initial points (coordinate steps ±ρ_beg, then pairs) so that the Lagrange functions and the inverse KKT matrix are available in closed, sparse form, which makes setup O(n²). | [S068 pp. 14–15; S025 pp. 14–16; S072 p. 8; S018 pp. 8–11, 37–41; S007 pp. 4–8] |
| A7 | **Origin shifts and differences** | Keep a base point near the best point, work with differences rather than absolute values, drop the constant term of the model, and shift the base point when ‖d‖² ≤ 10⁻³‖x_opt − x₀‖². | [S025 pp. 2, 14; S045 pp. 19–23; S124 p. 12; S018 pp. 15–16, 29; S007 p. 32] |
| A8 | **Implicit Hessian** | Store ∇²Q as a base matrix plus stored multipliers on known rank-one terms, so the model update stays O(m²) and a Hessian–vector product costs O(mn). | [S045 p. 13; S018 pp. 16–17; S007 p. 15] |
| A9 | **Exact solve on a cheap restricted set** | Instead of solving the full subproblem roughly, solve it exactly on n+1 or m−1 lines, and compare end-to-end #F rather than subproblem quality. | [S036 pp. 9–13; S007 p. 12] |
| A10 | **Guarded cheap step** | Accept a cheap heuristic step only if it passes an analytic lower-bound test, and fall back to a Cauchy-type step otherwise; for a crude subproblem solve, prove a worst-case ratio and log the empirical ratio. | [S036 p. 13; S007 pp. 12–13; S025 pp. 7–9] |
| A11 | **Switch on consecutive flags** | Trigger a model switch or a ρ reduction only after three consecutive flags (three small model errors; three flags before replacing Q by Q_int), not after one. | [S018 pp. 28–29, 31–32; S007 pp. 30–32] |
| A12 | **Dogleg** | Keep Levenberg–Marquardt's bias of short steps towards steepest descent, but replace its path by a piecewise-linear one that costs O(n²). | [S019 pp. 18–19; R007 p. 6] |
| A13 | **Cautious radius growth** | Enlarge the step bound only after two extrapolation estimates since the last reduction, by at most a factor of two, to remove grow-shrink oscillation. | [S019 p. 22] |
| A14 | **Keep the steps spanning the space** | Keep an orthonormal recency basis with a 30° independence test, or alpha steps that restore simplex volume, and insert a special step when the recent steps fail to span. | [S019 pp. 25–27; S093 p. 6] |
| A15 | **Floors tied to rounding** | Put a floor on the step bound at the finite-difference step, and never update a secant model from a displacement shorter than that floor. | [S019 pp. 21, 33] |
| A16 | **Model each constraint separately** | Give each constraint its own model or linearization and use the merit function only to accept or rank points. | [S014 pp. 24, 29–30; S050 pp. 2–3, 12; S030 pp. 3–5, 21; S115 p. 40] |
| A17 | **Near-active set and constraints on increments** | Build the first direction from all constraints within η₂Δ, and impose active constraints on step increments rather than on positions, so tiny steps onto nearly active constraints are avoided. | [S067 pp. 9–10] |
| A18 | **A change of variables that keeps the solver's invariant** | If a subproblem solver is rejected as too costly, look for a change of coordinates (orthogonal, so Euclidean lengths and the trust region are kept) that makes its linear algebra cheap. | [S165 pp. 12–16; S148 pp. 14–24] |
| A19 | **A shift of origin instead of an unbounded parameter** | If a method reaches the answer only as a penalty parameter goes to infinity, look for a shift that reaches it at a finite value, and identify the shift with a known dual quantity. | [R007 p. 7; S137 pp. 9–10] |
| A20 | **Partial or damped updates** | Keep a structural invariant (positive definiteness) by damping or partially updating, and state plainly what information is lost. | [R007 p. 15; S137 p. 13] |
| A21 | **Relax inconsistent linearized constraints** | Bound the residual ‖c + Aᵀd‖ by any ζ between the least residuals attainable in two smaller balls, so the proofs use only the two bounds and implementers may choose any ζ in between. | [S030 pp. 2–3] |
| A22 | **Rewrite a transformed method as a QP in the original variables** | Characterize the step of a method defined through a change of variables as a QP in the original coordinates, then extend the method by substituting new structure into that QP. | [S115 pp. 19, 25–27] |
| A23 | **Fix a free weight by scale invariance** | Work out how each term scales under x → σx to decide how a weight must depend on the length scale, and damp its recurrence so it cannot reinforce itself. | [S108 pp. 7–9] |
| A24 | **Approximate solve, then exact repair** | After an inexact solve, add the minimum-norm correction that restores the hard interpolation constraints exactly. | [S108 p. 25] |
| A25 | **Randomized ordering and feasibility sweeps** *(RBF / one project)* | Randomize the data ordering for cheap coarsening; solve minimax problems by feasibility sweeps plus bisection. | [S074 pp. 7–8; S057 p. 17; S170 pp. 4–5] |

## 3. Experiment protocols

| # | Technique | How to use it | Cards |
|---|---|---|---|
| E1 | **Random family with a planted minimizer** | Use a trigonometric sum of squares with random integer coefficients in [−100, 100], choose the constants so F(x*) = 0, start from x* plus a controlled perturbation, run 5 instances per n with the same random numbers for every variant, double n up to 320, and report min–max ranges. Powell used this family from 1963 to 2013. | [S001 p. 5; S019 pp. 37–38; S068 p. 12; S025 p. 22; S047 p. 14; S072 pp. 13–14; S029 pp. 10–11; S036 p. 12; S007 pp. 33–34; S093 pp. 28–29; S108 pp. 10–12; S137 p. 18] |
| E2 | **Per-resolution trace** | Print #F and the best F each time ρ is lowered, so readers see the marginal cost of each decade of accuracy. | [S068 p. 13; S025 p. 24] |
| E3 | **Rounding probes that change nothing in exact arithmetic** | Permute the variables, run a configuration in which the new code must equal the old one, add a large constant to F, rerun in single and double precision, perturb the start by 10⁻⁶, tighten the tolerance 100×, solve the mirrored problem, or monitor a sign invariant; report the spread. | [S018 pp. 32, 36; S036 pp. 14, 18–19; S124 pp. 23–24; S025 p. 26; S139 pp. 5–6; S007 pp. 36, 38; S170 p. 25] |
| E4 | **Fault injection and stress tests of a component** | Test an update formula alone for 10⁵ iterations with random points, inject errors, include a far-origin phase and a shrinking-radius phase, measure the invariants directly, and publish the phase that fails. | [S045 pp. 29–34] |
| E5 | **Follow up a surprise by removing its suspected mechanism** | Write down the mechanism you suspect, transform the problem so that it is removed (random diagonal scaling, bounds at ±10¹⁰, a spacing constraint), rerun, and report whether the surprise survives; for one surprising cell, run ten extra instances. | [S047 pp. 15–16; S036 p. 11; S108 p. 12; S074 p. 26; S170 pp. 25–26] |
| E6 | **One code base with the incumbent as a special case** | Run the predecessor inside the new code (θ = 0, m = m*, bounds ±10¹⁰, a switch turned off) with the same random numbers. | [S047 pp. 3, 11; S108 pp. 2, 10; S036 p. 11; S093 p. 28; S124 pp. 23–24] |
| E7 | **Cost reporting** | Report #F and seconds side by side, per-task timing shares, time/(n²·#F) across n, histograms of inner work per outer iteration, the first evaluation with F ≤ 1.001·F_final, safeguard costs in each row, and timing sweeps for the hidden constant and the crossover n. | [S047 pp. 16–17; S025 p. 28; S018 pp. 34–35, 37; S067 p. 24; S007 p. 34; S148 pp. 21–22; S089 pp. 14–15; S001 pp. 3–4] |
| E8 | **Failures in the main table** | Keep wrong-minimum runs (marked), use "?" for runs above the evaluation budget, keep non-converged runs, explain excluded variants, show raw outliers, and list local minima with their F values. | [S001 p. 5; S025 p. 23; S018 p. 36; S075 p. 9; S036 pp. 15, 17; S108 p. 12; S067 p. 23; S007 p. 38] |
| E9 | **Cases built to break one's own method** | Include a deliberately badly scaled case, a case with no solution, and a geometry built to make the fast variant diverge; explain the result analytically, so readers can tell an intrinsic difficulty from a bug. | [S019 pp. 39–42, 45; S055 p. 7; S075 p. 9; S085 p. 20; S170 pp. 23–24; S045 p. 33] |
| E10 | **Fix the parameters before the experiments** | Choose every algorithm parameter before any run and say so in the paper; state the tuning status of every parameter set. | [S093 p. 27; S050 p. 9] |
| E11 | **Rate experiments at extreme precision** | To see asymptotic rates, handle the known part analytically, carry error matrices, and rescale on the fly or store mantissa and exponent separately, so accuracies far beyond the floating-point range become visible. | [S139 pp. 5–6; S108 p. 15] |
| E12 | **The competitor's own problem** *(one project)* | When you compare with another method, use its own test problem and its published counts. (UOBYQA's baseline in S025 pp. 29–30 is Fletcher's published counts for Powell's own 1964 method, not a competitor.) | [S019 pp. 37–39] |
| E13 | **Expected results first** | Write down, before running, the result you expect for each variant, and label the surprises afterwards. | [S047 p. 3; S075 p. 9] |

## 4. Writing moves

| # | Technique | How to use it | Cards |
|---|---|---|---|
| W1 | **Status labels per component** | Label each component final, provisional or under development, with dates, and use plain words for the weak parts ("crude and provisional", "embarrassing"). | [S072 pp. 7–8, 12–15; S047 pp. 19–20; S036 p. 19; S030 pp. 5, 21; S050 p. 12] |
| W2 | **A decision record** | State what was tried, which counterexample or table ruled it out, and when the decision was taken. | [S067 pp. 1, 4, 16, 25, 30; S124 pp. 9, 22–24; S018 p. 15; S108 pp. 1, 13] |
| W3 | **The negative result in the abstract** | Put what did not work, or what could not be proved, in the abstract next to what did. | [S108 p. 1; S115 p. 2; S067 p. 1; S139 p. 1] |
| W4 | **Every constant labelled with its origin** | In the sentence that introduces a constant, say whether it comes from a proof requirement, a balancing equation, a magnitude estimate, intuition or experiment. | [S019 pp. 16–17, 21–23; S025 pp. 11, 14; S018 pp. 19, 23, 27–28; S007 pp. 23, 32] |
| W5 | **A survey as one essay per mechanism** | Give each mechanism its algorithm, its best theorem and its cleanest failure example, then state your own opinion separately, in the first person. | [S014 pp. 1, 17–18, 42–47; S029 pp. 2–11; S137 pp. 2–19] |
| W6 | **First-person candour about surprises** | Name surprises as surprises in the text ("staggering and unexplained"), and say when an explanation came only with hindsight. | [S047 p. 13; S072 p. 14; S108 p. 14] |
| W7 | **Skippable proofs signposted** | Tell readers which proofs can be skipped, and arrange the lemmas so that skipping them is safe. | [S045 p. 26; S093 pp. 13, 16, 20; S121 pp. 4, 10] |
| W8 | **Each unusual term justified by its proof** | Right after the algorithm, justify each unusual term of an acceptance test by pointing to the proof that needs it. | [S030 p. 4] |
| W9 | **Precise credit and narrow novelty claims** | Say how much of the literature you read, what you took from whom, and claim novelty only for the pieces you are sure of. | [S115 pp. 3–4, 41; S068 p. 10; S167 p. 4; S014 pp. 34–35] |
| W10 | **The scaling limit in the abstract** | State the method's limit as a number of variables tied to a cost formula. | [S025 p. 1] |
| W11 | **A software report organized for users first** | Put the parameters in a section a user can read without the method, and write each error message as a diagnosis with its likely causes. | [S019 pp. 9, 11, 13] |
| W12 | **A numbered flowchart shared with the predecessor** | Give the whole algorithm as a numbered flowchart that the predecessor also follows, then one section per box. | [S018 pp. 3, 5] |
| W13 | **Open with the concrete episode** | Open a methods essay with the concrete episode that made the question urgent, then restrict the scope explicitly. | [S029 pp. 1–3; S014 p. 2; S089 p. 2] |
| W14 | **Say where a lemma came from** | Tell the reader when a lemma was suggested by numerical calculations or rediscovered before a literature check. | [S045 pp. 26, 32; S068 p. 10] |
