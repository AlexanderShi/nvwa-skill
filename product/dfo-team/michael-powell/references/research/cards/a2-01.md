# Paper cards · batch a2-01 (abstract / metadata level, 1960–1969)

Batch intent: Catalogue 30 works from 1960 to 1969 that have no open full text or are not research items. This covers the Harwell years from the crystal-field physics papers to the augmented Lagrangian. The aim is to see how Powell's first optimization and approximation papers pose problems and state contributions, as far as their abstracts allow.
Focus dimensions: D1, D2 and D8, each taken from the abstract. Nothing beyond the abstract was read, except for the labelled **Secondary** lines.
Material roles: S002, S004 and S012 are core in INDEX, and the rest are supplement. At this read level every card is a reduced supplement card.

Sources and limits:
- Abstracts come from `references/sources/papers/abstracts.json`, and their origins are in `abstract-sources.json`. In the S061 abstract, `?` and `£` are the publisher's OCR renderings of Greek symbols, so quotes avoid them. The S080 abstract flattens superscripts: `O(n2)` means O(n²).
- Crossref and OpenAlex DOI lookups returned no abstract for S063, S168, S178, S181, S182, S117 and S143. OpenAlex marks all of them closed access. For S117, the OpenAlex `abstract` field holds only the scraped SIAM landing page (reference list and history), so it is used here as metadata, not as an abstract. For S143, the field repeats the citation.
- Crossref title search found no DOI or abstract for the no-DOI items S004, S056, D001, S206 and S210. OSTI records give report numbers but no abstracts: S092 is TP-372, S127 is AERE-R-5602 and S180 is AERE-R-6072.

### S061 · A Calculation of the Ground-State Splitting for Mn Ions in a Cubic Field (Proc. Royal Society of London A 1961, DOI 10.1098/rspa.1961.0214)
**Read**: abstract · **Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: J. R. Gabriel, D. F. Johnston
- **D1 Problem & entry**: The paper calculates the ground-state splitting and optical absorption spectrum of a (d5)6S ion in a cubic host crystal (abstract).
- **D2 Key idea**: It uses the simplest crystal-field approximation with a complete (d5) configuration. The results are expressed as functions of the Racah, crystal-field, spin-orbit and spin-spin parameters (abstract). The computational method was not read.
- **D8 Contribution**: A parametric calculation of the splitting and spectrum (abstract). The results themselves were not read.
- **Method links**: none from SKILL.md. Trajectory only: this is the physics-calculation phase before his optimization work. **Secondary**: the memoir says his early Harwell work was in *calculations in atomic physics and chemistry* (R007 p. 5).
- **Quotes**: "The ground-state splitting and optical absorption spectrum for a (d5)6S ion in a cubic host crystal are calculated" (abstract)

### S002 · An efficient method for finding the minimum of a function of several variables without calculating derivatives (The Computer Journal 1964, DOI 10.1093/comjnl/7.2.155)
**Read**: abstract · **Role**: core (per INDEX; only the abstract is read here) · **Coauthors**: none
- **D1 Problem & entry**: Derivative-free minimization. The entry point is the well-known method that changes one parameter at a time (abstract).
- **D2 Key idea**: The design is two variations of that classical method (abstract):
  - A *simple variation* chooses conjugate directions when applied to a quadratic, so the ultimate rate on general functions is fast.
  - A *further variation* makes convergence from a bad starting approximation efficient.
- **D8 Contribution**: The method, with satisfactory practical applications and numerical examples of up to twenty variables (abstract). No convergence theorem is mentioned.
- **Secondary**:
  - Each cycle makes n or n+1 line searches over directions that span the space. The direction to drop is chosen to maximize a determinant, the *natural measure* of linear independence (S014 pp. 20–21).
  - Later, Powell was worried that this choice may drop directions that are already conjugate (S014 pp. 44–45).
  - He cites it as a successful algorithm published without good theoretical justification (X001 p. 17).
  - It improved on a conjugate-direction method of Smith (R007 p. 6).
- **Method links**:
  - Method 5 ✅. The claim rests on practical use and numerical examples with a stated size range, up to twenty variables (abstract).
  - Method 1 ⚠ variant. The paper makes minimal variations to a classical method rather than to his own predecessor (abstract).
  - Existing candidate *determinant-ratio (Lagrange-function) geometry control* ✅. An early instance: the 1964 direction exchange maximizes a determinant (Secondary, S014 p. 21).
  - New-pattern candidate: *two-part design: one change gives the right behaviour on the quadratic (or Newton) model case, so the local rate is fast, and a separate change makes progress from a bad start* (abstract). The same split is stated for the 1968 hybrid method, which keeps the fast convergence of Newton's method but makes the iteration progressive far from the solution (S019 p. 6).
- **Transferable technique** [D2]: Present a new method as named, separate changes to a well-known one. Show that one change gives the right behaviour on the quadratic model problem (conjugate directions), and that the other handles starts far from the solution. Each change can then be judged on its own (abstract).
- **Quotes**:
  - "This variation is such that when the procedure is applied to a quadratic form, it causes conjugate directions to be chosen, so the ultimate rate of convergence is fast when the method is used to minimize a general function." (abstract)
  - "Practical applications of the procedure have proved to be very satisfactory, and numerical examples are given in which functions of up to twenty variables are minimized." (abstract)

### S012 · A method for minimizing a sum of squares of non-linear functions without calculating derivatives (The Computer Journal 1965, DOI 10.1093/comjnl/7.4.303)
**Read**: abstract · **Role**: core (per INDEX; only the abstract is read here) · **Coauthors**: none
- **D1 Problem & entry**: The classical generalization of the linear least-squares method (i.e. Gauss–Newton) minimizes sums of squares efficiently but needs derivatives (abstract).
- **D2 Key idea**: An original method that has comparable convergence but needs no derivatives (abstract). The mechanism was not read.
- **D8 Contribution**: The number of evaluations of the terms is roughly proportional to the number of variables. A system of 50 nonlinear equations in 50 unknowns was solved with fewer than 200 evaluations of the left-hand sides (abstract).
- **Secondary**: In 2005, Powell said the 1965 least-squares method without derivatives *is also used often in practice* (X001 p. 12).
- **Method links**:
  - Method 3 ✅. Cost is measured in function evaluations against n (roughly O(n)), the same measure Method 3 step 5 prescribes, forty years before NEWUOA (abstract).
  - Method 5 ✅. The headline claim is one concrete, large test with an exact count (abstract).
  - Method 1 ⚠ variant. The method keeps the convergence of the classical procedure and removes one requirement, derivatives (abstract).
  - New-pattern candidate: *same benefit as the classical technique, one requirement fewer*. The contribution sentence claims convergence comparable to the classical procedure, without any derivatives (abstract). S062 is a second instance.
- **Transferable technique** [D5/D7]: State the efficiency of a derivative-free method as evaluations relative to n. Back it with one large concrete instance and its exact budget (here n = 50, fewer than 200 evaluations), not with an average over small problems (abstract).
- **Quotes**:
  - "The number of times the individual terms of the sum of squares have to be calculated is approximately proportional to the number of variables." (abstract)
  - "Finding a solution to a set of fifty non-linear equations in fifty unknowns required the left-hand sides of the equations to be worked out fewer than two hundred times." (abstract)

### S177 · On the convergence of exchange algorithms for calculating minimax approximations (The Computer Journal 9(1) 1966, DOI 10.1093/comjnl/9.1.78)
**Read**: abstract · **Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: A. R. Curtis
- **D1 Problem & entry**: Exchange algorithms for minimax approximation seek, iteratively, the n+1 points where the maximum error occurs. Each iteration computes a best approximation on n+1 points (abstract).
- **D2 Key idea**: Regard the minimax error on the point set, η, and the optimal parameters, μ, as functions of the points. Then prove theorems on their first and second derivatives (abstract).
- **D8 Contribution**: Derivative theorems that *provide much insight* into the convergence of exchange algorithms (abstract). Whether a rate is proved was not read.
- **Method links**: none from SKILL.md. New-pattern candidate (weak, single work): *explain an iteration's convergence by differentiating its subproblem value with respect to the iterate* (abstract).
- **Transferable technique** [D4]: To analyse an iteration that re-solves a subproblem on a changing reference set, treat the subproblem's optimal value as a function of that set. Its first and second derivatives then describe how the outer iteration converges (abstract).
- **Quotes**:
  - "In this paper we present theorems on the first and second derivatives of η and μt." (abstract)
  - "They provide much insight into the convergence of exchange algorithms." (abstract)

### S054 · On the maximum errors of polynomial approximations defined by interpolation and by least squares criteria (The Computer Journal 1967, DOI 10.1093/comjnl/9.4.404)
**Read**: abstract · **Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: none
- **D1 Problem & entry**: Approximate f on [a, b] by a polynomial of degree ≤ n. How far are the cheap criteria, interpolation and least squares, from the best achievable maximum error (abstract)?
- **D2 Key idea**: Prove that their maximum errors are within factors of the minimax error, and that the factors do not depend on f. Give expressions for the factors and evaluate them for Chebyshev truncation and for interpolation at Chebyshev zeros (abstract).
- **D8 Contribution**: Small, explicit factors. For example, degree-20 interpolation is guaranteed to be within a factor of four of the minimax error (abstract).
- **Method links**: none from SKILL.md. New-pattern candidate: *bound the cheap method against the optimal one by a data-independent factor, then headline one concrete number* (abstract). S210 (1966, below) may be a meeting-abstract precursor (inferred from title).
- **Transferable technique** [D4/D7]: When the optimal method is expensive, prove that a cheap one is within an f-independent factor of it. Evaluate the factor for the choices practitioners actually use, and end the abstract with one concrete guarantee (abstract).
- **Quotes**:
  - "It is proved that the maximum errors of approximations defined by interpolation and by least squares criteria are within factors, independent of f(x), of the least maximum error that can be achieved." (abstract)
  - "The resultant numbers are not large: for example, if interpolation is used to evaluate a polynomial approximation of degree 20 it may be guaranteed that the resultant maximum error does not exceed the minimax error by more than a factor of four." (abstract)

### S080 · On the calculation of orthogonal vectors (The Computer Journal 1968, DOI 10.1093/comjnl/11.3.302)
**Read**: abstract · **Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: none
- **D1 Problem & entry**: Rosenbrock's (1960) minimization procedure needs a new orthonormal basis whose k-th vector is a combination of d0, d1, …, dk−1, and computing it costs time (abstract). **Secondary**: Rosenbrock regenerates his directions by Gram–Schmidt every n iterations (S014 p. 4).
- **D2 Key idea**: Compute the new orthonormal basis in O(n²) operations from the old basis and one extra vector d0 (abstract).
- **D8 Contribution**: Less computer time for Rosenbrock's procedure, and a proof that errors do not grow under repeated application (abstract).
- **Method links**:
  - Method 4 ✅. Operation count and error growth under repetition are the two stated results (abstract).
  - Existing candidate *self-correcting maintained basis* ⚠ variant. The property shown here is non-growth of errors, not active correction (abstract).
- **Transferable technique** [D4]: For any update that an outer algorithm applies many times, state its operation count and prove that errors do not accumulate over repeated applications, not only that one application is accurate (abstract).
- **Quotes**:
  - "The method is useful because it reduces the amount of computer time that is needed by Rosenbrock's (1960) minimisation procedure." (abstract)
  - "We show that any errors do not grow if the method is applied many times." (abstract)

### S123 · On best L 2 spline approximations (Numerische Mathematik Differentialgleichungen Approximationstheorie, 317–339 1968, DOI 10.1007/978-3-0348-5881-6_28)
**Read**: abstract · **Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: none
- **D1 Problem & entry**: The best L2 approximation of f by a spline of degree n with N knots whose positions are free. Free knots make the problem nonlinear (abstract).
- **D2 Key idea**: The theory is aimed at computing the best approximation. The paper gives a necessary condition for optimal knots that needs only f square-integrable and bounded. Stronger theorems follow for f with monotonic n-th derivative, a class motivated by *best* quadrature formulae (Sard) (abstract).
- **D8 Contribution**: Properties of best free-knot L2 splines, a necessary condition, stronger theorems for the monotone class, and comments on knot-adjustment algorithms (abstract).
- **Secondary**: The memoir says the error functional is minimized by the method of the 1963 DFP paper (R007 p. 20).
- **Method links**: none from SKILL.md. New-pattern candidate (single work): *two-tier theorem: a necessary condition under minimal hypotheses, then stronger results on a class that an application motivates* (abstract).
- **Transferable technique** [D3]: Separate the result that needs almost nothing (square-integrable, bounded f) from the stronger results on a structured class. Name the application that justifies the structured class (abstract).
- **Quotes**:
  - "The speciality of this paper is that the knot positions are variable, so we do not have a linear problem." (abstract)
  - "The theory is directed towards calculating the best approximation, and we obtain a useful necessary condition for optimal knot positions, that just requires f(x) to be square integrable and bounded." (abstract)

### S118 · A theorem on rank one modifications to a matrix and its inverse (The Computer Journal 12(3), 288–290 1969, DOI 10.1093/comjnl/12.3.288)
**Read**: abstract · **Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: none
- **D1 Problem & entry**: Some algorithms need the inverse of J + uvᵀ given J⁻¹. There are many O(n²) ways to compute it, but an error in J⁻¹ usually carries through (abstract).
- **D2 Key idea**: Among the algebraically equivalent expressions, identify one that tends to suppress the contribution of errors in J⁻¹ (abstract). The expression was not read.
- **D8 Contribution**: A theorem singling out an error-damping rank-one inverse update (abstract).
- **Method links**:
  - Method 4 ✅. Among equivalent formulas, the choice is made by rounding-error behaviour (abstract).
  - Existing candidate *self-correcting maintained basis* ✅. The formula tends to correct errors in the maintained inverse (abstract).
- **Transferable technique** [D2]: When an algorithm keeps an inverse or factorization current by updates, compare the equivalent update formulas by what each does to an error already present in the stored matrix. Choose the one that damps it (abstract).
- **Quotes**: "This calculation can be carried out in many ways using only of order n^2 computer operations, but if the inverse of J is wrong, then the resultant expression for the inverse of (J + uv^T) is usually wrong also." (abstract)
