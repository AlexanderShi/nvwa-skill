# Paper cards · batch a2-04 (abstract / metadata level, 1983–1991)

Batch intent: Catalogue 30 works from 1983–1991 that have no open full text or are not research items. This is the early Cambridge decade. It covers convergence theory for conjugate-gradient, variable-metric and trust-region methods, constrained-optimization codes (VMCWD, the Goldfarb–Idnani QP code, TOLMIN), matrix-updating numerics, and the start of the radial-basis-function strand. The aim is to place each work in the research trajectory and to record what its abstract supports about Powell's craft.
Focus dimensions: D1, D2 and D8, each taken from the abstract. Method links and transferable techniques are given only where the abstract supports them.
Material roles: S020, S005 and S010 are core in INDEX, and the rest are supplement. At this read level every card is a reduced supplement card.

### S059 · VMCWD: A FORTRAN subroutine for constrained optimization (ACM SIGMAP Bulletin 1983, DOI 10.1145/1111272.1111273)
**Read**: abstract · **Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: none

- **D2 Key idea**: A variable-metric method for constrained optimization combined with the *Watch-Dog technique*, which gives the acronym VMCWD (abstract). How the watchdog works was not read.
- **D8 Contribution**: A published code listing whose trade-off is stated in the abstract itself. The code is efficient in function and gradient evaluations, but its per-iteration overhead is expensive when evaluations are cheap (abstract).

  - Research Taste mark 4 ✅. The limitation is stated in the abstract, where every reader will see it (abstract).

- **Quotes**:

### S079 · The convergence of variable metric matrices in unconstrained optimization (Mathematical Programming 27(2) 1983, pp. 123–143, DOI 10.1007/bf02591941)
**Read**: abstract (new, Springer page; not in abstracts.json) · **Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: Ge Ren-Pu
- **D1 Problem & entry**: The iterates of DFP or BFGS with unit step lengths may converge. The question is whether the variable-metric matrices then converge too (abstract, Springer page).
- **D2 Key idea**: Assume a Lipschitz continuous second derivative, and assume the iterates converge to a point where ∇F is zero and ∇²F is positive definite. Under these conditions the matrix sequence is proved to converge (abstract, Springer page). The proof was not read.
- **D8 Contribution**: A convergence theorem for the matrices. For a strictly convex quadratic, the limit of the matrices is identified (abstract, Springer page).
- **Method links**: none supported by the abstract.

### S020 · Nonconvex minimization calculations and the conjugate gradient method (Numerical analysis 1984, DOI 10.1007/bfb0099521)
**Read**: abstract · **Role**: core (per INDEX; only the abstract was available) · **Coauthors**: none
- **D1 Problem & entry**: Global convergence of conjugate-gradient methods without restarts, for a twice continuously differentiable objective with bounded level sets. The setting assumes exact arithmetic and exact line searches (abstract).
- **D2 Key idea**: Examples with only two variables show that Polak–Ribière gradients can stay bounded away from zero. The same examples show that some variable-metric algorithms need not converge. A global convergence theorem is proved for Fletcher–Reeves (abstract). The construction of the examples was not read.
- **D8 Contribution**: A negative result for Polak–Ribière and for some variable-metric algorithms, alongside a positive theorem for Fletcher–Reeves (abstract). With 766 citations it is the most cited work in this batch after the two RBF surveys.
- **Method links**:
  - Existing candidate *minimal-instance counterexample / smallest-case analysis* ✅. The failure examples have only two variables (abstract).
  - New-pattern candidate: *negative result under the most favourable idealization*. The failure holds even with exact arithmetic and exact line searches, so it cannot be blamed on implementation (abstract).
  - New-pattern candidate: *pair a theorem with an example that marks its boundary*. There is a counterexample for one variant and a theorem for its sibling in the same paper (abstract). S144 is a second instance.
- **Transferable technique** [D4]: To show that a method can fail, grant it every idealization (exact arithmetic, exact line searches, smooth objective, bounded level sets) and build the smallest example that still fails. The negative result then says something about the method itself, not about a sloppy implementation (abstract).
- **Quotes**:
  - "Most of our attention is given to the Polak-Ribière algorithm, and unfortunately we find examples that show that the calculated gradients can remain bounded away from zero." (abstract)
  - "However, a global convergence theorem is proved for the Fletcher-Reeves version of the conjugate gradient method." (abstract)

### S033 · On the global convergence of trust region algorithms for unconstrained minimization (Mathematical Programming 1984, DOI 10.1007/bf02591998)
**Read**: abstract · **Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: none
- **D1 Problem & entry**: Known results give excellent global convergence when the second-derivative approximations are "not too large". The paper asks how large they must be before they prevent convergence, for an objective that is bounded below and continuously differentiable (abstract).
- **D2 Key idea**: Replace the qualitative hypothesis with a quantitative growth rate. The approximations may grow linearly with the iteration number (abstract). The proof was not read.
- **D8 Contribution**: A trust-region convergence theorem under this weaker bound (abstract).
- **Method links**:
  - New-pattern candidate: *quantify a qualitative hypothesis as an explicit growth rate*. The qualitative condition becomes a bound that grows linearly with the iteration number (abstract).
  - Method 2: no direct support. This is trust-region convergence theory in general, and the abstract does not mention RHO and DELTA.
- **Transferable technique** [D3/D4]: When a theorem needs a quantity to stay bounded, ask how fast it may grow before the conclusion fails, and prove the result under that growth rate (abstract).
- **Quotes**:
  - "We consider how large these approximations have to be, if they prevent convergence when the objective function is bounded below and continuously differentiable." (abstract)
  - "Thus we obtain a useful convergence result in the case when there is a bound on the second derivative approximations that depends linearly on the iteration number." (abstract)

### S169 · An application of gaussian elimination to interpolation by generalized rational functions (Rational Approximation and Interpolation: Proceedings of the United Kingdom 1984, DOI 10.1007/bfb0072431)
**Read**: abstract · **Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: T. Håvie
- **D1 Problem & entry**: Compute the value r(ξ) of a generalized rational interpolant p/q at a given point, where the coefficients must satisfy interpolation conditions (abstract).
- **D2 Key idea**: Apply Gaussian elimination to remove the unknown coefficients from the linear system and obtain r(ξ) directly. The intermediate equations turn out to be rational interpolants on subsets of the coefficients and data (abstract).
- **D8 Contribution**: The procedure breaks down only if r(ξ) or the coefficients are not properly defined. The paper proves the structural interpretation and gives one numerical example (abstract).

### S049 · How bad are the BFGS and DFP methods when the objective function is quadratic? (Mathematical Programming 1986, DOI 10.1007/bf01582161)
**Read**: abstract · **Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: none
- **D1 Problem & entry**: To explain gross inefficiencies of BFGS and DFP, study them with unit step lengths on quadratics of only two variables (abstract).
- **D2 Key idea**: The updating formulae imply nonlinear three-term recurrences between the eigenvalues of consecutive second-derivative approximations, and these recurrences are analysed (abstract). The analysis was not read.
- **D8 Contribution**: BFGS may need more than 10 iterations to reach the first decimal place, and DFP is far worse. This explains why DFP is often less suitable than BFGS. Quadratics are informative about efficiency far from the solution, where asymptotic analysis does not apply (abstract).

- **Transferable technique** [D4]: Reduce the method on the smallest non-trivial case (a two-variable quadratic with unit steps) to a recurrence on a few scalars, here eigenvalues. Then use it to count iterations in the pre-asymptotic phase that rate theorems ignore (abstract).
- **Quotes**:

### S040 · A tolerant algorithm for linearly constrained optimization calculations (Mathematical Programming 1989, pp. 547–566 per Crossref, DOI 10.1007/bf01589118)
**Read**: abstract · **Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: none
- **D1 Problem & entry**: Search directions under linear constraints come from two extremes. One takes all constraints into account. The other is an active-set method that treats selected constraints as equations and ignores the rest. Near-degenerate constraints hurt efficiency (abstract).
- **D2 Key idea**: An intermediate rule. Inequality constraints with "small" residuals are treated as inequalities with zero right-hand sides, and the others are disregarded, so small-residual constraints do not restrict the step. The tolerance that defines "small" is chosen and adjusted automatically (abstract).
- **D8 Contribution**: Implementation, convergence and performance. The main theorem needs only a bounded feasible region. The Fortran implementation is much more reliable than the software tested by Hock and Schittkowski (1981), and suits semi-infinite programming with many discretized linear constraints (abstract).
- **Method links**:
  - Method 1 ⚠ variant. The active-set framework is kept and one element is changed, namely which constraints shape the direction. This happens outside the DFO series (abstract).
  - Method 5 ✅. Comparison on an established test-set study, with a stated application niche (abstract).
  - Method 6 ✅. There is a Fortran implementation, and the TOLMIN package report S071 appeared the same year (S071 inferred from title).
- **Transferable technique** [D2]: When the literature offers two extreme rules, define an intermediate one controlled by a tolerance and make the tolerance self-adjusting. Then prove convergence under the weakest assumption you can, here only a bounded feasible region (abstract).
- **Quotes**:
  - "We prefer an intermediate method that treats all inequality constraints with “small” residuals as inequalities with zero right hand sides and that disregards the other inequality conditions." (abstract)
  - "The numerical results indicate that a Fortran implementation of our algorithm is much more reliable than the software that was tested by Hock and Schittkowski (1981)." (abstract)

### S076 · Least squares smoothing of univariate data to achieve piecewise monotonicity (IMA Journal of Numerical Analysis 1991, DOI 10.1093/imanum/11.3.411)
**Read**: abstract (superscripts flattened in the source text) · **Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: I. C. Demetriou
