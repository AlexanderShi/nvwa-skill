# Paper cards · batch a2-02 (abstract / metadata level, 1970–1975)

Batch intent: Catalogue 30 works from 1970–1975 (the Harwell years) that have no open full text or are not research items. The aim is to place them in the research trajectory and to record whatever their abstracts support about Powell's craft: how he turned practice into theory, his counterexamples, rounding-error analysis, and paper-plus-code pairs.
Focus dimensions: D1, D2 and D8 only, each taken from the abstract. Nothing beyond the abstract was read.
Material roles: S011, S015, S021 and S026 are core in INDEX, and the rest are supplement. At this read level every card is a reduced supplement card.

Sources and limits:
- Abstracts come from `references/sources/papers/abstracts.json`. Their origins are listed in `abstract-sources.json`.
- The S096 abstract was rebuilt from the OpenAlex inverted index, so it contains OCR typos ("minimizahon", "studmd") and missing spaces. Quotes from it avoid those spots.
- S015, S028 and S197 have DOIs, but Crossref and OpenAlex return no abstract for them, and OpenAlex marks all three as closed access. Crossref gives their pages: S015 is pp. 31–65 of *Nonlinear Programming*, S028 is pp. 1–27 of *Nonlinear Programming 2*, and S197 is a single page (p. 276) of *Computer-Aided Design* 7(4).
- Crossref title searches found no DOI or abstract for the no-DOI items S011, S053, S065, S066, S098 and S199.
- Web search was unavailable (the session search budget was exhausted) and OpenAlex title search was rate-limited. As a result, no open PDF was located for any work in this batch.
- "(abstract)" marks a claim resting on the abstract text. "(inferred from title)" marks a claim resting only on the title, venue, coauthors or Crossref metadata.

### S032 · On the convergence of the variable metric algorithm (IMA Journal of Applied Mathematics 1971, DOI 10.1093/imamat/7.1.21)
**Read**: abstract · **Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: none
- **D1 Problem & entry**: The entry is a gap between theory and practice. The variable metric (DFP) method was proved successful only for quadratic objectives, yet in practice it worked on many types of function (abstract).
- **D2 Key idea**: Convergence is proved whenever the second-derivative matrix is strictly positive definite for all values of the variables (abstract). The proof technique was not read.
- **D8 Contribution**: A global convergence theorem for DFP on this strictly convex class, plus a superlinear rate (abstract).
- **Method links**:
  - New-pattern candidate: *extend a quadratic-only theorem to the class that practice already covers*. The abstract's order runs from practice (many types of objective function handled successfully) to the limited proof (quadratic only) to the extension (strictly positive definite Hessian) (abstract).
  - Related existing candidate: *numerics → conjecture → proof or counterexample* ✅ (weak). The trigger here is practical success, not a numerical conjecture (abstract).
- **Transferable technique** [D7]: A three-sentence abstract. State (1) where the method is used, (2) the narrow class for which it is proved, and (3) the larger class you prove plus the rate (abstract).
- **Quotes**:
  - "However it has been proved only that the method is successful if the objective function is quadratic, although in practice it treats many types of objective functions successfully." (abstract)
  - "Moreover it is shown that the rate of convergence is super-linear." (abstract)

### S175 · The differential correction algorithm for rational L∞ approximation (Conference on Applications of Numerical Analysis 1971, DOI 10.1007/bfb0069455)
**Read**: abstract (the published item is itself a summary) · **Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: I. Barrodale, F. D. K. Roberts
- **D1 Problem & entry**: The task is best minimax rational approximation on N data points. Cheney and Loeb's original *differential correction* algorithm (ODC) was displaced by their modified version (DC), which is preferred because it has a convergence proof (abstract).
- **D2 Key idea**: Go back to ODC. Show that its convergence is *equally reliable*, and prove that its rate is usually quadratic, whereas DC's is only linear (abstract).
- **D8 Contribution**: Theory plus numerical examples, which confirm that ODC is faster and more accurate. The abstract says this is only a summary and that the full text was submitted to a SIAM journal (abstract). The full version is S048 below.
- **Method links**:
  - Existing candidate *make the theorem admit the practitioner's preferred choice* ✅. The version that works better in practice receives the convergence guarantee that had justified abandoning it (abstract).
  - Method 5 ✅ (weak). The numerical examples compare the two versions directly (abstract).
- **Transferable technique** [D1/D4]: When a modified algorithm replaced an original only because the modified one had a proof, try to prove the original's convergence and rate. Then run both on the same examples (abstract).
- **Quotes**:
  - "The purpose of this paper is to direct attention back to the original algorithm, because in practice ODC seems to be much better." (abstract)
  - "Moreover we prove that the rate of convergence of ODC is usually quadratic, but the rate of convergence of DC is only linear." (abstract)

### S147 · On the convergence of cyclic Jacobi methods (IMA Journal of Applied Mathematics 15 (3), 279-287 1975, DOI 10.1093/imamat/15.3.279)
**Read**: abstract · **Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: K. W. Brodlie
- **D1 Problem & entry**: The cyclic Jacobi eigenvalue method has no convergence proof except under a restriction on the rotation angle (Henrici, 1958) (abstract).
- **D2 Key idea**: Report a real computer calculation in which a cyclic Jacobi method failed because of rounding errors. This shows the angle restriction may be needed in practice. Then give a less severe bound for it (abstract).
- **D8 Contribution**: A documented practical failure, plus a weaker sufficient restriction (abstract). The proof of the new bound was not read.
- **Method links**:
  - Method 4 ✅. Rounding error decides whether an algorithmic safeguard is needed (abstract).
  - Method 5 ✅. An actual failure is reported rather than hidden (abstract).
  - Existing candidate *safeguard designed so the proof goes through* ⚠ variant. Here a proof-motivated safeguard, due to Henrici, turns out to be needed in practice, and the paper relaxes its bound instead of dropping it (abstract). This is the reverse of the existing candidate *question devices that exist only to assist proofs*.
- **Transferable technique** [D5/D4]: When a safeguard seems to exist only for the proof, look for a real computation that fails without it. If one exists, keep the safeguard and weaken its constant as far as the proof allows (abstract).
- **Quotes**:
  - "Now we report an actual computer calculation where a cyclic Jacobi method failed, due to computer rounding errors, so in practice the angle restriction may be needed." (abstract)
  - "A new bound for the angle restriction is given that is less severe than the one proposed originally." (abstract)
