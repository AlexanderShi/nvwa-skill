# Paper cards · batch c03 (core)

Batch intent: Follow NEWUOA from its first public report to the finished software (2002–2004), and set Powell's 2005 account of how he works against it. The batch has four parts. (a) S072 is the Hangzhou progress report: least-Frobenius-norm updating inside a working trust-region code, published with an explicit "not yet final" status. (b) S124 records the rounding-error crisis and the partial factorization of Ω that ended it. (c) S018 is the full NEWUOA description, with every design constant and nine test problems. (d) X001 is the 2005 SIAM oral history. The questions: how does a measured failure (18 months of rounding-error trouble) become a structural fix? How candidly is the status reported at each stage? Do Methods 1–6 survive contact with the most detailed description of NEWUOA? And does what Powell *says* about his process in X001 match what the three papers *do*?
Focus dimensions: D1–D8 (all four are core). Extra weight on D2 (what is kept from UOBYQA and what changes), D4 (linear-algebra stability devices and error-magnitude arguments), D5 (test protocol and how rounding sensitivity and failures are reported), D7 (status reporting), plus a say–do check of X001 against S072/S124/S018.
Material roles: S072 core · S124 core · X001 core (oral history, not a research paper; the dimensions are adapted to *how he says he works*) · S018 core.

Page references use the `[[page N]]` markers of the extracted texts. The three technical papers are DAMTP report versions: S072 = DAMTP 2003/NA03, S124 = DAMTP 2004/NA01, S018 = DAMTP 2004/NA05. Their page numbers are therefore report pages, not journal pages. In these extractions the Greek letters survive, but displayed formulas are broken across lines, and a few words carry a stray hyphenation character. ### S072 · On the use of quadratic models in unconstrained minimization without derivatives (Optimization Methods and Software 2004, DOI 10.1080/10556780410001661450)
**Role**: core · **Coauthors**: none (sole author) · **Position in trajectory**: This is the talk given at the first OMS conference, Hangzhou, December 2002. The report is dated March 2003, revised September 2003 (p. 1). It sits between UOBYQA (Powell 2002a) and the least-Frobenius theory report (Powell 2002b = S045) on one side and NEWUOA (S018) on the other. It is the first paper that couples the least-Frobenius update to an actual unconstrained algorithm. S018 later cites it as Powell (2004b) and says that the details were deliberately withheld from it (S018 p. 3). **Read**: full text, pp. 1–16, including the references.

- **D1 Problem & entry**: The entry has two parts.
  - *Analogy*: with first derivatives, the symmetric Broyden formula fixes the ½n(n−1) degrees of freedom left by the secant condition by minimizing ‖B_{k+1} − B_k‖_F (p. 3). Powell asks whether the same least-change idea works when the only data are m interpolation values of F (p. 5).
  - *Measured limitation*: UOBYQA interpolates at m* = ½(n+1)(n+2) points, so its routine work is of magnitude n⁴ per iteration, "tolerable for n≤25" but prohibitive for large n (p. 7). An intermediate diagonal-Hessian version with m = 2n+1 had been shown at Kyoto in 2001 (p. 7). The least-Frobenius method "was not tried by the author until January, 2002", and its results "seemed to deserve publicity as soon as possible" (pp. 7–8).
  - He also questions a standard device on the way in. Sufficient-decrease acceptance "was introduced to assist proofs of convergence" but has become standard practice, and he keeps simple decrease (1.3) instead (p. 2).
  - Transferable: frame a derivative-free design as the analogue of a known derivative-based device (here symmetric Broyden) and ask which of its properties survive when the gradient conditions become interpolation conditions (pp. 3–5).
- **D2 Core idea & algorithm design**:
  - *Key idea*: each new model minimizes ‖∇²Q_{k+1} − ∇²Q_k‖_F subject to Q_{k+1}(x_i⁺) = F(x_i⁺), i = 1..m, with m in [n+2, m*] (pp. 5–6). When one point moves, the update is Q_{k+1} = Q_k + {F(x_t⁺) − Q_k(x_t⁺)} ℓ_t⁺, where ℓ_t⁺ is the least-Frobenius Lagrange function (1.19)–(1.20) (p. 6).
  - *Kept from UOBYQA*: the rules for Δ_k and ρ (ρ is a lower bound on Δ that is never increased and is cut by about a factor of ten) (p. 8). He also keeps the rule that F is not evaluated when ‖d‖ < ½ρ, because the difference in F may be dominated by rounding errors (p. 8), the test (2.3) that all points lie within 2ρ of the best point (p. 9), and the termination logic (p. 9).
  - *Changed*: UOBYQA's model-adequacy tests relied on models being exact for quadratic F, so they are replaced. The work at the current ρ ends when the last three model errors ε_j are at most ½η_kρ², where η_k estimates the least eigenvalue of ∇²Q_k (p. 9). A geometry step maximizes |ℓ_t⁺(x + d)| within max[0.1Δ, ρ] (2.5) (pp. 9–10). The (m+n+1)×(m+n+1) matrix H = W⁻¹ is held explicitly and updated in O(m²) (pp. 10–11). The point to drop maximizes w_i²|σ_i|, with weights that are cubes of distance ratios (2.16)–(2.17) (p. 12).
  - *Initialization*: x₀ ± ρ_beg e_j, then pair points x₀ ± ρ_beg e_α ± ρ_beg e_β. The initial model has least ‖∇²Q₁‖_F and is built in O(n²) (p. 8).
  - *Provisional part*: x₀ is moved to x*_{k+1} iff ‖d_k‖ ≤ 0.1‖x*_{k+1} − x₀‖. Powell calls this rule "crude and provisional" (p. 12).
  - Transferable: when you move from *model determined by data* to *model updated by least change*, keep the predecessor's outer loop and step-acceptance machinery intact, and rewrite only the tests that relied on the old model being exact (p. 9).
- **D3 Assumptions & model**:
  - The interpolation points must admit a model for any right-hand side, and must not all lie in a hyperplane. So n+1 ≤ m ≤ m* (p. 5).
  - m is restricted to [n+2, m*] because at both ends the update is trivial: m = n+1 gives ∇²Q_{k+1} = ∇²Q_k, and m = m* makes Q_{k+1} independent of Q_k (pp. 5–6).
  - The theorem assumes F quadratic (p. 4). For general C² F, "careful attention has to be given" to the changes in ∇²F (p. 5).
  - He admits that the theory is harder than in the derivative case, because ∇²Q_{k+1} = ∇²Q_k can occur when a change is expected. "Therefore we are going to draw our conclusions from the numerical experiments" (p. 6).
  - The algorithm "is designed to be robust when the objective function is noisy" (p. 7, also p. 8).
- **D4 Analysis & proof devices**:
  - *Theorem (projection)*: if F is quadratic and F ∈ S, then ‖∇²Q_{k+1} − ∇²F‖²_F = ‖∇²Q_k − ∇²F‖²_F − ‖∇²Q_{k+1} − ∇²Q_k‖²_F (1.11). The proof is one line: Q_{k+1} is the orthogonal projection of Q_k onto S in the semi-norm ‖∇²Q‖_F (p. 4).
  - *Consequences*: the Hessian error is monotone (1.12), and the Hessian changes tend to zero (1.13) (p. 4). Through the identity (1.15), (1.13) yields the Broyden–Dennis–Moré condition (1.14) and hence superlinear convergence for quadratic F. This holds even if the Hessian error ‖∇²Q_k − ∇²F‖_F does not become small (pp. 4–5).
  - *Minimal counterexample*: n = 2, m = 4, three collinear points. If the point off the line must be replaced, ℓ_t⁺ is linear and the Hessian does not change at all (1.21) (pp. 6–7).
  - *Linear algebra*: the KKT form gives ∇²ℓ_j = Σ H_sj (x_s − x₀)(x_s − x₀)ᵀ with Σ_s H_sj = 0 and Σ_s H_sj x_s = 0 (2.7)–(2.8) (p. 10). W is nonsingular under conditions (1)–(2) (p. 11). Replacing one point changes W by rank at most two, so the update (2.14)–(2.15) costs O(m²). σ_t = α_tβ_t + τ_t² > 0 "can usually be achieved", because α_t, β_t ≥ 0 in exact arithmetic (pp. 11–12).
  - There is no convergence theorem for the algorithm itself (not attempted; p. 6).
  - Transferable: for a least-change update, prove the Pythagorean identity in the idealized case, then telescope it. The squared changes are summable, so the changes vanish, which feeds a Dennis–Moré-type condition without needing the model error itself to vanish (pp. 4–5).
- **D5 Experiments**:
  - *Scope*: one test problem, the random trigonometric sum of squares (3.1). S and C entries are uniform integers in [−100, 100], θ_j is log-uniform on [0.1, 1], and b is chosen so that F(x*) = 0, giving a Hessian condition number of about 100. The start is x* perturbed by 0.1·(random)/θ_j. ρ_beg = 0.1 and ρ_end = 10⁻⁶ (p. 13).
  - *Design*: n = 10, 20, 40, 80, 160. m ∈ {2n+1, about the geometric mean, m*}, with the larger m values dropped for large n to avoid long runs. Five random instances per row, reporting min–max of #F, final F and time on a Sun Ultra 10 (pp. 13–14).
  - *Candour*: all development was done with n = 3, and the switch "from development to producing numerical results" came less than a week before the conference (p. 12). "Only one test problem with more than three variables has been tried so far" (p. 13). The table was recomputed with a newer version, so it differs from the Hangzhou talk (p. 13).
  - *Failure reported*: for n = 40, m = 861 the range of #F is 3205–11335, which he calls "embarrassing". The counts are sensitive to Fortran changes that are neutral in exact arithmetic. UOBYQA on the same five problems needed 1970–2275 (pp. 14–15).
  - *Headline*: for m = 2n+1 and n = 160, #F ≤ 8208, whereas UOBYQA would need 13041 values just to build its first model. This suggests "at most about 60n" evaluations (p. 15).
  - Transferable: compare a new method against the predecessor's *floor cost*, the evaluations the old method needs before its first iteration (13041 for n = 160), not only against its full runs (p. 15).
- **D6 Positioning**:
  - He continues symmetric Broyden (Dennis–Schnabel 1983) and the superlinear theory of Broyden–Dennis–Moré 1973 (pp. 3–4), and builds on his own UOBYQA and S045 (pp. 7–8).
  - He departs mildly from the Conn–Gould–Toint mainstream by keeping simple decrease. The critique is phrased as history (sufficient reduction "was introduced to assist proofs of convergence"), not as attack (p. 2).
  - The references are eight items, all either classics or his own (p. 16). He thanks John Dennis for comments on the draft (p. 15).
- **D7 Writing & rhetoric**:
  - Dated development narrative: Kyoto 2001, January 2002, Hangzhou December 2002, and "less than one week before leaving Cambridge" (pp. 7–8, 12).
  - Status labels are explicit: "still under development" (p. 8), "crude and provisional" (p. 12), "embarrassing" (p. 14), "not in its final form" (p. 15).
  - The conclusion is hedged ("it may be much more efficient", p. 15). The last sentence is a forecast: "it may become possible to minimize functions of hundreds of variables to high accuracy without the calculation of any derivatives" (p. 15).
  - One theorem with a three-line proof (p. 4); the rest is prose explanation.
  - Transferable: attach a status label to each component of a preliminary algorithm (final, provisional, under development), so readers know which parts they may copy (pp. 8, 12).
- **D8 Contribution & limits**:
  - *Contribution*: algorithmic and empirical. It is the first demonstration that least-Frobenius updating with m = 2n+1 needs #F of magnitude n on a test family up to n = 160 (abstract p. 1; p. 15).
  - *Limits, stated concretely*: rounding errors sometimes damage efficiency severely (p. 8), the x₀ shift is provisional (p. 12), there is one test problem (p. 13), and the n = 40, m = 861 spread is huge (p. 14).
  - *Future*: reduce rounding damage in hard n = 3 cases (p. 13). The number of model parameters may be irrelevant to convergence, so hundreds of variables may be reachable (p. 15).

**Method links**:
- ✅ Method 3 (fewer evaluations than model parameters), origin evidence. This paper introduces the *2n+1 points + least Frobenius change* pairing and gives its first #F-versus-n table (pp. 5–6, 13–15).
- ✅ Method 1 (relax one limitation). The O(n⁴) UOBYQA cost is the named limitation. The outer machinery is kept and only the model update and the tests that depended on it are changed (pp. 7–9).
- ✅ Method 2 (RHO vs DELTA). ρ is a never-increased lower bound on Δ, reduced by about ten. F is skipped when ‖d‖ < ½ρ, and the ρ-completion test is three small model errors (pp. 8–9).
  - ⚠ The constants differ from the released code. Here the error test is ½η_kρ² (p. 9) and a step is "bad" if it gains less than half the predicted reduction (2.2) (p. 8). NEWUOA uses ⅛ρ²·CRVMIN and RATIO 0.1 (S018 pp. 5, 28–29). This supports Method 2's stated limitation that the constants are empirical and were tuned between versions.
- ✅ Method 4 (cost and rounding as part of the algorithm). x₀ is moved to reduce rounding contributions to (2.7) (pp. 10, 12), and F is not evaluated for tiny steps because of rounding (p. 8).
- ✅ Method 5 (honest scope). The embarrassing spread is reported (p. 14) and the status is not final (p. 15).
  - ⚠ Variant: the test set here is a *single* random family (p. 13), narrower than the 1–10 problems of Method 5 step 1. Powell accepts a narrow set for a progress report, with the scope stated.
- New-pattern candidate: *publish preliminary results with explicit status* (pp. 8, 12, 13, 15). The same name appears in c02/S047.
- New-pattern candidate: *judge a model by the steps it produces, not by its accuracy* (p. 5, p. 15). This recurs in S018 p. 3 and X001 p. 11.
- New-pattern candidate: *question devices that exist only to assist proofs* (simple decrease kept, p. 2).
- New-pattern candidate: *tiny explicit counterexample to justify a design choice*. The n = 2, m = 4 collinear case (pp. 6–7) motivates the geometry-keeping feature.

**Transferable techniques**:
- [D4] For a least-change update, prove the orthogonal-projection (Pythagorean) identity in the idealized case. Then use lim ‖change‖ = 0 to obtain a Dennis–Moré-type condition, which gives fast convergence without an accurate model (pp. 4–5).
- [D5] Build a random test family with a known minimizer: choose b so that F(x*) = 0, set the conditioning with log-uniform scale factors, start at x* plus a controlled perturbation, and report min–max over five instances per (n, m) cell (pp. 13–14).
- [D5] Put the predecessor's *startup cost* (evaluations needed to build its first model) next to the new method's total cost (p. 15).
- [D4] Before trusting an update rule, find the smallest configuration where it does nothing (n = 2, m = 4, collinear points) and add a mechanism that prevents it (pp. 6–7).

**Quotes**:
- "because we welcome every decrease that occurs in the value of the objective function." (p. 2)
- "This remark is embarrassing to the author, because he has found that these figures are highly sensitive to changes in the Fortran implementation of the algorithm, even when the changes would not alter the results in exact arithmetic" (p. 14)

### S124 · On updating the inverse of a KKT matrix (Numerical Linear Algebra and Optimization, ed. Ya-xiang Yuan, Science Press 2004, pp. 56–78, no DOI/arXiv; the venue is taken from the reference list of S018 p. 42, and the text is report DAMTP 2004/NA01)
**Role**: core · **Coauthors**: none (sole author) · **Position in trajectory**: Presented at Guilin in October 2003; the report is dated January 2004 (p. 1). It is the fix that turned the S072 algorithm into NEWUOA. The factorization of Ω was introduced in June 2003 (p. 22). NEWUOA itself was released in December 2003 (S018 p. 3). **Read**: full text, pp. 1–25, including all proofs (Lemmas 1–5) and the references.

- **D1 Problem & entry**:
  - The entry is a failure in his own software. The S045 update formula comes with a stability analysis, and "most of the errors … are corrected automatically", so "the author had not expected to write another paper on this subject" (p. 3). But the work "has been frustrated … by occasional huge losses in efficiency due to the effects of computer rounding errors" (p. 3).
  - The problem is stated abstractly. W is a KKT matrix: symmetric and nonsingular, with a conditionally positive definite leading n̂×n̂ block and a zero trailing m̂×m̂ block. Its inverse H is needed explicitly, and only one of the first n̂ rows and columns changes per iteration (p. 1).
  - He widens the audience: the situation "does not arise in algorithms for general constrained optimization" (SQP changes X by full rank, p. 2). Even so, "our results may be of interest to many researchers in mathematical programming" (p. 2).
  - Transferable: when an implementation fails for numerical reasons, restate the failing kernel as a self-contained linear-algebra problem (here, updating the inverse of a KKT matrix after one row and column change) and write it up for that wider audience (pp. 1–2).
- **D2 Core idea & algorithm design**:
  - *Key idea*: XΩ = 0 implies rank Ω ≤ n̂ − m̂. So Ω is stored as Z S Zᵀ, a sum of exactly n̂ − m̂ rank-one terms with s_j = ±1, which "guarantees this property, even in the presence of computer rounding errors" (pp. 3, 14).
  - *Kept*: the S045 updating formula (2.12), which is "the first updating formula of Section 4 of Powell (2002b)" (p. 7), and its w-form (3.8), which is preferred because β̂ and τ̂ are smaller and α̂, β̂ ≥ 0 (p. 13). Also kept: choosing t by computing σ for every candidate in O(m²) (pp. 12–13).
  - *Changed*: the storage of Ω. Z is updated by Givens rotations (5.4) that zero e_tᵀz_j for all but one column (or two, if the signs differ). The last column is then given in closed form (5.13)/(5.14) (pp. 17–19). Hyperbolic rotations (5.5) are avoided, because they fail when |e_tᵀa| = |e_tᵀb| and their use "tends to magnify any errors" (p. 20). Lemma 5 offers two formulae, chosen by the sign of β, to avoid cancellation (pp. 20–22).
  - *Origin shift*: the threshold changes from S072's 0.1 to ‖d‖ ≤ 10^{−3/2}‖x* − x₀‖, because each shift costs O(m²n) (p. 12).
  - *Rejected*: modifying β to keep σ away from zero (p. 9). The device σ̂ = max[α̂, 0]·max[β̂, 0] + τ̂² was "highly unfavourable" (p. 24).
  - Transferable: if a matrix should have a known rank or a known zero block in exact arithmetic, store it in a factored form whose *shape* forces that property, rather than correcting violations after they appear (pp. 14, 17).
- **D3 Assumptions & model**:
  - A_ij = ½(x_iᵀx_j)² has no negative eigenvalues, and the interpolation points are chosen so that W is nonsingular. Hence W is a KKT matrix (p. 4).
  - The theorem of §2 needs H_tt ≠ 0 and condition (2.3) (pp. 5–6); these are later removed analytically (p. 7).
  - The stability analysis of §3 assumes that the current iteration's calculation is exact while H carries old errors (p. 10).
  - The rounding analysis is done on one explicit configuration (n = 2, m = 5) and then generalized to a cluster of radius η at distance ξ (pp. 11, 16).
  - All s_j should be +1 in exact arithmetic; negative signs arise only from rounding (p. 20).
- **D4 Analysis & proof devices**:
  - *Theorem*: two rank-one steps through the singular matrix H̃ = H − He_te_tᵀH/H_tt. Nonsingularity is proved by a null-space argument (pp. 5–6). Eliminating H̃ analytically removes the division by H_tt and gives (2.12) with σ = αβ + τ² (p. 7).
  - *Lemma 1*: σ = det W⁺ / det W. The proof writes W⁺ = (I − e_tuᵀ)W(I − ue_tᵀ) + βe_te_tᵀ and uses the determinant product rule, after a continuity argument that assumes τ ≠ 0 without loss of generality (pp. 7–8). This gives a geometric meaning to *choose t with large |σ|* (p. 12).
  - *Lemma 2*: changing β alters only the t-th diagonal entry of (H⁺)⁻¹ (p. 9).
  - *Stability property (3.1)*: an exact update sets the t-th row and column of H⁻¹ − W to zero and leaves every other error unchanged. Errors in the first m rows and columns are wiped out once each point has been replaced, but errors in the bottom-right (n+1)×(n+1) block persist for ever (p. 10). He illustrates this with S045's Test 5 (10⁵ iterations, m = 101, n = 50): initial errors of about 10⁻³ drop to about 10⁻¹⁵ in replaced rows, but remain about 10⁻³ in the bottom-right block (p. 10).
  - *Closed-form rounding example*: the points (3.2) form a cluster of radius η at distance ξ. The explicit H (3.3) shows an error in σ of order (ξ/η)⁸ε, so "all accuracy may be lost … if we pick η≤0.01ξ" (pp. 11–12).
  - *Lemma 3*: if Ω = ZSZᵀ with only n̂ − m̂ columns, then the bottom-right m̂×m̂ block of H⁻¹ is exactly zero. The proof uses cofactors: the first n̂ rows of each relevant minor have rank at most n̂ − 1 (p. 14).
  - *Error reduction*: computing Σ s_j(z_jᵀŵ)² instead of ŵᵀΩŵ lowers the error in σ̂ from (ξ/η)⁸ε to (ξ/η)⁶ε. The reason is that XZ = 0 makes z_jᵀŵ = O(ξ²) even though its terms are O(ξ⁴η⁻²) (4.10)–(4.12) (pp. 15–16).
  - *Lemmas 4–5*: explicit factor-update formulae, proved by matching coefficients of the six outer-product terms (pp. 18–22).
  - Transferable: to see where rounding error enters an update, build the smallest configuration with one tunable geometric ratio (cluster radius η versus distance ξ), compute every term's magnitude in closed form, and let the resulting power of ξ/η set the practical threshold (here the origin shift) (pp. 11–12).
- **D5 Experiments**:
  - One table only, Table 1 (p. 23). The problem is the trigonometric sum of squares (6.1) with n = 40 and five random instances. Five methods are compared: UOBYQA (m = 861), Powell (2003) [= S072's algorithm] with m = 81 and m = 861, and NEWUOA with m = 81 and m = 861. Runs are in double precision on a Sun Ultra 10 (pp. 22–23).
  - *Control device*: with m = ½(n+1)(n+2) the least-Frobenius update is redundant in theory, so the Powell (2003) code "should be similar to that of UOBYQA". The gap (3205–11335 versus 2028–3035) therefore measures rounding damage, and NEWUOA with m = 861 (1996–2216) closes it (pp. 23–24).
  - *Diagnostics*: the signs of diag(Ω) were monitored. In Problem 1 there were 10473 updates, three negative signs appeared, and they survived for 8, 58 and 3 iterations (p. 24).
  - *Attribution*: the time advantage of NEWUOA over Powell (2003) at m = 81 (37 s against 62 s) is attributed mainly to truncated CG replacing an O(n³) subproblem solver, not to the factorization (pp. 23–24).
  - "Other experiments … are not reported here" (p. 23).
  - Transferable: choose one experimental configuration in which the new and old methods coincide in exact arithmetic. Any difference there is pure rounding damage (pp. 23–24).
- **D6 Positioning**:
  - He faces "the standard advice of many numerical analysts" to factorize W rather than update its inverse, and explains why inverses are hard: H is then a huge low-rank part plus an important small part (p. 25).
  - He declines to test the alternative: "He does not intend to do so", because NEWUOA performs well (p. 25).
  - Novelty is claimed modestly: "As far as the author knows" (p. 2).
  - The references are five items: Dennis–Schnabel, Fletcher, and three of his own (p. 25).
- **D7 Writing & rhetoric**:
  - §6 is a research-history narrative: "three of the main stages of the research of the author in the last five years" (p. 22). The update "performed brilliantly" (p. 22). The software effort was "frustrated for 18 months" (p. 22). Attempts to match UOBYQA "failed miserably" (p. 24). "With the benefit of hindsight" (p. 24).
  - He states his release habit explicitly (p. 22).
  - Abandoned work is kept in print as a lemma: Lemma 2 is labelled an "interesting result" of a technique "now … abandoned" (p. 9).
- **D8 Contribution & limits**:
  - *Contribution*: a linear-algebra technique (the partial factorization of Ω and its O(n̂²) update), a stability theorem (Lemma 3), and an error-magnitude analysis. Together they made NEWUOA possible (pp. 14–22, 24).
  - *Limits*: negative s_j can still occur through rounding (pp. 20, 24). Parameter repairs are not recommended even when Ω⁺ will clearly have a negative eigenvalue (p. 25). Factorizing W instead of H was not investigated (p. 25).
  - *Future*: none planned. Research on least-Frobenius updating "may be nearly complete" (p. 25). NEWUOA is free from the author by e-mail (p. 22).

**Method links**:
- ✅ Method 4 (cost and rounding error as part of the algorithm), strongest evidence in the batch. The whole paper exists because rounding made a correct algorithm inefficient (p. 3). The fix is chosen so that a structural property survives floating point (p. 14), and the O(m²n) cost of origin shifts is weighed against rounding damage (p. 12).
- ⚠ Method 4 variant (step 3, *named repair routines triggered by symptoms*). Powell *rejects* symptom-triggered repairs of β or σ: "if a need for the modification of parameters is detectable, then substantial errors must have occurred already" (p. 25). He relies instead on the self-correcting stability property (3.1) plus a representation that cannot lose the invariant (pp. 10, 25). Method 4 should distinguish *geometry repairs* (kept: BIGLAG and BIGDEN in S018) from *arithmetic patches* (rejected here).
- ✅ Method 1 (relax one limitation). The three stages UOBYQA → Powell (2003) → NEWUOA each remove one measured problem: O(n⁴) work, then rounding inefficiency (pp. 22–24).
- ✅ Method 3. The (m+n+1)-unknown system replaces ½(n+1)(n+2) parameters, and each update costs O((m+n)²) (pp. 4–5).
- ✅ Method 5. There is a control configuration (m = m*) and runtime monitoring of a theoretical invariant (pp. 23–24), and unreported experiments are declared (p. 23).
- ✅ Method 6. "Whenever the author has discovered techniques of this importance … he has developed Fortran software", and NEWUOA is free (p. 22).
- New-pattern candidate: *store factors so a theoretical invariant survives rounding* (pp. 3, 14). Related to c06's *preserve a structural invariant at the cost of information*.
- New-pattern candidate: *exact-arithmetic-equivalence control run* (m = m* must match UOBYQA, pp. 23–24). It also appears as the permutation reruns in S018 (pp. 32, 36) and the implementation-change remark in S072 (p. 14).
- New-pattern candidate: *self-correcting maintained basis* / rely on self-correction rather than patches (pp. 10, 24–25). Same name as in c01/S068.
- New-pattern candidate: *publish the rejected alternative* (Lemma 2 and the σ̂ device, pp. 9, 24). Same name as in c05.
- New-pattern candidate: *tiny explicit counterexample to justify a design choice*. The n = 2, m = 5 example sets the origin-shift threshold (pp. 11–12).

**Transferable techniques**:
- [D2] If exact arithmetic guarantees rank r or a zero block, store the matrix as ZSZᵀ with exactly r columns, so the property holds in floating point by construction. Update Z with Givens rotations plus one closed-form column (pp. 14, 17–19).
- [D4] Prove that the denominator of your rank-two update equals det W⁺/det W, so *maximize |σ|* becomes *keep the system far from singular* (Lemma 1, pp. 7–8, 12).
- [D4] Analyse error growth on a two-parameter geometric example (cluster radius η, distance ξ). Derive the power of ξ/η in the error of each term and set shift thresholds from it (pp. 11–12, 15–16).
- [D5] Run the new code in a configuration where it must equal the old one in exact arithmetic. Any difference measures numerical damage (pp. 23–24).
- [D5] During test runs, monitor a sign or rank invariant (diag Ω ≥ 0), and report how often it fails and for how many iterations each violation lasts (p. 24).

**Quotes**:
- "Whenever the author has discovered techniques of this importance to practical algorithms on previous occasions, he has developed Fortran software that makes the discoveries available for general use." (p. 22)
- "if a need for the modification of parameters is detectable, then substantial errors must have occurred already that require attention." (p. 25)

### X001 · An interview with Michael J. D. Powell (SIAM oral history, conducted by Philip Davis, 6 April 2005) (SIAM History of Numerical Analysis and Scientific Computing / Computer History Museum 2005, no DOI/arXiv)
**Role**: core (oral history, not a research paper; D1–D8 are adapted to *how he says he works*) · **Coauthors**: interviewer Philip Davis (p. 1) · **Position in trajectory**: April 2005, after the NEWUOA release (December 2003) and the NEWUOA paper (S018, November 2004). Powell says that "until last December" his research was "on a particular technique for minimization without derivatives", and that he has since moved to radial-basis-function questions (p. 13). He had then been retired for three and a half years (p. 3). This is two years after the Vicente interview (c02/X002), and it allows a say–do check against S072, S124 and S018. **Read**: full transcript, pp. 1–23.

- **D1 Problem & entry** (how he chooses problems):
  - *Avoid crowding*: he prefers uncrowded fields, where contributions "are more likely to be valuable" (p. 22). He takes "a very dim view of important fields, in quotations", because special funding polarizes research effort (p. 22).
  - *Neglected niche*: optimization without derivatives is "very much neglected … because most people in optimization assume that first and second derivatives are available" (p. 22).
  - *Application pull*: engineers use simulated annealing "too often". He wants "more powerful methods that are just as easy to use", and he gives that as the reason he "thought a lot recently" about optimization without derivatives (pp. 20–21).
  - *Origin*: at Harwell he "tried to improve on current techniques, which was often possible because those techniques were very primitive" (p. 7). He prefers "to specialize … and think in detail about particular parts of mathematics" (p. 7).
  - Transferable: justify a topic by naming a primitive method that practitioners actually use (simulated annealing) and a sophisticated method they avoid because it is hard to use. The research target is the gap between the two (pp. 20–21).
- **D2 Core idea** (the research loop he describes):
  - Ideas come "just by thinking about questions". His work is "heavily slanted towards developing algorithms". An algorithm that does not behave as expected gives "a basis of an idea", which he then tries to explain (p. 17).
  - Theorems follow numerics. Current convergence questions are "absolutely crystal clear from numerical results", and the difficulty of the proofs is "frustrating" (p. 17).
  - For RBF boundary effects: "You can find out answers easily by numerical calculations, and I’m trying to explain some of these theoretically, and this is only in very simple cases" (p. 13).
  - Transferable: log every run where the algorithm "doesn’t behave in the way I expect" as a candidate research question, and try to explain it before tuning it away (p. 17).
- **D3 Assumptions & stance on proof**:
  - "I don’t think people who develop algorithms should wait to provide theoretical support. If they get good results in practice, that’s the important thing" (p. 17).
  - His examples of successful algorithms without good theoretical justification are DFP and the conjugate direction method (p. 17).
  - He traces this to Harwell, where the important thing "was to provide good computer software that gave results" (p. 18). He knows when convergence has not been proved and is "very happy to go ahead and say that theoretical questions are still open" (p. 18).
- **D4 Analysis** (how he explains why methods work):
  - On quasi-Newton: DFP "doesn’t generate a very accurate second derivative matrix, but it does pick up the information that it needs automatically" (p. 11). Accurate curvature needs n steps, but convergence usually comes "in far fewer steps", so the Hessian approximation never becomes good (p. 11). This is the same explanation he gives for least-Frobenius updating in S072 (p. 5) and S018 (p. 3).
  - He names an open explanatory gap: BFGS beats DFP, "And the reason for it has never really been explained properly" (p. 10).
  - He works on the smallest instances first: RBF edge effects are studied "only in very simple cases" (p. 13).
- **D5 Experiments & practice advice**:
  - *Local minima*: all his methods "could be trapped by local minima". The usual advice is "trying a range of starting points and see what happens" (p. 12). But users often blame local minima wrongly, when the method "simply isn’t suitable for the objective function" (p. 12).
  - *Other methods*: simulated annealing is "a horrible method" (p. 12).
  - *Scope*: his software is "hardly ever applied for more than about two or three hundred variables". Difficulty does not track n: some problems with fewer than ten variables are hard, and some with millions are easy (p. 21).
  - Transferable: in a stalled-run diagnosis, test *method unsuited to this objective* as an alternative hypothesis before accepting *local minimum* (p. 12).
- **D6 Positioning, credit and collaboration**:
  - *Credit*: on DFP, the F and P contribution was "just finding Bill Davidon’s report and hardly changing it at all, running more examples, writing it up differently", so that one "could almost describe it as plagiarism" (p. 10).
  - *Being overtaken*: DFP was overtaken by BFGS (p. 10), and HSL by NAG, IMSL and MATLAB (p. 8).
  - *Collaboration*: fewer than half of his papers are coauthored (p. 14). He does not put his name on students' dissertation papers, and has published with about a third of his 18 students (p. 14). He was a coauthor with Iserles only because Iserles insisted (p. 15).
  - *Working alone*: "I’d quite like to crack them myself" (p. 14). Visitors should work on their own project, with contact "maybe once a month" (p. 15).
- **D7 Writing & communication**:
  - Duty to publish: "if you do research in mathematics you have a responsibility to write papers on your discoveries, but its mathematics itself that I prefer" (p. 15).
  - Usability is part of the product. Quasi-Newton is not "chippified" in packages because the user must still specify line-search accuracy (p. 11). Better methods need to be "just as easy to use" as primitive ones (p. 20).
  - He writes about half as many papers at Cambridge as at Harwell (p. 19).
  - On when a problem is solved: "I would hedge and say define the problem" (p. 22).
- **D8 Self-assessment & limits**:
  - *Pride*: starting the Harwell Subroutine Library, "one of the first libraries of collections of numerical algorithms" (p. 8).
  - *Admitted failure*: he could not build numerical analysis at Cambridge. Efforts to appoint a lecturer were "futile for more than ten years" (p. 16).
  - *Regret*: "I regret very much the success of primitive methods" (p. 20).
  - *Agenda*: too many outstanding questions, so he has to be "selective" (pp. 20–21). He would not expect saturation to be a reason to leave a field (p. 23).
- **Say–do check** (X001 against S072/S124/S018):
  - ✅ "don’t delay publication while waiting for proof" (p. 18). S072 is published without a convergence theorem, with conclusions drawn from experiments (S072 p. 6).
  - ✅ "doesn’t behave in the way I expect … basis of an idea" (p. 17). The VARDIM misbehaviour led to the Q_int modification (S018 pp. 31–32), and the rounding failures led to the Ω factorization (S124 p. 3).
  - ✅ Working alone (p. 14). All three technical papers are sole-authored, and acknowledgements name only a few discussants (S072 p. 15; S018 pp. 31, 41).
  - ✅ Scope of a few hundred variables (p. 21). S018 tests up to n = 160 and release testing went up to 200 (S018 pp. 30–31).

**Method links**:
- ✅ Method 5 (honest scope), stated side. Scope is given in n ("two or three hundred variables", p. 21), with multiple-start advice for local minima (p. 12).
- ✅ Research Heuristic 5 (local minima: several starts), evidence (p. 12).
  - ⚠ Refinement: Powell adds that slow runs are often wrongly blamed on local minima when the method is unsuitable (p. 12). This belongs in Workflow B step 3.

- New-pattern candidate: *judge a model by the steps it produces, not by its accuracy* (DFP explanation, p. 11). The same argument appears in S072 p. 5 and S018 p. 3.
- New-pattern candidate: *publish algorithms without waiting for proof; state open questions* (pp. 17–18).

### S018 · The NEWUOA software for unconstrained optimization without derivatives (Large-Scale Nonlinear Optimization 2006, DOI 10.1007/0-387-30065-1_16)
**Role**: core · **Coauthors**: none (sole author) · **Position in trajectory**: Presented at the 40th Workshop on Large Scale Nonlinear Optimization, Erice 2004. The report is dated November 2004 (p. 1). It is the definitive description of NEWUOA, which was released in December 2003 (p. 30). It combines S045 (the update), S072 (the first algorithm) and S124 (the factorization) with the details that S072 had withheld (p. 3). It is the direct source of the constants quoted in SKILL.md Method 2. **Read**: full text, pp. 1–42, including the appendix (proofs of Lemmas 1–3, pp. 37–41) and the references.

- **D1 Problem & entry**:
  - A quadratic model has ½(n+1)(n+2) parameters, and that many evaluations is "prohibitively expensive in many applications with large n" (p. 2). So models are built from m ∈ [n+2, ½(n+1)(n+2)] interpolation conditions, with m = 2n+1 recommended (pp. 2, 4).
  - The paper exists to deliver what the progress report withheld. Powell "resisted pressure from the editor and referees" of S072 to describe the algorithm in detail, because of occasional instabilities. After the Ω factorization, "the remaining development … became straightforward", and this paper gives the details (p. 3).
  - Dates: discovery in January 2002, software finished in December 2003 after 18 months of rounding trouble (p. 3).
- **D2 Core idea & algorithm design**:
  - *Why the update works* ("win/win"): if |F(x_new) − Q_old(x_new)| is small, the model predicted well even with a poor ∇²Q. If it is large, interpolating the new value improves the model significantly (pp. 2–3). This rationale was found "with hindsight", after O(n) evaluation counts had been observed (p. 3).
  - *Architecture*: a 15-box flowchart (Figure 1, p. 5). The outline "applies also to the UOBYQA software", apart from m and the update (p. 3).
  - *ρ and Δ*: ρ is a lower bound on Δ. Its purpose is "to maintain enough distance between the interpolation points", limiting damage "when there are substantial errors in each computation of F" (p. 4). Δ follows the RATIO rule (7.1) with thresholds 0.1 and 0.7, and is set to ρ when the intermediate value is ≤ 1.5ρ, "to sharpen the test in Box 10" (p. 27). The ρ schedule (7.6) uses 16ρ_end and 250ρ_end, with the geometric-mean step chosen to balance two final reductions (p. 28).
  - *Short steps*: if ‖d‖ < ½ρ, F is often not computed (p. 7). The Box 14 test ends the work at the current ρ when at least three new F values have been computed for it and, on the three most recent of those iterations, ‖d‖ ≤ ρ and |Q − F| ≤ ⅛ρ²·CRVMIN (pp. 28–29).
  - *Geometry*: if the farthest point has DIST ≥ 2Δ, it is replaced by a step from BIGLAG (maximize |ℓ_t|) (pp. 6, 20–23). BIGDEN (maximize |σ|, a quartic handled as a degree-4 trigonometric polynomial in θ) is called only if |σ| ≤ 0.8τ², which is "very rare" (pp. 23–26). BIGLAG's radius is max[min{0.1·DIST, 0.5Δ}, ρ], justified by three stated considerations (p. 23).
  - *Linear algebra*: H = W⁻¹ with Ω stored as ZSZᵀ (pp. 11–15). The constant term of Q and the (m+1)-th row and column of H are dropped. This gains accuracy "by diverting attention from actual values of F and x … to the changes" (pp. 15–16). ∇²Q is held implicitly as Γ + Σγ_j(x_j − x₀)(x_j − x₀)ᵀ, which gives O(mn) Hessian–vector products (pp. 16–17).
  - *Subproblem*: truncated CG (Fletcher–Reeves, no preconditioner because the norm is Euclidean), with empirical 10⁻² truncation tests, then a two-dimensional boundary search (pp. 17–20).
  - *Choosing the point to drop (MOVE)*: maximize w_t|σ_t| with w_t = max[1, (‖x_t − x*‖/max[0.1Δ, ρ])⁶]. The sixth power is argued from |σ_t| = O(Δ⁴/‖x_t − x_opt‖⁴) and deliberately strengthened (pp. 27–28).
  - *Origin shift*: if ‖d‖² ≤ 10⁻³‖x_opt − x₀‖² (p. 29).
  - *Initial points*: for m > 2n+1, the extra points use signs σ_p chosen from the first 2n+1 values, which biases them "towards smaller values of the objective function". The initial Ξ, Υ and Z are then given in closed form (pp. 8–11).
  - *Post-release fix*: after VARDIM, if RATIO ≤ 0.01 and ‖∇Q_int(x₀)‖ ≤ 0.1‖∇Q(x₀)‖ on three consecutive iterations, Q is replaced by the least-‖∇²Q‖_F interpolant Q_int (pp. 31–32).
  - Transferable: give the whole algorithm as a numbered flowchart that is shared with the predecessor, then devote one section to each box. Readers see at once what is inherited and what is new (pp. 3, 5).
- **D3 Assumptions & model**:
  - F can be evaluated for any x (p. 1).
  - ρ_beg should make the initial values informative "especially when the computations may include some spurious contributions that are larger than rounding errors". ρ_end is the required accuracy in x (p. 4).
  - The method, "in particular the use of the bound ∆≥ρ", is "intended to be suitable" for functions with first-derivative discontinuities (p. 33).
  - The rows of X must be linearly independent (p. 2).
  - He admits that an infinite loop with ρ fixed "may happen in theory", and that finite precision bounds it in practice (p. 7).
  - No convergence theorem is claimed; none appears anywhere in §§1–8 or the appendix (pp. 1–41).
- **D4 Analysis & proof devices**:
  - *KKT structure*: ∇²D = Σλ_j(x_j − x₀)(x_j − x₀)ᵀ with Σλ_j = 0 and Σλ_j(x_j − x₀) = 0, which gives the (m+n+1) system (3.10) (p. 9).
  - *Lagrange link*: ℓ_t(x) = (He_t)ᵀw(x), so τ = ℓ_t(x_opt + d). The denominator therefore depends on d through the Lagrange function (p. 21). For m = m* the determinant ratio is exactly ℓ_t (p. 22, citing Powell 2001).
  - *Stability (4.13)*: each update zeroes the t-th row and column of the error W − H⁻¹. The factorization guarantees the zero bottom-right block (4.14) "even in the presence of computer rounding errors" (p. 13).
  - *Origin-shift error magnitudes*: with ‖x_opt − x₀‖ = M, the error in β is O(M⁸ε). The form (4.26) reduces it to O(M⁶ε·cos²θ), and the experiments set the threshold at 10^{2.5} (p. 29).
  - *Appendix*: Lemmas 1–3 prove the closed-form initial Ξ, Υ and Z. The devices are Lagrange functions as columns of W⁻¹, divided differences (A.4)/(A.5), and checking the constraints (3.7) for λ = ZZᵀr (pp. 37–41).
  - *Complexity*: O((m+n)²) per iteration, except for O(m²n) origin shifts (pp. 12, 30). Measured average work is close to O(mn) (p. 30).
  - *Self-critique*: BIGDEN's choice of s₁ "ignores the objective function σ" (p. 24).
- **D5 Experiments**:
  - *Problems*: nine (p. 1). VARDIM (Buckley 1989); TRIGSSQS (random trigonometric sum of squares); TRIGSABS (its nonsmooth |·| version); SPHRPTS (points on a sphere, "invented by the author recently"); ARWHEAD, CHROSEN and PENALTY1–3 (Buckley, Conn et al.) (pp. 30–36).
  - *Grid*: n ∈ {20, 40, 80, 160} and m ∈ {2n+1, m(av), ½(n+1)(n+2)}, averaged over five random instances. Metrics are #F and ‖x_fin − x*‖_∞. Dashes mark runs that were not attempted because of running time (pp. 33–34).
  - *Rounding-sensitivity protocol*: the variables are reordered, which "makes no difference" in theory, and both results are reported (VARDIM p. 32; PENALTY3 p. 36). PENALTY3 at n = 160 gives #F = 629582, F = 25447.688 in one ordering and #F = 16844, F = 0.001002 in the other, "due entirely to computer rounding errors" (p. 36).
  - *Failures shown*: "?" marks #F > 500,000 (PENALTY2 and PENALTY3, n = 160) (p. 36). PENALTY2 at n = 160 is "too difficult in REAL*8 arithmetic" (p. 36).
  - *Effort profile*: for SPHRPTS, the index of the first F ≤ 1.001·F(x_fin) is printed beside #F. "most of the work is spent on marginal improvements" (pp. 34–35).
  - *Complexity check*: time/(n²·#F) is roughly constant, 8.0–9.3×10⁻⁶ for ARWHEAD and PENALTY1 across n = 20..160 (p. 37).
  - *Headline*: with m = 2n+1, #F falls below the number of degrees of freedom of a quadratic for large n (TRIGSSQS n = 160: 6013 on average) (p. 33).
  - *Provenance*: the VARDIM modification came from discussing problems with Nick Gould at Erice, and all later tables were recomputed with the modified code (pp. 30–31).
  - *Baselines*: there is no external solver. Comparisons are only across m and between NEWUOA versions (Tables 1–5, pp. 32–36).
  - Transferable: report two numbers per run, the total evaluations and the evaluation at which F first came within 0.1% of its final value. The gap shows how much of the budget went to marginal improvements (pp. 34–35).
- **D6 Positioning**:
  - He builds on symmetric Broyden (Dennis–Schnabel pp. 195–198, p. 2), truncated CG (Conn–Gould–Toint 2000; Fletcher–Reeves 1964, pp. 17–18) and his own chain: Powell 2001, 2002, 2003 and 2004a/b/c (pp. 41–42).
  - Test problems are credited to Buckley (1989) and Conn et al. (1994), and the extra experiments to discussion with Nick Gould (pp. 30–31, 35).
  - He questions his own design choice openly: "the author has had doubts about the use of the explicit inverse matrix" (p. 37).
- **D7 Writing & rhetoric**:
  - Every numerical constant is printed: 0.1, 0.7, 1.5ρ, ⅛, 16, 250, 10⁻³, 0.8, 1.1, 10⁻² and 50 grid points. Some get a reason (pp. 23, 27–28), and others are labelled plainly: "The implementation of this aim is empirical" (p. 19).
  - Hedging is moderate: "seems to be suitable in practice" (p. 23), "Rough answers" (p. 30).
  - The development history is candid: "The work was very frustrating" (p. 37).
  - The paper ends with a free-software invitation (p. 37).
  - Transferable: for every magic constant, either give a one-line reason (a balancing equation, a magnitude estimate) or write "empirical". Never leave it unlabelled (pp. 19, 27–28).
- **D8 Contribution & limits**:
  - *Contribution*: a software-level algorithm description with complete details, the closed-form initialization with proofs, the post-release Q_int modification, and evidence that #F grows roughly like n for m = 2n+1 (pp. 1, 31–33, 37).
  - *Limits, stated concretely*: results are "still highly sensitive to computer rounding errors" (p. 37). Two failures occur at n = 160 (p. 36). An infinite loop is possible in theory (p. 7). BIGDEN has a criticized step (p. 24). The explicit inverse is doubted (p. 37).
  - *Conclusion*: "the least Frobenius norm method for updating quadratic models is highly successful" (p. 37).
  - *Future*: none listed beyond inviting requests for the code (p. 37).
