## What stayed constant (from the full texts)

## Anatomies of two classic works (moved from SKILL.md)

These two anatomies were in `SKILL.md` after the deep-reading pass and were moved here to keep the skill file short; `SKILL.md` keeps a pointer to them. They show Methods 1, 4, 5, 7 and Heuristic 10 before the DFO period.

#### Anatomy: A FORTRAN subroutine for solving systems of nonlinear algebraic equations (AERE Harwell report R 5947, 1968; Harwell routine NS01A)
Rests on the full report [S019]; the listing pages were read through their comments only.

| Dimension | Content |
|---|---|
| Origin | Harwell's remit to write subroutines others could use [R007 p. 5; X002 p. 3]; Newton-type methods fail when the Jacobian becomes singular [S019 p. 5]. |
| Why then | Broyden's rank-one update, and his advice to drop line searches: one function value per iteration [S019 p. 9]. |
| Key insight | A step bound Δ adjusted by the ratio of actual to predicted reduction, with a dogleg step that keeps Levenberg–Marquardt's bias of short steps towards steepest descent at O(n²) cost [S019 pp. 18–23]; Broyden updates at O(n²) per iteration [S019 pp. 9, 23–25]. |
| Minimal evidence | Rosenbrock's equations in 27 evaluations against 30–90, on the competitors' own problems and counts [S019 pp. 35–39]; a no-solution case and a badly scaled system [S019 pp. 39–42]. |
| Abandoned paths | Line searches [S019 p. 9]; Levenberg–Marquardt's exact path, O(n³) [S019 p. 19]. |
| Reception | 777 Google Scholar citations (scholar.md). The memoir says the dog-leg "enabled the construction of a convergence proof" [R007 p. 6]. |
| Methods shown | Methods 1, 2 (ancestor), 4, 5, 6, 7 (a safeguard kept so that a theorem applies [S019 p. 16]) |

#### Anatomy: A rapidly convergent descent method for minimization (with R. Fletcher; The Computer Journal 6(2):163–168, 1963, DOI 10.1093/comjnl/6.2.163)
Rests on the full paper [S001], Powell's later statements [X001; X002] and the memoir [R007].

| Dimension | Content |
|---|---|
| Origin | Davidon's 1959 report "has been little publicized" [S001 p. 1]; Powell and Fletcher had reached it independently and pooled their work [R007 pp. 6–7]. Powell later called the paper "mainly a description of work by Davidon" [X002 p. 6]. |
| Why then | *Inference, with its basis*: computers had just made 100-variable tests feasible [S137 p. 4; S001 p. 5; R007 p. 7]. |
| Key insight | Quadratic termination as the filter on competing methods [S001 p. 1]: keep Davidon's variable-metric core, change two named things, so that H equals G⁻¹ after n exact line searches on a quadratic [S001 pp. 1–3]. |
| Minimal evidence | A random trigonometric sum of squares with n from 5 to 100; five runs reached a different minimum and stay in the table, marked [S001 p. 5]. |
| Abandoned paths | Powell's own 1962 method, slower and more expensive per iteration [S001 pp. 1, 3]. |
| Reception | 7118 Google Scholar citations, Powell's most cited work (scholar.md). Overtaken by BFGS [X001 p. 10]. |
| Methods shown | Methods 1, 3 (precursor), 5, 7; Heuristic 10 |

## After 2015: what happened to the codes (verified)
- **Custodianship.** Powell had asked Zaikun Zhang and Nick Gould to maintain his solvers [secondary: PRIMA README]. In his last months he chose to "lend finishing touches to software" [R007 p. 24].
- **PDFO** (Ragonneau & Zhang): cross-platform MATLAB/Python interfaces wrapping Powell's Fortran. "PDFO: a cross-platform package for Powell's derivative-free optimization solvers", Math. Program. Comput. 16:535–559 (2024), DOI 10.1007/s12532-024-00257-9. PDFO is Chapter 3 of Ragonneau's PolyU thesis [secondary: PRIMA README; https://github.com/pdfo/pdfo].
- **PRIMA** ("Reference Implementation for Powell's methods with Modernization and Amelioration"): started by Zhang in July 2020 on the basis of PDFO. The modern Fortran version was finished by December 2022, with C, Python, MATLAB and Julia interfaces. It is mathematically equivalent to Powell's code except for intentional bug fixes and improvements, and faithfulness is checked by differential testing. Cite as Zenodo DOI 10.5281/zenodo.8052654 [secondary: https://github.com/libprima/prima].
- **SciPy 1.16.0** replaced the F77 COBYLA under `scipy.optimize.minimize` with PRIMA's Python translation [secondary: PRIMA README].
- **Other translations:** NLopt (C, close to f2c style), Py-BOBYQA (NAG; a "true translation … with significant improvements"), R `minqa` [secondary].

## Pivots and their triggers (analysis)
| Pivot | Trigger (evidence) | Type |
|---|---|---|
| Derivative-based (1960s–80s) → derivative-free model-based (1990s–2015) | *First pass, speculation:* the 2007 essay's premise that "the vast majority of unconstrained calculations do not employ any derivatives" [primary fragment]. *Now documented:* IMSL wrapped his gradient-based TOLMIN with differences because customers would not supply derivatives; he was unhappy about that and about the popularity of simulated annealing and genetic algorithms; a four-variable, ten-constraint helicopter problem led to COBYLA [S029 pp. 1–3; S014 pp. 2, 45] | Demand-driven |
| Linear (COBYLA) → quadratic (UOBYQA) | *First pass, speculation:* linear models converge slowly near a solution. *Now documented:* COBYLA's testing "gave favourable results when most of the freedom in the variables is taken up by active constraints" but exposed "some severe inadequacies when second derivative terms are important" [S068 p. 4]; a well-conditioned simplex far from the solution can give a linear-model step pointing away from it [S029 p. 9] | Accuracy-driven |
| UOBYQA → NEWUOA | UOBYQA's O(n⁴) work, n ≤ 20 [primary]; measured: 20 → 1087 seconds from n = 20 to 40, prohibitive at about n = 50 [S045 p. 3] | Cost-driven |
| Explicit Ω → factored Ω (inside NEWUOA) | Occasional huge losses in efficiency from rounding errors over eighteen months [S124 pp. 3, 22] | Stability-driven |
| NEWUOA → BOBYQA → LINCOA | Constraint class widened one step at a time [primary: READMEs]; NEWUOA's success with 320 variables encouraged bounds [S036 p. 6] | Scope-driven |
