# Paper cards · batch s01 (supplement)

Batch intent: Follow Conn's constrained-optimization toolkit (slack variables, penalty functions, augmented Lagrangians) across three decades and three kinds of paper. The batch has a 1994 CGT note that repairs the linear algebra of LANCELOT, two IBM team papers (vision 2016; AutoML 2019/2020) that carry augmented-Lagrangian or ADMM splitting into machine-learning problems, and a posthumous 2023 penalty formulation of logical constraints that answers a question Conn asked in 1981. It tests Method 5 (structure before black box) and Method 6 (transplant proven machinery). It also checks whether the IBM-era team papers keep the standards of Method 1 (theory–code–test) and Method 2 (test bed).
Focus dimensions: the supplement set: D1, D2, D3, D4 (brief), D5 (one line) and D8. D6 lineage notes are added where a paper itself names Conn's earlier work (S057, S141).

### S089 · A note on exploiting structure when using slack variables (Mathematical Programming 1994, DOI 10.1007/bf01582214)
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: N. Gould, Ph. L. Toint · **Position in trajectory**: LANCELOT period of the CGT trio. It is Report 92/18, revised 19 November 1993, with Conn at IBM T.J. Watson (p. 2). It was written after the LANCELOT book (Conn et al. 1992a, cited p. 4) as a short fix-note on the linear algebra in LANCELOT's bound-constrained solver SBMIN (pp. 4, 8). **Read**: full text (pp. 1–10, including references; the whole note is short).

- **D1 Problem & entry**: The entry is a weakness in the authors' own code.
  - Setting: problem (1.1) has inequalities l_y ≤ c(x) ≤ u_y and bounds. Slacks y convert it to equalities (1.2), which are solved by sequential minimization of the augmented Lagrangian (1.3). The simple bounds are kept out of Φ (p. 3).
  - Weakness: SBMIN treats slacks "in the same way" as the problem variables. When m ≫ n, as for a semi-infinite constraint discretized at many parameter values, the linear algebra then involves matrices of order O(n+m) (p. 4).
  - Claim: handling the slacks explicitly reduces this to O(n) (p. 4).
- **D2 Core idea & algorithm design**:
  - Structured Hessian approximation (1.7): B approximates the Lagrangian Hessian, and the term ρ[AᵀA, −Aᵀ; −A, I] is built from first derivatives (p. 4).
  - Factorization: in the Newton system restricted to the free variables (3.3), the block-triangular factorization (3.4) zeros the upper-right block. The same step cancels the A_{Iy,Ix} terms in the upper-left block (p. 7).
  - Payoff: the intermediate solves (3.5)–(3.7) need no inversion. Only (3.8) has to be factorized, an n_i × n_i system with matrix B_{Ix,Ix} + ρA_{Ay,Ix}ᵀA_{Ay,Ix}. The slack step is then recovered explicitly as q_{Iy} = µλ^b_{Iy} + A_{Iy,Ix}p (3.9) (p. 7).
  - Iterative solvers: any p_{Ix} that satisfies the reduced descent condition (3.10) gives, through (3.9), a descent direction for the full model (3.11) (p. 8).
  - Kept: the augmented-Lagrangian outer loop, the quadratic model (2.3), the base point (the generalized Cauchy point in trust-region methods, p. 5) and the projected search back into the model-feasible region (p. 7). Changed: only the linear algebra.
- **D3 Assumptions & model**:
  - The active variables are fixed at the base point for the iteration. How the active set is chosen is explicitly left out of scope (p. 6).
  - All derivative information is evaluated at (x, y), although the model is minimized from the base point (p. 6).
  - A convex model needs B_{Ix,Ix} + ρA_{Ay,Ix}ᵀA_{Ay,Ix} to be positive definite (pp. 7, 8).
  - An infinity-norm trust region is called convenient because it aligns with the bound box (p. 5).
- **D4 Analysis (brief)**: There is no theorem. The note has two devices and a cost analysis.
  - Exact cancellation: block elimination whose Schur complement after the m_i slack pivots is exactly the matrix (4.1), so the ρA_{Iy,Ix}ᵀA_{Iy,Ix} terms cancel in exact arithmetic (pp. 7–9).
  - Descent lift: the full directional derivative equals the reduced one minus µ‖λ^b_{Iy}‖² (3.11). Ψ is convex with Ψ(0) = 0, so any descent method started at 0 on Ψ, such as truncated CG, yields the reduced condition (3.12)–(3.13) (p. 8).
  - Costs of ignoring the structure (pp. 8–9):
    1. The system has dimension n_i + m_i instead of n_i.
    2. A symbolic factorization cannot see the exact cancellation. It then over-reserves space, may "fail for lack of space", and spends flops on values that should be zero.
    3. Iterative methods do more work per iteration and face a wider spectrum, by eigenvalue interlacing, which slows CG.
    4. Finite termination comes after n_i + m_i steps rather than n_i.
  - The authors say other derivations are possible and that this one was chosen to show the saving (p. 7).
- **D5 Experiments**: None. The gain is stated as a belief: LANCELOT should improve considerably when m ≫ n, bringing the work per iteration to the level of barrier and Lagrangian-barrier methods that treat inequalities directly (p. 9).
- **D8 Contribution & limits**:
  - Contribution: algorithmic and software-engineering, namely linear algebra for a released solver.
  - Limits: no numbers; the active-set strategy is deferred to Gill et al. and Bertsekas (p. 6).
  - Positioning: slacks are standard in LP (Dantzig, Murtagh, Chvátal) and NLP (Murtagh–Saunders) (p. 3). The same trick has "apparently" been used in MINOS but was not publicized (p. 4).
  - Stated future: exploit the structure in LANCELOT (p. 9).
  - The acknowledgment thanks a referee whose report was almost as long as the manuscript (p. 9).

**Method links**:
- Method 5 ✅ (evidence): the structure of the slack variables is exploited in the linear algebra instead of treating all variables alike (pp. 4, 7). The Hessian approximation (1.7) keeps the known first-derivative structure and approximates only the second-order terms (p. 4).
- Method 1 ⚠ (variant): the note ties algebra to the authors' own code (SBMIN options, p. 8) but reports no numbers, and the performance claim is a stated belief (p. 9). It reviews one's own released package; it is not a full triad.
- new-pattern candidate: **Self-audit note on one's own solver**. Publish a short note that names a specific inefficiency in your own released code (SBMIN, pp. 4, 8) and gives the fix.
- new-pattern candidate (batch-level): **Split by type, give each block its natural treatment**. Here slack variables are separated from problem variables (pp. 4–7). The same move appears in S032 and S141 (see Cross-card notes).

**Transferable techniques**:
- When a block of auxiliary variables enters the Hessian only through an identity block (slacks), factor the system block-triangularly. The auxiliary unknowns are then eliminated in closed form, only the Schur complement in the original variables is factorized, and the auxiliary step is recovered by formula (3.4)–(3.9) (p. 7).
- To justify an inexact inner solver in a reduced space, compute the full directional derivative of the recovered step. Show it equals the reduced one plus a nonpositive term. Then any descent method started at 0 on the reduced convex quadratic is safe (3.10)–(3.13) (p. 8).
- When arguing for a reduction, give its effect on four separate costs: dimension, memory in the symbolic factorization (exact cancellations are invisible to symbolic analysis), Krylov convergence (eigenvalue interlacing after block elimination) and the finite-termination count (pp. 8–9).

**Quotes**:
- "However, if slack variables are handled explicitly, we shall show that the linear algebra need only involve matrices of order O(n)." (p. 4)
- "The description given here is intended to emphasize the reduction in computational effort possible when the structure inherent in the linear system (3.3) is properly exploited." (p. 7)

### S057 · Removing clouds and recovering ground observations in satellite image sequences via temporally contiguous robust matrix completion (Proceedings of the IEEE Conference on Computer Vision and Pattern Recognition 2016, DOI 10.1109/cvpr.2016.301; arXiv 1604.03915)
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: J. Wang (University of Chicago), P. A. Olsen and A. C. Lozano (IBM T.J. Watson). Conn is third of four authors (p. 1). · **Position in trajectory**: IBM-era application paper. SKILL.md already cites the satellite-imagery paper (CVPR 2016) as practice evidence for Method 4. **Read**: full text of arXiv v1 (pp. 1–10, including references).

- **D1 Problem & entry**: The entry is an application limit and a named failure of the standard tools.
  - Cloud contamination largely limits the usefulness of optical satellite images (p. 1).
  - Earlier cloud-removal methods need a cloud mask or non-optical sensors (p. 1).
  - Matrix completion (MC) and robust MC (RMC) need uniformly or weighted sampled observations, and every image must keep some cloud-free pixels. They fail when whole frames are covered, as happens in tropical regions (pp. 2, 4; Fig. 3).
  - Problem statement: recover a 4-way tensor (latitude × longitude × colour × time), reshaped to an mn × ct matrix, from the cloud-free entries Ω (p. 3).
- **D2 Core idea & algorithm design**: The method has two stages (Fig. 1, p. 2).
  - Detection: threshold the dark channel (the minimum over RGB) at γ (eq. 1). A post-processing step keeps "always white" pixels as stationary background, using the median pixel vector and a kNN search to recover cloud-free instances (Alg. 1, p. 3). An SVM classifier was tried and was, in the authors' experience, typically no better than thresholding (p. 3).
  - Ideal model: minimize rank subject to at most κ1 disagreements on Ω and a temporal squared-difference budget κ2 (p. 3).
  - Convex surrogate: this ideal model is replaced term by term by TECROMAC, ‖P_Ω(Y−X)‖₁ + λ1‖X‖_* + (λ2/2)‖XR‖²_F, where R is a discrete temporal-derivative matrix (eq. 2, p. 4).
  - Table 1 shows TECROMAC as the only option that has low rank, robustness and continuity together (p. 4).
  - Solvers: an augmented Lagrangian method (ALM) on the split Y = X + E (eqs. 3–7), with one of two inner schemes (pp. 4–6):
    1. Inexact proximal gradient. Each ALM iteration makes one X update, by singular-value thresholding (eq. 8), and one E update, by soft thresholding (eq. 9).
    2. An SVD-free alternating gradient scheme on X = UVᵀ, using ‖X‖_* = min ½(‖U‖²_F + ‖V‖²_F) (eq. 10, Alg. 3, p. 6).
- **D3 Assumptions & model**:
  - The modelling assumptions are stated as intuitions: low rank (few land types), robustness (sparse detector errors) and temporal continuity (p. 3).
  - The majorizer needs cI ⪰ AAᵀ = H (p. 5).
  - The text states no convergence or recovery guarantee for TECROMAC or either algorithm (none found).
  - The white-background post-processing is called "primitive" and cannot handle heavy snow (p. 3).
- **D4 Analysis (brief)**: There are no theorems.
  - Majorize–minimize: Q(X, X̂) = F(X) + (c/2)‖X−X̂‖²_F − ½‖(X−X̂)A‖²_F ≥ F(X). Completing the square turns the X-step into SVT applied after a gradient step (p. 5).
  - Nuclear-norm factorization identity for the SVD-free scheme (p. 6).
  - Footnote 3 contrasts this ALM with the optimizers' version. Here both the multipliers and the penalty are updated at every iteration (µ^{r+1} = ρµ^r). Optimizers would update one or the other, depending on feasibility versus stationarity, citing CGT 1991 [10] (p. 5).
- **D5 Experiments**: The setup (pp. 6, 8):
  - Clouds are added synthetically to three background-modelling videos (Escalator, Lobby, Watersurface), scored by relative reconstruction error.
  - Baselines are MC, RMC, interpolation and TECMAC, which drops the ℓ1 robustness term and serves as an ablation.
  - One fixed setting is used throughout: γ = 0.6, λ1 = 20, λ2 = 0.5 (Table 2).
  - IPG and ALT are compared for accuracy and time at rank 20 (Table 3, p. 6).
  - Real MODIS data (181 images of 400 × 400) are judged visually only (p. 8).
- **D8 Contribution & limits**:
  - Contribution: a model, two scalable algorithms and an application (pp. 1, 8). The conclusion claims superior performance on simulated and real data (p. 8).
  - Limits: the detector is heuristic and fails under snow (p. 3), and the real-data evaluation is visual. No future work is stated (p. 8).
  - Positioning: the paper derives the model from RPCA and matrix completion. It dismisses applying RPCA or SPCP directly because they "usually fail" under dense cloud, "as we have observed in practice" (pp. 2, 4).

**Method links**:
- Method 6 ✅ (evidence) with a ⚠ variant: augmented-Lagrangian machinery, citing CGT 1991 (p. 5), is carried into robust matrix completion. The variant is that the paper uses the ML-style ALM, which updates multipliers and penalty every iteration, and flags in a footnote how optimizers would do it (p. 5).
- Method 4 ✅ (weak evidence): an application-first paper published in the users' venue (pp. 1, 8). The text contains no account of a user or partner, so the user's-door part of Method 4 is not evidenced here.
- Method 5 ✅ (weak evidence): known structure (low rank plus temporal smoothness) is built into the model instead of using a generic recovery method (pp. 3–4).
- Method 2 ✗ (contradiction of its standard; also Taste warning signs 3 and 6): the evidence is three synthetic videos, one visually judged real case and one fixed parameter setting, yet the conclusion claims superior performance (pp. 6, 8). Authorship caveat: Conn is a middle author, so this evaluation standard cannot be attributed to him.

**Transferable techniques**:
- Write the ideal combinatorial model first (rank objective, a counting budget κ1, a smoothness budget κ2). Then replace each nonconvex piece by its convex surrogate, one term at a time, so the reader sees what each regularizer stands for (pp. 3–4).
- Use a property table (method × modelling assumption) to show which baselines violate which assumption (Table 1, p. 4).
- Include your own model minus one term as a baseline (TECMAC = TECROMAC without ℓ1) to justify that term (p. 8).
- Derive a proximal step for a quadratic plus nuclear-norm objective from the majorizer Q(X, X̂) with cI ⪰ H and completion of the square (p. 5).

**Quotes**:
- "Mathematical optimizers would only update one or the other" (p. 5)
- "Tropical regions for example can be completely covered by clouds as often as 90% of the time." (p. 4)

### S032 · An ADMM based framework for automl pipeline configuration (Proceedings of the AAAI Conference on Artificial Intelligence 2020, DOI 10.1609/aaai.v34i04.5926; arXiv 1905.00424)
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: S. Liu and P. Ram (equal contribution), D. Vijaykeerthy, D. Bouneffouf, G. Bramble, H. Samulowitz, D. Wang, A. Gray, all at IBM Research AI. Conn is eighth of nine authors (p. 1). · **Position in trajectory**: a late IBM-era team paper in machine learning. DFO appears as one named sub-solver option, citing the Conn–Scheinberg–Vicente 2009 book (p. 4). **Read**:
- Main text: pp. 1–9 in full.
- Appendices read: App. 4 (CMAB, pp. 13–14), App. 5 (solver swap, pp. 14–15), App. 9 (sensitivity, p. 20), App. 10 (baselines and parity, p. 21), App. 13–15 (adaptive ADMM, splitting benefit, artificial objective, pp. 24–27).
- Appendices skimmed: App. 1–3 (derivations and BO, pp. 10–13).
- Not read beyond headings: App. 6–8 and 11–12 (data table, search space, ensembles, per-dataset plots, fairness metric), and App. 16 beyond its opening (TPOT pipelines).

- **D1 Problem & entry**:
  - AutoML pipeline configuration (joint algorithm selection and hyper-parameter optimization, "CASH") is recast as mixed continuous–integer black-box nonlinear programming. Two challenges are named: the coupling between algorithm choice and hyper-parameters, and an objective with no analytic form or gradients (p. 1).
  - A second entry comes from deployment, regulatory and fairness requirements: latency, memory, an upper bound on the false-positive rate, and disparate impact. These enter as black-box constraints (p. 2).
  - Penalty-in-objective treatments are dismissed as sensitive to the penalty choice and unable to guarantee feasibility (p. 3).
- **D2 Core idea & algorithm design**:
  - Reformulation (1): explicit Boolean z_i with 1ᵀz_i = 1 per module, and continuous and integer hyper-parameters kept separate (p. 2).
  - A continuous surrogate f̃ evaluates f at the projection onto the integer set (4). A consensus equality θ̃^d = δ lets ADMM split the problem (5)–(7) (p. 3).
  - Each ADMM iteration solves three subproblems (Alg. 1, p. 4):
    1. θ-min: black-box HPO over only the active set of continuous variables, fewer than 15 and typically fewer than 10, against |θ| ≈ 100 (fn. 2, p. 4). It can be solved by BO, direct search or trust-region DFO (p. 4).
    2. δ-min: projection and rounding in closed form (p. 4).
    3. z-min: a combinatorial bandit solved by Thompson sampling with probabilistic rewards (p. 4; Alg. A1, p. 13).
  - Black-box inequalities g_i ≤ ε_i become equalities with bounded slacks u_i ∈ [0, ε_i] inside the augmented Lagrangian (13)–(14), Alg. 2 (pp. 4–5).
  - Rationale: the number of black-box evaluations needed grows with dimension, typically O(n) to O(n³), citing Larson–Menickelly–Wild (p. 3).
  - The paper presents a framework to "mix and match" solvers (p. 5). Adaptive ADMM increases the subproblem effort at each outer iteration (p. 7).
- **D3 Assumptions & model**:
  - No convergence guarantee: ADMM theory needs convexity or smoothness, which AutoML violates, and no baseline has a guarantee either (p. 5).
  - Conditional hyper-parameters are flattened into extra algorithm choices (p. 2).
  - The cheap artificial objective lists the assumed properties of the real one (p. 27):
    - deterministic;
    - nonconvex and possibly discontinuous;
    - conditional dependence on z;
    - small changes in θ give small changes in f;
    - each module depends on the output of the previous one.
- **D4 Analysis (brief)**: There are no theorems. The subproblem derivations are in App. 1–2 (pp. 10–12, skimmed). The key device is the projection-based surrogate plus the consensus equality (4)–(5), which makes an operator splitting over mixed variables well defined (p. 3). The slack reformulation (13) keeps the subproblems almost unchanged when constraints are added (pp. 1, 4).
- **D5 Experiments**: The setup and results (pp. 5–8, 20–21, 25–27):
  - Main comparison: 30 binary-classification datasets (UCI, OpenML, Kaggle); objective 1 − AUROC on a fixed 80/20 split; 1 hour, 10 trials.
  - Baselines: Auto-sklearn without ensembles or meta-learning, TPOT with population 50, and random search. All share the same search space and run single-threaded. Results are shown as average rank over time (pp. 5–6; App. 10, p. 21).
  - Constrained case: Kaggle Home Credit, with latency and disparate-impact constraints. The constrained method (CST) is compared with an unconstrained run whose results are filtered afterwards (UCST) (pp. 6–7).
  - Splitting ablation against joint BO (JOPT) on 8 datasets, up to 153× speedup (Table 1, p. 8).
  - Sensitivity to ρ and f̂ (p. 20) and a cheap artificial objective (pp. 25–27).
  - Two inconsistencies:
    - p. 1 says Auto-sklearn was best on 27% and TPOT on 20% of datasets, but p. 6 gives ASKL 6/30 (20%) and TPOT50 8/30 (27%).
    - The Fig. 2 caption has d_I = 0.07 where the text says d_I = 0.7 (p. 6).
- **D8 Contribution & limits**:
  - Contributions: a formulation, an ADMM framework, what the paper calls the first AutoML framework to handle general black-box constraints explicitly, and empirical evidence (p. 1).
  - Limits:
    - no convergence theory (p. 5);
    - experiments on binary classification only (fn. 3, p. 5);
    - no single sub-solver is best across datasets (App. 5, p. 15);
    - the comparison is acknowledged to favour TPOT, whose search space is larger (p. 21).
  - The conclusion states no future work (p. 7).

**Method links**:
- Method 5 ✅ (evidence): structure is exploited before black-box search. Boolean, continuous and integer variables are separated, each block gets its natural solver, and only the active set enters the black-box HPO (pp. 2–4). The rationale given is DFO's dimension-dependent cost in evaluations (p. 3).
- Method 6 ✅ (evidence): augmented Lagrangian and operator splitting are carried into AutoML. The slack plus augmented-Lagrangian treatment of black-box inequality constraints, (13)–(14) (pp. 4–5), mirrors the slack plus augmented-Lagrangian setting of S089 (p. 3 there). The DFO book is cited as a sub-solver option (p. 4).
- Method 1 ⚠ (variant): code and numbers without a convergence theorem, and the paper says so openly (p. 5).
- Method 2 ⚠ (variant): public datasets, an explicit parity protocol, 10 trials and ablations meet the spirit of Method 2. But the cost unit is wall-clock time, not evaluations (p. 6; p. 21), and a cheap artificial objective is added to isolate optimizer overhead (p. 27).
- new-pattern candidate: **Split by type, give each block its natural treatment** (see Cross-card notes).

**Transferable techniques**:
- To split a mixed-integer black-box problem, define the continuous surrogate f̃(θ̃) = f(P_D(θ̃)) and add a consensus variable δ ∈ D with θ̃ = δ. ADMM then alternates a continuous black-box solve with a closed-form projection (pp. 3–4).
- Turn black-box inequality constraints g_i ≤ ε_i into equalities with bounded slacks u_i ∈ [0, ε_i] and put them in the augmented Lagrangian, instead of adding penalty terms to the objective (pp. 3–5).
- State benchmark parity explicitly (p. 21):
  - use the same search space and single-threaded runs;
  - disable baseline pre- and post-processing;
  - assign the worst score until a method's first result;
  - say which baseline the setup favours.
- Build a cheap artificial objective that matches a listed set of properties of the real problem, to separate optimizer overhead from evaluation cost (p. 27).
- Show a constrained method is needed by running the unconstrained optimizer and filtering its results post hoc (UCST vs CST) (p. 6).

**Quotes**:
- "We highlight that our ADMM based scheme is not a single AutoML algorithm but rather a framework that can be used to mix and match different existing black-box solvers." (p. 5)
- "these schemes are very sensitive to the choice of the penalty function and do not guarantee feasible solutions." (p. 3)

### S141 · The continuous quadrant penalty formulation of logical constraints (Open Journal of Mathematical Optimization 2023, DOI 10.5802/ojmo.28)
**Role**: supplement · **Coauthors**: S. Cafieri, M. Mongeau (ENAC, Université de Toulouse) (p. 2) · **Position in trajectory**: posthumous. The paper is dedicated "In memory of our dearest friend Andy Conn" (p. 2); it was submitted 14 April 2022 and revised 17 July 2023 (p. 1). It is a companion to Cafieri–Conn–Mongeau (EJOR 2023, [5]) on the same air-traffic application (pp. 10, 12). The paper traces its own lineage (pp. 3–5):
- Conn's 1971 thesis and his 1973 SINUM ℓ1 exact penalty paper;
- Pietrzykowski, Conn's advisor;
- Conn's 1981 NATO question;
- Gamble–Conn–Pulleyblank (1991) and Conn–Mongeau (1998), cited on p. 5.

**Read**: full (pp. 1–13, including references).

- **D1 Problem & entry**:
  - Logical constraints are usually modelled with a binary variable. A conditional constraint (t(x) > 0 implies f(x) ≥ 0) is equivalent to an alternative (t ≤ 0 or f ≥ 0). The binary goes into a big-M formulation (3), whose large constants cause numerical instability, or a complementary formulation (5), which is nonlinear (pp. 2–3).
  - The question is stated in the abstract's first line: can continuous optimization handle logical constraints efficiently? (p. 2)
  - It is framed explicitly as an answer to Conn's 1981 question about penalty functions in discrete or global optimization (pp. 4, 13).
  - Tight relaxations of disjunctive programs are declared out of scope (p. 3).
- **D2 Core idea & algorithm design**:
  - Map the constraint to (t, f) ∉ S, where S is the open fourth quadrant (8)–(10), and penalize only membership in S (p. 4).
  - Name the properties a penalty needs (pp. 4, 6):
    - "S-violation dipstick": zero outside S and positive in S (Def. 1, p. 4);
    - "leans outwards S": every descent direction from a point of S leaves S after a finite step (Def. 5, p. 6);
    - "S-repulsive": a dipstick that leans outwards and is continuous (Def. 6, p. 6).
  - Narrow the design space with impossibility results before constructing anything:
    - no convex dipstick exists when the complement of S is nonconvex (Prop. 2, p. 4);
    - no two-piece piecewise-linear dipstick exists unless S is a half-space (Prop. 3, p. 5).
  - The three-piece piecewise-linear g_α = max(0, min(t, −f/α)) is unique up to scaling (Prop. 4, p. 5) and S-repulsive (Prop. 7, p. 6). It is not smooth, and squaring it does not smooth its ridge (p. 7).
  - A smooth piecewise quadratic needs at least four pieces (p. 7). The family g_β with β > 1 is then constructed (Prop. 8, p. 7; proof pp. 8–9).
  - Unlike classical penalties, g_β is not a monotone function of the distance to the feasible set (p. 2; Remark 2, p. 9). It approaches g_α² as β → 1 (Remark 3, pp. 9–10).
  - β = 3 is recommended because it spreads the breakpoints evenly along a transverse line (Remark 4, p. 10).
  - Only the logical constraints are penalized. All other constraints go to a standard NLP solver (p. 4).
- **D3 Assumptions & model**:
  - t and f are real-valued functions of x (p. 3).
  - Both the penalty (Prop. 2) and the feasible set are nonconvex, so a local solver can stop at a local infeasibility (p. 11). A general guarantee would need global optimization (p. 2).
  - Each logical constraint gives a 2-D model. Rescaling t and f changes the slope of the symmetry axis (Remark 1, p. 9).
- **D4 Analysis (brief)**: The proofs are elementary and constructive. Devices:
  - a direct contradiction with convexity (Prop. 2, p. 4) and a frontier argument (Prop. 3, p. 5);
  - uniqueness from the Conn–Mongeau 1998 result that, for a continuous piecewise-linear function, the jump in gradient across a ridge is a multiple of the ridge normal (Prop. 4, p. 5);
  - "leans outwards" by case analysis on the active gradient piece, with an explicit threshold step γ̄ = −t̄/d̄1 or −f̄/d̄2 (Prop. 7, p. 6; Prop. 8, p. 9). In case b on p. 6 the text prints γ̄ = f̄/d̄2 without the minus sign, either a sign slip or an extraction loss;
  - positivity of the central concave piece by restricting to the transverse line f = t − 2X and evaluating the concave quadratic at the endpoints of its interval, which gives 4X²/(β+1)² > 0 (pp. 8–9);
  - C¹ smoothness by matching the gradients (15) on the four boundary lines (p. 9);
  - a counterexample: the ℓ1 penalty for an ellipse constraint does not lean outwards (p. 6).
- **D5 Experiments**: Aircraft conflict avoidance with speed and heading changes (pp. 10–12, Table 1).
  - Instances: 35 Random Circle Problems with n = 10, 20 and 30 aircraft (10, 10 and 15 instances).
  - Model: minimize Σ g_β(t^m_ij, f^m_ij) subject to (22)–(28), with β = 3.
  - Solver: AMPL with IPOPT 3.12, tolerance 1e-5, random multistart.
  - Results: zero penalty was reached on all 35 instances, and 2 instances needed a second start. Mean CPU times were 0.23, 1.05 and 3.68 s, with a maximum of 20.06 s.
  - There is no direct comparison with MINLP. In [5], these upper bounds let a 3-phase branch-and-bound prove global optimality on the hardest instances, which the MINLP approach alone does not solve within 600 s (p. 12).
- **D8 Contribution & limits**:
  - Contribution: a modelling device with proved properties, plus one application (p. 12).
  - Limits:
    - only feasibility problems are solved; weighting the penalty against an original objective is left for future work (p. 13);
    - solutions are local (p. 12);
    - one application only.
  - Future: other problems in which logical constraints would need too many binary variables (p. 13).
  - Lineage (D6): Pinar–Zenios smoothing is rejected because it applies only to convex functions (p. 7). Conn–Mongeau's decomposable-PL approach does not apply because g_α is not decomposable at the origin (pp. 6–7).

**Method links**:
- Method 6 ✅ (strong evidence; this is the paper's own framing): Conn's penalty-function machinery (1971 thesis, 1973 ℓ1 penalty, 1981 question) and the Conn–Mongeau piecewise-linear theory [11] are carried into logical, that is discrete, constraints (pp. 3–5, 13).

**Transferable techniques**:
- Before constructing a function with required properties, prove short impossibility results (no convex one, no two-piece one, at least four pieces) that fix the minimal complexity. Then construct the minimal object and prove uniqueness where possible (pp. 4–7).
- Encode a disjunction as the complement of an open set in the 2-D image (t(x), f(x)) and penalize only membership in that set (pp. 4, 11).
- Name the behavioural property you need from a penalty ("leans outwards": every descent direction leaves the bad set after a finite step). Prove it by case analysis on the gradient pieces with an explicit threshold step (pp. 6, 9).
- Prove properties of a 2-D piecewise function by restricting it to a family of transverse lines and studying a univariate piecewise quadratic. Use the same restriction to choose the parameter (β = 3 spreads the breakpoints evenly) (pp. 7–10).
- When the global optimum of a nonconvex penalty model is known (zero at feasibility), report how often multistart was needed as the failure measure (p. 11).
