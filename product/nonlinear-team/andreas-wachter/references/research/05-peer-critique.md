### A1. The 2000 counterexample: what it shows is itself contested

The example (Wächter & Biegler, *Math. Program.* 88(3):565–574, 2000, DOI 10.1007/PL00011386) is min x₁ s.t. x₁² − x₂ − 1 = 0, x₁ − x₃ − 0.5 = 0, x₂, x₃ ≥ 0. His own reading, in the thesis: "most of the current interior point NLP algorithms in the line search category as well as some trust region methods fail on this innocuous example" (thesis p. 51) [stated, P]. Critics read it in at least four different ways.

**What the episode shows about his craft** [inferred]: a minimal, well-posed counterexample on which his own code failed became the shared benchmark of a whole subfield. Rivals were invited to "solve the Wächter-Biegler problem", and they did so with heuristics (LOQO), trust regions (KNITRO), penalty-barrier methods (Chen–Goldfarb, Tits et al.) and one-phase schemes (Hinder–Ye). His own standard for judging those fixes was "is there a global convergence analysis?" (thesis pp. 53–54), not "does it solve the example?".

### A4. The two-phase design: restoration phase and infeasibility detection

This is the most **persistent and quantified** line of criticism, 2014–2018. It targets the design choice he defended in the thesis: a filter main phase plus a separate feasibility-restoration phase. His own 2002 caveat was: "the restoration phase is the only step where the filter line search method can fail, and therefore inherits all the difficult cases" (thesis p. 160, quoted in 02).

## B. How he answers criticism (the pattern across A1–A11)

These moves are supported by the evidence above. 03 §3 covers his handling of *user* bug reports and is not repeated.

1. **Judge a rival fix by whether it comes with theory, not by whether it solves the example.** He says so of BSV's heuristics twice: "no global convergence analysis is given" (thesis pp. 53–54; RC 23149 p. 1). [stated, P]
2. **Concede where the rival is better, and name the mechanism.** "SNOPT probably doesn't have any problems with this … has better ways to handle degeneracies" (2004). [stated, P]
3. **Check the critic's settings before accepting the verdict.** In the same 2004 reply he asks about a termination tolerance of 1e-1, the augmented-Lagrangian option instead of the default filter, scaling turned off, and a large Jacobian relaxation. In 2005, on a Lancelot comparison: "The fact that Ipopt runs out of iterations could be due to giving it incorrect Hessian information." [practice, P]
4. **Absorb a good rival idea as an option, not as the default.** Examples: the inertia-free curvature test (3.12.4, default off); adaptive μ (default monotone); `expect_infeasible_problem` (default off). [practice, P] [inferred] This keeps the published algorithm and the default behaviour of the code identical, at the price of leaving improvements unused by default.
5. **Recruit the critic.** Tits et al. (2003, co-author after the critique; 01/03). He later co-authored with Nocedal/Waltz (a KNITRO-side school) and with Curtis (penalty-steering school), and those papers are his answers to the inertia and linear-algebra critique (A5a). [practice, P]
6. **When the weakness is structural, change the method class instead of patching the IPM.** Warm starts led to active-set hot starts (2015) and conic SQP (2024) (A8). [practice, P]
7. **Ask for the failure before the user leaves.** "before you abandon Ipopt because it doesn't seem to work well, please give us a chance to see if we can fix problem or improve the code." (11 Sep 2003) [stated, P]
8. **State the limit plainly in user-facing text.** Stationary points, not minimizers (2009). LICQ is assumed and "could be violated in practice" (2007). Warm starts are "very difficult" (2012). [stated, P]

## E. Era and resource context of the critiques

- **2000–2006 (the counterexample and filter theory).** Single-workstation codes (LOQO, KNITRO, IPOPT Fortran with MA27), CUTE/COPS test sets, and small research groups at Princeton, Northwestern, Maryland, Columbia and IBM. The competition was among three interior-point schools (LOQO line search, KNITRO trust region, IPOPT filter). Critiques were about convergence theory.
- **2009–2018 (maturity, benchmarks).** IPOPT 3.x was the "de facto open-source standard" (Tasseff et al. 2019, context) with HSL MA27/MA57 or MUMPS. CUTEst had about 1,150 problems. Critiques shifted to infeasibility detection, degeneracy and m > n, and came from authors of rival codes (WORHP, Algencan/IR, SNOPT, OnePhase in Julia).
- **2014–2026 (hardware).** Parallel, distributed and GPU computing (Argonne's PIPS-NLP, ExaTron, MadNLP). The critique moved from the algorithm to its linear-algebra contract (inertia, pivoting). Meanwhile Wächter moved to Gurobi (see 01), whose 2026 "NL barrier" is a preview feature. Its globalization is not documented publicly (Gap).

## Contradictions (kept, not reconciled)

1. **Does IPOPT solve its own counterexample?**
   - Thesis (2002, Fortran IPOPT, start (−2, 3, 1)): "Ipopt encounters no problems solving (3.26) using the filter approach, requiring a total for 23 iterations, of which 4 are taken within Tron." (p. 82).
   - Gill–Saunders–Wong (2015, IPOPT 3.11.8 + MA57, CUTEst `WACHBIEG`, whose SIF start point is (−2, 1, 1)): `wachbieg` is among the problems IPOPT wrongly declared infeasible (pp. 10, 12).
   - SLEQP (2023) stops at the infeasible point (−1, 0, 0).
   - Different versions, different start points, possibly different option sets. Not reconciled.
2. **Is the filter IPM strong or weak at feasibility?**
   - Chen–Goldfarb (2005): filter methods "enjoy excellent robustness compared with penalty-based methods, especially in achieving feasibility".
   - Hinder–Ye (2018): "The algorithm has difficulties detecting infeasibility". Huang–Mehrotra (2017), Kuhlmann–Büskens (2018) and Birgin et al. (2015) point the same way on infeasible problems.
   - (Achieving feasibility on feasible problems and certifying infeasibility are different tasks. Both claims stand as written.)
3. **Adaptive versus monotone barrier update.** His co-authored 2009 paper: adaptive choices "outperform monotone strategies". IPOPT's default: monotone (current source). His own Bonmin: adaptive. His list advice: adaptive "often leads often to fewer iterations (but cost per iteration is slightly more)" (2006).
4. **Two answers to the inertia problem, pursued in parallel by him:**
   - keep inertia but approximate it with a preconditioner (Schenk–Wächter–Weiser 2008);
   - drop inertia altogether with inexact, matrix-free steps (Curtis–Nocedal–Wächter 2009; Curtis–Schenk–Wächter 2010).
   - The production code kept exact inertia by default and gained an inertia-free option only through Chiang–Zavala (2015).
   - Outside view: Chiang–Zavala report inertia-free as efficient as inertia detection, while Harvey et al. report that δI-based inertia correction steers toward the wrong local minima in their domain.
5. **What the counterexample proves.**
   - Wächter (2002): most line-search IPMs "fail on this innocuous example".
   - BSV (2000): it is "classical jamming", and they give three resolutions.
   - BSV's own wording shrinks from "primal-dual interior-point methods" (2000) to "certain interior-point methods" (2004).
   - Chen–Goldfarb: failure at rank-deficient infeasible points.
   - Byrd–Marazzi–Nocedal: the same failure occurs without bounds.
   - Hinder–Ye: LP-style infeasible-start IPMs "cannot be adapted … without significant modification".
6. **Hinder–Ye's own results point both ways.** Fewer failures (21 vs 39), but IPOPT is faster (median 0.6 s vs 3.3 s) and needs fewer iterations when both succeed. The comparison also turned off IPOPT's scaling and bound relaxation.
7. **Kuhlmann–Büskens point both ways.** "IPOPT solves slightly more problems" on feasible CUTEst, yet it is outperformed on infeasible CUTEst.
8. **Benchmark fairness.** He states that fair comparisons need an independent party and matched criteria. Most of the critical comparisons were run by rival authors, and two changed his defaults. The one independent benchmark (Mittelmann 2026) runs defaults and shows IPOPT well behind KNITRO and COPT on speed, while solving 46 of 47.
9. **The Chen–Goldfarb sentence differs between versions.** The CORC preprint (2005, read) says "unless strong conditions can be guaranteed". The Semantic Scholar snippet of the journal version (2006) reads "unless additional conditions can be guaranteed". Kept as found.

## Implications for Phase 2 (for the synthesiser; all [inferred])

- For "improve our solver" requests, the critique record gives a **ready checklist of where a filter-line-search IPM is known to be weak**:
  1. infeasibility certification and a restoration phase called near feasible points;
  2. m > n and fixed-variable-induced rank deficiency;
  3. CQ failure and degeneracy (MPCCs);
  4. convergence to saddles and maximizers under δI inertia correction;
  5. linear-solver lock-in (inertia and pivoting on GPUs);
  6. monotone μ defaults;
  7. warm starts.
- A Wächter-style answer to each would ask: *is there a counterexample, which assumption of the convergence proof does it break, and can the fix be proven, or is it an option-level heuristic?*
- His way of handling criticism (§B) is itself distillable: check the critic's settings, concede mechanisms honestly, absorb ideas as options, collaborate with the critic, and change method class when the weakness is structural.
