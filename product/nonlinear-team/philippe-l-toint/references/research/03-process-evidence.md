## 1. Experiments and execution (layer 4)

### 1.1 How baselines are chosen

- **[practice, P] Re-implement the classical method in the same frame (X1).** In the 1994 non-monotone assessment (solo; report TR94-14, published as "An Assessment of Nonmonotone Linesearch Techniques for Unconstrained Optimization", *SISC* 17(3) 725–739, 1996, DOI 10.1137/S106482759427021X) he codes all three methods in Fortran himself, BLS (monotone), GLL1 and GLL2 (Grippo–Lampariello–Lucidi). All three use the same search-direction procedure, the modified conjugate-gradient method of Arioli et al. So the only thing that differs is the acceptance rule. He deliberately departs from the original authors' setup, which used a modified Cholesky factorization: "At variance with the original experiences reported by these authors, we have used a (preconditioned) truncated conjugate-gradients technique to compute the search directions" (conclusion; quote rejoined from PostScript). The acknowledgement shows where the building block came from: "The author is indebted to N. Gould for kindly providing a Fortran code for the modified conjugate-gradient technique".
- **[practice, P]** ARC Part I (report TR07-05a, 29 Sep 2007; *Math. Prog.* 127(2) 245–295, DOI 10.1007/s10107-009-0286-5), §7: "By way of a comparison, we have also implemented a standard trust-region method [4, Alg. 6.1.1.]", where [4] is their own *Trust-Region Methods* book. It uses GLTR for the subproblem, the same stopping rule as ARC, and the radius rules given in the text.
- **[practice, P]** OFFO second-order paper (arXiv:2203.09947 v2, 4 May 2022; *SIOPT* 33(3) 1621–1646, DOI 10.1137/22M1499522), §5: the baseline is "the standard second-order regularization algorithm AR2", with every parameter fixed from their 2022 book ("See [11, page 65] with η1 = 10−4, η2 = 0.95 …", footnote 4).
- **[practice, P]** First-order OFFO paper (arXiv:2203.01647 v3; *OMS* 41(2) 478–508, DOI 10.1080/10556788.2023.2296431), Table 1: 16 variants, plus "sdba standard steepest-descent with backtracking (e.g. [10, Algorithm 2.2.1])". Adam is added "since the Adam algorithm using (3.32) is so commonly used [in] the stochastic context". The only outside ML baselines are the ones the theory is about (Adagrad, Adam, Adagrad-Norm), and all are re-implemented in Matlab.
- **[practice, P]** Structured BFO (arXiv:2001.04801 v2, 12 Jan 2021), Table 4.2: every instance is run with the structure-exploiting and the standard version of *the same* code, "structured/unstructured versions of BFO".
- **[practice, P] Outside-code comparison only with the rival's author, and only once it can be made fair (X5).** The LANCELOT experiments paper (report TR92-16, Sep 1992; *Math. Prog.* 73(1) 73–110, 1996, DOI 10.1007/BF02592099) defers the comparison: "However, due to the diversity of algorithmic options and complexity of these two packages, a fair and informative comparison is, in itself, a major research effort. It will be reported on separately." The comparison came five years later as Bongartz, Conn, Gould, **Saunders** and Toint, "A numerical comparison between the LANCELOT and MINOS packages for large-scale nonlinear optimization", Report 97/13, FUNDP Namur, 1997 (with "the complete results", Report 97/14), where Saunders is MINOS's co-author. The report lists five reasons why "A fair comparison between two pieces of software such as LANCELOT and MINOS seems extremely difficult", among them that "each algorithm has an associated folklore about options that are best under certain circumstances". Only default versions were compared, and it says so. [practice] It appears on his list only as reports 97/13 and 97/14, and DBLP has no record of it. **I found no journal version** (see Gaps).
- **[practice, S: agent 01 §2 D]** In the 2016 filter tribute talk the baseline is the unsafeguarded Newton method ("Filter vs. free Newton"). This is the same logic: compare against the least-safeguarded version of one's own method.

### 1.2 How test problems are chosen, and what is excluded

- **[stated, P] Large test sets to avoid bias.** LANCELOT 1992, §6: "Our first decision was to test and report on a large number of test cases. In our experience, this is essential for a true assessment of reliability and performance, as smaller test sets are more likely to introduce unwanted bias." The same paper then adds a subset of the largest problems, because only that subset "depends more obviously on the way in which the problem structure is handled" (rejoined; the size threshold digit was lost in extraction).
- **[practice, P] Exclusion rules are stated and applied (X3).**
  - 1994: problems where methods reached different minimizers are dropped: "six small ones and one large exhibited more than one local minimizer and were therefore rejected from our test sets". Detailed comparisons are restricted to problems "coherently solved by both methods, that is for which both methods converged to critical points with identical objective function values".
  - 2003 FILTRANE (report TR03-17; *ACM TOMS* 33(1), 2007, DOI 10.1145/1206040.1206043): "Efficiency comparisons are made after the problems for which different local solutions were found by different variants are removed". GROWTH is excluded from one sweep for this reason.
  - 2011 ARC σ-update study (report naXys-08-2011; *COAP* 53(1) 1–22, DOI 10.1007/s10589-011-9446-7): twelve CUTEr problems are excluded "as no algorithm succeeded in solving these problems for any tested parameter choice".
  - 2022–23 OFFO: problems where variants converge to far-apart points are listed in a footnote and removed ("It is therefore impossible to meaningfully compare the convergence performance to such points across algorithmic variants", 2203.09947 v2, §5).
- **[practice, P] Scale is set by the tooling, and he says so.** ARC 2007: "for those whose dimensions may be adjusted, we chose small variants simply so as not to overload our (Matlab) computing environment, most particularly the CUTEr interface to Matlab"; 131 CUTEr unconstrained problems. OFFO 2022–23: "the low dimensional instances of the problems" of the OPM collection, each from "their standard starting point" (footnote markers omitted) (117 problems listed in the 2022 table, by my count).
- **[practice, P] Test problems built to satisfy the theory's assumption (X9).** In the sketched OFFO paper (Bellavia, Gratton, Morini, Toint, arXiv:2310.16580; *JOTA* 209(2), 2026, DOI 10.1007/s10957-026-03004-3), v2 (30 Jan 2025) enlarges 14 OPM/CUTEst problems by an orthonormal discrete-cosine embedding. This yields "problems with Hessians of rank at most n̂ and ensuring (49) when n grows", where (49) is the low-rank condition the second-order theory needs. The text is open about the restriction: (49) is "thereby limiting the applicability of the result for p > 1 when considering general problems with full-rank Hessians. But this can be acceptable for a class of problems with low-rank Hessian, as we illustrate in Section 5." [inferred] The experiment illustrates the regime where the theorem applies. It is not a test of general usefulness, and the paper's own wording concedes this.
- **[practice, P]** A problem is modified when its data would stop the test early: "The components of the standard starting point for problem morebv were multiplied by 25 in order to avoid termination at x0" (2310.16580 v1, footnote 6). The S2MPJ release notes do the same kind of thing: "minor modifications to the starting point in problems DITTERT, LIN, S365 and S365MOD to avoid dividion by zero at the starting point" (6 II 2026, sic).

### 1.3 Ablation: one change from the default (X2)

- **[practice, P] LANCELOT A, 1992.** "We next considered basic variants of this default choice, that is a choice of algorithmic options that differs in just one instance from the default." Fourteen variants (preconditioners by bandwidth, direct vs iterative, SR1 vs BFGS, scaling, accurate BQP, finite-difference gradients) are each compared with the default (the SR1 label was lost in extraction, but the text names "The two quasi-Newton variants"). Failures are classified as succ / stall / infs / mem / iters / cpu / error.
- **[practice, P] LANCELOT B, 2002** (Gould, Orban, Toint, "Results from a numerical evaluation of LANCELOT B", Report 02/09): the new default (non-monotone), then "The default, except that a monotone descent strategy is used", "The default, except that approximations to the second derivatives are used", and so on, plus LANCELOT A with its defaults "By way of a comparison". The test set is all of CUTEr "save linear and quadratic programs for which there are much better specialized packages within GALAHAD".
- **[practice, P] FILTRANE, 2003.** Every filter-specific constant is swept one at a time: filter on/off, model choice and inertia, subproblem accuracy, trust-region relaxation factor, signed vs unsigned entries, margin type and size, weak acceptance, preconditioning, dominated entries, grouping. The stated aim: "This discussion provides a first experimental study of the parameters inherent to filter algorithms." In the source, the filter itself is a switch ("use-filter NEVER|INITIAL|ALWAYS", GALAHAD `src/filtrane/filtrane.F90`), so "filter vs pure trust region" is one option flip.
- **[practice, P] ARC σ-update, 2011: coordinate-wise tuning.** "Taking into account these performance measures, we fix the “best” value for the parameter p ∈ Ip and we proceed with the analysis of the subsequent parameter in the list." Global tuning is named and not done: "We note that a more sophisticated choice, in which the globally optimal parameters for our test set is determined [1], is possible but has not been performed."
- **[practice, P] Then tuning by optimization (BFO, 2015–2020).** Porcelli & Toint turn the benchmarking measures into training objectives (maximize the area under a data or performance profile; "A note on using performance and data profiles for training algorithms", arXiv:1711.09407; *ACM TOMS* 45(2), 2019, DOI 10.1145/3310362). In the structured-BFO paper the one new constant ι is trained on "the instance of third smallest dimension (mostly n≈100)" and then used at every size; "The experimental guess of ι = 1.25, was only very marginally improved to 1.2550." [inferred] The progression is: hand sweep (1992–2003) → coordinate-wise sweep (2011) → automatic training with a derivative-free optimizer (2015–2020).

### 1.4 Metrics, reporting and measurement discipline (X4)

- **[practice, P] Aggregate plus disaggregate, with a stated defence.** LANCELOT 1992 uses averages plus five "performance classes (excellent, good, satisfactory, fair and poor)". The average "sometimes obscures the performance of algorithmic variants on the easier problems in comparison with the harder ones", but "The authors of course realize that this scheme is not the only one that can be defended." In 1994 the cumulative table misleads him: disaggregated rankings show that GLL1 and GLL2 perform "much better than suggested by" the cumulative table (rejoined; table number lost). He then adds win/tie/loss counts and the min / first quartile / median / third quartile / max of the differences. **Tie rule**: two CPU times "are reputed equal" when they correspond to identical iterates, or differ by less than a stated percentage or half a second (the same rule reappears in the 1997 MINOS comparison).
- **[practice, P] Performance profiles from 2003, then their area.** FILTRANE uses Dolan–Moré profiles with the horizontal axis cut at a stated factor. ARC 2007 uses an iteration-count profile. From 2022, because "the standard performance profiles … for our selection of 16 algorithms would be too crowded to read", the global measure π_algo (1/50 of the area under the profile on [1, 50]) is reported together with a reliability score ρ_algo (2203.01647 v3; 2203.09947 v2). This is the same area idea as the 2019 training note.
- **[practice, P] Complete results released separately.** "Intensive numerical tests with LANCELOT (Release A): the complete results" (Report 92/15), "… non-monotone linesearch techniques …: the complete numerical results" (Report 94/15), "… LANCELOT and MINOS …: the complete results" (Report 97/14), ARC 2007 "We give the complete set of results in Appendix A". In 1994 this is justified by volume: the number of test runs made detailed tables "impossible to include" (rejoined; count lost), and they were distributed "by anonymous ftp".
- **[practice, P] No number that the instrument cannot support.** ARC 2007: "We would have liked to have given a similar figure for CPU times, but the Matlab CPU timer proved too inaccurate for this purpose—we defer such a comparison until we have produced a careful (Fortran) implementation and tested it on larger examples in a controlled computing environment." [practice, P: GALAHAD `src/arc/arc.F90` header] The Fortran ARC was released in GALAHAD 2.5 on 13 May 2011, with "Principal authors: Nick Gould and Margherita Porcelli". The promised careful implementation was done, but by Gould and Porcelli (then at Namur, per the address on the 2011 report), not by Toint. Later OFFO papers measure derivative evaluations, not time ("Efficiency is measured in number of derivatives’ evaluations (or, equivalently, iterations)", 2203.01647 v3).
- **[practice, P] Machine and budget always stated, often in a footnote.** 1994: a DEC workstation, CPU limit in hours. 2002: "a single processor of a Compaq AlphaServer … Each run was terminated unsuccessfully after [a time limit] minutes". 2003: "a Dell Latitude portable computer". 2007: "a single processor of a 3.06 GHz Dell Precision 650 Workstation … Matlab 7.2". 2022: "In Matlab on a Dell portable computer under Ubuntu 20.04 with sixteen cores and 64 GB of memory". 2025: "a Dell Precision laptop with 16 cores at 2.6 GHz and 62.5 GB of memory, running Matlab 2024a".
- **[practice, P] Noise protocol (2022–23).** Relative random Gaussian noise of 5%, 15%, 25% and 50%, added to derivatives (and to f for methods that use f), averaged over 10 runs, with the tolerance loosened to 10−3. Result in 2203.09947 v2, Table 3: AR2 reliability falls from 40.67% to 6.81%, while OFFAR2a goes from 80.76% to 56.30%. Randomized methods are averaged over several runs, with fewer runs for larger instances ("For small problems, the reported results are an average of 30 independent runs, for smallish 10 runs for medium ones, 5 runs and a single run for the large ones", 2001.04801 v2; wording as printed).

### 1.5 Setting constants

- **[practice, P]** 1994: "After some experimenting", the non-monotone memory, the initial step threshold and N were fixed (values lost in extraction). 2007 ARC: σ is doubled on failure, "(the choice of the “2” factor is for simplicity, and it is likely that better values are possible in a similar vein to [15])", and "similar performance being observed for other choices of initial parameters". 2022: "The constants 1/2 and 9/10 may of course be modified, but we found these to work well in our tests" (2203.09947 v2, footnote 2).
- **[practice, P] A theoretically weaker constant, checked first, then used.** 2310.16580 (both versions): the expensive ‖S_k‖ is replaced by a constant β. "This change was made after running the more expensive code using κS,k = ‖Sk‖ as suggested by (19) on a few problems and observing that the results obtained with the theoretically weaker κS,k = β did not decrease the code efficiency, if at all." (v1 wording; v2 says "did not degrade".)

## 2. Judging results (layer 5)

- **[practice, P] Hedges are part of every numerical section (X8).** 1994: "As is the case for all practical comparisons between optimization algorithms, the results presented here do not pretend to be either complete or decisive." 1997: only default versions were compared, and "the best advice they can offer to practitioners is to try both packages themselves". 2007: "While we should regard our results as tentative, since improvements to both algorithms are most likely, we are delighted with the overall performance of our new algorithm." 2022 v1 (2203.01647): "These simple numerical illustrations are obviously not meant to replace significant numerical testing, but, albeit caution must be exercised not to extrapolate from limited data, they still suggest a few tentative comments." 2023 v3: "The authors are of course aware that the very limited experiments presented here do not replace extended numerical practice and could be completed in various ways." Numerical sections are titled "Numerical illustration", not "results", from 2022 on.
- **[practice, P] Negative findings are printed.**
  - LANCELOT 1992: "The ability of the generalized Cauchy point to determine the correct active set is disappointing in practice." On scaling: "Keeping such an option available therefore seems to be of some value, but it should not be used as a default." Weak points found by reading runs, "that cannot be inferred directly from the summaries": slack variables for many inequalities, no provision for linear or network constraints. On the first of these: "The authors are well aware of this aspect of their implementation".
  - 1994: "A first observation is that preconditioning slightly deteriorates reliability on our set of test problems", and on his own preconditioner: "We note that this preconditioner has not been formally analyzed, and thus that its effect is essentially unknown, although often beneficial."
  - 1997: "LANCELOT proves to be less reliable than MINOS on linear programming problems, but somewhat more reliable on problems involving nonlinear constraints." (abstract).
  - FILTRANE 2003: "The weak acceptance rule does not appear to bring any improvement"; on keeping dominated filter entries, "we could not isolate any significant difference".
  - ARC 2007: of the three stopping rules, the one "which is less concerned with provably superior worst-case complexity, appears to be more promising".
  - OFFO 2022: "Obviously, if we were to consider noiseless problems only, there would be little motivation (beyond theoretical curiosity) to consider the OFFAR2 algorithms."
  - First-order OFFO 2023: "The use of limited memory Hessian approximation (adagbfgs3) appears to enhance the performance of adagrad, but this is not the case of the Barzilai-Borwein approximation (adagbb) or, remarkably, for the use of the exact Hessian (adagH)."
  - 2022 v1: a larger μ handles smaller learning rates better, "a behaviour admittedly not predicted by our theory".
- **[practice, P] Per-problem diagnosis instead of averages when behaviour is heterogeneous.** 2310.16580 v1 (Oct 2023): "Because of this diversity in behaviour across problems, it is the authors’ opinion that aggregate performance measures such as performance profiles or other statistics averaged on problem’s sets are less informative than a discussion of specific cases." It then explains the failures one problem at a time: lminsurf's early gradients lie in "a very low dimensional subspace … a structure which is lost by random sampling"; for msqrtals "we suspect the nonconvex multilinear nature of its objective function and its bad conditioning". Its verdict: "general conclusions remain elusive". v2 reran with the second-order variant on embedded low-rank problems and reports averaged tables (see Contradictions).
- **[practice, P] Failures inspected before they are counted.** FILTRANE: "detailed analysis showed that some variants were very close to the problem solution despite them reporting that no further progress could be made. These occurences are counted as successful in our discussion." (sic). LANCELOT 1992: GMPS-preconditioner overflows are traced to the eigenvalue-perturbation threshold, and "A posteriori experiments" with a larger threshold show "that the overflow problems can be avoided" (rejoined; threshold values lost).
- **[practice, P] An idea not pursued, with the reason printed.** 2310.16580 v2: a quadratic-regularization variant is analysed in an appendix, but "Unfortunately, our numerical experience matches the cautious conclusions of this reference, which is why we do not investigate it further." A tested competitor setting that failed is recorded: ADAM "Using the momentum discounting factor β = 0.9999. It failed to converge on most problems with smaller values." (footnote 6).
- **[practice, P] Unreported experiments mentioned in theory papers.** arXiv:2604.17423 v3 (theory only, no numerical section): "One should however be careful not to identify better theoretical convergence properties with better practical performance on specific classes of problems. Indeed, limited numerical experiments suggest that the second momentum variant might outperform the first." The experiments themselves are not shown.
- **[practice, P, crude count]** Of the 53 arXiv abstracts by Toint (2017–2026), 21 mention numerical experiments, illustration or tests. This is a lower bound: 2203.09947 has a numerical section only in v2, and its abstract does not say so.

## 3. Idea generation seen in the record (layer 3)

- **[practice, P] Assess someone else's idea → locate its failure case → design a fix → carry it into his own framework.** 1994: the stated purpose is to validate Grippo et al.: "Surprisingly, and despite their obvious potential practical implications, the findings of Grippo and his coauthors didn't lead to any substantial validation by other researchers, at least as far as the author is aware. … It is the first purpose of this paper to re-examine their proposals and contribute to their evaluation." The new variant NMLS targets a single observed failure: "NMLS is in fact designed to overcome the deficiency of GLL2 on that problem and similar ones" (problem FMINSURF). The last sentence announces the next step, "the extension of the non-monotone approach to trust-region algorithm is the object of ongoing research". That became TR94-24, *Math. Prog.* 77 (1997), DOI 10.1007/BF02614518, and the non-monotone default of LANCELOT B (2002).
- **[practice, P] A conversation triggers a counterexample note (X13).** arXiv:2308.00720 (solo, dated 19 VI 2023, posted 1 Aug 2023): "In a recent discussion at the June 2023 Thematic Einstein Semester on Optimization and Machine Learning in Berlin, it was suggested that, although likely, no explicit example of failure of Adam with fixed stepsize was available for the deterministic case (where the variance is zero). This prompted the author to produce the (very simple) one which is, for the record, detailed in the next section." The construction is the one used for his sharpness examples: first fix sequences of iterates, function values and gradients, then show that a function with Lipschitz gradient interpolates them.
- **[practice, P] A recurring objection triggers a paper.** arXiv:2409.16047 (solo, 24 IX 2024; *Math. Prog.* 2025, DOI 10.1007/s10107-025-02286-1): "These sharpness results all hinge on exhibiting of a typically quite contrived function … Because of this contrived nature, one is naturally led to the question (1) of how “exceptional” these examples are." Footnote (1): "Adressed to the author in several occasions." (sic). The answer is to rebuild the example and show that it survives on a set of nonzero measure.
- **[practice, P] Benchmarking tools become design tools.** 2019 note: the profile area becomes a training objective for BFO. 2022–23: the same area becomes the π_algo reporting statistic. Toint repeatedly reuses his evaluation apparatus as an algorithmic component.

## 9. Contradictions (kept, not reconciled)

1. **Averages: trusted in 1992, distrusted in 1994.** LANCELOT 1992 defends the average because it "seems to correspond to our intuitive appraisal of the variants". In 1994 the cumulative measures understate GLL1/GLL2 and he turns to rankings and quartiles. Both papers report both kinds of measure.
2. **Aggregate profiles vs case discussion within one paper's history.** 2310.16580 v1 prefers "a discussion of specific cases" over "performance profiles or other statistics averaged on problem’s sets". v2 of the same paper reports averaged tables over 10 runs and 14 problems as its main evidence. The 2022–23 OFFO papers compress profiles further into π_algo.
3. **"Careful (Fortran) implementation" promised in 2007 vs who did it.** The deferral is stated by Cartis–Gould–Toint. The Fortran ARC is by Gould and Porcelli (2011). Toint's later experiments stay in Matlab with evaluation counts. So the CPU comparison deferred in 2007 was never made by Toint himself as far as the record read shows (the 2011 σ-update study compares ARC and trust-region, but in evaluations).
4. **Published paper vs repository for S2MPJ.** The OMS article (2025) is the citable version. The repository calls its Julia procedure "obsolete" and the OMS version "incomplete".
5. **The theory scope of experiments.** 2310.16580 v2 openly limits the second-order result to low-rank Hessians and then tests only low-rank problems. The first-order v1 evidence ("general conclusions remain elusive") is absent from v2. Both versions are Toint's.
6. **Validation discipline vs CI.** He builds elaborate manual cross-language validation, yet removes the automated CI his co-author added ("removing testing stuff").
7. **Twin preprints.** Posting two overlapping papers on the same day (arXiv overlap flag) sits badly with the care shown elsewhere in versioning. The two were later separated cleanly by claim type.
