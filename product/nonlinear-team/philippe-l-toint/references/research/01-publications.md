## 0. Two warnings before reading any statistic

1. **Author order in this field is alphabetical, so "first → last author shift" does not apply.** [practice, P: DBLP] Of the 121 multi-author, non-preprint DBLP records, 120 list authors in alphabetical order of surname. The one exception is a 2024 preface. "Tshimanga Ilunga" looks like an exception but is alphabetical by his real surname, Tshimanga. With a surname starting with T, Toint is last on almost every paper: Conn–Gould–Toint, Cartis–Gould–Toint, Gratton–…–Toint, Bellavia–Gurioli–Morini–Toint. The OpenAlex table in `../sources/publications/publications.md` therefore shows "last author" for 42 of 52 papers in 2010–2014 and 52 of 56 in 2020–2024. **This is an artifact of alphabetical order, not a sign of a PI role.** The student-detection heuristic ("other first × Toint last") in that file is equally invalid here. The only author-order signals that carry information are:
   - **Pre-convention papers.** In 1977–1978 Toint appears *first* ahead of Callier and Strodiot in two JOTA papers, DOI 10.1007/BF00933294 and 10.1007/BF00933295, and a Brussels annals paper. This is non-alphabetical, which suggests his thesis period. [practice, P: Crossref + homepage list]
   - **Solo papers.** These are the only places where his own voice is unmixed. See §2.2.
2. **The databases disagree on size.** [practice] DBLP has 150 records (1981–2026; it misses most pre-1991 SIAM and Math. Comp. papers, including the 1988 and 1991 SINUM papers). OpenAlex has 355 works, with duplicates (arXiv + repository + journal copies of the same paper) and possible misattributions. The Namur research portal (Pure) has 321 research outputs. Toint's own list (perso.unamur.be/~phtoint/publications.html) has about 300 items, including transport reports, books in French and poetry; its journal section stops at 2023. arXiv has 53 papers, the first posted in April 2017 (arXiv:1704.06919). **Pattern** [inferred from the table]: the core never leaves smooth nonlinear optimization with a strong software side (his homepage research statement is quoted verbatim in §1.5). The questions move in roughly decade-long waves: sparsity and structure (1977–86) → globalization for large-scale constrained problems plus software (1988–2000) → non-monotone and filter acceptance (1994–2010) → worst-case evaluation complexity (2008–2022) → objective-function-free and adaptive (Adagrad-type) methods for noisy and ML problems (2021–26). Transportation modelling ran in parallel as a second career from 1975 to about 2015.

### E. The complexity turning point: Cartis, Gould, Toint, "Adaptive cubic regularisation methods for unconstrained optimization. Part I: motivation, convergence and numerical results", *Math. Prog.* 127(2) 245–295, DOI 10.1007/s10107-009-0286-5, and "… Part II: worst-case function- and derivative-evaluation complexity", *Math. | Dimension | Content |
|---|---|
| Origin | [stated, P: Part I report TR07-05a, 29 Sep 2007, abstract and pp. 1–2] The algorithm generalizes "an unpublished method due to Griewank (Technical Report NA/12, 1981, DAMTP, Univ. of Cambridge), an algorithm by Nesterov & Polyak (Math. Programming 108(1), 2006, pp 177-205) and a proposal by Weiser, Deuflhard & Erdmann (Optim. Methods Softw. 22(3), 2007, pp 413-431)". Their aim: "Our purpose here and in [2] is to unify and extend these contributions into a coherent and numerically efficient algorithmic framework, for which global and asymptotic convergence results can be proved under weaker assumptions and with simpler proofs, while preserving the good complexity bound shown by Nesterov and Polyak [25]." A precursor inside his own work [stated, P: TR09-14 p. 3]: "The authors are only aware of the analysis by Gratton, Sartenaer and Toint (2008), (Corollary 4.10) where a bound on the complexity of an inexact variant of the trust-region method is shown to be of the same order as that of steepest descent". So the multilevel trust-region paper (DOI 10.1137/050623012) already carried a complexity corollary. |
| Why then | [inferred, with a stated basis] Nesterov–Polyak (2006) had proved a better worst-case bound but "no numerical results were provided" (Part I p. 2). The CGT trust-region toolkit (the ratio test, Cauchy point, Lanczos/GLTR subproblem solver from 1999) made an adaptive, inexact, large-scale version immediately buildable. Resource context: EPSRC grant GR/S42170 (Part I footnote); the Namur project "Complexity in nonlinear optimization" (Toint, Gould, Cartis) started 2008-11. |
| Key insight | Replace the global Lipschitz constant by an adaptive σk updated like the inverse of a trust-region radius, and minimize the cubic model only approximately (on Krylov subspaces), yet keep the O(ε^-3/2) bound. In his 2015 Cambridge talk (p. 7) he lists the "Obvious questions: can we avoid the global Lipschitz requirement? YES! can we approximately minimize m and retain good worst-case function-evaluation complexity? YES ! does this work well in practice? yes". [stated, P] |
| Minimal evidence | [stated, P: Part I abstract] "Numerical experiments with small-scale test problems from the CUTEr set show superior performance of the ACO algorithm when compared to a trust-region implementation." Also (p. 4): "Our investigations suggest that the model can be globally minimized surprisingly efficiently, provided the factorization of the matrix Bk is (inexpensively) available." |
| Abandoned paths | (1) **Name**: the 2007 reports call it "Adaptive Cubic Overestimation (ACO)". The published title reads "Adaptive cubic **regularisation**", and the method is later "ARC", "AR2", "ARp". [practice, P: report vs Crossref] (2) In Part I, a termination rule that gives Q-quadratic convergence "is not the most efficient (see §7)" in the experiments (p. 3). [stated, P] (3) Unregularized Newton as a complexity-competitive method was dropped after they built slow examples: "the steepest descent and Newton's method … may be both require a number of iterations and function evaluations arbitrarily close to O(ε^-2) … and that Newton's method may be as slow as steepest descent in the worst case" (TR09-14 abstract, verbatim including the typo). The 2010 NAXYS talk calls this "A big surprise" (p. 21). The ICM 2018 paper concludes "that Newton's method is suboptimal" (arXiv:1709.07180 abstract). [stated, P] |
| Reception | ARC Part I and II and the steepest-descent/Newton paper are among his most cited (§1.5). Invited ICM lecture 2018 (Rio), and ICM 2022 per his list. The 2022 SIAM monograph consolidates the line. Complexity dominates his invited talks 2009–2021 (talks page). [practice, P] |
| Turning point | [practice, P] After 2008 his output moves from convergence plus numerical results to worst-case evaluation-complexity theorems plus **sharpness examples**: steepest descent and Newton (2010), high-order optimality (FoCM 2018), "Sharp worst-case evaluation complexity bounds…" (SIOPT 2020, DOI 10.1137/17M1144854), slow examples "not isolated" (2024/25), o(ε^-2) refinements (2025). The same machinery is then carried to noise and inexactness (Bellavia–Gurioli–Morini, 2018–23) and to **OFFO** (2022–26). The OFFO paper motivates this in his own terms: "it is our point of view that their deterministic (noiseless) counterparts are good stepping stones to understand their behaviour" and asks "Is such an improvement in complexity also possible for (noiseless) OFFO algorithms? We answer this question positively in what follows." (arXiv:2203.09947v1 p. 1). [stated, P] The trigger for OFFO is stated as an observation in the literature: "a number of these contributions … indicate that, when the (noisy) objective function is evaluated, its accuracy is significantly more critical to ensure convergence than that of the computed (noisy) derivatives" (arXiv:2203.01647v1 p. 1). [stated, P] |
| Method it shows | Import a new theoretical result (Nesterov–Polyak), strip its impractical assumptions, make it adaptive and inexact inside the trust-region frame, test on CUTEr; then prove bounds **and construct examples showing they are sharp**; then transplant the same machinery to the next setting (constraints, high order, noise, no function values, ML optimizers). The ICM 2018 slides end on "Can this be (more) practical?" (p. 43). [stated, P] |

**Cross-index to candidate methods** (for Phase 2; these are hypotheses, not validated methods): M-a "safeguard minimally, benchmark against unsafeguarded Newton" (D, E); M-b "theory + test twin with complete results" (A, B, D); M-c "unify a class under one assumption set" (C, the 2013 nonlinear stepsize-control paper, the 2026 unified adaptive theory); M-d "prove the bound, then build the slow example" (E, the 2016/2023/2024 counterexamples); M-e "maintain the shared test infrastructure yourself" (B); M-f "carry the machinery into the next setting" (E → OFFO → Adagrad/Muon).

## 4. Era and resource context

[practice, P unless stated]
- **1974–1990**: Namur, a small university. Early software was published through the **Harwell Subroutine Library** (TD03AD 1980, VE08AD 1983, VE10AD 1987), in effect a link to the UK Harwell numerical-analysis group [inferred]. Computing on a Siemens 4004 (report 76/4, 1976) and later vector machines (1988 paper). Transportation consulting reports for local authorities ran alongside the optimization work.
- **1988–2007, CGT**: a three-person collaboration across Namur, the UK (Harwell/RAL/Oxford) and North America (Conn, at IBM Watson by 2000 per the SIAM blurb). The Fortran packages (LANCELOT, CUTE, CUTEr, GALAHAD) reflect a team where theory, code and test set were built together. The trust-region book was a 3-year funded project. Toint was at the same time founder-director of the Transportation Research Group and head of the university computing services (2000 blurb), and later vice-rector for research and IT (homepage). **Heavy administrative load in parallel with the most productive software decade** [inferred].
- **2007–2022, complexity**: pen-and-paper theory with small numerical illustrations on CUTEr/CUTEst. Supported by EPSRC fellowships (2002, 2003, 2009), Leverhulme/Balliol (2015) and FNRS (slides). Seniority: full professor, MOS chair 2010–2013, SIAM Fellow 2009.
- **2017–2026**: emeritus. Output moves to arXiv-first. The main partner is Gratton in Toulouse (ANITI "3IA" funding acknowledged on the OFFO papers). Tools are Matlab-level experiments (BFO, OPM, S2MPJ in Matlab/Python/Julia), and the testing machine is a single workstation ("Dell Precision computer with 64 GiB of memory", S2MPJ p. 21). ML problems (neural-network training, PINNs) appear as applications, not as large-scale GPU experiments. [inferred from the papers read]

## 5. Contradictions (kept, not reconciled)

1. **Title of ARC Part I/II.** His homepage lists "Adaptive cubic **overestimation** methods for unconstrained optimization. Part I …", *Math. Prog.* 127(2) 2011. Crossref and DBLP give "Adaptive cubic **regularisation** methods …". The 2007 reports use ACO.
2. **Dates.** ARC Part I is 2009 in Crossref and OpenAlex (online) but 2011 in DBLP and his list (issue). "NLP without a penalty function or a filter" is 2008 in Crossref and 2010 in DBLP. CUTEst is 2014 in OpenAlex and 2015 in DBLP and his list. The OFFO first-order paper (DOI 10.1080/10556788.2023.2296431) is 2024 in Crossref and 2026 in DBLP.
3. **Counts.** Publications: DBLP 150 / Namur portal 321 / OpenAlex 355 / his list about 300. Citations of the TR book: 3019 (OpenAlex) / 1851 (S2) / 2230 (Crossref). CUTEst: "more than 2300" on Google Scholar per Toint himself (for the whole CUTE family) vs 341/458/291 for the 2015 paper alone.
4. **Affiliation.** OpenAlex's "current institutions" lists eight Toulouse/Bordeaux institutions plus Namur. His homepage and his papers' address line say Namur (NAXYS), "partly supported by ANITI".
5. **arXiv:2604.17423 title.** DBLP and the Namur portal carry the v1 title with "Shampoo". arXiv v3 has no Shampoo.
6. **Newton in practice vs Newton in the worst case.** 2009–2010: Newton's method, when it converges, "is usually remarkably effective" (TR09-14 p. 2). The filter work is built on the observation that unmodified SQP "is able to quickly solve a large proportion of test problems" (Brief History p. 1). The same group proves Newton can need about ε^-2 evaluations ("A big surprise", 2010) and calls it "suboptimal" (ICM 2018). Both views are held at once.
7. **Filter commitment.** He promotes filters ("Filters: an efficient tool for nonlinear programming", Lausanne 2006; the 2016 tribute) while also developing, in the same period, a method with "without a penalty function or a filter" in its title (2007–2010), one of whose stated motivations is to be a "backup for a new filter method".
8. **The OpenAlex role heuristic.** It labels Toint "last author (PI)" on about 90% of papers, which contradicts the alphabetical-order evidence (§0). Anyone reading `../sources/publications/publications.md` must discount that table.
