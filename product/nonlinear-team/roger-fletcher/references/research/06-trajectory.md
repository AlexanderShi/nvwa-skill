## 0. Trajectory patterns seen three or more times (read this first)

1. **A turn usually starts when someone hands him something, and he then computes it himself.** Examples: Davidon's report from Reeves (about 1962), Reeves's idea for nonlinear CG (1963–64), Powell pointing him to Wolfe's degeneracy method ("a long time ago"), Margaret Wright urging him to publish his Wolfe-method work (published 2014), Dai's reply to his negative referee report citing Raydan's large-scale BB results (date unknown; before 2001 is [inferred] from the homepage), Zoppke-Donaldson's "tolerance tubes" before the filter (early 1990s [inferred] from the ECOSSE dates). [stated, P: Optima 99 pp. 2, 4; Dai 2006 Q3, Q10] [observed, S: memoir pp. 132, 139, 140]
2. **A turn is often set off by numbers that disappoint.** Steepest descent "didn't make a lot of progress" (PhD, about 1962). MSc students' SDP runs "always converged slowly" (about 1981). Penalty functions threw away SQP steps "when it's obviously working well" (mid-1990s, which led to the filter). CG "not proved to be very suitable" inside his SLP null-space solver (late 2000s, which led to LMSD). MINRES would not work on Rockafellar's augmented Lagrangian (early 2010s, which led to the box-QP transformation). [stated, P: Optima 99 pp. 2, 4; LMSD report 2009 p. 1]
3. **Changes of place or funding opened new topics.** Harwell gave him the Harwell Subroutine Library (HSL) and a run of constrained-optimization codes (1969–73). Dundee gave him numerical linear algebra and the two books (1973–81). The ECOSSE chemical-engineering project gave him mixed-integer nonlinear programming (MINLP) and funded students (1988/89 onwards). EPSRC grants paid for the MPEC work (about 2001–06). [observed, S: memoir pp. 133–139] [stated, P: homepage 2001/2017]
4. **He often entered a topic before there was a consensus on it, published once, and moved on. Many of these works "never caught on".** Examples: indefinite QP (1971), bi-CG (1976), SDP (1981/1985), composite nonsmooth optimization (1980–82), expected conditioning (1985), exact augmented Lagrangian (1970), ultra-BFGS, LIU factors. [stated, P: Optima 99 pp. 2–4]
5. **He also changed his mind in public and came late into topics he had dismissed.** Examples: Barzilai–Borwein (BB) steps (sceptical referee, then 2001–2012 co-author and developer), single precision (believed Powell's rule, then "I don't believe it anymore"), convex analysis ("never very useful for computation", then needed it for semidefinite matrices). [stated, P: Optima 99 pp. 2, 4, 5] [observed, S: memoir p. 140; SIAM News 2016]
6. **He never retired from coding.** After 2005 he produced the low-rank quasi-Newton scheme and filterQN (2005), the LMSD method (2009/2012), SLCP and filterSD (2011/2012), a Wolfe-method paper (2014) and a box-QP method tested on up to 10⁸ variables (published after his death, 2017). [practice, P: papers and code] [observed, S: memoir p. 141]

- **C8. Date of death.** 15 July 2016: memoir title and Wikipedia; the body was found then. "died on 5 June 2016": Royal Society Fellows Directory. "† June 2016": Dundee staff page (2017 snapshot). "passed away in June 2016": SIAM News.

- **C10. Was the filter unprecedented?** Fletcher (2015): "A simple idea there – nobody thought of it". His 2006 history with Leyffer and Toint: "Filter methods for NLP were developed independently of earlier similar ideas" (it cites Surry et al. and Lemaréchal et al.). The memoir credits Zoppke-Donaldson's unpublished tolerance tubes with "helping to pave the way".
- **C11. Barzilai–Borwein: sceptic, or early adopter who forgot?** 2009: "I have therefore returned to some thoughts that I had some 20 years ago (Fletcher [8]), occasioned by innovative ideas inherent in the Barzilai-Borwein (BB) methods" (the 1990 paper). 2015: as referee of Dai's BB paper "I said this paper was of no interest". Memoir: "Roger was initially cynical". Homepage: "interested (again!)". The record shows interest in 1990, dismissal at some point before 2001 (date unknown), and a return in 2001.
- **C12. Prediction and his own later practice.** 2006: "I find it very hard to envisage significant new ideas in nonlinear CG and quasi-Newton methods." 2009–12: he presents LMSD as "a competitive and more simple alternative to the state of the art l-BFGS limited memory method" (LMSD abstract). Curtis (2015): "Fletcher himself (the "F"!) is reexamining them and developing viable alternatives."
- **C13. Stated wish and later practice.** 2006: "I'd like to get back to working more on applications at some time." 2006–2016 output: mostly algorithms and software (LMSD, SLCP, Wolfe, box QP), with SVM training (2010) and image processing (2015, stated) as the only applied items found.
- **C14. Single precision.** Memoir (citing Leyffer 2015): he advocated single precision "both from an efficiency perspective, but also as a signal of inherent instability when developing methods" (p. 138). Fletcher (2015): "It was a sort of misapprehension on my part ... I wouldn't write in single precision now."
- **C15. How much he published.** "I have over 120 publications in my CV" (2006). "at least 130 papers and technical reports" (memoir p. 141). OpenAlex 114 works, 91 after cleaning (01-publications.md).
- **C16. Author list of the 1964 FR paper.** Crossref gives R. Fletcher alone for DOI 10.1093/comjnl/7.2.149. Fletcher, the memoir and SIAM News give Fletcher and Reeves.
- **C17. First student.** Memoir: Shirley Lill was "his first PhD student" (1967), and Womersley (1977) "his first notable PhD student". MGP's earliest listed student is Womersley (1981), and it lists no Leeds students.
