- **Dimension**: research agent 01 of 06, signature works and the publication landscape (nuwa research-craft Phase 1).
- **Research date**: 2026-09-28.
- **Sources consulted**: 43. 35 were read in whole or in part; 8 were identified but blocked or not read (marked "not read" below). Of the 35 read, 26 are primary: his homepage, CV and Oxford profile, 21 of his own papers or notes read as full text, abstract or key sections, the GALAHAD repository, and the JOSS paper. - **Method**: I started from his own publication list (homepage and 2023 CV, [S01][S02]), cross-checked it against DBLP (SPARQL), OpenAlex (script output in `../sources/publications/publications.md`), Semantic Scholar, arXiv, Crossref and DataCite, and then read the author-posted PDFs on his RAL page for the signature works. **Tags.** [stated] = what Gould (or Gould with co-authors, in their own text) said. [practice] = what the record shows he did (papers, code, dates, CV entries). [observed] = what others said about him. [inferred] = my reading, with no primary source for it. Each item is also marked primary or secondary. "p." gives the page of the full text I read.

## 2. Signature works: anatomy (framework §五)

I chose five works. Two are the most cited (*Trust-Region Methods* and the CGT augmented-Lagrangian/LANCELOT line; CUTE). One is the work Gould singles out himself: his homepage biography names his **three books** as its own sentence ("I have published three books…", [S01]). Two are turning points (GALAHAD 2003, where the record documents an abandonment, and ARC 2011, which opened the complexity program). The linear-algebra line (GLTR and KKT preconditioning) is included because it is the thread that runs through every period.

### A. CGT 1988 → augmented Lagrangian 1991 → LANCELOT 1992 (→ *Trust-Region Methods*, 2000)

Works: *SINUM* 25 (1988) 433–460, 10.1137/0725029, with correction 10.1137/0726044; *Math. Comp.* 50 (1988) 399–430, 10.1090/S0025-5718-1988-0929544-3; *SINUM* 28 (1991) 545–572, 10.1137/0728030; *LANCELOT*, Springer Series in Computational Mathematics 17 (1992), 10.1007/978-3-662-12211-2; *Trust-Region Methods*, MPS-SIAM (2000), 10.1137/1.9780898719857.

| Dimension | Finding |
|---|---|
| Origin | [stated, primary] The aim was large-scale constrained NLP from the start. 1988 testing paper: "The final aim of this research is actually to produce effective methods for solving general nonlinear programming problems." | Why then | [stated] The 1991 introduction draws the analogy explicitly: "A similar situation existed for unconstrained optimization in the early 1970s." [inferred] The plan was to transfer those three ideas to constraints by way of a bound-constrained inner solver. Era and resources: a transatlantic trio (Conn at Waterloo, Gould at Harwell/RAL, Toint at Namur) funded by a NATO travel grant (1990–95, [S02]) and a US ARPA/AFOSR contract (acknowledgements in [S16], [S26]); distribution by anonymous ftp |
| Key insight | [practice] Generalize the Cauchy point to bound constraints so that the unconstrained TR convergence theory carries over ([S27] p.400). | Minimum evidence | [practice, primary] Deliberately small: "The purpose of the current work is to demonstrate the viability of the methods proposed (and whose convergence is studied) in our previous paper. The software was explicitly deferred: "the design of sophisticated software for such problems is a major task that we postpone for a future paper" (p.400) |
| Surprises and abandoned paths | [stated] "updating schemes which are held in good repute for line-search based methods are not necessarily the best within a trust-region context. Over the long run, LANCELOT's augmented-Lagrangian approach was judged to have reached its limit (see D) |
| Reception | [practice] The Beale–Orchard-Hays Prize 1994 (CV [S02]; the Namur portal lists it as awarded to Toint "with A.R. Conn and N. Its **origin and preface: not read** (SIAM returned 403) |
| Method it shows | Theory paper paired with a small testing paper, then a large-scale software release, then an "intensive tests" paper, then a book that consolidates the theory (cross-index: candidate methods M1, M3 and M7 in §3) |

### B. CUTE (1995) → CUTEr (2003) → CUTEst (2015)

Works: *ACM TOMS* 21 (1995) 123–160, 10.1145/200979.201043; *ACM TOMS* 29 (2003) 373–394, 10.1145/962437.962439; *COAP* 60 (2015) 545–557, 10.1007/s10589-014-9687-3. Related: *ACM TOMS* 43(2) (2016) Art. 15, 10.1145/2950048.

| Dimension | Finding |
|---|---|
| Origin | [stated, primary] A by-product of building LANCELOT: "It is inevitable that, during the process of developing a software package, the designers concern themselves with problems of testing. … All of these situations occurred during our own researches, and indeed, many of the facilities described in this article were originally produced and tested in conjunction with the software package LANCELOT" ([S16] p.124). The CV lists SIF format proposals from 1989 (Waterloo report CSS-89-61) and 1991 (FUNDP 91/8) [S02] (reports not read) |
| Why then | [stated] In a co-authored survey: when problems grew large, "the likelihood of introducing coding errors or slight variations in test problems grew and made software comparison very awkward. An electronically transferable format for test problems was therefore desirable." ([S12] preprint p.4; text re-joined from a font-encoded PDF) |
| Key insight | [practice] Separate the problem from the solver: a portable problem format (SIF, an extension of MPS), a decoder that generates Fortran for element and group functions, a classification scheme with a select tool, and interfaces to other people's solvers (MINOS and OSL in 1995) [S16] |
| Minimum evidence | [inferred] The LANCELOT test campaigns themselves: the 1996 paper reports tests "during the 8 months of nearly uninterrupted computation required for obtaining the results presented here" ([S26] p.106) |
| Abandoned paths and self-criticism | [stated] On the name: "This format, ambitiously (and, with hindsight, perhaps rather arogantly [sic]) called the Standard Input Format, or SIF". | Later critique of evaluation practice | [stated, primary] With Scott, a caution on the community's standard comparison tool: "caution should be exercised when trying to interpret performance profiles to assess the relative performance of the solvers". With more than two solvers, profiles do not rank the non-best solvers, so they propose removing the best solver repeatedly ([S21] pp.15:1–15:4) |

### C. The subproblem linear-algebra layer: GLTR (1999) and KKT constraint preconditioning (2000)

Works: Gould, Lucidi, Roma, Toint, *SIOPT* 9 (1999) 504–525, 10.1137/S1052623497322735; Keller, Gould, Wathen, *SIMAX* 21 (2000) 1300–1317, 10.1137/S0895479899351805. Continuations: Gould–Hribar–Nocedal 2001; Dollar–Gould–Schilders–Wathen 2006; Gould–Simoncini 2009; Gould–Robinson–Thorne 2010 (DOIs in §1.1); Gould–Orban–Rees, "Projected Krylov Methods for Saddle-Point Systems", *SIMAX* 2014, 10.1137/130916394; Gould–Simoncini, "Error estimates for iterative algorithms for minimizing regularized quadratic subproblems", *OMS* 2020, 10.1080/10556788.2019.1670177; Al Daas–Gould, arXiv:2511.11135.

| Dimension | Finding |
|---|---|
| Origin | [stated, primary] GLTR starts from an observed failure of the standard method: "The Steihaug–Toint method is basically unconcerned with the trust region until it blunders into its boundary and stops. This is rather unfortunate, particularly as considerable experience has shown that this frequently happens during the first few, and often the first, iteration(s) when negative curvature is present." ([S19] p.505). A position on norms: "We believe that weighting the norm is essential for many large-scale problems." (p.504). For constraint preconditioning, the paper frames the problem as systems "of a sort which arise in constrained optimization as well as in least-squares, saddle-point, and Stokes problems" ([S20] p.1300). Who initiated it: **not found** (Keller was the Oxford student; Gould was his external advisor per the CV [S02]) |
| Why then | [inferred] LANCELOT and trust-region experience had made the approximate TR subproblem the inner bottleneck, and Lanczos/Krylov theory was mature. Resources: the British Council–MURST grant with the Rome group ([S19] p.504, footnote), and the Oxford numerical linear algebra group (Wathen) for the preconditioning line |
| Key insight | [stated] "the trust-region problem within the currently generated Krylov subspace has a very special structure which enables it to be solved very efficiently" (abstract, p.504). So keep the CG iteration while it is interior, and switch to Lanczos and continue once the boundary is hit. For KKT systems: keep the constraint blocks exactly in the preconditioner and analyse the spectrum and minimal polynomial of the preconditioned matrix (abstract [S20]) |
| Minimum evidence | Numerical sections **not read** (only the abstract and introduction were read). The KKT paper illustrates its eigenvalue claims on an example "taken from the CUTE collection" ([S20] p.1307) [practice] |
| Abandoned paths | Not documented in what I read [gap] |
| Reception | [practice] GLTR shipped as HSL_VF05 ("The resulting software package is available as HSL VF05 within the Harwell Subroutine Library", abstract), later as the GALAHAD package GLTR and a Matlab `gltr` [S02][S18]. It was reused as the trust-region solver in the ARC Part I comparison ([S14] p.289). Citations: GLTR 340 and KKT preconditioning 390 (S2). The line was still alive in 2025 with TREK/NREK |
| Method it shows | Treat the linear algebra of the subproblem as a first-class research object and ship it as a reusable library component (candidate method M3) |

### D. GALAHAD (2003): the turning point away from LANCELOT, and the SQP that was suspended

Work: Gould, Orban, Toint, *ACM TOMS* 29(4) (2003) 353–372, 10.1145/962437.962438, read together with Gould's single-author SIAG/OPT essay of April 2003 [S11].

| Dimension | Finding |
|---|---|
| Origin | [stated, primary] "It is fair to say that we (and others) recognised its limitations from the outset, and it has long been our goal to eventually provide a suitable successor." Work on a Fortran 90 LANCELOT B "started in 1995", with "a working prototype" by 1997 ([S18] p.354) |
| Why then (trigger) | [stated] Other groups' benchmarks: "a number of our colleagues had started to release the results of comparative tests of their new codes — for instance SNOPT …, LOQO …, KNITRO …, and FilterSQP … — against LANCELOT A, and the results made frankly rather depressing reading for us … LANCELOT often, but far from always, being significantly outperformed." Their conclusion: "the limit of what might be achieved by augmented Lagrangian methods such as LANCELOT A had probably been reached." ([S18] p.354) |
| Key insight | [stated] Build the QP layer first and release it as a library: "we decided that our next goal should be to produce high-quality QP codes for eventual incorporation in our own SQP algorithm(s)", and "far from producing a single package, we are now in effect building a library of independent but interrelated packages" (p.354). Version 1.0 was openly called "a stop-gap" (p.354) |
| Minimum evidence | [stated] Internal QP comparisons: "Considerable numerical experience has indicated to us that the interior-point version, QPB is almost always vastly superior for large problems" ([S11] p.4) |
| Abandoned paths | [stated] Months later the SQP plan was suspended: "Our experience has been that without QP truncation, the cost of the QP solution so dominates that other non-SQP approaches (such as IPOPT [33], KNITRO [4] and LOQO [32]), in which truncation is possible, have made significant progress even before our QP code had solved its first subproblem!" and "Since we have now all but given up our SQP developments, we have now turned to what we consider to be the other possibility, namely to solve general constrained optimization problems by sequential barrier-function minimization, using the lessons learned when designing and evaluating QPB." ([S11] p.4). The stated principle behind it: "if there is one lesson we should have learned from large-scale unconstrained minimization, it is to aim to solve the subproblem as inaccurately as possible consistent with overall convergence" (p.4). A parallel bet was the LP-plus-equality-QP approach with Byrd, Nocedal and Waltz (*Math. Program.* 2004, 10.1007/s10107-003-0485-4): "We are more enthusiastic about an SLP-QP approach we are currently developing [3], since LP truncation is in principle easier and since the QP phase is restricted to a problem with equality constraints for which a truncated conjugate-gradient iteration is possible." (p.4) |
| What happened next | [practice] SQP came back in papers (second-derivative SQP with Robinson, 2010; filter SQP with Loh and Robinson, 2014–2015), but as of HEAD 2026-09-26 no general-NLP SQP solver has been promoted out of `src/forthcoming` (FiSQP) [S09]. Judging by directory names, the only released general constrained-NLP solver is still `lancelot` (agent 03 to confirm); newer attempts (COLT 2023, EXPO 2024–25) are also in `forthcoming` [S09]. The interior-point / barrier NLP solver announced in 2003 does not appear as a released package either [inferred from the directory tree; confirm with agent 03] |
| Reception | GALAHAD: 194 (OpenAlex) / 221 (S2) citations. JOSS 2023 paper reviewed by Frank E. Curtis and J. A. J. Hall [S10]. Now shipped with C, Python (PyPI `galahad-optrove`) and Julia interfaces [S09] |
| Method it shows | Let other people's benchmarks overrule attachment to your own code, build the enabling layer (QP, subproblems) as reusable packages, and say publicly what you gave up (candidate methods M4 and M5). **Tension**: stated abandonment versus a later return (see Contradictions) |

### E. ARC, Parts I and II (2011), → the evaluation-complexity program → the 2022 SIAM book

| Dimension | Finding |
|---|---|
| Origin | [stated, primary] Synthesis of three independent lines, Griewank's unpublished 1981 DAMTP report, Nesterov–Polyak 2006 and Weiser et al. 2007: "Our purpose here and in [2] is to unify and extend these contributions into a coherent and numerically efficient algorithmic framework, for which global and asymptotic convergence results can be proved under weaker assumptions and with simpler proofs, while preserving the good complexity bound shown by Nesterov and Polyak [23]." | Why then | [inferred, with dated evidence] Nesterov–Polyak's 2006 complexity result for cubic regularization had no numerical results, "no numerical results were provided" ([S14] p.247). A global minimizer of the model over a suitable subspace (Lanczos/Krylov) suffices for the good complexity bound (p.248) |
| Minimum evidence | [practice, primary] Matlab 7.2 on a 3.06 GHz Dell workstation; all 131 small unconstrained CUTEr problems; an iteration-count performance profile of ARC against "a standard trust-region method" (with GLTR as the TR solver); ARC variants failed 3 times and TR 8 times (p.289–290). Honest limits: "We would have liked to have given a similar figure for CPU times, but the Matlab CPU timer proved too inaccurate for this purpose—we defer such a comparison until we have produced a careful (Fortran) implementation and tested it on larger examples in a controlled computing environment." (p.289) |
| Abandoned paths | [practice] The preprint title said "cubic **overestimation**" (RAL-TR-2007-007) and the published one says "cubic **regularisation**" (DataCite [S33]); the reason is not stated. [stated] Their own results undercut the complexity-driven stopping rules: "Of the three variants of the new method, that which is less concerned with provably superior worst-case complexity, appears to be more promising." (p.289). BARC, the bound-constrained version, has sat in GALAHAD `forthcoming` since 2008 [S09]. A 2014 constrained-complexity result had to be corrected in 2017 [S24] |
| Reception | [practice] ARC I 466 citations (S2) with 111 influential, the highest ratio among his top works. The SIAM book in 2022. [stated] Their own framing of worst-case results: "Despite its pessimistic outlook, the worst-case perspective is nonetheless reassuring as it allows us to know what to expect in the worst-case from methods we might use. Clearly, the view of the optimization world we most commonly encounter involves the typical-case performance of methods, which is usually far better than the bounds and behaviour discussed here." ([S13] p.9) |
| Method it shows | Unify scattered proposals under weaker assumptions, pair the theory with a small but honest CUTEr test, and treat worst-case complexity as a guarantee, not as a predictor of practical speed (candidate methods M1, M6 and M8) |

## 3. Patterns across the record: candidate methods for Phase 2

These are patterns only. Each needs the Phase 2 four-way check (the stated-versus-practice comparison comes from agents 02 and 03).

- **M1: Pair a theory paper with a testing paper, and a method with shipped code.** [practice] CGT 1988 *SINUM* + *Math. Comp.*; 1991 theory → LANCELOT 1992 → 1996 tests; GLTR → HSL_VF05; ARC I (theory and CUTEr tests in one paper) → GALAHAD `arc`; arXiv 2511.11135 → `src/trek`, `src/nrek`.
- **M2: Build and maintain test infrastructure; question the evaluation tools.** [practice + stated] CUTE → CUTEr → CUTEst over roughly 20 years; the performance-profile caution (2016); on software testing: "At first sight, software testing and comparision [sic] may seem a rather mundane and unchallenging part of the algorithmic development process, but fortunately this view has now been widely replaced with the realization of its crucial nature." ([S12] preprint p.4, co-authored with Toint).
- **M3: The subproblem is the product.** [practice] The TR subproblem (1999, 2010, 2020 with Simoncini, 2025), KKT and saddle-point preconditioning (2000–2014), and QP (1991 10.1093/imanum/11.3.299; 2002 working-set, 10.1016/s0168-9274(02)00120-4; 2004 preprocessing; 2013 trajectory-following, 10.1007/S12532-012-0050-3; 2017 dual gradient projection) recur in every period.
- **M4: Publish negative evaluations, including of popular methods and of your own code.** [stated, primary] "Unfortunately, particularly given the large literature which might make one think otherwise, numerical tests indicate that in general none of the variants considered are especially effective or competitive with more sophisticated alternatives." and "despite the large number of theoretical papers devoted to generalizations and convergence issues, there appears to have been little effort to investigate how they really perform in practice. In this paper, we attempt to do so in perhaps the most favourable circumstances" ([S28] pp.1–2). Also the "depressing reading" admission about LANCELOT ([S18] p.354), and the note that a comparison with MINOS is "in itself, a major research effort" ([S26] p.74).
- **M5: Suspend a line when benchmarks say so, and say it in print.** [stated] LANCELOT B (2003), SQP (2003). **But** see Contradictions: SQP returned in 2008–2015.
- **M6: Correct errors in public.** [practice] 1989, 2012 and 2017 errata or corrigenda (§1.7).
- **M7: Transfer what worked for unconstrained large-scale problems to the constrained case.** [stated] 1991 *SINUM* p.546; the SIAG 2003 "one lesson" on truncating subproblems.
- **M8: Tune and state algorithm parameters on large test sets.** [stated] "the numerical efficiency of these algorithms can easily be improved by choosing appropriate parameters. Recommended ranges of values for these parameters are exhibited on the basis of extensive numerical tests." (*4OR* 2005 abstract [S29]). ARC I reports its σ-update constants and says "the choice of the "2" factor is for simplicity, and it is likely that better values are possible" ([S14] p.289).

## Contradictions (kept, not reconciled)

1. **SQP: stated abandonment vs later practice.** In April 2003: "Since we have now all but given up our SQP developments…" ([S11] p.4). In practice he then wrote second-derivative SQP papers with Robinson (*SIOPT* 2010, 10.1137/080744542 and 10.1137/080744554; *IMA JNA* 32 (2012), online 2011, 10.1093/imanum/drq043), filter SQP with Loh and Robinson (*SIOPT* 2014 and 2015), and the FiSQP Fortran code (translated in 2014, header revised in 2025) [S09]. The 2003 GALAHAD paper promised "the next-generation SQP solvers we intend to introduce in Version 2 of the library" ([S18] p.354). No SQP solver is released in GALAHAD as of 2026-09-26 [S09].
2. **LANCELOT B: "abandoned" vs shipped.** GALAHAD 2003 says "we abandoned any plans to release LANCELOT B at that time", yet GALAHAD 1.0 "includes the upgraded B version of LANCELOT" ([S18] p.354). It still ships as `src/lancelot` in 2026 [S09].
3. **Editor-in-Chief of *SIAM J. Optim.*: "from 2005 until 2010"** (homepage [S01]) **vs "2004–2010"** (CV [S02]).
4. **Citation counts** differ widely: *Trust-Region Methods* 3019 (OpenAlex) vs 1851 (S2); CUTE 1995 is missing from OpenAlex's profile but has 955 citations in S2; OpenAlex splits LANCELOT into two records (482 + 293) against 714 in S2.
5. **Years (online vs issue)**: ARC I 2009/2011; CUTEst 2014/2015; the least-squares preconditioners 2016 (CV) vs 2017 (DBLP); CGT constrained complexity 2012 online vs 2014 issue. This matters for any per-period count.
6. **Erratum authorship.** The published trust-funnel erratum lists Gould and Toint ([S22]), while the technical report it defers to, and the CV entry, add D. P. Robinson ([S22] ref. 4; [S02] item 49).
7. **Complexity focus vs practical preference inside ARC I.** The paper launched a complexity program, yet its own numerics favoured the variant "less concerned with provably superior worst-case complexity" ([S14] p.289).
8. **OpenAlex vs reality.** OpenAlex attributes law, medical and physics papers to him and labels whole periods with the wrong topics (§0).
