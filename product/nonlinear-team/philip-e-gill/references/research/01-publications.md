## 0. A warning on reading Gill's author order (it breaks the usual "first-author to last-author" test)

- The usual signal (first author early in a career, last author as PI later) **does not apply** to Gill. His optimization-methods papers list authors **alphabetically**, from the NPL papers with Murray (1972 onward) to the papers with his students in the 2020s: Gertz & Gill 2004, Erway & Gill 2009, Forsgren–Gill–Wong 2016, Ferry–Gill–Wong–Zhang 2024, Brust & Gill 2024.
  - [observed · primary, co-author's first-hand account] Margaret H. Wright, INFORMS History & Traditions interview (2019, auto-captions, 00:26:51): "decided that we would always have all four names on what we did and that they would be an alphabetical order Phillip liked that a lot because it would always be Gil at all" [sic, auto-caption]. The stated reason follows in the same passage: "we decided that there was no way we could work closely together and have arguments about you know who gets the credit whose name gets to go first".
  - [practice · primary bibliographic check] I tested the author order of the 154 multi-author OpenAlex records. 129 are alphabetical by surname. Four of the 25 exceptions are metadata artifacts, where OpenAlex stores "Murray,Walter" in surname-first form and the real order is alphabetical. Six are works by other people named Gill, misattributed to him (see §1.1). The genuine exceptions are almost all application papers led by other disciplines: Gorelick et al. 1984 (hydrology), Petzold et al. 1997, Masic–Skelton–Gill 2005 (tensegrity), Chan et al. 2011 (video), Gu et al. 2011, Li–Conte–Gill 2019 and Subramanian et al. 2020. There are also three outliers. The 1992 AIAA paper is ordered Gill, Murray, Shi, Nelson, Young, Saunders (Crossref, DOI 10.2514/6.1992-734). The 2004 report is ordered Kroyan, Gill. The GAMS/SNOPT manual has Drud and Kalvelagen appended after the original three authors.
  - Consequence: the OpenAlex table of first-author and last-author shares (for example, "first 22 / last 0" for 1985–89) measures the alphabet, not seniority. The shift from hands-on work to supervision has to be read from **who the co-authors are** (§1.4), not from author positions. [inferred]

## 2. Signature works (5), dissected per framework §五

Selection: the most cited (SNOPT; *Practical Optimization*), the earliest high-impact foundation (1974 factorization updates), the turning point into interior methods (1986), and the late-career turn that Gill himself narrated (2011 slides → the stabilized/regularized SQP line, 2010–2024). Gill's own ranking of his works was **not found** (see Gaps).

### 2.1 "Methods for modifying matrix factorizations" (Gill, Golub, Murray, Saunders; Math. Comp. 28 (1974) 505–535; DOI 10.1090/s0025-5718-1974-0343558-6)

- **Full text**: not read (AMS returned HTTP 403 to curl and WebFetch). The abstract was read via OpenAlex's index.
- **Origin**: [inferred] It comes out of the NPL work on quasi-Newton methods, which already recurred a factorization of the Hessian approximation. The 1972 abstract says the method "is based on recurring the factorization of an approximation to the Hessian matrix" and that this allows "greater flexibility when choosing the direction of search while minimizing the adverse effects of rounding error" (IMA J. Appl. Math. 1972, abstract via OpenAlex). The Stanford link: Gill visited Stanford around 1973 (Wright interview, about 00:18: "Philip Gill who worked at the National Physical Laboratory in England had come to Stanford as a visitor I think in 73"), where Golub and his former student Saunders were. [observed · primary, auto-caption]
- **Why then**: [inferred] Rank-one updating algorithms existed but were scattered across applications. The abstract says so directly (next bullet).
- **Key insight** (abstract, verbatim): "These methods have always been given in the context of specific applications and this has probably inhibited their use over a wider field." and "A uniform notation has been used and emphasis has been placed on illustrating the similarity between different methods." So the contribution was to unify updating algorithms into reusable, numerically stable primitives, and to add "a new algorithm … for modifying the complete orthogonal factorization of a general matrix". [practice · primary abstract]
- **Minimum evidence**: not read.
- **Abandoned paths**: no source found; [inferred] none can be stated.
- **Reception**: 586 OpenAlex citations on the AMS DOI, plus 102 on the JSTOR DOI. Reprinted in *Milestones in Matrix Computation* (2007; DBLP incollection, listed with Golub first). The same primitives reappear as infrastructure 30 years later: the SNOPT paper cites it and relies on "sparse rank-revealing LU factors" and factor updates (SIAM Rev. 2005, p. 100). [practice]
- **Method it shows** (candidate): *make the linear-algebra primitive robust and general first, then build optimization methods on top of it*; *unify scattered special cases in one notation*.

### 2.2 *Practical Optimization* (Gill, Murray, Wright; Academic Press 1981; SIAM Classics reissue 2019, DOI 10.1137/1.9781611975604)

- **Full text**: not read (SIAM front matter returned 403; the Internet Archive copy is borrow-only). The chapter list was verified through the Crossref and OpenAlex chapter DOIs: 1 Introduction, 2 Fundamentals, 3 Optimality Conditions, 4 Unconstrained Methods, 5 Linear Constraints, 6 Nonlinear Constraints, **7 Modelling, 8 Practicalities** (DOIs 10.1137/1.9781611975604.ch1–ch8). [practice · primary metadata]
- **Origin**: [observed · primary, Wright interview 00:27:53–00:28:09, auto-caption] The Gang of Four "had talked for quite a while about writing a book"; Saunders declined ("I don't want to be a co-author … it's going to be kind of away from the research that I want to do"); "so the three of us started working on this book practical optimization". Wright says it was typeset in early TeX, with camera-ready copy given to Academic Press ("the second book to be published using tech" [sic] is her claim, not verified).
- **Why then**: [inferred] By 1981 the NPL and NAG library work (1970s) and the SOL codes had produced a body of implementation know-how, such as finite-difference intervals (1983 paper) and modelling advice ("Aspects of mathematical modelling related to optimization", Appl. Math. Model. 1981, DOI 10.1016/0307-904x(81)90002-0), which the existing literature did not cover.
- **Key insight**: [inferred from the chapter list] It treats modelling and "practicalities" (scaling, derivatives, choosing a method, interpreting output) as part of optimization proper, at book-chapter weight.
- **Minimum evidence / abandoned paths**: not found.
- **Reception**: Semantic Scholar 7,607 citations (all editions); reissued by SIAM Classics 38 years later. [practice]
- **Method it shows** (candidate): *write down the tacit implementation knowledge*; *judge a method by how it behaves on real models, including badly posed ones*.

The group's 1970s expertise in penalty and barrier methods (Murray's thesis on penalty trajectories; Wright's thesis on barriers) met a new LP claim at the moment when almost nobody else still held that expertise. The Forsgren–Gill–Wright 2002 abstract confirms the wider context: "By the early 1980s, barrier methods were almost without exception regarded as a closed chapter in the history of optimization … in 1985, a formal connection was established between his method and classical barrier methods." [stated · primary, co-authored abstract]
- **Key insight**: Karmarkar's projective method is a projected Newton barrier method with a particular choice of barrier parameter. In Wright's description of the 1985 ISMP talk (00:37:44): "I gave the theorem showing there was of equivalence you had to choose the barrier parameter in a certain way but the steps were identical and then I gave our numerical results" [sic].
- **Minimum evidence**: [observed] a theorem plus numerical comparisons with the simplex method. The interviewer recalls the results as "sometimes it is better than simplex and sometimes it's not" (interviewer's paraphrase, not Gill's words).

### 2.4 SNOPT (Gill, Murray, Saunders; SIOPT 12 (2002) 979–1006, DOI 10.1137/S1052623499350013; expanded SIGEST version SIAM Rev. 47 (2005) 99–131, DOI 10.1137/S0036144504446096) — most cited

- **Full text**: read in part (both versions; pages cited from the SIAM Review version unless noted).
- **Origin**: [stated · primary] Users were outgrowing NPSOL. "Although NPSOL has solved OTIS examples with as many as two thousand constraints and over a thousand variables, the need to handle increasingly large models has provided strong motivation for the development of new sparse SQP algorithms. Our aim is to describe a new SQP method that has the favorable theoretical properties of the NPSOL algorithm but is suitable for a broad class of large problems, including those arising in trajectory optimization." (SIAM Rev. 2005, p. 101). Gill's 2011 timeline dates the SNOPT work "1992–" (slide 6).
- **Why then**: [inferred, with basis] Aerospace trajectory models (OTIS at McDonnell Douglas) had grown beyond dense SQP, and sparse LU technology (LUSOL, MINOS) already existed in the same group. The 1992 AIAA paper with the McDonnell Douglas engineers (DOI 10.2514/6.1992-734) and the 1994 trajectory chapter (DOI 10.1007/978-3-0348-8497-6_3) sit at the start of the SNOPT timeline. Both were verified by DOI and not read.
- **Key insight**: a sparse SQP method with a limited-memory quasi-Newton Hessian, a reduced-Hessian active-set QP solver (SQOPT), a smooth augmented Lagrangian merit function, and an **elastic (ℓ1) mode** for infeasible constraints. It is "best suited for problems with a moderate number of degrees of freedom (say, up to 2000)" (abstract). [stated · primary]
- **Minimum evidence**: [inferred] Production trajectory runs. The conclusion says "the sparse-matrix techniques have permitted production runs on increasingly large trajectory problems" (p. 127).
- **Honesty about failures** [practice · primary]: The paper names every failed CUTEr problem. For example: "Of these 19 problems, all but 2 cases must be counted as failures because they are known to have feasible points." (p. 121). It also lists 32 cases stopped by the major-iteration limit and 9 that "could not be improved at a nonoptimal point". It reports an expensive rank-detection failure in detail: on drcavty2, 13 TPP factorizations were needed before the basis was accepted, and "the repeated failures were rather expensive" (p. 114). That episode is the stated reason SQOPT includes a special "BR factorization".
- **Abandoned or limited paths**: [stated] The conclusion marks what the design could not do: "Future work must take into account the fact that second derivatives are increasingly available. The QP solver should allow for indefinite QP Hessians, and additional techniques are needed to handle even more degrees of freedom." (p. 127). Section 8, "Alternative QP Solvers", discusses range-space, least-squares CG and Schur-complement options as "future possibilities" (pp. 124–126). [practice] These limits become the agenda of the next 20 years: SQIC and the Gill & Wong QP work (2012–2015), convexification in DNOPT (2015), and stabilized SQP (§2.5).
- **Reception**: "Received by the editors January 11, 1999; accepted for publication (in revised form) November 15, 2001" (SIOPT footnote), almost three years in review, and the NA 97 reports date the method to 1997. Selected for SIGEST: "This paper originally appeared in SIAM Journal on Optimization … All sections have been expanded to cover new algorithmic features and more extensive test problems." (SIAM Rev. 2005, p. 99 footnote). The test set grew from "about 900 examples" (2002) to "about 1020 examples" (2005). Most-cited item in every source. [practice · primary]
- **User loop**: "We extend sincere thanks to our colleagues Dan Young and Rocky Nelson of the Boeing Company (formerly McDonnell Douglas Space Systems …) for their constant support and feedback during the development of SNOPT." (p. 127). [stated · primary]
- **Method it shows** (candidate): *start from a real user population's scale wall*; *keep the proven theory of the predecessor code while re-engineering the linear algebra*; *report results on the entire public test set and name every failure*; *state in print what the design cannot do, then work on exactly that*.

### 2.5 The regularized/stabilized SQP and shifted penalty-barrier program (2010–2024) — the self-narrated late turn

Core papers (all verified): Gill & Robinson, "A primal-dual augmented Lagrangian" (COAP 2010/2012, DOI 10.1007/s10589-010-9339-1); Gill & Robinson, "A Globally Convergent Stabilized SQP Method" (SIOPT 23 (2013) 1983–2010, DOI 10.1137/120882913); Gill, Kungurtsev & Robinson, "A stabilized SQP method: global convergence" (IMA JNA 37 (2017) 407–443, DOI 10.1093/imanum/drw004) and "…: superlinear convergence" (Math. Prog. 163 (2017) 369–410, DOI 10.1007/s10107-016-1066-7); Gill, Kungurtsev & Robinson, "A Shifted Primal-Dual Penalty-Barrier Method for Nonlinear Optimization" (SIOPT 30 (2020) 1067–1093, DOI 10.1137/19M1247425); Gill & Zhang, projected-search interior-point method (COAP 88 (2024) 37–70, DOI 10.1007/s10589-023-00549-1). The fair-comparison paper that motivates the line is Gill, Saunders & Wong (2015, DOI 10.1007/978-3-319-23699-5_5).

- **Origin**: [stated · primary, 2011 Manchester slides] Slide 26: "In the late 1980s/early 1990's, research on SQP methods declined. Three reasons (but interconnected): The rise of interior-point methods / The rise of automatic differentiation packages / Computer architecture evolved". Slide 32: "Many important applications require the solution of a sequence of related optimization problems … The common feature is that we would like to benefit from good approximate solutions." Slide 35 lists the aims: "to define an SQP method that exploits second derivatives", "to provide a globally convergent method that is provably effective for degenerate problems", "allow the use of modern sparse matrix packages", "Do all of the above as seamlessly as possible!"
- **Why then**: [stated · primary] Slide 36: "Methods based on sparse updating are hard to speed up / Reformulate methods to shift the emphasis from sparse matrix updating to sparse matrix factorization / Thereby exploit state-of-the-art linear algebra software / Less reliance on specialized “home grown” software". Slide 28 ties this to multicore hardware ("Moore's Law is fading"). The slide states the shift in emphasis. Calling it a reversal of the 1974–2005 bet on custom factorization *updating* (§2.1, §2.4) is my inference [inferred].
- **Key insight** (Gill & Robinson 2013 report, p. 3): "A crucial feature of the method is that the QP subproblem is based on the exact Hessian of the Lagrangian, yet has a unique bounded solution." Benefits: "(i) each QP subproblem is regularized so that the resulting linear systems are always nonsingular and third-party solvers may be used; and (ii) the QP subproblem always has a known feasible point." The obstacle it addresses (p. 2): "The complexity of the QP subproblem has been a major impediment to the formulation of conventional second-derivative SQP methods". [stated · primary]
- **Minimum evidence**: [stated · primary] The 2015 comparison: "The conventional wisdom is that when solving a general nonlinear problem “from scratch” (i.e., with no prior knowledge of the properties of a solution), software based on an IP method is generally faster and more reliable than software based on an SQP method." To test it: "The tests are formulated so that the same derivative information is provided to both packages" (SNOPT7 vs IPOPT on 1153 CUTEst problems; preprint p. 3). The conclusion was "Ultimately, for every problem that is best solved by an SQP code, there will likely exist another that is best solved by an IP code." (p. 26). Section 3 opens: "we present numerical results that have motivated our work" (preprint p. 6).
- **Abandoned paths, renamings and dropped preprints** [practice · primary]:
  - "Regularized Sequential Quadratic Programming" (Gill & Robinson, UCSD NA-11-02; Optimization Online 2011/10). Its abstract language matches the 2013 SIOPT paper ("pairs a primal-dual generalized augmented Lagrangian merit function with a flexible line search"). [inferred] It was retitled "stabilized" for publication.
  - "A Regularized SQP Method with Convergence to Second-Order Optimal Points" (Gill, Kungurtsev, Robinson; Optimization Online 2013/10). No journal version under this title was found in DBLP, OpenAlex or the homepage refereed list. Its fate is unknown (Gaps).
  - "A Shifted Primal-Dual Interior Method for Nonlinear Optimization" (CCoM-18-1; Optimization Online 2018/01) was published in 2020 as "A Shifted Primal-Dual **Penalty-Barrier** Method". [inferred] The reframing puts the penalty-barrier merit function in the foreground.
  - The 2015 paper announced "the forthcoming SNOPT9" (p. 26). The public download page (fetched 2026-09-28) offers trial versions of SNOPT7 and SQOPT7 only, and no SNOPT9 release was found (Gaps; [inferred] delayed or not publicly released).
- **Reception**: modest compared with the software papers (OpenAlex: 94, 50, 52, 29, 14 and 3 citations for the six core papers). [practice]
- **Method it shows** (candidate): *carry one device across the SQP/interior boundary* (dual shifts and primal-dual augmented Lagrangians used for both stabilized SQP and interior methods); *require global convergence plus a local equivalence to a known fast method* ("is equivalent to a variant of the conventional stabilized SQP method in the neighborhood of a solution", Gill & Robinson 2013 report abstract; "the method is equivalent to the conventional path-following interior method in the neighborhood of a solution", Optimization Online 2018/01 abstract); *test the conventional wisdom with controlled comparisons before designing*.

## 5. Candidate research-craft patterns visible in the publication record (for Phase 2; not yet validated)

Each pattern cites at least two independent works. All are [inferred] from practice unless marked otherwise, and they still need the "stated vs practice" check (agents 02 and 03).

1. **Linear algebra first.** Methods are designed around the factorization they need and how it is updated or regularized. Evidence: 1972 IMA, 1974 Math. Comp., 1984 SISSC, 1987 LAA, 1992/1996 SIMAX papers, SNOPT §4–5, and the 2011 slide 36 reversal ("from sparse matrix updating to sparse matrix factorization" [stated]).
2. **Software is the research artifact, with the manual as its paper.** Evidence: NPSOL, LSSOL, QPOPT, SQOPT, SNOPT, DNOPT and SQIC guides; the NPSOL guide is among his five most-cited items; the 1979 TOMS library design paper.
3. **Report every problem in a public test set and name the failures.** Evidence: SNOPT 2002/2005 (CUTE/CUTEr + COPS, failures by name), the 2015 SNOPT vs IPOPT comparison on 1153 CUTEst problems, and the 2020 "Supplementary Numerical Results" report (CCoM 20-08).
4. **Test conventional wisdom with a controlled comparison** (same derivative information to both codes; split results by degrees of freedom). Evidence: 2015 PROMS paper [stated]; Gill & Runnoe 2022: "there is a real need for extensive experimental testing to justify the theoretical basis of each approach. The goal of this report is to implement and test these methods in a uniform, systematic, and consistent way." [stated · primary, CCoM 22-4 abstract]
5. **Unify and survey at length** (one notation, similarities between methods). Evidence: 1974 abstract [stated]; SIAM Review 1991 and 2002; the Gill & Wong 2012 SQP chapter (78 pages); two books.
6. **Problems come from users with a scale or robustness wall.** Evidence: OTIS/NPSOL → SNOPT [stated]; the Boeing acknowledgment; the 1992 AIAA and 1994 trajectory papers; Northrop Grumman and NASA grants; the 2011 slide 32 list of sequences of related problems, MINLP and PDE mesh refinement [stated].
7. **Recognize old methods in new clothing and keep unfashionable expertise.** Evidence: the 1986 Karmarkar equivalence; the barrier-method background [observed]; later primal-dual augmented Lagrangians that revive Hestenes–Powell ("a primal-dual variant of the augmented Lagrangian proposed by Hestenes and Powell in the early 1970s", Optimization Online 2011 abstract [stated]).
8. **Global convergence plus local equivalence to a known fast method** as the acceptance test for a new method. Evidence: 2013 SIOPT, 2018/2020 shifted penalty-barrier, and the 1986 Karmarkar equivalence (a steps-identical theorem).
