## 2. Lineage

### 2.1 Upward (Mathematics Genealogy Project) [observed] (S)
Gilbert Ames Bliss → Magnus R. Hestenes (Chicago 1932) → Richard A. Tapia (UCLA 1967, co-advised by C. B. Tompkins; thesis "A Generalization of Newton's Method with an Application to the Euler-Lagrange Equation") → Jorge Nocedal (Rice 1978, "On the Method of Conjugate Gradients for Function Minimization") → **Frank Edward Curtis** (Northwestern 2007; MGP id 130450).

[inferred] The line runs through calculus of variations and Newton-type methods (Hestenes, Tapia), then quasi-Newton and conjugate gradients (Nocedal). Curtis's own lifelong objects are Newton-SQP steps, merit functions and BFGS, which sit squarely inside this line. In a 2021 public lecture he drew nonlinear continuous optimization as two parallel histories. The slide 17 diagram (labels, not running text) puts Powell, Fletcher, Goldfarb and Nocedal under "smoothness", "sufficient decrease" and fast local convergence, and Fenchel, Rockafellar, Nemirovski and Nesterov under "convexity", "subgradient inequality" and complexity guarantees. The slide then says: "These worlds have (finally) collided! Has it been beneficial?" [stated] (P) ECOM 2021 public lecture. [inferred] His own lineage and papers put him on the smoothness side.

## 3. Turns in the trajectory and what triggered them

Each turn below gives what changed, the trigger, the timing relative to the field, and the resource context.

### T0. Combinatorial matrix theory → optimization (W&M, 1999–2006)
- **What.** [practice] (P) His first paper is pure combinatorics (JCTA 2004, 10.1016/j.jcta.2003.10.001). The second, with OR faculty member Rex Kincaid, is "Determinant Optimization on Binary Matrices" (AJMMS 2006, 10.1080/01966324.2006.10737660). He then entered an IE/OR PhD in 2003.
- **Trigger.** **Not documented.** No statement of why he moved from matrix theory to optimization was found. [inferred] Having two honors-thesis advisors, a mathematician (Li) and an OR modeler (Kincaid), made the bridge possible, and the double major in CS and mathematics fits the later "code written by me" habit.

### T1. PhD: inexact / matrix-free SQP for very large (PDE-constrained) problems (2004–2009)
- **What.** [practice] (P)
  - Byrd, Curtis, Nocedal, "An Inexact SQP Method for Equality Constrained Optimization", SIOPT 19(1) 2008, 10.1137/060674004 (received 2 Nov 2006).
  - "An inexact Newton method for nonconvex equality constrained optimization", MP 2010, 10.1007/s10107-008-0248-3.
  - Curtis, Nocedal, Wächter, "A Matrix-Free Algorithm for Equality Constrained Optimization Problems with Rank-Deficient Jacobians", SIOPT 2009, 10.1137/08072471X.
  - Curtis, Schenk, Wächter, SISC 2010, 10.1137/090747634, which put the method inside IPOPT.
- **Trigger (stated).**
  - The 2008 paper aims at "very large problems with t ≤ n for which the exact computation of steps in contemporary methods can be prohibitively expensive. One class of problems of this type that demands algorithmic improvements are those where the constraint functions are defined by systems of partial differential equations (PDEs)." Its aim: "The main purpose of this paper is to determine the accuracy with which the SQP subproblems must be solved in order to ensure global convergence in the context of a practical algorithm". [stated] (P) ByrdCurtNoce08 pp. 351–352.
  - The ICCOPT 2007 conclusion slide ("Matrix-free Optimization"): "… focused on a particular class of problems to which contemporary optimization techniques cannot be applied". [stated] (P)
  - **The trigger was an application class that existing solvers could not reach, not a new mathematical tool.**
- **Resource context.** [practice] (P)
  - The 2008 paper footnotes support for Curtis from DOE grant DE-FG02-87ER25047 and for Nocedal from NSF and "a grant from the Intel Corporation". Curtis interned at Intel in 2005 (CV).
  - Experiments were run as Matlab prototypes with Krylov solvers; note 01 (not re-checked) records GMRES on CUTEr/COPS problems.
  - His university seminars in Feb–Mar 2007 (CMU Tepper, Emory, UNC, Courant; CV [221]–[224]) were all titled "Inexact Methods for PDE-Constrained Optimization" or "Numerical Methods for PDE-Constrained Optimization". [inferred] These were job-market talks, going by their timing just before the Courant postdoc.
- **Timing.** [inferred] He entered **while the question was open**. Inexact Newton–Krylov for PDE-constrained optimization existed, but a globally convergent full-space line-search inexact SQP did not. His contribution was the convergence condition, and it was then pushed into a production code (IPOPT).
- **Exit.** [practice] The PDE application framing fades after about 2012:
  - last CV talks with "PDE" in the title: Apr 2009 (Wisconsin) and Oct 2009 (COR@L seminar); the last session he organized on it was "Large-Scale/PDE-Constrained Optimization" at INFORMS 2010;
  - the course "IE496 Numerical Methods for Optimal Control" was taught once, F'12.
  - The *principle* survives (see §4).

- **What.** [practice] (P)

- **Trigger.** A **move**: a new host with a different toolkit. [practice] (P) He was a postdoc at Courant with Overton, 2007–09. [stated] (P) The paper frames the idea as a combination: "The successes of these and other methods for smooth constrained and nonsmooth unconstrained problems have laid the groundwork for nonsmooth constrained optimization algorithms."
