**Shape of the evidence.** Nesterov has not written a "how I do research" essay, and no advice-to-students text turned up. The closest thing is a short programmatic essay whose title is itself a methodology claim, *How to advance in Structural Convex Optimization* (Optima 78, 2008), which he re-used almost verbatim as his ICM 2010 paper. Around it sit: the introduction of his 2013 Doctor of Sciences dissertation (the fullest statement of his research program, in Russian), a 2012 Optima discussion column, the introductions and discussion sections of his own papers (read as statements of what he thinks a contribution is), and two recent interviews (2023, 2025) where he talks about convexity, proof, students and the state of the field. His book prefaces (1994, 2004, 2018) and his 1997 position paper were not readable through legitimate open channels (see Gaps).

## 2. Problem choice: where problems come from, why now, when to leave

| # | Claim | Source | Tag |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | Look for the hidden flaw in the accepted theory. "After a certain period of time, it became clear that, despite its mathematical excellence, Complexity Theory of Convex Optimization has a hidden drawback." The flaw: convexity can only be checked from the structure of the functions, so "the functional components of the problem are not in the black box the moment we check their convexity and choose minimization scheme. However, we put them into the black box for numerical methods. | P2 | The field looked finished, then cracked: "Таким образом, в то время казалось, что все важные вопросы в этой области были уже заданы и (почти) все отвечены." / "Однако к 2000му году этот монолит дал трещину." tr.: So at that time [mid-1980s] it seemed that all the important questions in this field had been asked and (almost) all answered. / But by 2000 this monolith had cracked. | [S4] 2013 | [stated] P |
| P3 | Why now: the environment changed faster than the methods. Однако полностью вернуться на позиции середины 80х годов оказалось невозможным." tr.: By the end of that period it became clear that users' demands had grown faster than the new methods' abilities. … A worse situation for interior-point methods could not be imagined, since their cost grows at least as the cube of the dimension. … Hence in the early 2000s the question arose of partially rehabilitating the almost forgotten gradient-type methods; but a full return to the position of the mid-80s proved impossible. | [S4] 2013 | [stated] P |

## 3. Idea generation

| # | Claim | Source | Tag |
|---|---|---|---|
| I1 | **The "Golden Rules."** He shows how Cholesky-based solution of Ax = b could be rediscovered, then states: "Imagine for a moment that we do not know how to solve the system of linear equations. In order to discover the above scheme we should apply the following Golden Rules" — "1. Find a class of problems which can be solved very efficiently." "2. Describe the transformation rules for converting the initial problem into desired form. 3. Describe the class of problems for which these transformation rules are applicable." Then: "In Convex Optimization, these rules were used already several times for breaking down the limitations of Complexity Theory." IPMs are his first example (easy class: self-concordant functions under Newton's method), smoothing the second (easy class: smooth problems, read off his complexity table). | [S1] p. 3–4; [S2] pp. 2970–2973 (same rules, numbered (15)) | [stated] P |
| I2 | Start from classical examples: "As usual, it is better to look at classical examples." | [S1] p. 3 | [stated] P |
| I3 | Open the black box, or change the oracle; then a better-than-lower-bound rate is no contradiction. "It is interesting that in fact we do not get any contradiction. Indeed, in order to minimize a smooth approximation of nonsmooth function by an oracle-based scheme, we need to change the initial oracle. Therefore, from mathematical point of view, we violate the Black-Box assumption. On the other hand, in the majority of practical applications this change is not difficult. Usually we can work directly with the structure of our problem, at least in the cases when it is created by us." | [S1] p. 4 | [stated] P |
| I4 | Same move, other years: "As we have already mention [sic], the progress in Convex Optimization was mainly related to discovering the different possibilities for opening the Back Box [sic] for numerical methods." (2010) · Smoothing: "Our approach can be considered as an alternative to black-box minimization." (2003) · Problem-oriented methods beat black-box limits: "This can be done, of-course, only by an appropriate use of problem structure, violating one of the main assumptions of the Black-Box concept." (2013) · Superfast methods beat the known second-order lower bound: "Our progress can be explained by a finer specification of the problem class." (2021) · Co-authored, 2024: the superfast rate "is not a contradiction with classical complexity theory for second-order methods." | [S2] p. 2967; [S12] CORE DP 2003/12 abstract; [S9] p. 2; [S11] JOTA 2021 abstract; [S15] Math. Program. 2024, §1 (with M. Ahookhosh) | [stated] P |
| I5 | Structure is hard to formalize, and fixing the analytic form is fragile: "Intuitively, we always hope that the structure of the problem can be used for improving the performance of minimization schemes. Unfortunately, structure is a very fuzzy notion, which is quite difficult to formalize." … "It can help, but this approach is very fragile: If we add just a single constraint of another type, then we get a new problem class, and all theory must be redone from scratch." | [S1] p. 3; [S2] p. 2969 | [stated] P |
| I6 | Progress comes from refining the *model of the objective*: "Их разработка стала возможной благодаря уточнению понятия модели целевой функции. Полезно различать три типа моделей." tr.: Their development became possible by refining the notion of a model of the objective function; it is useful to distinguish three types of models. These are (paraphrase): geometric models (a simple set, such as the Dikin ellipsoid or a first/second-order upper approximation, sits inside a complicated object), algebraic models (an explicit max-representation of the function, used in primal-dual subgradient methods and smoothing), and structural models (a simple object paired with a complicated one). On the last: "Простой объект может иметь плохие функциональные характеристики. Однако он должен допускать решение некоторых вспомогательных задач с его участием в явном виде." tr.: The simple object may have bad functional properties, but it must allow explicit solution of certain auxiliary problems involving it. | [S4] 2013 | [stated] P |
| I7 | The analyst's first step is a model of the problem class: "Optimization problems are usually related to some models of the real-life situations. On the other hand, in order to develop a method for solving such problems, a numerical analyst starts from creating an appropriate model of a particular class of optimization problems." … "Finally, fixation of the model allows us to perform a worst-case complexity analysis and to develop the optimal schemes." And: "The progress in performance of methods in Convex Optimization during the last three decades is closely related to evolution of the above models and to better understanding of significance of the help we can offer to numerical schemes by opening an access to the structure of problem instances." | [S2] pp. 2964–2965 | [stated] P |
| I8 | Small change of problem class, big change of conclusion: "Let us show that a minor change in the initial conditions dramatically changes our conclusions." (He then changes the nonconvex problem class so that the lower bound becomes exponential in n.) | [S3] p. 11 | [stated] P |
| I9 | Old ideas deserve a second look, with a new ingredient. Prototypes list in T1; "Actually, in a verbal form, the optimality of the extra-gradient method was known already for a couple of decades. However, a rigorous proof of this important fact and discussion of its consequences for Structural Nonsmooth Optimization was published only in [7], after discovering the smoothing technique." ([7] = Nemirovski 2004.) For huge-scale sparse problems: "We managed to find only two simple subgradient methods for nonsmooth minimization, which can get a full advantage from the sparsity of subgradients. These are the oldest subgradient methods by Polyak [8] and Shor [10]." For tensor methods: "The idea of using the high-order approximations in Optimization is not new." | [S1] pp. 4–5; [S8] p. 2; [S10] Math. Program. 2021, §1 | [stated] P |
| I10 | Find the one property that makes the auxiliary problem tractable: "The key observation, which underlies all results of this paper, is that an appropriately regularized Taylor approximation of convex function is a convex multivariate polynomial." Earlier attempts "failed by the standard obstacle related to the enormous complexity of minimization of nonconvex multivariate polynomials." | [S10] §1 | [stated] P |
| I11 | Ask whether an easy class can help a hard one: "Can the easy problems from C2 help us somehow in finding an approximate solution to the difficult problems from C1? And the evident answer is: Yes, of course!" | [S1] p. 4 | [stated] P |

## Era and resource context of these statements

- **USSR period, 1977–1992**: researcher at the Central Economic Mathematical Institute (Wikipedia [S22], S); PhD 1984, Institute of Control Sciences, thesis "Numerical methods for degenerate optimization problems" (AE CV [S21], P). The black-box model "fitted very well the existing computational practice, where the interface between the general optimization packages and the problem data was established by Fortran subroutines created independently by the users" ([S1] p. 2). On contact and pace: letters once a month, "no rush" [S5]. The IMU write-up attributes the Russian school's weight on worst-case analysis to this era: "At the time of his thesis, Russian mathematicians emphasized worst-case analysis of algorithms much more than mathematicians in the West." [S17] [observed] S.
- **UCLouvain from 1993**: the IPM decade, then the 2000s shift to structure. The 2013 dissertation ties the shift to hardware and internet-driven growth of model size (P3). Papers of this period are single-authored CORE Discussion Papers, first released as preprints (practice; agent 03).
- **2010s**: huge-scale problems (internet, PageRank-type), experiments on a single PC [S8]. Grants: ERC Advanced Grant 788368 and Russian Science Foundation 17-11-01027 acknowledged in [S10] (practice/context). Many lecture series abroad [S21].
- **2023–2025**: emeritus, AI boom; statements about overflow, email load, citation indices and students' habits [S5, S6]. These are the views of a senior figure looking back and should be read as a senior person's strategy (framework §七 "资深者策略"), not as early-career advice.

## Contradictions and tensions (kept, not reconciled)

1. **Worst-case theory as selector vs. practical cost as veto.** 2008/2010/2013: complexity analysis selects the promising methods and may matter more than preliminary experiments (T1–T4). But the tensor paper (2021) lets log factors "kill" a theoretically optimal method (J2), the universal paper (2013) says theoretically attractive procedures were never tested because they were impractical (J8), and in 2023 the usefulness test is "an hour … reduced to one minute" (T10).
2. **Trusting complexity results.** He makes complexity analysis the main selector (T3), yet in 2023 asks of a complexity guarantee computed on a machine: "But how can you trust that? It is impossible to guarantee, without additional conditions on the function…" (J6). In 2008 he calls worst-case help "limited" (T3).
3. **Nonconvexity.** 2023: "Non-convexity is just the first step", with nonconvex neural-network models seen as not yet understood (T7, P10). But in his own texts he presents nonconvex coverage as a feature: of cubic regularization, "Метод работает и на невыпуклых функциях." (tr.: the method also works on nonconvex functions) [S4]; composite problems "both in convex and nonconvex cases, can be solved with efficiency typical for the good part of the objective" [S14]; and he proves a nonconvex lower bound in [S3].
4. **Black box: criticized and still developed.** The main progress is "opening the Back Box [sic]" (I4), yet he keeps designing black-box methods and says "However, it appears that the Black-Box methods did not reach yet the limits of their performance." [S9] p. 2 (2013); primal-dual subgradient methods are advertised as optimal for black-box lower bounds [S13].
5. **Structure vs. general theory.** 2008/2010: advance by exploiting specific structure, with the caveat that fixing the analytic form is "fragile" (I5). 2023: the field needs "a new and general theory, which covers all possible situations and methods" (P8).
6. **Growth vs. alarm (same 2023 interview).** "I see only more progress." and more problems need more methods, set against "an unprecedented overflow", too much to teach, and a publishing rush that is "Not very comfortable to me" (P8, R1, R2, R4).
7. **Pure mathematics for beginners (same 2025 interview).** "dangerous for beginners because it deals with immortal problems" vs. "valuable because it teaches how to build comprehensive theories" (R6).
8. **Early vs. late.** No statement before 2004 was read, so early-versus-late disagreement cannot be tested; the one early fragment (2004 preface, T5) agrees with the later statements.
